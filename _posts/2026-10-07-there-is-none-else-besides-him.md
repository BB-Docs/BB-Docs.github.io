### Opening

On a day marking terrible events, the group returns to the most fundamental article on the path. Saying "there is none else besides Him" is easy. Saying that the single force behind everything, including every horror, is **good that does good** seems impossible. Rav Laitman says that gap is exactly our place of work: we can never perceive the Creator Himself, only our own vessels against His unchanging background, so the only thing that can change is us.

## Part 1 — We Only Know What We Feel

- The problem is how to define our relation to the Creator. Every attitude comes from us, from how we think and feel, "not according to the Creator, about whom we don't know anything."
- Man consists of feeling and understanding, heart and mind, which influence each other. We examine what we feel; whatever doesn't reach our senses "is philosophy, imagination." So we ascribe to the Creator "not what He is, but what we feel toward Him."
- We only know there is an upper force felt as a kind of environment. When it's limited we call it nature, this world; when it opens up through a person's development, it's called the upper nature. "Everything we talk about in the wisdom of Kabbalah is with respect to the receiver."
- Even "there is none else besides Him" is how a person feels it. When Kabbalists say the Creator gets angry or rejoices, "that's what man feels."
- Still, we must define our attitude: when I feel good, does it come from me, from Him, or from some equivalence between us? Otherwise "we regularly get confused," and it affects our work, our connection, our families, and how a person relates to himself.

## Part 2 — Why There Seem to Be Other Forces

- A person's life divides into times when he is certain that everything comes from a single force, and times when he forgets, hesitates, or is mistaken.
- The Creator confuses him on purpose, as if there were other forces, and "disappears," so that the person will reach certainty that he is under the rule of the Creator alone. This is the correction called **the left rejects and the right pulls closer**.
- **Bring everything to one source.** In all 613 desires there are confusions, since next to each desire there is a thought. From the most concealed, unscrutinized states, I must define one thing: I am under the rule of the single force.
- **Keeping Torah and Mitzvot** means ascribing my whole self, the feeling that I'm alive and who I am, to that force; "my feeling entirely depends on Him." The way of the Creator is to keep grabbing hold of the fact that "I'm inside a single force."
- Humanity, too, is beginning to discover that the world is a global, integral system run by a single force, "call it nature, call it the Creator, call it God. Doesn't matter."

#### The environment and despair

- Because the world is in concealment, a person gets no reinforcement from it. People without a point in the heart pull him toward other goals, so his environment fails him.
- He reaches despair: for a moment he pictures that there is a single force, and a moment later he "finds himself with his thoughts and desires in a different place." Baal HaSulam's point is that this isn't really the environment's influence. "It's through the environment that the Creator confuses you," so that you will search for how to hold on to Him through every disturbance.

## Part 3 — The Cry That Becomes Prayer

- After many awakenings and falls, the upper light brings him to feel that "only He can and will help you. He sent you all these disturbances in order for you to finally know how to ask Him to save you."
- He doesn't want to escape his troubles. He wants to come out from under the rule of his ego, "from the rule of Pharaoh, to become the servant of the Creator," because that is the truth "and all the rest of your states are false." Then the affliction (*nega*) turns into pleasure (*oneg*).
- **Help from above comes as disturbances.** Only someone with a true need to discover the single force gets this help: new desires and inclinations that leave him unable to form any picture, and the sense that everyone else is closer to the Creator than he is.

#### Good that does good, in a world like this

- He can't justify the Creator's behavior. "All the cruelty in the world, where does it come from, if not from the upper force?" Terror, rape, hate, all of it comes from "none else besides Him," and on top of that he must define it as coming from a force that loves without limit.
- This isn't a philosophical question to settle in thought; it's his whole attitude to life. He reaches the point of "if the Creator does not reveal Himself to me, I curse Him... I have no choice. That's what I feel."
- **This is the true demand:** "I can't keep on relating to Him as an evil force. In order to see Him as the good force, I need His help."

## Part 4 — Never Leave His Domain

- Even in the worst state, from which there can be no greater decline, a person must not think there is another authority, the **Sitra Achra**, keeping him from holiness. "As it says, even if a sharp sword hangs over your neck, do not despair of mercy." Descents can be extremely harsh, as much as a person has prepared himself for.
- Pharaoh, Balaam, Balak, Haman, every evil force in the world: "They're angels." The hearts of kings and ministers are in the Creator's hands. In all our dissemination and education, "we push only to one point": there is one force operating everything.
- **Other gods.** The Baal Shem Tov said that one who says there is another force in the world, Klipot, is serving other gods. If for a single moment I think a friend, someone on the street, or I myself initiated a thought or action, "I bow to some other authority, where there's no such thing in the world." Saying "yesterday I didn't want to follow God's ways" is also heresy, but this realization is applied after the moment, not in advance.

## Part 5 — Q&A: Disaster, Memory, and Dissemination

- **How not to just shut our eyes** to national tragedies like the Holocaust, or to personal torment? The opinion of Kabbalah is opposite to that of the **Baal Bait** (landlord), who lives under the control of his ego and defines life from concealment.
- Rav Laitman, born after the war in 1946, grew up where it was "very felt." But from inside concealment one can't define these events any other way. All we can do is disseminate, to ourselves and the world, that there is a single force that is good and does good, "and it's hard to call it an exercise, like the Holocaust." From our low state "we can't define anything." The prophets wrote of worse to come before the full correction.
- "None else besides Him, okay, somehow. But that He is constantly good that does good, that's impossible. But specifically that is our place of work." If we don't reach it each moment, the gap comes back at us in other forms.

#### The notebook

- Less than two months after he came to Rabash, Rabash gave him the notebook in which he had written these articles as he heard them from Baal HaSulam, and didn't give it to anyone else. A veteran saw him reading from it and "went crazy," and Rabash yelled at him not to show it. After Rabash passed away, he published the articles and let students copy the originals, "so no one would have any doubt. These are the most fundamental articles in man's work toward the Creator. That's all the work that we have."

#### Is the request only for us?

- People without a point in the heart "have no one to ask from." Even for us, the request is not for the Creator to change. He treats you 100% good and does not change. "What are you going to ask for, that He'll treat you with 101% goodness?"
- **Prayer** is the development of our vessels of perception: judging ourselves and changing until I discover Him fully. "The upper light is always at complete rest."

## Part 6 — Everything Depends on Me

> The whole difference between us and the common folk is that I say it all depends on me.

- The world asks the Creator to change: "We'll fast, we'll cry out to You, only change for the better." That's why they don't correct their evil inclination. We say the opposite.
- "I only have to change, no one else. Not the world, not the friends, not the group." When it seems the group goes through stages and I get angry at them, "it's me working with my shadow." It's the same with the Creator: "He who flaws, does so from his own flaws."
- Besides Him there is only the will to receive, a point created from absence. That point feels itself inflate into Malchut of Ein Sof, then the worlds and every change, but "nothing changes, only in her feeling." The whole journey of creation is for that point to find that it is inside "none else besides Him, good that does good."

#### Three attitudes to the upper force

| Who | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Secular | Treats reality like any other living being, "like animals." |
| Religious | An upper force rewards good and punishes bad; the question is what He wants (sacrifices, fasting, honor, money), and each religion answers differently. Like a child told to "hit the table back," it ascribes good and bad to the force. |
| Kabbalists | The force is a general, unchanging law of nature with no personal attitude toward me. Everything depends on how much I change. |

- Getting angry at a computer or a car is the same mistake: "besides metal, plastic and electricity, what is there?" That is how faith in a force that rewards and punishes begins.
- So what obliges me to change, if He gains nothing from me? Only **faith above reason**: building an attitude toward Him with no benefit for myself. "He hangs the earth upon nothing" (*Toleh Eretz al Blimah*), the Sefirot of Blimah. The reward is only resembling Him: "the reward of a Mitzvah is to know the Commander."

#### What is above reason?

- About four months after he came to Rabash, driving to the spa in Petah Tikva under a bridge, he finally asked what "above reason" means. He had even written a reminder by the steering wheel without knowing what it was.
- Rabash's example: a friend borrows a thousand shekels and repays you with an envelope holding one shekel, saying, "That's it, I've paid you back." Above reason is not forgiving him but reaching a correction in which you truly received the thousand. This comes only from inner efforts: faith is bestowal instead of reception.
- **If He's constant, why does each feel Him differently?** "We can never feel Him; we feel ourselves," each according to his soul, qualities, and desires. The whole world you feel, which seems outside you, "is your vessel. It's all inside."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **We only know our own impressions.** Everything said about the Creator is said from the receiver's side.
2. **Concealment is deliberate.** The Creator confuses us through other forces and the environment so that we will search for Him and hold on.
3. **The disturbances are help from above.** For one with a true desire, they build the heartfelt demand that only the Creator can answer.
4. **Good that does good is the place of work.** Every horror in the world must be traced to the single force and defined as good, which we can't do without His revelation.
5. **Never grant another authority.** Pharaoh, Haman, the Sitra Achra, even "I didn't want to," are His messengers.
6. **Prayer changes us, not Him.** The light is at absolute rest; we pray to develop our vessels of perception.
7. **Faith above reason is resembling Him.** It is an attitude with no reward but becoming like the Commander.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Ein Od Milvado** | "There is none else besides Him": no force in the world can act against the Creator; all apparent opposition is His will. |
| **Good That Does Good** | The Creator's unchanging attitude to creation, which a person must come to define in every state, however harsh. |
| **The Left Rejects, the Right Pulls Closer** | The correction by which rejections (the left) build a person's need for the Creator's help, followed by drawing near (the right). |
| **Sitra Achra** | "The other side," a seemingly separate force against holiness; in truth also from the Creator. |
| **Baal Bait (Landlord)** | One who lives under his ego's control and defines life from concealment, the opposite of Kabbalah's view. |
| **Prayer** | Not asking the Creator to change, but developing one's own vessels of perception until He is revealed. |
| **Faith Above Reason** | An attitude toward the Creator with no benefit to oneself: bestowal instead of reception. |
| **Toleh Eretz al Blimah** | "He hangs the earth upon nothing": working with no calculation of reward. |
| **Upper Light** | Simple light in absolute rest; all change is in the vessel's perception. |
