### Opening

After a shared reading of Rabash's article — whose central image is the Midrash that every blade of grass has an angel above it striking it and telling it to grow — Rav Laitman moves into his own archival discourse with a much larger claim: a person actually lives inside two completely separate "kinds of reality," the corporeal and the spiritual, and whichever one he is standing in, he cannot understand or even imagine the other. The lecture and Q&A that follow work out what these two realities actually are, why nothing outside a person's own feeling can be called real, and what it would take — and what it would cost — to be moved from one into the other.

## Part 1 — Two Systems, Not Two Places

Rav Laitman states the foundation directly: what a person calls "spirituality and corporeality," or "altruism and egoism," are not locations but two internal systems — "vessels number one" and "vessels number two," or equivalently "senses number one" and "senses number two" — through which a person's thoughts, desires, and calculations run. They do not exist "somewhere out there in space, behind some planets," but only within a person himself.

> "These are simply two systems that exist in us... they don't exist in some form somewhere out there in space, behind some planets, but rather within a person there are two systems."

Whichever system currently has hold of a person determines which world he is in: catching hold through system one is felt as the corporeal world; sensing through system two is felt as the spiritual world. Both are entirely subjective — "a very subjective matter, a personal thing" — and nothing about them can be verified or known from outside a person's own feeling.

## Part 2 — Reality Is Only Feeling

Because nothing exists for a person except what he feels, each of the two states is a complete, closed reality in itself — a "closed box." What a person feels within himself simply *is* "this world" he lives in; there is no separate, objective world to compare it against.

> "Besides feeling, there is nothing. We're only talking about feeling, sensing... What we feel within us is what we call this world that I live in."

This is why the two states are mutually unintelligible: a person standing in one genuinely cannot recognize or picture the other. The corporeal state is bound up with limitation; the spiritual state, Rav Laitman says, is "a feeling of living an eternal life, being in wholeness, being in complete attainment" — unlimited where the first is bounded. No one chooses which state to start in, and the two are never given by request: a person must actively participate in the transition through his own exertion, "our feeling, our effort on our behalf."

## Part 3 — Recognizing the System You're In

The starting system, Rav Laitman explains, is simply the desire to receive — "who I am, altogether." Every person and everything around him gets processed through a single filter: what benefit does this bring me. Moving toward the opposite system, bestowal, requires first honestly studying and recognizing the system already in place — naming it as egoistic and as the direct opposite of system two — before it becomes clear that shifting toward bestowal is, even by egoism's own accounting, "more worthwhile."

> "To first and first of all study the system that I'm in, this egoistic system of this world... which is in its foundation evil and is opposite to system number two... And then I disconnect from the egoistic form and think about how to move to system number two."

Only after this recognition and disconnection does a person begin, in the transition itself, to acquire something new: the ability to control — rather than be controlled by — the first system, seeing its "negativity" and its "lacks" instead of being ruled by them as before.

## Part 4 — What the Transition Actually Buys

Before the shift, a person is entirely governed from outside himself — "my nature, my egoistic nature controls me... this world controls me, and the society controls me." Moving into system two, to whatever extent a person can actually control it, reverses this: he begins to acquire *both* realities, this world and the next, both corporeal and spiritual, rather than being simply subject to the first one.

Rav Laitman is careful to locate the mechanism of this shift in labor, not luck: "this transition that happens through my labor... gives me the possibility to truly control all of reality, both corporeal and spiritual." And the difficulty of that labor is not generic — it lands differently on every person, "personal towards each and every one," since each person's struggle against his own desire to receive, and his struggle to actually acquire the desire to bestow, are each their own separate battle.

## Part 5 — The Personal Cost: Stigma, Language, and Identity

Part of what makes this work hard, Rav Laitman says, has nothing to do with spiritual mechanics at all: a person must go on functioning in ordinary life — work, family, the opinions of society — while wrestling with a vocabulary that has been "spoiled" by upbringing. Words like Torah, Mitzvot, Creator, Created Being, and "righteous" arrive already loaded with corporeal, religious associations that have to be consciously stripped away and reclarified in their "pure spiritual meaning."

He extends this point in two directions: people who never grew up with Torah language carry their own equivalent stigmas about what "Torah" even means, and the Jewish people specifically carry an additional, unchosen burden — an internalized antisemitism "injected into them" from outside — that adds its own layer of "burdening" and "heaviness" on top of the ordinary difficulty of recognizing that two kinds of reality even exist.

## Part 6 — Desire to Receive Is Neutral; Intention Builds the World

A key clarification follows: the desire to receive itself is not evil. The Creator created it "without intention" — it belongs to neither the system of Klipah (the shell) nor the system of Kedusha (holiness); it is simply "matter... which you can use however you want." What actually determines whether an action belongs to the corporeal or the spiritual system is the *intention* layered onto that neutral desire — in order to receive, or in order to bestow.

> "The desire to receive itself does not belong to the system of holiness of Kedushah nor to the system of Klipah. It's simply matter, which you can use however you want."

Because a person's original, default intention was forced onto him — "we were put forcefully into the intention in order to receive" — bestowal initially feels entirely foreign. But Rav Laitman argues the deeper truth runs the other way: since the soul itself is close to the Creator, whose quality is bestowal, a person's real essence is actually closer to bestowal than to the will to receive, which is only "a fiction for a certain process that the soul has to go through."

## Part 7 — Building the Created Being Out of Reflected Light

If identity is not the will to receive, and not simply the will to bestow either, then what is it? Rav Laitman locates it in something built between the two: a "screen and reflected light that did not exist before" — a new reality that belongs only to the created being, since everything else, coming from above downward, is credited to the Creator.

> "What is created by this action is a new reality... it only belongs to the created being. This is actually the created being, because all the other things are the account of the Creator."

The Creator, by contrast, has no will to receive at all — only the desire to do good to His creations, which Rav Laitman calls direct light. A person's own answering intention, built from the substance of his desires plus his chosen direction (to receive or to bestow), is reflected light — and the ongoing work of building that reflected light is, in his words, "the process of giving birth to Adam, to man."

## Part 8 — Why the Path Is Deliberately Confusing, and Why It's Called "Above Reason"

Asked directly why the article — and the material generally — is written in such a confusing way, Rav Laitman does not treat this as an accident. Confusion is deliberate: forcing a person through repeated "landmines" is what produces the "polishing" that reflected light requires; there is, he says, no other way to reach it. He adds a second reason specific to language: Kabbalistic writing avoids the Bible's own religious vocabulary on purpose, because ordinary words like Torah, commandment, and God already come pre-loaded with corporeal associations a reader can't easily shed. Moving from a word's corporeal "branch" to its spiritual "root" is itself part of a person's advancement — which is why, he acknowledges, "for beginners, really it's a big problem."

This same logic explains why the whole shift is described as a *miracle* and reached only through *faith above reason*. The switch from state one to state two happens through a third force — the light of Torah — that belongs to neither system on its own. Even someone still studying Lo Lishma, without the right intention, is gradually changed by this light, which Rav Laitman describes as replacing a person's inner "software": his priorities, his sense of what matters, his felt reality — much as a computer is switched from one operating system to another.

> "This is a code that even when he studies Lo Lishma... the light in the Torah reforms him, meaning that through the light he receives the importance of the Torah."

## Part 9 — Q&A: What Carries Over, and How Sudden the Switch Really Is

A questioner asks where the connection lies between years of unaware labor and whatever a person eventually receives — pointing out that what arrives is often not at all what was expected. Rav Laitman answers that the earlier actions still count, even though they were done "like a baby, without a mind, without discernments": a person touches, without realizing it, the very same desires he will later work with consciously in the spiritual system — he is simply passing, without awareness, through the same terrain, felt for now only as the system of the Klipah.

A second question closes the lesson: is the transition between states sudden, like waking up to a new day, in the way the Exodus from Egypt happened "in haste"? Rav Laitman confirms that yes, it happens in an instant, with no advance warning and no way to plan for it —

> "You have no preparation for that... you could even think beforehand [that] now, or in a few more [moments], it could happen. There's no such thing. You can't say it in advance."

— before the recording is cut off mid-answer.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. What a person calls "spiritual" and "corporeal" are not places but two internal systems (vessels/senses number one and two); each is a complete, self-contained reality that the other cannot be understood from.
2. Nothing exists for a person except what he feels — "besides feeling, there is nothing" — which is why the two states are mutually unintelligible rather than simply different.
3. Advancing toward bestowal requires first honestly recognizing and naming the egoistic system already in place, since it only becomes worthwhile to leave once it is truly seen for what it is.
4. The desire to receive is itself neutral, belonging to neither Klipah nor Kedusha; it is the intention layered onto it — to receive or to bestow — that determines which reality an action belongs to.
5. True identity (the created being) is neither the will to receive nor the will to bestow alone, but the new "screen and reflected light" built through their friction — the only thing genuinely credited to the created being rather than to the Creator.
6. Confusion in the material is deliberate, not accidental — it is the mechanism that forces the "polishing" reflected light requires, and it is why the shift between states is called a miracle, reached through faith above reason rather than understanding.
7. Even study done Lo Lishma still works: the light of Torah gradually "reforms" a person's inner priorities, much as software is replaced in a computer, until state two becomes more important than state one.
8. Unaware effort still counts — earlier actions done "like a baby" touch the same desires later worked with consciously — and the actual switch between states, when it comes, is sudden and unplannable.
