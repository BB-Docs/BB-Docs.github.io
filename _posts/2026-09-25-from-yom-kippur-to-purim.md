### Opening

On the eve of Sukkot, Rav Laitman traces the whole chain of Jewish holidays, from Yom Kippur through Sukkot, Simchat Torah, and Hanukkah to Purim, as one continuous inner process. It moves from uncovering the imperfection of the Kli to its complete correction and filling. His conclusion is practical: the path is made of ascents and descents, and a person controls how fast they pass by **never looking at his own state** and always aiming forward, at the goal, which is found only in the common Kli.

## Part 1 — Garment and Palace: Compensating for Incomplete Vessels

- The inner vessels are formed for **Nefesh, Ruach, and Neshama**. For **Haya and Yechida** (the light of Hochma) we have outer vessels, called **Levush (garment)** and **Heichal (palace)**.
- In our world, clothing and a house (a palace, a temple) stand in for the lack of correction in our vessels. "If we were whole inside, we wouldn't need either a garment, like Adam HaRishon, or a palace."
- As a person is corrected, he stops needing them. Those vessels come to exist *within* him.

## Part 2 — Yom Kippur: The Light That Shows the Kli's Imperfection

- On Yom Kippur we receive an enormous light. We don't take it in; we feel it **before us**, and it shows the depth of our egoism.
- That is why we don't eat, drink, anoint, have marital relations, or wear leather shoes. We are in a state of **opposition to the light**.
- **The ten days of repentance** come before it. Under this light, each part of the ego, arranged in ten Sefirot, examines itself in turn down to **Malchut**, the last, which feels completely separate from and opposite to the light. It is when "a person judges himself: who he is, what he is, and what can be done."
- **From Yom Kippur to Sukkot** we prepare for correction. The correction itself begins on the first day of Sukkot.

#### The language of prayer

Kabbalists wrote the prayers so that anyone can use them. One person simply turns to the Creator without understanding; another sees in the words his own inner desires and how to direct them. It is the same language the Torah is written in, open to "everyone regardless of spiritual degree," from the root up to complete correction, and it covers every one of a person's 620 desires.

## Part 3 — The Holidays as Spiritual Degrees

- **Moshe** wrote everything about receiving the Torah and the soul's rise **up to Bina**. Moshe is "the force that raises all of a person's desires from Malchut to Bina," up to entering the Land of Israel.
- That is why he dies at the Jordan, seeing the land from afar. What comes after is correction beyond Moshe's quality. **Hanukkah and Purim** are holidays a person passes through *after* receiving the Torah.
- "We're not talking about Moshe" as a person leading a flock through the desert. You can read it that way too, "it doesn't matter," but everything is written according to spiritual roots.
- **Every spiritual root has to touch this world.** Everything that happens in the spiritual world has to happen here too. As of today, all of it has happened except **Purim, the end of correction**.

## Part 4 — Sukkot: Building the Kli

On Sukkot we build ourselves a special helper vessel, like a garment or a house: the **sukkah**.

| Feature | Inner meaning Rav Laitman gives |
|---|---|
| **Walls** can be of any material, even artificial | Not what matters |
| **The roof (covering)** must come from the leftovers of the threshing floor and winepress: straw, branches | Made from what is "waste," built on spiritual roots, "not just out of habit" |
| **Seven days** | Only the seven lower Sefirot (**VAK**) are corrected now, not the upper three (Keter, Hochma, Bina). Those are added later, through Hanukkah to Purim. |
| **No gap wider than three handbreadths (Tefachim)** | Light departing from a vessel passes to the upper one, forms in the lower, and rises again. By the third degree the connection between upper and lower disappears entirely. So a person keeps no gap of more than "three handbreadths" between the entering and departing light, "in every state he wants to be in contact with the light." |
| **Each evening's guest** (Abraham, then Moses, Joseph…) | Each day corresponds to a quality being corrected, e.g. Abraham = **Hesed** |

- The laws were written down in the Talmud tractate *Sukkah*, with diagrams, after the destruction of the Temple so they wouldn't be forgotten. Rav Laitman connects them to the **Great Assembly (Knesset HaGedolah)** of seventy great Kabbalists, "great in height," which is Hochma, "and in breadth," which is Bina.
- During the seven days, **Surrounding Light** shines through seven aspects and purifies the seven Sefirot, from Hesed to Malchut.

## Part 5 — Simchat Torah, Hanukkah, and Purim

- **VAK** is "a small illumination of the light of Hochma." On the last day, **Simchat Torah** ("Joy of the Torah"), the rest of the light enters, the light that can be in a vessel not yet corrected. Wine stands for the light of Hochma, hence the joy. *Torah* comes from *Ohr*, light.
- **Hanukkah:** the war of the Maccabees against the Greeks. The Greeks represent the victory of the mind, knowledge of this world, and the craving to work with the light of Hochma on this world's level. The **Maccabees** represent **faith above reason**, the victory of the vessels of Bina. Once a person rises above his uncorrected vessels, he wins and begins to receive a special light.
  - **Oil and wick:** oil (the light of Hochma) doesn't burn by itself, and a wick (the **Masach**) doesn't burn without oil. Together they give light. Hanukkah is "the festival of light, when the light of Hochma begins to burn in the vessel," and it continues until Purim.
- **Purim** is **Gmar Tikkun**, the complete correction and filling of the whole Kli. That is why Yom Kippur is also called *Yom HaKippurim*, "a day *like* Purim." It is a vessel already corrected but still waiting to be filled. "In our world this holiday has not yet occurred. This is the only thing that remains."

## Part 6 — What to Take from This: Aim Forward, Not at Yourself

> Our entire path consists of ascents and descents. There cannot be anything within a person that doesn't break and then come back into balance, only through the good and the bad.

- Only through the "superposition" of seemingly good and seemingly bad qualities can the inner vessel be prepared and then filled.
- **Never ask "what state am I in?"** That is the wrong attitude. Ask what you are aspiring toward. Thinking about yourself makes you fall immediately, and "enormous amounts of interferences appear." Think "from yourself outward."
- **The engine of states:** the more a person aims at the goal, the faster the chain of **Reshimot** turns and the faster states pass. When he stops and thinks about himself, the chain stands still "and everything around him dies." "This depends only on me."

## Part 7 — Q&A: Aspiring Forward vs. Correcting Myself

**Question:** On one hand I shouldn't think about myself. On the other, I need to correct myself. How do both work?

- "Who aspires forward? Me." But thinking about my *current* state keeps me inside it. Thinking about the **next state** takes me out of myself without dropping me.
- The strength of that aspiration sets the speed, "like pressing the gas in a car." Going forward means concentrating on the goal as much as possible. Thoughts about yourself will come up at once, but you **discern them and go through them**, putting yourself back into the goal.

#### Where is the group in this?

> Without a group, you cannot short-circuit yourself on the goal. Concentrating on the Creator is the same as concentrating on the group.

- "The Creator" is a symbol for everything that surrounds me. He is pictured "through the force that is within the group."
- When Baal HaSulam speaks of a personal relationship to the Creator, there really is no personal one. The relationship can only come **from the common Kli**, the collection of souls, "that is where the Creator comes into being."
- "I am like a mother." Through love for all the others I relate to the Creator, standing behind them. The Creator is "the good who does good to all the souls," so He can't be found any other way.
- The first thing a person feels near the Machsom is exactly this: the Creator filling and leading all of humanity, found **in people's souls**. "There is no other place" to meet Him.

#### The Congress ahead

- For about ten days together, the aim is states that unite everyone toward one goal. Hence the slogan **"All of us as one, together."** If they focus on it, some will feel it clearly and others less so, and it will lay foundations to advance toward Pesach.
- "The chosen people" means being chosen today to rise first, and then raise everyone else. Rav Laitman sees a **critical mass** in the room, and perhaps 10–15 more from Moscow, Israel, or America, ready to feel these states.
- **There are no leaps.** A person has to live through every state. What he can do is **hasten** them, sometimes to "within a second."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The holidays describe inner states.** Yom Kippur shows the imperfect Kli, Sukkot begins correcting it, Hanukkah lights the light of Hochma in it, and Purim is complete correction, the only one of these not yet realized in our world.
2. **Sukkot corrects only the VAK.** The seven days, guests, and Sefirot purify the seven lower qualities with Surrounding Light. The upper three come later.
3. **Stay connected to the light.** The "three handbreadths" law is a reminder never to let the gap between the entering and departing light grow so wide that the connection is lost.
4. **Faith above reason wins.** Like the Maccabees against the Greeks, a person rises above his uncorrected vessels. The Masach (wick) plus the light of Hochma (oil) gives the light of Hanukkah.
5. **Never look at your own state.** Aim at the next state. How strongly you aspire sets how fast the Reshimot unfold.
6. **The Creator is found only in the common Kli.** There's no private relationship with Him. Concentrating on the group *is* concentrating on the Creator.
7. **No leaps, only hastening.** Every state has to be lived through, but aspiration can make it pass in a moment.
