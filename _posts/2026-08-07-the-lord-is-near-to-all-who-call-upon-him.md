### Opening

After a reading of Rabash's article on what it means to call upon the Creator "in truth" — and why only a truthful cry, not a proud one, is heard — Rav Laitman moves into the archival 2002 lecture proper, opening again on the Zohar's phrase "whoever wishes to awaken things above." The lecture traces a single question across every part that follows: since a person cannot directly change his own desire, how does he actually build the "true vessel" the Zohar says is required to awaken anything above? The answer moves from the basement metaphor for how spiritual perception dawns, through why growth can only happen by deficiency and never by direct effort, into why nobody can know their next spiritual state in advance, why a group is the one indispensable means, and why direction — not the size or feeling of a desire — is the only thing that actually matters. The lecture closes with a story about what a single lesson used to cost in Baal HaSulam's day, and a round of students sharing what they took from it.

## Part 1 — The Basement Metaphor: Nothing Changes but Our Perception of It

Rav Laitman opens by describing the Creator and the created being as existing in a fixed, complete state — nothing is actually added or subtracted as a person "progresses."

> "The reality we exist in is a fixed reality. There is the Creator and there is the created being... With nothing more and nothing less, and nothing changes... All the changes in the created being's sensation of where it is are only the inner sensation of the created being — regains consciousness, or [like] someone who is in a basement, and little by little the lights in the basement are turned on, and then he sees what is there in it, with him, in the basement. [The] basement, from being a closed dark place full of filth and completely devoid of anything good, becomes *Mushlam* — a perfect place filled with every good thing."

What looks like spiritual development is really the gradual illumination of a state that was already complete. "The end of the act is in the initial thought" — the wisdom of Kabbalah is specifically the wisdom of perceiving this correctly, and what a person perceives depends entirely on the questions his own deficiency is asking.

## Part 2 — Deficiency First, Then Quantity: Why You Can't Just Ask for More

Development, Rav Laitman says, moves only in one direction — a stronger sensation of the true reality — but the vessel that carries that sensation has to be built in a specific order.

> "Each time the deficiency — the deficiency should not merely be greater, it needs to be aimed at bestowal. That comes first, and then afterward comes the quantity, because quantity can also exist in opposition to the truth, in falsehood. There too, great desires rule. And if a person enters into great desires that are of the opposite quality — that is, not in order to bestow but in order to receive — that already demands correction."

A large desire aimed at reception is not progress; it just demands more correction later. The Kli must "become more genuine, more similar to the form of bestowal" before its size matters at all — so a yearning for the Creator has to be checked first for what it's actually yearning for: closeness itself, or simply relief from one's own dissatisfaction.

## Part 3 — No Foreknowledge: Every State Is Cut Off From the One Before It

Asked how a person can know what the "right demand" even looks like, or what the next stage requires, Rav Laitman is blunt: it cannot be known in advance, by design.

> "You'll never know because between the degrees, between spiritual states, there is always a break... We see this with Galgalta and AB. Although Galgalta underwent refinement, and the screen rose to the place of Rosh, and seemingly nothing remains of it, afterward the records of Dalet Gimel emerge. These records... although they emerge after the refinement of Galgalta, do not belong to Galgalta. In truth, Malchut of Ein Sof awakens them... What you do is merely let it now reach realization — that alone. And what is the next Reshimo? You have no idea."

He extends the image to a seed that must rot completely in the ground before a plant can grow from it: "every degree needs to completely lose its form and exist only as the cause of the next degree... every state is a state entirely unto itself, completely separate, like one slice completely cut off from the next." Since desire itself cannot be directly redirected by will, the only real work is indirect — using the available means to let it change on its own.

## Part 4 — A Vessel Built Between People, Not Inside Any One of Them

A student asks whether a group's "records" are shared, or whether the group is just an environment that lets each person produce his own. Rav Laitman's answer redefines what a group is for.

> "There is such a concept as a common, collective Reshimo of a group, provided that it's truly a group — then there is, as with Rabbi Shimon and his group, ten Sefirot, which is one vessel. Without that vessel, the Zohar would not have been revealed... A group means that each person must have a spiritual role, and through connection with several others like him, the vessel will become created... If you draw ten points, then each of them is like a soul, a root of a soul. Then, between these points, draw the vessel: between the first and the second, between the second and the third — that is the vessel."

Ten people each individually pursuing spirituality only produces ten separate points of surrounding light, not a spiritual vessel — "even when everyone is working at 100%." The actual vessel is the desire *between* one person and another, built from thinking about the friend rather than about oneself; without that connective structure, he says plainly, "there's nothing to talk about."

## Part 5 — This World as a Laboratory

Asked how a person is supposed to build a desire he's never felt and can't even imagine, Rav Laitman says the entire visible world — family, work, problems — exists to provide exactly this kind of practice space.

> "This world was built [with] this special, unique opportunity — like in a laboratory, where here you can go and check yourself, examine yourself in all the states, before you go to act, to the action itself... So all the people around you, and the work, and the family, the home, the children, the problems — you're given all of it so that if you use it in the right way, you put yourself into a state which is similar to the spiritual state. It helps you build the correct desire for spirituality."

He compares it to being handed a toy gun before being trusted with a real one. Group work and even dissemination are meant to be used the same way — as means for shaping the right desire, not ends pursued for their own sake: "We want to take dissemination as a means, not to do dissemination just for the sake of dissemination."

## Part 6 — The North Star: Direction, Not Feeling, Is the Only Instrument

The lecture's central practical image answers how to take each next step without being able to see the path.

> "I'm in a state where — my eyes are shut. On all sides, an abyss. One step you take forward, you fall... And you're given some instrument with which you can calculate where that road is... If it's me, and the Creator, and the goal, all together — then I need to combine all these factors together. If I combine them together, then I will see that road, then I will take a step and not fall into the abyss... This is the device by which you can tell that you are taking the next step correctly."

A ship or an aircraft with no fixed reference point cannot tell north from south — it needs a gyroscope, something "certainly constant," to measure against. In the work, that constant reference is the goal itself: equivalence of form with the Creator. Rav Laitman is explicit that acting without first making this calculation is worse than useless: "it's better not to run around and do things in haste... It's preferable to sit and not act [than] to perform [a step] not properly]."

## Part 7 — Honesty Before Magnitude: The Crying Child Nobody Turns To

Pressed on why some prayers and cries go unanswered, Rav Laitman returns to the theme of truth from the article, now applied to the size versus the sincerity of a desire.

> "A baby standing in the street, shouting — does anyone pay much attention?... Because they know it's for nothing: in a moment's time he'll be running around, forgetting about it entirely... A desire which isn't real — nobody pays attention to it, even in our world... What his inner desire is, and what he expresses outwardly, [aren't] the same thing... So what if you have a big cannon, but you're not aiming towards the goal exactly? Then what?... First of all, a person has to take care of the honesty, the truthfulness of his desire, and then the magnitude of it."

A small, honest desire aimed correctly outweighs a large, false one — "it's better not to have such a desire" than to mistake volume or intensity of feeling for genuine direction toward the Creator.

## Part 8 — What a Lesson Used to Cost: Baal HaSulam, Rabash, and the Group's Value

Closing the lecture, Rav Laitman recalls what studying with Baal HaSulam actually required, through a story Rabash told him directly.

> "With Baal HaSulam, a lesson — it's not a simple thing. First of all, you had to pay money, and a lot of money — like your income for a week or two, for one lesson. And secondly, it was on occasion; they wouldn't know when exactly, and suddenly he says, 'There is a lesson.'"

Rabash himself described sneaking into one such lesson as a boy — arriving to see his father privately, finding a room full of students who hadn't told him a lesson was happening, and sitting down to "take the whole lesson" for himself, unaware he was intruding on a jealously guarded gathering. Rav Laitman contrasts that scarcity with today: "we are the first generation starting with this more popular method... we have no alternative" but to build the same quality of desire through a group that, historically, was almost never freely available.

## Part 9 — Closing: What the Students Took From It

Several students share brief impressions before the recording moves on. Their summaries, in Rav Laitman's telling, converge on the same handful of points: the lack/deficiency that opens each new stage; the break between degrees that makes each state impossible to predict from the one before; the three means — book, group, Rav — as the only real levers on a desire that can't be changed directly; and the reminder that "if me and the goal are together, I have to combine all these things" in order to take the next step without falling into the abyss. One student adds a final formulation of the direction-over-feeling theme: "It's not how I feel with my heart, good or bad — that's nonsense. Until I scrutinize my spiritual path and what's preferred to do in the next step, it's best not to do anything."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Spiritual "progress" is not the acquisition of something new but the gradual illumination of an already-complete, fixed reality — like lights slowly turning on in a basement (*Mushlam*, "perfect," once it's fully lit).
2. A deficiency has to be aimed at bestowal before its size matters at all; a large desire aimed at reception is not an advantage, since "quantity can also exist... in falsehood."
3. No spiritual state can be known or predicted in advance — there is always a break between degrees, and a person can only let the next Reshimo (record) come into realization, not choose or foresee it.
4. A spiritual vessel is not the sum of separate people's private desires for spirituality; it is built *between* people, from each one's desire toward the other — which is why a genuine group (Rabbi Shimon's Ten Sefirot) can reveal what no individual can reach alone.
5. This entire world — family, work, group, problems — functions as a laboratory in which to build and test the correct desire before it can be used for real spiritual action.
6. Since desire cannot be changed by direct effort, the only usable "instrument" is a fixed reference point — the goal, equivalence of form with the Creator — checked before every step, the way a compass or gyroscope is checked before every course correction.
7. The honesty of a desire matters before its magnitude: a small, truthfully aimed desire is heard; a large but falsely aimed one, like a child's insincere cry, draws no real response.
8. Access to authentic group study has historically been scarce and costly (illustrated by Baal HaSulam's era and Rabash's own account); today's more available method is itself part of what makes disciplined, honest use of the group and the books necessary.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Mushlam | Hebrew for "perfect/complete"; in this lesson's basement metaphor, what a state of filth and darkness becomes once fully illuminated — the same fixed reality, only now correctly perceived. |
| Reshimot (Reshimo) | "Records" — spiritual recollections carried forward from prior states; in this lesson, the mechanism by which one spiritual state gives rise to the next without any continuity a person can predict or control in advance. |
| Malchut (of Ein Sof) | The tenth Sefira, "Kingdom"; in this lesson, specifically Malchut of Ein Sof, described as the force that awakens the next set of records (e.g., "Dalet Gimel") once a prior degree (Galgalta) has completed its refinement. |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes," two of the five parts of a Partzuf; in this lesson, used as the example degree whose refinement completes before an unrelated new set of records ("AB") can emerge. |
| Sefirot | The ten emanations/vessels through which light is expressed in a Partzuf; in this lesson, the structure of "one vessel" said to be built collectively by Rabbi Shimon's group of ten, without which the Zohar could not have been revealed. |
| Baal HaSulam | Rabbi Yehuda Ashlag, author of the Sulam commentary on the Zohar; in this lesson, recalled (via Rabash) as having taught lessons so scarce and costly that a single one could cost a week or two's income. |
| Rabash | Rabbi Baruch Shalom Ashlag, son and successor of Baal HaSulam; in this lesson, the source of a firsthand story about sitting in on one of his father's guarded private lessons as a boy. |
