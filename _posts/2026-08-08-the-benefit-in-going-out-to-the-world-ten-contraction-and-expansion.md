### Opening

Before the recording begins, the group frames why they're watching it: to prepare for an upcoming European Regional Congress by returning to a lesson about what a "world Ten" — a much larger circle than one's own local group — actually adds to the work. The lesson itself opens with Rav Laitman's own question to the room: "So what do we gain by each one besides being in his own tent... annulling himself before the friends, accepting their desire... along with that we connect to bigger tents. What's the benefit from all this?" Everything that follows — the one-soul teaching, the magnifying-glass image, and a long, wide-ranging Q&A — is an answer to that single question.

## Part 1 — One Soul, Divided

Rav Laitman opens with the foundational teaching he attributes to Baal HaSulam:

> "Rasulam [Baal HaSulam] writes, there is only one soul in the world and that soul exists in all the children of Israel, in all humans, complete in each one, as in Adam HaRishon. Rather that one soul is divided in the will to receive of each person... the minute we begin to connect one another, we begin to understand [that] we are working to attain the one soul, but each time in a more clarified way."

Connecting to a bigger group doesn't create anything new. Nothing is added to reality by expanding outward — the one soul is already there, whole, in everyone. What changes is only how precisely a person can perceive and work with it.

## Part 2 — The Magnifying Glass: Precision, Not New Content

Asked directly what a "big Ten" gives that a small one doesn't, Rav Laitman answers with an extended image:

> "Imagine that you take a magnifying glass... you see some dot on a white page. It's just a dot. You take your glasses, you put them on, and you see, oh it's not a dot, it's something... You take a magnifying glass and you see, whoa, yes, it is something. Some biological creature... you can put it under a microscope and study it even more... the more you magnify it, ten times more, a hundred times more, a thousand times more — to that extent you have scrutiny of this object. This is how we study the Creator."

The same logic applies to white light, which looks uniform until it's studied closely enough to reveal seven colors, then millions of frequencies. "In the multitude of people is the king's glory" — the Creator cannot be revealed through a small, simple force, only through something built of many different, even opposing, qualities. Going from ten people to a hundred, a thousand, or a billion doesn't change what's being perceived; it changes the resolution.

## Part 3 — Not Leaving the Ten, But Returning to It Stronger

Rav Laitman is explicit that connecting to the wider group is not a replacement for one's own local Ten:

> "We're not leaving the Ten in order to replace it, but rather in order to return to it with a more broad and powerful [force]."

Later, answering a question about the risk of the bigger connection pulling people away from local work, he uses the image of eyeglasses: "If I had diopter one, or I need to wear diopter ten or a hundred... I can't keep both. It's the same in every [instrument]. You increase the font size, you go to a different perception. Just don't be afraid — you are in the [same] previous state but in a greater resolution, a better resolution... but you must do it in the same [instrument]." The bigger connection is a stronger lens on the same local Ten, not a separate track.

## Part 4 — States, Sleepiness, and Not Fighting the Process

A student asks about friends who visibly drift into a kind of "twilight" or sleepiness during sessions, and whether this is something to fight. Rav Laitman answers that it must be resisted, but also accepted as natural:

> "We have to understand that these things are natural. And each and every student — part of his time he is very alert, and another part he spends in a state of slumber... When I give a lesson in my usual place, out of four hundred people in front of me, a hundred to a hundred and fifty are always disconnected in some way... it's not so important for me. A person invested, he comes, he's tired... each one has his own mood."

He connects this to the principle of "teach a child according to his ways" — the Creator arranges each person's state, alert or dim, according to a calculation only He makes; the person's job is simply to keep sitting and trying.

## Part 5 — Only the Prayer: The Creator in the Center of the Ten

Asked directly for his own personal prayer for the group's success, Rav Laitman turns the question back to the mechanism of prayer itself:

> "Eventually only the prayer helps here, only the request. When the person raises his request from within his heart to the Creator, and the Creator is hidden there in the center of the Ten... imagine your Ten [as a hub], and that they are somehow connected, and imagine that there inside is the Creator, and he's the one you need to turn to. That's how it works. This is the entire secret."

He adds a caution later in the Q&A, when asked how to make a prayer for the friends more precise from the vantage point of a bigger Ten: don't take specific problems out of your own Ten and drop them onto the world Ten. "You shouldn't extend the specific problems of your Ten into the bigger world Ten... you only want to reach a broader, stronger connection."

## Part 6 — Forces, Not People: The News as a Mirror of the Soul

Responding to a question about why it's so hard to annul before others and see only their merits, Rav Laitman widens the frame to everything a person perceives outside himself, including world events:

> "Let's say I'm watching the news, the nine PM news, and it gives a world survey — what's happening, what horrors, how people kill one another, what are they arguing about, so much trouble they give one another. This is all my soul. This is a theater. Instead of people, imagine forces. Not people — forces. And all these forces are in your soul. And you have to bring the reforming lights to them, and arrange them in such a way that they will all be connected beautifully. And then these forces reveal the Creator, and for you it will be your end of correction."

The ego holds a person inside itself and resists this reframing, but he insists it's "extremely close," not a fantasy — the state is corrected by the upper light to the extent a person actually wants it corrected.

## Part 7 — Two Paths, and Why Europe Now

Asked what's lacking to actually realize what's been discussed at the Congress, Rav Laitman describes the two general paths of advancement:

> "The path to correction includes two steps: the path of light, or the path of suffering... If we draw the light more and more, and we advance towards it all the time, meaning towards greater and greater bestowal, greater and greater connection, then it moves well, we advance. But if we don't keep up with the plan of creation... then they push us through suffering, meaning blows. And then we run ahead in order to escape the blows."

He applies this directly to the moment: "Europe is in the most dangerous state of the entire world. It's like modern day Babylon. It must start the correction... this is the first meeting of all the representatives from all the European countries... speaking about the connection between them in order to build a [vessel] that includes all of Europe" — a Klee shattered and divided, he says, since ancient Babylon, more than three thousand years ago.

## Part 8 — Mashiach: The Force That Pulls

Asked whether the "state of Messiah" is a beginning, and whether it is permanent once reached, Rav Laitman defines the term directly from its root:

> "The state of Messiah, Mashiach, is a period of time, an era where everyone begins to be pulled towards the end of correction... Mashiach [comes] from the word *limshoch*, to pull. It's a force that pulls all of humanity towards the end of correction. That's why it's called Mashiach... When we finish the corrections, that force that pulls will stop existing... everything will be a constant state, rest forever."

He adds that Baal HaSulam describes this generation itself as the generation of the Messiah — the force pulling from the will to receive into the will to bestow is already active, whether or not it is recognized as such.

## Part 9 — Annulment Before a Friend vs. Before the Ten

A student asks about the difference between annulling oneself before a single friend and annulling oneself before the whole Ten. Rav Laitman frames it as a difference of precision, not kind:

> "It's a difference of the quality of my spiritual work... the more the number of details you can take into consideration in order to calculate something... that's why the more you can connect those details to the goal [the better]."

Elsewhere he acknowledges directly that annulling before a single friend is, in practice, often harder than annulling before the whole world Ten or the general group — because with a friend you know too much, and the feeling of their "greatness" is harder to hold onto than an abstract, larger connection.

## Part 10 — Faith: A Choice and an Earned State

Two related questions ask whether faith is something chosen at the outset or something earned through the work of the Ten. Rav Laitman does not treat these as contradictory:

> "Of course, we have to work hard in order to reach faith. I have nothing else to say. Of course I need to choose faith to reach it."

Faith above reason, in his answer, is both: a starting choice and a state that is subsequently built and reinforced through sustained work with the group.

## Part 11 — A Note on Men, Women, and Connection

Answering a question from a woman about how connection and incorporation apply differently to men and women, Rav Laitman describes what he presents as a natural difference in starting orientation, while insisting the goal — going above nature, toward connection — applies to everyone regardless of that difference:

> "Each and every woman is a world of its own. She doesn't need connection with her friends, other women... the man needs that, he wants that — for him, the group, the society, friends... Now we are talking about the fact that we have to go above nature... a woman also must have connection... it's not that they have to do it to the very last detail — that's for the men — but all in all, they do have to try to go through it."

This framing reflects Rav Laitman's own stated view in this lesson as transcribed; it is presented here as part of the lecture's content, not as independent commentary.

## Part 12 — Closing: The Magnifying Glass, Moved to Europe

Closing the session, Rav Laitman ties the whole lesson back to the reason for gathering that day:

> "If in the previous Congress the magnifying glass was on our basic unit, which is the Ten, now we're moving the magnifying glass to something that is actually a group of tens. Then in the big convention in February, we will put the magnifying glass on the whole world — [one] clean whose center is here in Israel... The purpose of this Shabbat is to switch gears in our preparation toward the Regional Congress... to search together how to take the magnifying glass and place it on a collection of tens, on a greater connection."

He frames the goal in explicitly practical terms: building the systems and spiritual center that let a whole region function the way a single Ten already does, so that new friends and new Tens can eventually be received "within it."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Connecting to a bigger group doesn't add anything new to reality — the one soul already exists, whole, in everyone; a wider connection only increases the resolution at which it can be perceived and worked with (the magnifying-glass image).
2. Going out to a "world Ten" is not a substitute for one's local Ten but a way of returning to it with more force — like changing the resolution on the same lens, not switching to a different one.
3. States of alertness and "slumber" during study are natural and Creator-arranged, not to be fought with shame; a person's job is only to keep sitting and trying, part alert and part not.
4. Prayer is directed to the Creator specifically as He is "hidden in the center of the Ten" — and should stay aimed at connection itself rather than at specific problems exported from one's local Ten into the wider group.
5. Everything perceived outside oneself, including world news, is reframed as forces belonging to one's own soul, to be corrected through connection rather than judged as external events.
6. Correction advances by one of two paths — the path of light (voluntary, increasing bestowal) or the path of suffering (blows that push from behind) — and Europe's current fragmentation is presented as a specific, urgent instance of this choice.
7. Mashiach (from *limshoch*, "to pull") names the pulling force, active in this era, that draws humanity from the will to receive toward the will to bestow — not a person or single event, but a process that ends when correction is complete.
8. Faith above reason is treated as both a choice made at the outset and a state built and reinforced through sustained work with the group — not one or the other.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| World Ten | A much larger circle of connection than one's own local Ten — a "Ten of Tens" or regional/global grouping; in this lesson, the object of the magnifying-glass image, offering greater precision of perception, not new spiritual content. |
| Mashiach | "Messiah," from the Hebrew root *limshoch* ("to pull"); in this lesson, defined as an era-long force pulling humanity from the will to receive toward the will to bestow, which ceases once correction is complete. |
| Adam HaRishon | "The First Man"; the single collective soul, complete in each person, that Baal HaSulam describes as divided only in each person's individual will to receive. |
| Klee (Vessel) | The corrected connective structure built between people, not within any one of them; in this lesson, applied to Europe as a whole, described as shattered and divided since ancient Babylon and now in need of rebuilding at a regional scale. |
| Path of Light / Path of Suffering | The two general routes of spiritual advancement: voluntary movement toward greater bestowal and connection ("the light"), versus being pushed forward by suffering ("blows"); both are said to lead to the same correction. |
| Reforming Light (Ohr Makif) | The corrective force a person "brings" to the forces/impressions of the outside world (including world news, reframed as forces within one's own soul) in order to arrange and connect them. |
