### Opening

The article's own claim is stark: a person doesn't conquer the land himself, it is only ever given. So what does the actual, felt work of "coming to the land" look like from the inside? The archival lecture answers by walking through the whole arc a person passes through — from first feeling worse rather than better, through states that change faster than can be tracked, to a final condition that can't even be put into words — before closing on a striking claim about this world itself: that nothing seen here can be changed here at all.

## Part 1 — The Work Begins With Feeling Worse, Not Better

Rav Laitman opens the archival lecture by describing what actually happens when a person first begins working with his own desire:

> "In the beginning, when work with the desire is revealed to him, he starts to feel himself in a bad state — he lacks forces, he has the recognition of evil, and he doesn't understand why he feels that bad. Later on he starts to understand that this feeling of evil comes as a result of seeing himself opposite the qualities of the Creator — he sees that he has become worse than before: weaker, more lazy, more cruel than before. So this is the beginning of the work with the will to receive, during the preparation."

## Part 2 — Two Kinds of Impression: The Light and the Vessel

Asked why a person's inner state keeps swinging so unpredictably, Rav Laitman traces it to two separate sources of impression:

> "I receive an impression from two things — from the will to receive itself, and from what it is filled with: from the light and from the vessel. As a result of the collision between the qualities of the light and the qualities of the vessel within me, I can feel that I am very evil. And another time I can feel differently — that I simply feel good. The upper light, when it shines, shines in a uniform manner. But the will to receive, when it opens up, opens up according to its five parts — Keter, Chochma, Bina, Zeir Anpin, and Malchut — and each part that opens has a different nature. And so I begin to feel changes of states: one time I feel the recognition of evil, one time I feel good, one time I feel that I need to go and do something, one time I reach Malchut and feel that I have no power for anything, even for some small thought."

What matters, he adds, isn't which state a person is in: "It doesn't matter what I feel. What is important for me is only that these feelings will pass through as quickly as possible."

## Part 3 — Turning Suffering Conscious, and the One Real Scrutiny in Every State

A person can't stop the states from changing, but he can change his relationship to the suffering inside them:

> "I can shift the suffering from beastly suffering to conscious suffering. And when I put my awareness into it, I no longer suffer — I can even be happy. It's like a person who didn't know what illness he had and suffered for years, and now he discovers he needs an operation. Even though he goes through the surgery and still continues to suffer for some time, he already knows and sees the outcome he'll get out of it — so he even pays for the surgery and is happy, because he sees it as a correction, not suffering. He receives some sweetening."

Whatever the state, only one thing is actually worth working on inside it: "Even though he is going through all kinds of states, he should be thinking about one thing only. A person should be concerned about discovering his connection with the Creator in whichever state it is. If he discovers this point of connection, it's a sign that he has realized this state and he's ready to move on to the next one — as if he's glued that state to the end of correction, and he's already worthy of moving on."

## Part 4 — Why Repetition Isn't Really Repetition

Asked how a person can keep returning to what feels like the same point without it becoming stale, Rav Laitman reframes the repetition as never actually identical:

> "It's not that it's impossible to repeat it. Even today if I ate, and tomorrow I'll eat, and yesterday I ate — let's say it was even the exact same dish — nevertheless a new taste is revealed. Why? Because it's a new desire. You can look at how many elements we have in food — tomato, cucumber, bread, onion, meat, rice, let's say twenty or thirty — and how many different recipes are there? Thousands, endless. The combination between them gives a different taste. So we have the ten primary Sefirot, and when they divide and join together, it creates an endless number of different states, where every state is a completely different taste."

Understanding a given degree, he adds, only comes in hindsight: "Each and every degree, you don't know its connection with all the other degrees — you'll discover that in a more advanced state. Let's say at the age of six you attain what it means to be at the age of six only with respect to your whole life, later on, when you become a grown-up and begin to understand what that age actually meant."

## Part 5 — The End of Correction, and What Lies Beyond Words

Asked directly what the end of correction actually is, Rav Laitman defines it narrowly, then marks its limit:

> "The end of correction means a vessel that is corrected to receive in order to bestow — all of its reception is because he is giving, bestowing. And as a result, he is in adhesion with the upper force, and he truly understands what the upper force is, in his own state, in his own degree. This is called the end of correction. After that, with a vessel that's ready, we begin a new life about which we don't know anything yet."

Nothing beyond that point can even be described: "We are not able to understand the reality which is beyond the end of correction. They don't write about it, because besides the fact that it won't give us anything right now, it will only confuse us — we need something practical for our work. And besides that, as much as they do say, they say that it is impossible to express it with our words, because our words come from the second restriction. To tell us about the reality above the end of correction — this we cannot understand; we have no possibility to even express it."

## Part 6 — The Biggest Surprise: You Never Know If You're Stuck

Asked how a person can tell whether he's actually advancing or stuck in place, Rav Laitman says this is unknowable by design:

> "How do I know that I'm not stuck? It's impossible to know. Our way is full of surprises — the biggest surprise is that you never understand where you are, how you advance, or whether you're advancing at all. This is done in a special way, so that we will seemingly concede all the results to Him. It doesn't matter to me even if I don't advance — what I want to remain with is one piece of knowledge: that I have a connection with the upper force. That's it, here in our lack of knowledge."

He illustrates the feeling with a technical analogy: "Let's say you get some new device — you need to calibrate it, reset it, put a hundred or two hundred grams inside. In the meantime, you are being calibrated. You feel — I fell, I'm ascending — but you don't yet feel the actual measures, this is more, this is less. Later you'll start to have differences — this is more, this is less — until you have a scale. Then your scale is ready to work, and you start working. This is already beyond the barrier."

## Part 7 — Complaining Is Fine; Forgetting Is the Danger

A participant worries that feeling bad and turning to the Creator in that state amounts to cursing Him. Rav Laitman turns the worry around:

> "The Creator is not afraid that you will curse Him, believe me. He agrees that you will think about Him all the time and curse Him all the time — as long as you don't let go of Him. That's the whole problem: if you want to advance, your problem is that you forget about Him. Do not forget — in the worst possible states, precisely there."

The participant draws his own conclusion from it: "So at least you know that you're cursing Him — earlier you didn't even know whom you were cursing. Now I know, so it's a preparation for correction."

## Part 8 — Nothing Is Ever Lost, and Nothing Happens All at Once

Asked why progress can't simply be granted faster, Rav Laitman ties the pace directly to the shattering itself:

> "This is what we learn from the shattering: everything is divided into hundreds of thousands of degrees — a thousand, it doesn't matter which. You can't get it all at once. You can't get just one blow."

He contrasts this with ordinary effort in this world, where investment can simply be lost: "In corporeality, a doctor studies for fifteen years. Let's say in the third year he sees he's not good at the studies, he loses the desire, and he leaves the university to become a worker instead — everything he paid for the studies, it's all gone. In spirituality it's not gone. It is kept, it is saved. You can quit, and many people do, but they don't really leave — they exist together with us still, in truth. You can ask that person ten years later, and he'll say, 'I heard about you, and I intend to come.' He wasn't there for ten years, but he intends to come. This is how it is. He is connected with us."

## Part 9 — Closing: Israel and the Nations as One Soul, and Why This World Is Only a Copy

A question about the ongoing conflict between Israel and the nations draws out the lesson's most sweeping claim: that nothing in this world is actually determined here at all.

> "What we have in our world all comes from above — in this world we don't determine anything, the nations don't determine anything. Not we, and not them, are guilty. We're only speaking on a spiritual level, where we can actually change something. Everything that happens here is the result of changes in spiritual states between us and them, and all the parts of creation."

He explains with an analogy: "It's a copy — like if you see pictures, and there are copies of it somewhere else. Let's say I'm drawing something here on the board, and a camera is sending what I drew a thousand kilometers away, to some other place. You look at that other place, at that depiction, and you want to change it — do you change it there? No — you change it here, where you draw the picture. Then it will change there. There's nothing to change here." He roots the conflict itself in a shared origin: "In Kabbalah, Israel and the nations are two parts of the same vessel, called Adam HaRishon — one soul that, for the sake of correction, is divided into two parts. Israel needs to correct themselves first, and that's why Israel has an obligation to correct — that's why it's called the chosen people: chosen to make a correction on itself and then show everyone how to do it. As much as Israel is late relative to the general plan of their own correction, the same uncorrected states are felt as suffering. So it turns out every suffering, all the suffering in the world, comes from those forces we didn't correct in our own souls yet." Asked why the nations themselves aren't at fault, he compares it to nature itself: "It's like asking about a bee that stings you — what can you complain about, it's her nature. You want her to stop stinging? Go change it in the program, at the root — why is she guilty about it?" The session closes, as the group often does, with a song.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Beginning the work with one's own desire feels like getting worse, not better — recognizing cruelty, laziness, and weakness in oneself is the sign of correctly seeing oneself opposite the Creator's qualities, not a setback.
2. Inner states swing unpredictably because every feeling is a collision between two separate sources — the light, which shines uniformly, and the vessel, whose five parts (Keter through Malchut) each have a different nature.
3. Suffering itself can be shifted from "beastly" to "conscious" simply by understanding its purpose, the way a person facing necessary surgery experiences pain differently once he sees it as correction rather than affliction.
4. Across every changing state, only one thing is worth actually working on: finding the point of connection with the Creator, which is what allows a person to move on to the next state at all.
5. No two states are truly repeats of each other, any more than the same handful of ingredients ever really produces the same dish twice — the "sameness" is only apparent from outside a state a person hasn't yet advanced past.
6. Never being able to confirm whether one is advancing or stuck is described as deliberate, not a flaw in the system — the only thing to hold onto is the fact of a felt connection to the Creator, not proof of progress.
7. Complaining to or even cursing the Creator during a bad state is treated as harmless, even useful, since it keeps Him in mind; forgetting Him entirely is named as the only real danger.
8. Effort in spiritual work is never lost the way corporeal investment can be (the fifteen-year doctor who quits in year three) — and this world's visible events, including conflict between Israel and the nations, are described as a mere "copy" of spiritual states that can only actually be changed at their root.

| Recognition of Evil (Hakarat HaRa) | Seeing one's current state as worse, not better; this lesson locates the very start of real work here — feeling more cruel, lazy, and weak than before is the sign of correctly seeing oneself opposite the Creator, not a setback. |
| Five Parts (Keter, Chochma, Bina, Zeir Anpin, Malchut) | The structural divisions of the will to receive; this lesson uses them to explain why inner states swing unpredictably — each part, once opened, has a different nature, producing a different collision with the uniformly shining light. |
| Machsom | The barrier; this lesson locates it at the point where a person's "scale" is finally calibrated enough to actually register measured difference rather than raw, unplaced sensation. |
| Malchut | The tenth Sefira, "Kingdom" — the receiving vessel; this lesson names it as one of the states a person cycles through, specifically the one where "I have no power for anything, even for some small thought." |
| Adam HaRishon | "The First Man" — the single collective soul of all humanity; this lesson locates Israel and the nations of the world as its two divided parts, split specifically so that Israel could correct itself first and then pass the method of correction on. |
