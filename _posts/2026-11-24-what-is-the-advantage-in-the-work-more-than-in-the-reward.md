### Opening

Rabash's article tells of a person who asks a friend for a loan the friend doesn't have, though he can raise it from others. By each one claiming he only wanted to show his love, "the love is established forever." The article ends: when a person puts his self-love to death and cares only to increase the glory of heaven, all that the Torah promised comes true. Rav Laitman reads the loan as a lesson in why the work itself, the doubts and the effort, is worth more than the money. From there he turns to very practical rules for life among friends: never demand spiritual work of a friend, act from the heart rather than the calculator, keep starting without expecting to finish, keep spirituality out of the workplace, and prepare for the morning lesson from the night before.

## Part 1 — The Loan from a Friend Who Has None

- Rabash took this from life. They lived very frugally: weddings in a courtyard with twenty or thirty guests and a cup of tea with cake, children sometimes going to school with nothing to eat. "So he felt on himself what this is."
- When I borrow from a friend who has the money, the relationship is simple. "When he has none, I obligate him to a completely different work": he must go to others, humble himself before them, and work on faith in whether I'll repay him. "This work already incorporates several kinds of work within it."
- All of this is arranged "so that we develop vessels for receiving abundance, for receiving the purpose of creation." The money passes externally from one to another "just in order to awaken the work in us at deeper, more internal levels."

#### The difficulty is the desirable part

- "The faith I am working on here, the doubts I'm in here, are the desirable things." If I'm glad of the chance to be in doubt toward a friend, to see how incapable I am and how I suddenly suspect him, "then I'm happy that I have a difficulty here."
- To the extent of the difficulty I scrutinize the recognition of evil in me, and on that I build "a different difficulty, a hardening": the desire for forces to work above my animal nature and its doubts and fears.
- What comes into a person's head at that moment, whether to settle things quickly or to see an opportunity for faith and love of friends, "depends on the preparation. At that moment there's nothing left to decide." It must be above reason, "and we also need to clarify what above reason means, because one can do foolish things in this."

## Part 2 — Never Demand Spiritual Work of a Friend

- **Q: So I can ask a friend for $2,000 and tell him it's his chance to work above reason?** "No, no, no. If you told a friend he should remember the goal at the moment you ask him for two thousand dollars, then you are stealing the work itself from him."
- He must receive the goal "from this general spirit" that hovers in the group, "but you're forbidden to be his guide directly like that."
- A request must first stand on the ordinary level: would it be acceptable in any normal society? "If it is so, then I'm allowed to do it. And if not, then it's forbidden," because then "I'm playing on the other's decency." Just as you mustn't leave money lying around to tempt a guest, or provoke someone at a cliff's edge, "it is a commandment from the Torah."
- "You're forbidden to obligate him to do spiritual work. You can awaken him to it indirectly, help him in it." In such matters "relate to a friend as if you were in a completely ordinary society." "There's a great obstacle here for the friends, because each one demands spiritual work of the other... Each one must obligate himself by himself alone."

#### General spirit, not personal pressure

- Giving someone a task and reminding him of the goal with it "is called Geneivat Da'at, deception. Literally the theft of the mind." But "come help me peel potatoes, and while we're doing it let's listen to some article" is something else.
- Not "carry 20 sacks of cement because of love of friends, the Creator will pay you." "I see here and there exploitation precisely in this, exploitation of friends."
- Awaken by personal example, a general song or article, so the atmosphere is for everyone. But not even a personal example that obligates him. "The group gives support, gives opportunity to work. But the work is nevertheless personal for each and every one."
- **Q: And someone who walks into the kitchen and calls out to think about the Creator?** "If someone shouts to everyone, guys, let's rise higher... It's fine, because he's addressing everyone." He isn't obligating you out of his own thoughts; "you're working anyway and I lift you up a little in a general way."

## Part 3 — Act from the Heart

- **Q: What are the two conditions by which love between friends is established forever?** "Our problem is that we bring too much intellect where there should be feeling... Act according to the heart and you will see that it is impossible to err."
- With your own child you don't calculate; you act by feeling, "and it always turns out for his good." The same toward a friend. "I walk around all the time with a calculator... By kilos, by calories, by what will you count? Rather act according to the heart. If you love him, what would you do?"
- If the friend doesn't respond, "what do you care?" "The fact that I don't receive a response is even better than receiving one. If you receive a reaction back, you have a problem. You are receiving payment." If it's worthwhile to love him for a spiritual reason, "that is already work." "We don't take the reaction into account. We take into account what I was given from above."

#### People who seem like rocks

- "There are people here with whom it's very hard to connect," and that may be exactly the opportunity. Some are rich inside, full of feeling, but by character can't open up.
- Rav Laitman tells how he was amazed to see such a person suddenly open: "I suddenly discovered very special things in people who I thought still had a very thick skin." "So try. Just the opposite. Don't look for a reaction. A reaction misleads a person."

## Part 4 — Only to Keep Starting

- **Q: If the group sets a line that's too big for me?** "We are in a free country." Anyone can leave. If the question of life's meaning obliges him, he'll stay; if not, "the question is not yet raging in him that much."
- "The fact that I see that what is now being placed before me is beyond my strength, in spirituality this is a natural thing." There's a wall we can't climb, "and when we try, there are guards and policemen who will throw you off a thousand times."
- "It is not for you to finish the work." Rav Laitman describes his own impatient character: he touches something, sees nothing comes of it, throws it away and starts something new. "The moment you know that you don't have to finish anything... the Creator seals it. He finishes and He performs the action. You only need to keep starting." "Even such an approach merits success."
- "Society can demand effort from the individual, but not results." He recalls efforts like the communal village and the housing project: at the time you push as hard as possible, and when it's clear it isn't working, let go. On this impossible thing "I need to try a thousand times," until the discernment comes that it's truly impossible, together with real yearning. "Desire I have from the start. Yearning is something I still have to add by myself."

#### A double life

- **Q: Can a person choose to be in doubt or to decide?** "A person always has the possibility to go according to his beastly nature or according to spirituality at every moment," in every thought. That isn't about having doubts, "it's about what you want to determine everything for you."
- Keep a spiritual quality inside and clothe it outside in corporeal things, work and family: "inside it's like living a double life."

## Part 5 — Keep Spirituality Out of the Workplace

- At a job with a boss, "the work must be exactly like everyone else's. Don't bring spirituality into it. Don't obligate people at your workplace to relate to you according to spiritual laws."
- "If you start bringing spirituality into the corporeal work, you'll spoil your work, you'll get thrown out of the job... they will think you are crazy." And don't think "the Creator will repay them, it's very good that they suffer from me." "It's a failed attitude."
- **Q: If coworkers watch TV and play cards in free time, must I?** No, you can read, but "you also shouldn't look down at him. You are with that and I'm with this... It attracts me." You could just as well read a science book.

## Part 6 — Preparing from the Night Before

- **Q: Where is the boundary between difficulty in the work and preparing for tomorrow?** As Part 12 of the Study of the Ten Sefirot teaches, "the preparation for tomorrow begins in the evening," when it begins to get dark: in spirituality, "the revelation of the new vessels, which tomorrow will come to correction and to filling with the light of day."
- Before sleep, after the news, "I take one of our books. Five minutes is enough... and with that I fall asleep." Even after time with your wife, it's still advisable to wash your hands, sit at the table and read for five minutes. "That's my advice."
- Dreams aren't from previous incarnations; they're the day's thoughts loosened from matter. So "your dreams will be different," and you'll wake with the thoughts you went to sleep with. Guard them "like a very delicate and precious thing" until you come in. Don't start by fixing a door or a faucet; open a book, talk with the friends, and carry those thoughts through the three hours. The rest of the day "is already a different work."
- **Q: I read before sleep and look for filling, is that a problem?** "It doesn't matter what thoughts the article brings you, negative or positive... as long as you're in them."
- **Q: Doesn't preparation start when the morning lesson ends?** "I deliberately don't want to talk about the day," when a person is under other forces. From the Shema before sleep until the morning Shema, "that's the time when you really make the preparation of the vessels."

## Part 7 — Group Preparation and the Society as a Business

- "Group preparation is very important too, until it becomes a habit." Set an order, one calling everyone or each calling one other, and "as a commitment before the friends," agree on a verse or excerpt everyone reads before sleep on the same day, with someone in charge of choosing it.
- **Q: What's the difference between self-preparation and group preparation?** "Self-preparation for the morning lesson you owe to yourself. In group preparation you owe to the society. According to that you are rewarded and according to that you are fined."
- "A society is a business. I walk into the society, I contribute to the society, I want to gain," say fifteen times fifteen, 225-fold. "But if I don't do it, I lose to the same extent."
- So one who really hurts the society "will be ejected from here," whether or not anyone throws him out. "The slightest disregard or disrespect, because it's against the body," comes back and is seen by all a hundredfold. And what to think about in preparation? "You're looking for a recipe for life. Search."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The work is worth more than the reward.** In the loan, the faith, doubts and effort are the desirable part; they build the vessels for the purpose of creation.
2. **Never demand spiritual work of a friend.** Requests must be acceptable in any ordinary society; reminding him of the goal with a task is theft of the mind.
3. **Awaken only through the general spirit.** A general call, song or article lifts everyone; the work itself stays personal.
4. **Act from the heart, not the calculator.** Love that expects no reaction is already spiritual work, and hard-shelled friends are often rich inside.
5. **Keep starting, never expect to finish.** The Creator completes; the society can ask effort, not results, and yearning is what we add.
6. **Keep spirituality inside.** At work behave like everyone else; live a double life, spiritual within and ordinary outside.
7. **Prepare from the night before, together.** Five minutes of reading before sleep carries into the lesson; group preparation is a debt to the society, which repays or fines many times over.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Advantage of the work over the reward** | The value of the effort, doubts and faith themselves, which build vessels, over the corporeal result. |
| **Loan from a friend who has none** | Rabash's allegory: the friend must work on faith, effort and humbling himself before others, and through mutual claims of love, the love is established forever. |
| **Hardening (Kishuya)** | The desire built on the recognition of evil, for forces to work above one's animal nature. |
| **Playing on the other's decency** | Making a request that wouldn't be acceptable in an ordinary society, relying on the friend's spiritual work; forbidden. |
| **Geneivat Da'at (theft of the mind)** | Attaching a reminder of the goal to a personal request or task, stealing the friend's own work. |
| **General spirit** | The atmosphere of the group, from which each friend picks up the goal by himself. |
| **Acting from the heart** | Relating to a friend by feeling, as to one's child, without calculation or expecting a reaction. |
| **"It is not for you to finish the work"** | Only to keep starting; the Creator completes the action. |
| **Double life** | A spiritual quality inside, clothed in ordinary corporeal life outside. |
| **Preparation from the evening** | Reading for a few minutes before sleep, from the Shema at bedtime to the morning Shema, preparing the vessels for the lesson. |
| **Society as a business** | Contributing to the society yields many times over; harming it costs to the same extent. |
