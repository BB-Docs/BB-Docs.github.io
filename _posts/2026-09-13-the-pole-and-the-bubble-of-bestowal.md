### Opening

Rabash's letter opens with a paradox: the "pole" that connects a person to the Creator is precisely the place where the upper one's own smallness (Katnut) — his AHP — falls into the lower one's degree, so that the lower one tastes "dust" rather than sweetness. Why would connection run through concealment rather than revelation? The archival lecture and its long live Q&A spend the entire session on that single question, working outward from Rosh Hashanah as the moment a person determines this point of connection, through why a person cannot free himself from a bad state alone, into an extended, often very personal exchange on how dissemination, the surrounding light, free choice, and the difference between Lo Lishma and Lishma all trace back to the same mechanism: a Creator who must stay hidden in order to leave any real choice to the person at all.

## Part 1 — The Pole: How the Upper and Lower Connect

Rav Laitman opens by explaining why connection depends on concealment rather than revealed goodness:

> "There's no other connection between the lower one and the upper one except for when the AHP of the upper one rests in the Galgalta ve Eynaim of the lower one... Regardless of the feeling of good or bad, it's the will to receive. The good feeling, to fill him, to give him a good feeling, the upper one can't do it, because by that it will lower the lower one to a beastly degree, and the lower one will always remain subordinate to the feeling and its vessels, and will never be able to rise above the feeling."

Instead, in order to let the lower one rise above feeling entirely and relate "not according to bitter and sweet but according to true and false," the upper one must conceal himself — which is precisely what allows the lower one to want the connection for its own sake, rather than for the feeling it produces.

## Part 2 — Rosh Hashanah as Determining the Point of Connection

Rav Laitman ties this directly to the work of the Ten Days of Repentance:

> "To bring himself to a state where I'm asking for this attitude to be according to the compatibility between Galgalta ve Eynaim and AHP, to have the intention to bestow just as the upper one has the quality to bestow to me — this is the work of man that belongs to what he checks during the Ten Terrible Days. And when he determines this attitude, it means that he determines the connection in the pole between him and the upper one, and this is called Rosh Hashanah."

He adds a striking claim about how much this determines in advance: "In Rosh Hashanah it's already determined how many good days will be, how much rain will come down... but a person determines in what way he will realize it, and by that he determines the effectiveness of the year."

## Part 3 — Why a Person Cannot Free Himself Alone

Asked how a person grasps this point while still immersed in a bad feeling, Rav Laitman is blunt about the limits of self-effort:

> "He is not capable of changing his attitude, because he dwells in a feeling, and he can come out of the feeling only with the help of some external reason — a teacher, the books, the society, something that's not in him... It says, 'a prisoner cannot break himself out of prison.' He can't. He's unable to do it."

The alternative to waiting for the upper force directly is investment beforehand: "to the extent that he prepared in advance his connection with the society, with the books, with the teacher... he can later receive support." The society's real value, he adds, is mutual, ordinary attention: "we pull each other up through this physical participation, and by that we give each other the feeling of importance of bestowal."

## Part 4 — Crossing the Barrier: Grabbing the Quality of Bestowal

Asked directly what it means to cross the Machsom, Rav Laitman describes an act of will performed in spite of feeling, not because of it:

> "To cross the barrier means to establish the first connection with the Creator, in spite of the feeling that He's giving me. I don't measure it, I can't measure the feeling. In spite of the feeling, no matter the feeling, I establish a connection with Him by grabbing the quality of bestowal. I decided this is the most important thing out of all my feelings, and I want to be in adhesion to that."

He explains why the upper one gives emptiness rather than fulfillment as part of the same mechanism: the AHP of the upper can freely provide ordinary "plenty and abundance" — beastly life — but the moment it wants to raise a person's Galgalta ve Eynaim, it must hide that fulfillment and show the quality of bestowal instead. "I suffer from both," he says of this stage — no pleasure from the AHP, and the unfamiliar, cold form of bestowal alongside it — "and bit by bit, by that, I learn."

## Part 5 — The Drop of Semen and the Forty Days of the Embryo

Asked where the certainty comes from that a bad feeling won't simply keep controlling him, Rav Laitman answers with the language of gestation:

> "I'm going through the three days of absorbing that drop of semen. I wish to truly adhere to Him in this point, in this discernment, where I and He are bestowing... That means that I'm adhering that drop of semen to the womb. I am doing this glue, I'm creating it, and I'm starting to develop. Until I attain the degree of Galgalta ve Eynaim, the degree of bestowal, that it will exist in me — this is called the forty days of creating the embryo."

Even past that point, he notes, there remains "danger of abortion" — further risk and further degrees of coarseness to pass through — but these are simply the ordinary stages of strengthening a connection already begun, not evidence that the connection itself is false.

## Part 6 — The Beneficial Lie: How Dissemination Actually Works

A student asks why true bestowal can't simply be announced to the world. Rav Laitman explains why it can't be sold the way ordinary bestowal is:

> "If we speak of true bestowal, without any profit to the will to receive... there's no support for such a will to receive. That's why we cannot disseminate this idea on the street. Maimonides writes that you should not reveal it, only slowly, gradually, until they grow wiser — then you reveal to them this secret."

He tells the story of asking Rabash, before his first public lecture, how to explain Kabbalah at all:

> "He says, you will read the definition — the wisdom of Kabbalah is the revelation of the Creator to the created beings in this world — and you explain to them that it brings them good, all the good they wish to hear... Of course it's a lie, it's true, but it's a beneficial lie. Each time you're going to tell him a lie, and each time he's going to hear not about Kabbalah but how you're deceiving him about this concept. True — but for the time being, the surrounding light influences him."

## Part 7 — The Surrounding Light Builds a Bubble of Bestowal

Rav Laitman explains how repeated contact with the material — even while still framed in terms of self-benefit — quietly changes what a person can tolerate hearing:

> "By drawing the surrounding light, the surrounding light arranges the will to receive for you in such a way where inside of it there's this new... blemish, something from the will to bestow. And then you're willing to hear a little bit about the will to bestow."

He locates the reason a person doesn't simply feel annihilated by this process: "inside our will to receive, there's something from the will to bestow... he feels that this will ensure his existence." This small, forming "vessel of bestowal" is what eventually lets a person hear about self-negation without panicking at the prospect of disappearing entirely.

## Part 8 — Man Creates the Creator: Free Choice and the Image of the Host

Asked why the Creator doesn't simply reveal Himself as a generous host from the outset, Rav Laitman ties the answer directly to free choice:

> "If He discloses it, then all of my actions will come from the will to receive in a natural way, without any ability to insert anything from myself, from my free choice... It's like Behina Aleph comes Behina Bet in the four phases of direct light — does the created being do something? No. There's no choice in the will to receive. The miracle is how to make it that I will make these actions, out of my own free will."

This leads to one of the lecture's sharpest formulations:

> "The Creator created man, and man creates the Creator. A person establishes before him the image of the Creator, each time higher than he himself is, more sublime, more giving — and he compares himself and tries to make himself similar to that image, and depicts to himself an even higher image, and pulls himself higher up... This is called a person's work."

## Part 9 — Using the Will to Receive in a Controlled Manner

An extended back-and-forth follows on whether a person is meant to select among his own desires. Rav Laitman resists framing it as picking and choosing:

> "A person uses wisely within his will to receive in order to get to the will to bestow. This is called the maidservant serving her mistress... The difference between the maidservant and the mistress is the intention. It's the same vessels."

Pressed on what "controlled manner" actually means, he clarifies that it isn't about managing which pleasures to feel: "A controlled manner means that I want that through it, the will to bestow, will grow in me. I don't relate to this in a controlled way, pleasures — what kind of pleasures? But when I work in the society, in the study, in the books... it will bring in me more relation to His quality of giving rather than expectation of pleasures."

## Part 10 — Lo Lishma and Lishma: Loving the Giver, Not Just the Gift

Asked how to actually advance toward Lishma, Rav Laitman distinguishes two objects of love with a memorable image:

> "One desire is that I want the giver Himself. The other desire is that I want the quality of the giver... What does it mean that I love Him? I love Him because He is the giver, or what I get out of Him? It's called loving fish — I love the fish here in my throat, it gives me pleasure at the point that feels taste. You can't love fish any other way."

Lo Lishma and Lishma, he explains, aren't sequential stages so much as a mixture that gradually shifts: "if we didn't have this Lo Lishma, where I receive pleasures from Him, I would throw away that Lishma, for sure. And that's how I advance — in the relation between them, where the Lishma grows bigger and bigger... I don't want to leave the Lishma." Even Baal HaSulam's own example of romantic love — loving someone you can't cleanly separate from the pleasure he gives you — is offered as a legitimate, necessary stage on the way, not something to feel ashamed of.

## Part 11 — Short Way, Long Way: Direct Entry vs. Years of Preparation

A student pushes for immediacy — not wanting to wait years to "get it." Rav Laitman's answer is that directness is available now, in principle, through a single equation:

> "Directly means you're not running away from this condition, love your friend as yourself... If you accept it this way, and you place in all those places the equal sign — bestowal, Creator, love of friends, spirituality — if you begin to accept these definitions as one, there's nothing but that, reception or bestowal... then you are already on a direct line."

But he's equally candid that most people take the long way, needing years before articles like "The Giving of the Torah" stop sounding like children's morality and start sounding like a real vocation: "sometimes it takes ten to fifteen years before a person returns to this article and is willing to listen... It's called a short way and a long way." Either way, he adds, a person needs to actively build an inner and outer system around the realization once it lands, "so it doesn't let me run away."

## Part 12 — Building the Environment From Within

Near the close, a student asks what mutual bestowal even means if each person's real work happens privately, inside himself. Rav Laitman answers that influence runs on levels a person doesn't fully control or even perceive:

> "We bestow upon each other, we influence each other on several levels of connection... I don't know myself and my real importance and influence on the world, just like a newborn baby doesn't know how many people are working to sustain it just because it was born. So now, in my influence on the society, on the friends, on our advancement — there are things we do to each other without knowing that we're doing them."

This is also why, he notes, Kabbalists traditionally forbid discussing one's inner spiritual work directly with a friend: ordinary conversation should stay on functional matters, "so you will know that you're not working with your words" — the real work is the private intention, felt but not spoken. Asked whether the environment's overall spiritual "height" matters, Rav Laitman had earlier made clear that only its direction does: "even if they understand just a little bit and somehow agree, according to their own lie, that's enough... I have to build the environment towards me; I have to build it from within me." The session closes there, moving into a round of participants' impressions and a song.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Connection between a person and the Creator runs specifically through concealment — the upper one's own smallness falling into the lower one's degree — because open revelation of goodness would leave no room for real free choice.
2. Rosh Hashanah is reframed as the moment a person determines the point of connection with the upper one; the year's whole effectiveness follows from how that connection is realized, not from external circumstance.
3. A person cannot free himself from a bad state through his own effort alone — "a prisoner cannot break himself out of prison" — which is why investment in teacher, books, and society beforehand is what makes later support possible.
4. Crossing the barrier means grabbing the quality of bestowal as most important in spite of whatever feeling accompanies it, not waiting for feeling itself to change; the process is likened to the three days and forty days of an embryo's early, fragile development.
5. Dissemination necessarily works through what Rav Laitman calls "a beneficial lie" — presenting Kabbalah in terms of the good a person wants to hear — because true, unrewarded bestowal has no natural support inside the will to receive at all.
6. The surrounding light gradually builds a small "bubble" of the will to bestow inside a person's will to receive, which is what allows him to keep hearing about self-negation without feeling he will simply disappear.
7. The Creator conceals Himself specifically to preserve free choice — "the Creator created man, and man creates the Creator" — since a person must build and pursue his own ever-rising image of the Giver rather than be swept along by revealed pleasure.
8. Lo Lishma and Lishma describe two inseparably mixed loves — for the giver's gifts and for the giver himself — with advancement measured by the changing ratio between them, not by discarding one for the other; a direct, one-equation entry ("bestowal equals Creator equals love of friends") is available in principle, though most people take years to reach it.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| The Pole | The connecting column between the Creator and Malchut of Ein Sof, formed specifically where the AHP (smallness) of the upper one rests in the Galgalta ve Eynaim of the lower one — the sole channel through which upper and lower actually connect. |
| AHP / Galgalta ve Eynaim (GE) | The two halves of a Partzuf; here used to describe the upper one's concealed quality of bestowal (AHP) as it falls into and shapes the lower one's own intention (GE), rather than his feeling. |
| Machsom (Barrier) | Crossed by establishing a first connection with the Creator in spite of one's present feeling — deciding that the quality of bestowal matters more than whatever the vessels of reception report, good or bad. |
| Ibur (Gestation) | Here described through the "drop of semen" and the "forty days of creating the embryo" — the fragile early stage of attaining Galgalta ve Eynaim, still subject to further risk and further degrees of coarseness to pass through. |
| Surrounding Light (Ohr Makif) | The force that gradually builds a "bubble" of the will to bestow inside a person's will to receive, making him able to tolerate hearing about — and eventually wanting — self-negation. |
| Lo Lishma / Lishma | Not-for-Her-sake / for-Her-sake, here distinguished as loving the giver's gift versus loving the giver himself; the two remain mixed throughout a person's advancement, with Lishma simply growing relative to Lo Lishma rather than replacing it. |
| Maimonides (Rambam) | Cited here for the principle that Kabbalah's secret must be revealed only gradually, to the extent a person's own wisdom and readiness allow — the basis for why dissemination cannot simply announce bestowal directly. |
| Direct Line | Rav Laitman's term for entering spirituality immediately by equating bestowal, the Creator, love of friends, and spirituality as one single concept, rather than needing years before the equation feels real. |
