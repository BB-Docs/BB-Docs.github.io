### Opening

The article returns to "judges and officers," because this is the Torah portion. A person is influenced from within and from without by endless thoughts, disturbances and pieces of advice, and there is far too much written about all of it. What he needs instead is something simple: a kind of codex, one inner point of scrutiny that judges every influence without bribe or bias. Rav Laitman explains how that point rises to Bina, why it needs a second line to weigh against, why the judge so often can't carry out his own verdict, and why the vessels for all of this can be acquired only from the society, not from above.

## Part 1 — Building the Judge

- From the article we see that "certainly there is something that depends on us." It is not that everything comes from above and I am a passive part through which positive and negative influences pass.
- A person must build a special relation to all the influences that come to him, "so that through that he sees spirituality." Without it, the picture remains "an emotional picture without understanding, without deeper vision."
- The countless disturbances that throw us from one state to the next demand that we put "all our time and energy and attention" into the point of scrutiny, so that it is as independent as possible: not dependent on the mood or on what I'll get from it.

#### From Bina, the judge sees Chochma

- "The judge has no more than what his eyes can see." Bit by bit the scrutiny has to rise to the height of **Bina**, and from the height of Bina one can see **Chochma**.
- First the judge must be independent of anything, which is the quality of Bina. Then, to judge, he already needs the light of Chochma.
- From there he scrutinizes all his desires, thoughts and intentions and knows what to determine for each quality, in accordance with how that quality appears in the Creator, and with what he sees from the right and from the left.
- In the execution, through the officers, he builds the **middle line**, "which is essentially the man." The inner point from which he builds is called the judge.

> So the main point is to always strengthen that independent point within us, which is truly the point from which the middle line will later grow.

- The same order holds from above downward. The Creator first appears to us as a judge, through the month of **Elul** and the Day of Atonement. Then, through limitations and fasts, we come to the fulfillment of an awakening from above, and through the officers we make the corrections of the seven days of **Sukkot**.

## Part 2 — The Second Pan of the Scale

- **Q: Where do I find the second point from which to judge?** To judge, "I need the two pans of the scale": the qualities of the created being, the qualities of the Creator, and the person in the middle.
- "You attain the qualities of the Creator through your work in the left line." But there is no such thing as engaging in the left alone: "You can't engage in the left if you don't go to it from the right." Then you can see how opposite it is.
- "You can't see just one line." If you saw qualities from one side only, you wouldn't see them at all, "because you're already under their control."

#### No point outside myself

- It's like us in this world: it seems "I am acting," but forces are pulling me, thoughts are sent to me, and "my machine just processes and does everything automatically."
- I can talk about this intellectually, "but I don't feel that, so it doesn't help."
- To be truly outside myself means "to begin making a restriction on the intentions for self." By that you move away from your nature and come out "as if between two worlds," and from outside you can see the correct picture.
- "By what do you exit one nature? Only by looking from the second nature." There is no state in between: there is the Creator and the created being, and the most we can consider in between is the middle third of Tifferet, **Klipat Noga**.

## Part 3 — Kabbalah Isn't Hidden

- **Q: The world knows that to attain truth one must be independent. So why is Kabbalah hidden?** "Kabbalah is not hidden. Who said it's hidden?" It was hidden from humanity because "humanity was in its diapers."
- If your desires are only for food, sex, family, money, honor and knowledge, you don't need Kabbalah. You chase them and live them, possibly for thousands of years, until a desire develops above them. Then you need a method.
- This additional sense "doesn't develop naturally, it only develops through your labor," independent, deliberate labor.
- Kabbalists always looked for people who cared deeply, and there were many students over the generations, "but it was quietly, in modesty." Making noise would only awaken hatred from every side, "because none of them cares about this."
- "No one is hiding it. It's just revealed according to the need." Even with 24 hours on television, "you have to match your cry to the one who's willing to respond to it." Scattering it to people who can't appreciate it is like handing a bag of coins to a tribe that doesn't know what money is.
- We are building the method for the next generation. The demand could come in five or ten years, or twenty or thirty.

#### What people call truth

- Many people are looking for truth, but by it they mean the right attitude toward women, children, the sick and the needy. "Truth is the state of the Creator. That's it."
- There are degrees: preparation, concealment, then revelation. One who enters spirituality is in the truth first as an embryo, "annulled within that truth," then as a small one, then as an adult.
- All of humanity is heading toward it, yet only a few thousand people in the world can be in this process consciously.

## Part 4 — The Mind Serves the Will

- **Q: You say a person must scrutinize, yet the judge comes from another nature. Where do I begin?** "You shall place judges and officers in all your gates that the Lord gives you." The gates are the conditions the Creator sets before you; your part is to relate to them the right way, first as a judge and then as an officer.
- "There's mutual work here. The Creator gives and the person also acts." Without your own work you won't have the discernments by which to become similar to Him "in thoughts, in actions, in everything," realizing the **Reshimot** from below upward.
- We don't do this with the mind. "Our mind, on each and every degree, serves the will." Put a person in a village and in a few years he is like the animals he tends; put him in a university and he develops demands. "Everything depends on the environment."
- So "we need to make sure the will changes." To raise my desires from degree X to X+1, "I need to receive that addition from someone. I don't have it in me." That addition is the society, and "my mind needs to work now to convince me to bow down before the society."

#### The vessel is in the group

- It doesn't depend on the size of the society. Even a group of beginners who are inspired by divinity carries the vessel, though it isn't felt where they are. You use it to the depth of your degree, not theirs.
- "That's why it's written, From all my students I have learned." The teacher receives, and the student remains a student.
- Each of us holds all the vessels, up to infinity, and you can use them to the extent you're willing to participate in the friend's sorrow and development and connect with him internally.
- Active work, as Rabash writes: to lower oneself before the friends on one hand, and on the other "to demand of the group to bestow upon you the greatness of the Creator."

#### Awakening from the group, not from above

- "An awakening from above will not bring you closer to the Creator. An awakening from the group will." It comes as you gather the broken vessels together with your own vessel, "that is actual work."
- An awakening from above isn't an addition to your vessels. He opens the recognition of His greatness by lending you His **NHY**, His vessel, and later takes it back, leaving only memories, "which aren't really records that you can use."
- "You can't acquire vessels from above. You have to acquire them from the society." So don't wait for an awakening from above; it promotes only the one who already works.
- Who does the work of the judges and officers? "It's the society, the desire of the society." Nobody can recognize from outside how a person does it within himself.
- Don't let the person or the group become the goal: "only the Creator." The focus of the scrutiny is the judge; carrying it out and keeping the boundaries, "oriented exactly towards the goal," is the officer.

## Part 5 — When the Judge Can't Carry It Out

- **Q: The judge in me says "guilty," but the officer in me can't carry it out. How do I overcome that?** "If you can decide correctly in your mind, and then you can't execute properly with the body, then you have the shattering."
- This happens deliberately, from above downward, to give records of the shattering, so that later, in correcting it from below upward, we know how it must be done.
- "For every step we take, we have to first of all discover the negative aspect of it," our failure, confusion and despair, "and from that we begin to rise. There's no other way."
- It's not like this world, where you sometimes find a mistake. "Here every action starts from a mistake," because the vessels we receive are all broken.
- If the vessel is completely broken, every action toward correction is a successful one. So you never really go down: "There are only ascents in holiness, never descents."

#### Connecting three points

- To perform a correct action, connect three points: me, my action and the Creator, so that they align.
- I need to understand exactly what I am, sense the Creator as a degree a bit above myself, and make the action one that brings me closer to that more advanced quality. This relates to concessions and to more refinement.
- "The power for this you can receive only from the society." If the higher degree is revealed to you as important, even against your nature, you'll go through it like a person who goes into surgery because "otherwise I'm dead."

## Part 6 — Women, Little Ones, Slaves, and Work from Fear

- The person must scrutinize all that is in him: "the women, the little ones, and the slaves and the general population." These are the types of broken vessels in him.
- He has no **Gever** (man), from *Hitgabrut*, overcoming, and no **Chaver** (friend), from *Hitchabrut*, connection toward holiness. The women are the will to receive; the little ones, smallness of reason; the slaves, those who can't emerge from under its control, including the Hebrew slave who doesn't want to be freed. The Gemara tells many stories of how to treat each desire.
- **Q: What is work from fear, taught first?** A person in the will to receive "you can't turn to with anything but the fear." The question of the meaning of life comes from fear that there is no pleasure now and no meaning for the future: "What'll be with me?" These are questions below the barrier.
- Above the barrier the question is different: "What am I for Him?" There is a meaning, "but it's all aimed towards Him."
- So with beginners you start from **Lo Lishma**, with a reward that they'll feel good: "They tasted and saw that the Lord is good." As they grow wiser and the light of Chochma comes, the secrets are revealed to them gradually.
- **Q: Does he mean the time of preparation or after the barrier?** "He writes it for everyone." And all the questions and answers "only fill your ears"; there can never be a fulfillment through the ears.

#### If I am not for me, who is for me?

- **Q: How does "if I am not for me, who is for me" fit this article?** It is not the left line. "Because then you miss the point of the Adam in you."
- "It seems to you that if you take the right, your self disappears." If it disappears, it isn't the right; you're an embryo with nothing in it.
- Right and left are qualities of the spiritual nature that you approach and absorb, and you perceive them from the goal. "If you don't start from me and the goal and then go from right to left, it's not right and left. You just don't know where you are."
- A person must hold this point all day long. Rav Laitman recalls being amazed to see it in Rabash: "For him, it was just his inner atmosphere." For us, it's an effort even to think about it.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Build one independent point.** Instead of endless advice, a simple inner judge that scrutinizes every influence without bribe or bias.
2. **The judge rises to Bina and sees Chochma.** From there it weighs each quality against how it appears in the Creator, and the officers build the middle line.
3. **No line is seen alone.** The Creator's qualities are attained through work in the left line, approached from the right; one nature is exited only from the second.
4. **Kabbalah is revealed according to need.** It was never hidden, only waiting for a desire beyond the corporeal, which develops only through deliberate labor.
5. **Vessels come from the society.** The mind serves the will, so the will must be changed through bowing before the group; an awakening from above adds no vessels.
6. **Every action starts from a mistake.** When the judge can't execute, that is the shattering, given deliberately so that the correction has records to work with.
7. **Start from me and the goal.** Lo Lishma and fear are the entry for beginners, but right and left have meaning only when held toward the goal all day.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Asherah by the altar** | Appointing an unworthy judge; in the work, building on "happy are they," serving only when it feels good. |
| **Judge** | The independent inner point of scrutiny, which rises to Bina and from which the middle line grows. |
| **Officer** | The execution of the verdict, keeping the boundaries oriented exactly toward the goal. |
| **Gates** | The states and conditions the Creator sets before a person, at each of which he must set judges and officers. |
| **Bina / Chochma** | Independence from self-interest, the height from which the judge can see the light of Chochma. |
| **Middle line** | What the person builds between the right and left lines through the officers; "essentially the man." |
| **Klipat Noga** | The middle third of Tifferet, the closest thing to a state between Creator and created being. |
| **Awakening from above** | A temporary loan of the upper's NHY that reveals His greatness but adds no vessels of one's own. |
| **Awakening from the group** | Gathering the friends' broken vessels with one's own, the actual work that brings one closer. |
| **Shattering** | Deciding correctly in the mind but being unable to execute in the body, given to leave records for correction. |
| **Women, little ones, slaves** | The will to receive, smallness of reason, and desires that can't leave its control. |
| **Gever / Chaver** | "Man" from overcoming (Hitgabrut) and "friend" from connection (Hitchabrut), both still lacking in preparation. |
| **Work from fear** | The entry for one in the will to receive: questions of "what will be with me," below the barrier. |
