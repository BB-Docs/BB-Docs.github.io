### Opening

"I am the first and I am the last, and there is no God besides Me." The central issue, Rav Laitman says, is the uniqueness of the Creator: one force that created everything, operates everything and brings it to correction. Besides discovering it, "we don't need to discover anything and there is nothing to discover." The lesson explains why we can't simply declare this and sit back. The beginning and the end of every state are sealed, but the middle is our work: determining our attitude toward everything that happens and uniting it with Him, not by diminishing our desires but from their full height.

## Part 1 — One Force, and a World in Confusion

- There is one force: one that created and one that brings to correction, "and there's none else besides Him." We can't picture this, because for us creation is made of lights and vessels, two opposite things.
- Sometimes it seems we're in control, according to the level of concealment. Then we discover that the Creator does everything, according to the measure of revelation. Until the end of correction, "we're in a maze."
- As in Shushan the capital, "there is a king and Mordechai and Haman, and they don't know which one is right." A person's desires and thoughts are all in confusion and lack of comprehension.
- The more a person is worthy of corrections, "the more this confusion grows and hinders him greatly." A regular person is just alive. He is "disconnected even from concealment," and nobody talks to him about revelation, so he has no problem.
- One who wants to enter the domain of the One is prodded "with problems which go against unity," so that he feels them and they force him to look for a true solution.

## Part 2 — First, Last, and Shabbat

- "I am the first and I am the last": the beginning and end of creation, and of every action. We must first come to unity between these two points and the operations in the middle, the corrections we make. Sometimes we're in the domain of the many, sometimes in the domain of the One.
- The whole Tractate **Shabbat** discusses the domain of the many and the domain of the singular, and how we may move from place to place.
- Shabbat corresponds to the end of correction. A person who hasn't reached it yet can be in a seeming end of correction, called the spiritual Shabbat. He doesn't make it himself; he prepares, and it is done to him from above.
- Then he is brought from the domain of the many to the domain of the singular, "and forbidden to exit the boundaries of the Shabbat." Only in certain desires can he feel the unity. The desires not yet corrected, where it seems other forces are at work besides the Creator, "we simply freeze and do not use."
- At the end of correction, all the desires display this unity, and we are above all the prohibitions. Everything enters the **middle line**, and even the thoughts for and against assist it and build the corrected state.

## Part 3 — You Can't Skip to "He Does Everything"

- The **Introduction to The Book of Zohar** asks the questions everyone asks: how does the Creator create and sustain the shells, why did He create evil, and where is He concealed while He works?
- Do we have actions to perform until we discover that He is one, unique and unified? "That depends on our corrections." A person who isn't corrected can't say, "I've read that the Creator does everything, so there's nothing to do."
- To the extent you haven't yet corrected the vessels in which you feel His uniqueness, you must work by yourself to complete them. "That's why the doctor is given permission to heal," and "if I am not for me, who is for me?" These are states a person must live and act in until he gathers them all into the domain of the singular.
- Then all the qualities of the Creator and the created being connect "as two Partzufim," in the unending coupling, **Zivug de lo Pasik**.

#### Where to read about it

- There are many beautiful writings on this unity, especially in Hasidut, from the **Baal Shem Tov**, who began scrutinizing it in a more emotional way. Baal HaSulam writes about it in his letters.
- All of **Shamati** comes from its first article, "**There Is None Else Besides Him**": "This is the main article, the Keter, the crown, and the rest of the book is just an outcome from that."

## Part 4 — Aiming at the One

- Everything else connects to one goal, to discover the One. So in all the problems of life we check "how much we are or aren't uniting ourselves with the path, with the action... and the goal."
- We can't aim at the Creator the way we'd aim at a mountain with a compass. Instead, "I describe to myself a state where I unite everything that happens to me in my thoughts and desires and all the circumstances," and that essence I call the Creator.
- Then I see where I'm still not unifying Him with everything. "Then it means that I'm seeing the path" between my current state and the state where He is revealed as one.
- When we study and speak of the light that reforms, "the Torah which heals one's flesh," we should aim at what needs healing: "this disease, which is our personality, which is too prominent."

#### Ten utterances

- "The world was created with ten utterances," to punish the wicked and give the righteous a place for work and reward. One who feels confused, disconnected and powerless, with no inclination to connect them to the Creator, is called wicked. Through blows and effort he repents and becomes righteous.
- "Without being wicked, he can never become righteous." The question is only the speed and the attitude. Each one must descend to the depth of every shattering in him and feel it in all its oppositeness from the Creator.
- The **ten utterances** are the ten Sefirot that first disconnect Malchut from Keter as coverings, letting her exist in the worst state. Through them, influences descend by cause and effect and bring her to states from which she can awaken to correction.
- Gathering all these impressions, a person reaches the end of correction with every discernment present. The ten utterances become ten Sefirot that reveal His uniqueness, and all the names come together as "**the good who does good**."
- Anyone can say "the good who does good, there's none else besides Him." "But did you break yourself with every thought and every action?" There is no one who keeps a commandment "unless he failed in it," and a commandment is the correct use of each desire, uniting it to the Creator.

## Part 5 — The Middle Is Your Work

- "The beginning and the end are sealed. In the middle it's your work," by the path of Torah or the path of suffering.
- Every state also has a beginning and an end that you don't determine: the **Reshimot**. "My decision is in what way to cross from one record to the next, from degree to degree."
- So don't worry about where you must be at the end, and never look back at your origins. "The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh." Looking back is Lot's wife: "You turn into something that never changes, into salt."

#### Change the attitude, not the state

- "Not to change the states themselves, but to change my attitude toward them." I am given blows: attribute them to the Creator. I am given good things: attribute them to the Creator. "If you attribute them to the Creator, they immediately become sweetened."
- "This is our work, and this is called scrutinizing everything through intention."
- Our belief that we are the ones acting "is precisely what leads us to corruptions." All of humanity's "progress," in quotation marks, has revealed the wrong path. Had we concerned ourselves only with our attitude, we would have produced what's necessary for life and devoted the rest to the Creator's uniqueness.
- The less we interfere with reality and the more we attend to how we receive it, the more corrected our state, until nothing changes, as the light of **Ein Sof** is in absolute rest.

## Part 6 — Unity from the Full Height of the Desire

- **Q: Eastern methods also focus on attitude rather than action. Is there value in that?** There's a resemblance, "but there is no screen, no Masach." They don't build the vessel through which everything is unified, "so they remain passive and that's it."
- Here, "you need to actively change your attitude so that each time, with the screen, you enter into unity with the greatest possible desire to receive."
- Annulling the self means that "at the height of pride 100%, at the height of envy 100%, at the height of hatred 100%," with the greatest desires for money, honor, knowledge and bodily pleasures, "you unify all of them with the Creator from the full height of those desires." Diminishing them first makes you a seed: "You can do that, but then it's dead."
- We are constantly in the split between the vessel and the light, "and this split gives us no rest." An Indian sits and is genuinely happy, needing nothing. But with great envy and lust, a person feels his true self, and the coarseness and the screen can't be discovered without revelation from above.

#### Only what you can unify is revealed

- Each soul is a large part of Malchut of Ein Sof, but you are shown only the part you can deal with and attribute to unity.
- The breaking of the vessels is the breaking of the screen, of the connection between the vessel and the Creator. In a corrected vessel there is direct light and reflected light, which becomes inner light down to the **Tabur** of the Partzuf. "That's where unity exists."
- Where you can't do so is the Sof of the Partzuf, which you keep frozen, and beyond it are many vessels you don't feel at all. "If the whole vessel were revealed to you, you wouldn't be able to perform a restriction upon it." For example, ten kilograms are revealed, you restrict them, and with two of them you achieve unity.

## Part 7 — Determining One's Attitude

- **Q: What does it mean to determine one's attitude?** "I, the Creator, and the action I'm engaged in, the state I'm in, must all be as one." Then "I have positioned myself in the correct state" (letters, p. 64).
- On p. 70 of the letters, the state is divided into world, year and soul (**Olam, Shanah, Nefesh**). When they come together in me, connected to the Creator, "it's a sign that I too am included in Him."
- They don't exist somewhere outside me; they exist in me. Like docking two airplanes, you need three parameters for one to fit into the other. Unify each with the Creator, as the cause of all three, then bring them together. "This will be called truly attaining unity."
- He gives the example of the High Priest entering the Holy of Holies on **Yom Kippur**: soul is the degree of the High Priest, year is Yom Kippur, and world is my attitude to all the surrounding souls, the whole vessel. "The rest already depends on the Creator."
- "Of course we don't have a button," but it is revealed gradually. In the end, "it's my attitude toward myself and my attitude to those in my surroundings. I have nothing beyond myself and my world."

#### A blessing over the coffee

- To unify means "that He created it, He operates within it, and He brought it to the state." "The vessels do not actually break and the vessels do not actually become corrected, so to speak."
- "The desire itself is not bad nor good. It is bad if I do not connect it to Him, and good if I connect it to Him."
- Going to drink coffee, I say a blessing over it: I unify what is in the coffee, in my vessel and in the pleasure revealed in it, with Him. The screen and reflected light unify the vessel and its fulfillment with the giver. "This is called the revelation of unity."

#### What we feel

- "Our feeling is the outcome of the gap between unity and disunity." I'm cold, hungry, in pain, someone shouts at me: these are "different garments of closeness or distance from the Creator."
- "There is none else besides Him is the whole foundation of our work and the entire principle upon which this world is built." All the laws of physics and chemistry are forms of connection, separation and distance.
- The desire itself needs no correction. "What needs correction is the thought accompanying the desire," whether it is used in order to bestow.
- **Q: Can we do this before the barrier?** No. Before the barrier we scrutinize how distant we are. Transgressions, intentional and unintentional, are impressions of the absence of unity. Beyond the barrier, to the extent we unify with Him, we are an incomplete or a completely righteous one. Degrees are measures of equivalence of form, as he explains in "Inner Observation," part one of the Study of the Ten Sefirot.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **There is one force and nothing else to discover.** All our confusion, which grows as we advance, exists to push us toward revealing His uniqueness.
2. **You can't skip to the conclusion.** Until our vessels are corrected, "if I am not for me, who is for me" applies, and we must work.
3. **No righteous without first being wicked.** The ten utterances conceal and then reveal; each shattering must be felt before all the names build the Good Who Does Good.
4. **The beginning and end are sealed.** Our only freedom is how we cross from Reshimo to Reshimo, so don't look back or ahead.
5. **Change the attitude, not the state.** Attributing blows and good alike to the Creator sweetens them; acting as if we are the doers corrupts.
6. **Unify from the full height of desire.** Unlike passive annulment, the screen lets us bring envy, pride and lust into unity, as much as is revealed to us.
7. **I, my action and the Creator as one.** World, year and soul unified with Him in every desire, even a cup of coffee, is the revelation of unity.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Uniqueness of the Creator** | That one force creates, operates and corrects everything; the only thing we ultimately need to discover. |
| **I am the first and I am the last** | The Creator sets the beginning and end of creation and of each state; the middle is man's work. |
| **Domain of the many / of the singular** | Feeling other forces besides the Creator, versus feeling that He alone acts. |
| **Spiritual Shabbat** | A seeming end of correction given from above, in which uncorrected desires are frozen and not used. |
| **Zivug de lo Pasik** | The unending coupling of the Creator's and the created being's qualities at the end of correction. |
| **Ein Od Milvado** | "There is none else besides Him," the first article of Shamati and the foundation of the whole work. |
| **Ten utterances** | Ten Sefirot that first conceal and lead Malchut through states, then reveal His uniqueness at the end of correction. |
| **Good Who Does Good** | The general name revealed after all the names, once every vessel is corrected. |
| **Determining one's attitude** | Uniting myself, my action and the Creator, and attributing every state to Him. |
| **World, Year, Soul** | Olam, Shanah, Nefesh: the three parameters of every state, unified with the Creator to attain unity. |
| **Masach (screen)** | What lets a person unify his desire with the Creator at full strength, which passive methods lack. |
| **Sof of the Partzuf** | Where a person can't yet unify the revealed desire and keeps it frozen. |
| **Blessing** | Unifying the vessel and the pleasure in it with the giver through the screen and reflected light. |
| **Transgressions** | Impressions of the absence of unity; their opposite, the measure of unity, is the degree of righteousness. |
