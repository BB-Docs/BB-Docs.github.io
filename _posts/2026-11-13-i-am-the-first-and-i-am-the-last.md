### Opening

"Except for that, we don't need to discover anything, and there is nothing to discover." There is one force that created, operates and brings everything to correction. Yet we live among lights and vessels, two opposites, and see everything through a maze. If the Creator is the first and the last, what is left for us in the middle? Rav Laitman answers that the beginning and end are sealed, and our whole work lies in between: not in changing the states that come to us, but in determining our attitude toward them, until we unify world, year and soul with the Creator.

## Part 1 — One Force, Seen Through a Maze

- The central issue is the **uniqueness of the Creator**. All bestowals come to us only from Him, and it's only His uniqueness that we have to adapt ourselves to.
- We cannot imagine this, because for us all of creation is lights and vessels, "two things that are opposite to one another." Sometimes it seems we're in control, according to the measure of concealment; sometimes we discover it's the Creator doing everything, according to the measure of revelation.
- Until the end of correction, "we're in a maze." As written of Shushan the capital, there is a king, **Mordechai** and **Haman**, and no one knows which is right. "The city of Shushan was bewildered": all of a person's desires and thoughts are in confusion.

#### Who feels the confusion

- In a regular person there is no problem. He is "completely disconnected even from concealment," and nobody talks about revelation there.
- The more a person is worthy of corrections, the more this confusion grows and bothers him. One who wants to enter the domain of the One is pushed forward with problems that go against unity, so that they will force him to look for a true solution.

#### First, last, and the middle

- "I am the first and I am the last": the beginning of creation and the end of creation. We must first of all come to unity between these two points.
- In the middle are the corrections we make, actions with a sense attached to them, in which "sometimes we're in the domain of the many, sometimes the domain of the One."

## Part 2 — Shabbat: The Domain of the Many and the Domain of the Singular

- The whole tractate of Shabbat discusses this: the **domain of the many** (Reshut HaRabim), the **domain of the singular** (Reshut HaYachid), and how it is forbidden to carry from one to the other on Shabbat.
- **Shabbat corresponds to the end of correction.** A person who hasn't reached it yet can be in a state "as if" the end of correction, called the spiritual Shabbat. He didn't make it; he prepared himself for it to be done to him from above, an **awakening from above**.
- Not leaving the boundaries of Shabbat means staying only within the desires where he can feel the unity. The desires still in the domain of the many, where it seems other forces act besides the Creator and "that he himself first of all exists," he isolates. "Those desires we simply freeze and do not use."
- At the end of correction all desires display the unification, there is only the domain of the One, and we are above all the prohibitions. Everything enters the **middle line**, which unites the two lines; the thoughts for and against assist it and build the corrected state.

## Part 3 — Who Is Acting?

- Baal HaSulam asks this repeatedly in the Introduction to the Book of Zohar: how does the Creator create and sustain the **Klipot** and evil? Is He at work or not, and is anything against His uniqueness? "These questions are questions that everyone asks."
- The answer depends on our corrections. A person who is not yet corrected cannot say, "I've read, I heard that the Creator does everything, so there's nothing to do."
- To the extent you haven't corrected the vessels in which His uniqueness works on you, "you need to work by yourself to complete this." That's why "the doctor is given permission to heal," and why "If I am not for me, who is for me?"
- A person must collect all his desires, thoughts and decisions into the domain of the Singular. Then everything connects in the **Zivug de lo Pasik**, the unending coupling, all the qualities of the Creator and of the created being as two Partzufim.

#### Where to read more

- There are many beautiful writings on this unity in Hasidism, beginning with the **Baal Shem Tov**, who scrutinized it "in a more emotional way." Baal HaSulam writes about it in his letters (e.g. page 25).
- "All of Shamati is actually from that first article," **There Is None Else Besides Him**. It is the main article, "the crown," and the rest of the book is an outcome of it.

#### How to aim at the Creator

- **Q: How do we aim at a goal that is the Creator?** We can't just see Him and walk toward Him with a compass. "Seeing the Creator means that I describe to myself a state where I unite everything that happens to me," in thoughts, desires and circumstances, as one unique and unified thing. "That essence is called the Creator."
- Then I orient myself toward it and see where I still don't unify Him with everything. That is seeing the path between two states: my current one, where He is not yet one, and the one where He is revealed as One, Unique and Unified.
- When we speak of the **Reforming Light** that "heals one's flesh," we must know what we want healed: "this disease, which is our personality, which is too prominent."

## Part 4 — The Ten Utterances Become Ten Sefirot

- "The world was created with ten utterances," to punish the wicked and reward the righteous. One who sees himself confused, disconnected and powerless, without inclination to connect what happens to the Creator, "is called **wicked**." Through blows and failures he begins to correct them and becomes **righteous**.
- "Without being wicked, he can never become righteous." The only question is the speed, and the attitude toward what happens. Each one must descend to the depth of every shattering in his vessel, feel it in all its oppositeness from the Creator, and come to a decision of his own to ask for the power to correct.

#### What the ten utterances are

- They are the ten **Sefirot** that, to begin with, disconnect **Malchut** from **Keter** as coverings, allowing Malchut to exist in the very worst state.
- On the other hand, through them Malchut receives influences, by way of cause and effect, that bring her time after time into states where she can awaken toward correction. "Malchut herself cannot plan the path."
- A person gathers his impressions from each state until he reaches the end of correction and feels it "in all of the discernments he was in, and none of them is missing."
- Then the ten utterances become ten Sefirot that reveal His uniqueness: every concealment becomes revelation, and all the holy names build the general name, **the Good Who Does Good**.

#### Serving and not serving

- Anyone can say, "certainly the Good Who Does Good, there is none else besides Him." "But did you break yourself with every thought and every action that was brought to you? Did you fail in all that, and later correct it?"
- That is the difference between one who serves the Creator and one who does not, because "there's no person who stands on a commandment unless he failed in it." A **commandment** is the correct use of each desire, uniting it with the Creator.

## Part 5 — The Middle Is Yours: Attitude, Not Action

- The beginning and the end are sealed. In every state there is a beginning and an end you don't determine; those are the **Reshimot**. "My decision is in what way to cross from one record to the next, from degree to degree": by the path of Torah or the path of suffering.
- "Don't worry about where you need to be at the end, and never look back at your origins." Looking back is pointless, like "the fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh," or like **Lot's wife**, turned into something that never changes, into salt.
- What we determine is our attitude toward the states that change in us. "I am given blows: attribute them to the Creator. I am given good things: attribute them to the Creator." Don't change the things themselves, not even the blows.

> If you attribute them to the Creator, they immediately become sweetened. This is our work, and this is called scrutinizing everything through intention.

#### Leaving reality alone

- We think "we are the ones who act," and that is precisely what leads to corruption. A person doesn't need to do anything except determine his relationship to what's happening. "Change your attitude toward what's being done and then everything will fall into place."
- All of humanity's "progress," in quotation marks, is the revelation of the wrong path we have taken. Had we concerned ourselves only with our attitude, we would have produced only what is necessary for life and devoted the rest to the Creator's uniqueness. None of this development brings a person any benefit.
- The more we leave reality alone and concern ourselves with how we receive it, the more corrected our state becomes, until there is no change whatsoever: "the light of Ein Sof is in absolute rest," when I have corrected my attitude toward every state that can come from the Creator.

## Part 6 — Not Like Eastern Methods: Unity With a Screen

- **Q: Does a technical practice have any value?** People instinctively try such a path. Eastern methods also tend to focus on the attitude to what happens rather than on entering into it. "This is the difference between East and West in general."
- But "there is no screen, no Masach. They don't build the vessel through which I can unify everything." So they remain passive: you diminish your desire and enter a seeming unity "because you're like something inanimate."
- Here it's the contrary: actively change your attitude so that each time, with the screen, you enter unity with the greatest possible desire to receive. You rise above all of creation, and creation follows you into unity.

#### Annulment at full height

- Annulment of the self means that at the height of pride, envy and hatred, "100%," with the greatest desires for money, honor, knowledge and bodily pleasures, you unify all of them with the Creator. If instead you shrink yourself into a drop, a seed, "you can do that, but then it's dead."
- The inner split between the vessel and the light, between the desire to receive and the screen, "gives us no rest," and all our states arise from it. An Indian sage, by contrast, "needs nothing, and he's genuinely happy."
- The coarseness and the screen cannot be discovered on one's own; this "truly requires a method from above."

#### Only what you can unify is revealed

- Each soul is a large part of **Malchut of Ein Sof**, but only the part you can attribute to unity is revealed to you. That is the **breaking of the vessels**: the breaking of the screen, of the connection between the vessel and the Creator.
- Where direct light and **reflected light** become **inner light**, down to the **Tabur** of the **Partzuf**, that is where the Creator and the created being are together, in all five parts of **NRNHY**. Where you can't, that is the Sof, the end of the Partzuf.
- Beyond the Sof are many more vessels you don't feel at all. If ten kilos are revealed to you, you can perform a **restriction** on all ten, and on two of them you may even achieve unity. "The method is to use the desire to receive as much as possible in order to unify it with the Creator."

## Part 7 — World, Year, Soul: Determining One's Attitude

- **Q: What is the process of determining one's attitude?** It means that "I, the Creator, and the action that I'm engaged in, the state I'm in, must all be as one." Then I'm in the correct state, in the correct direction. (Baal HaSulam's letters, page 64.)
- On page 70 he divides every state into three parameters: **world, year, soul**. When I determine that all three are one, connected to the Creator, "it's a sign that I too am included in Him."
- These parameters don't exist outside me; "they exist in me." Like two things that must fit precisely, you need all three for one to enter the other. In each desire, world, year and soul are specific to that desire.
- **To unify with the Creator means He is the cause of all three.** Take each one separately until you can say it is unified with Him, then bring all three together: "this will be called truly attaining unity."

#### The high priest on Yom Kippur

- His example: the soul must reach the degree of the **high priest**; the year, the degree of **Yom Kippur**, the Day of Atonement; and the world, all the surrounding conditions, the **Temple, the Holy of Holies**.
- "I don't have a button to press... No, it happens gradually." Gradually it is revealed what these mean: the combination of my inner conditions and the external conditions around me.
- "At the end of it all, it's my attitude toward myself and my attitude to those in my surroundings." I have nothing beyond myself and my world, and unifying both with the Creator is attaining unity with Him.

#### A blessing over coffee

- To unify means "He created it, He operates within it, and He brought it to the state." Who am I? I merely revealed this. "The vessels do not actually break, and the vessels do not actually become corrected, so to speak."
- The desire itself is neither bad nor good; it is bad if I don't connect it to Him and good if I do. So if I'm going to drink coffee, I say a blessing: I unify what is in the coffee, in my vessel, and the pleasure revealed in it with Him. The screen and reflected light unify the vessel and its fulfillment with the Giver. "This is called the revelation of unity."

## Part 8 — Feeling, Sin and Degrees

- **Q: And what about my feeling?** "Our feeling is the outcome of the gap between unity and the absence of unity." Cold, hunger, pain, someone shouting at me: "all these things ultimately are different forms, different garments of closeness or distance from the Creator."
- **There Is None Else Besides Him** is the whole foundation of our work and the principle on which this world is built. Physics and chemistry discover it too: all the laws of nature are forms of connection, separation and distance, attraction and repulsion.
- The desire itself doesn't need correction. "What needs correction is the thought accompanying the desire, how I use this desire, whether it's in order to bestow."
- **Q: Can we do that before the barrier?** No. Before the **Machsom** we scrutinize our state, the extent to which we are distant. Intentional and unintentional transgressions are "impressions of the absence of unity."
- Beyond the barrier, to the extent we unify with the Creator, our state is that of a **Tzadik**, incomplete or complete. **Degrees** are measures of **equivalence of form**, the extent to which we are unified with Him. "Everything is according to a single principle." (He explains this clearly in the Inner Observation, Part 1 of the Study of the Ten Sefirot.)

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **There is one force and nothing else to discover.** Our confusion between "I act" and "He acts" grows precisely in one who is worthy of correction, pushing him to seek unity.
2. **The first and the last are sealed; the middle is our work.** We don't choose the Reshimot, only how we cross from one to the next.
3. **Change the attitude, not the state.** Attributing blows and good things alike to the Creator sweetens them; acting on reality to change it leads to corruption.
4. **Unity comes with a screen, at full height.** Unlike passive Eastern annulment, we unify the greatest desires with the Creator through the Masach and reflected light.
5. **Wicked before righteous.** The ten utterances become ten Sefirot only after a person fails, feels each shattering, and corrects it, until he knows the Good Who Does Good.
6. **Unify world, year and soul.** He is the cause of all three; uniting them, in myself and toward my surroundings, is the high priest in the Holy of Holies on Yom Kippur.
7. **Every feeling is a measure of unity.** Pleasure and pain, sin and degree, are all garments of closeness to or distance from the Creator.
