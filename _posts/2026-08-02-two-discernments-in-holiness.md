### Opening

The lesson opens with a claim that reorganizes everything that follows: holiness is measured only by intention, never by the will itself or by any action. Working outward from Rabash's article on the two discernments of holiness, Rav Laitman and an extended live Q&A trace what that actually means in practice — why "bestowing in order to bestow" and "receiving in order to bestow" are two different degrees rather than the same thing, why spiritual distance is entirely a matter of equivalence of form rather than literal separation, and why the real discipline of the path is simply remembering, again and again, to return to contact with the Creator.

## Part 1 — Two Discernments of Holiness: Intention Determines the Degree, Not the Vessel

Rav Laitman opens by naming intention, not action, as the entire substance of spiritual work.

> "Holiness means in order to bestow. Meaning it's the intention. The action doesn't matter — the action testifies that the intention is truly in order to bestow... So the main thing is the intention. From it we begin, and with it we conclude the path. Spirituality is all only intention."

He then draws the lesson's central distinction: using the intention to bestow on the will to receive at its lowest levels (roots one and two) isn't yet "receiving in order to bestow," because that will isn't yet felt as real pleasure — only once the intention rides on the will at its higher, more strongly felt levels (roots three and four) does genuine reception in order to bestow begin.

> "If you use the intention of bestowal with the will of reception on the coarseness levels of roots one and two, it's not actually receiving in order to bestow — only bestowing in order to bestow... because the will to receive on those levels isn't yet felt as receiving pleasure. Only if the intention to bestow rides upon the will to receive at levels three and four, then we are receiving in order to bestow — because at that degree there's already a sensation of the receiver: the sensation of pleasure, the sensation of shame."

He maps this directly onto the structure of a soul: the work on Galgalta veEinayim is the work of Ibur and Katnut (smallness); the work on the vessels of reception at coarseness levels three and four — the AHP — is the work of Gadlut (greatness).

> "Each soul, each vessel, is divided into Galgalta veEinayim and the AHP, and the work is different... These are the two discernments of holiness."

## Part 2 — Where the Real Action Happens: The Calculation in the Rosh

Asked whether intention can exist without action, Rav Laitman locates the actual work in the calculation a person makes before anything outward occurs at all.

> "Action means, first of all, I take care of the intention. First I have to find the Creator, to have some grasp on him. Then I check what kind of connection I have to him — maybe it's in order to receive, maybe there's no connection. When there is a connection, I check if it's in order to bestow."

The real question, he says, is precisely how much pleasure a person can honestly receive while still being certain the intention is to bestow — and that calculation itself, made "in the Rosh" (the head) of the soul's structure, is what spiritual action actually is.

> "What is the maximal quantity, in quality and quantity, that I can calculate — where I can receive the pleasure in order to bestow, for sure? That is the calculation made in the Rosh of the Partzuf. Then comes the action. But the calculation in the Rosh — that is actually the action itself in spirituality, because after that, by default, the action takes place."

## Part 3 — Why We Can Never Truly "Bestow" Like the Creator

Asked whether receiving in order to bestow is even higher than the Creator's own bestowal, Rav Laitman corrects the premise: a created being, by definition, never stops receiving — only the quality of his reception can change.

> "The Creator bestows by nature — this is his will, that's what he does. Because the created being is the will to enjoy. If, with the pleasure he feels, his intention is to give pleasure to the giver of the pleasure, the Creator, that means he's bestowing... But in truth, he's always receiving. He has no other option. I'm always using my will to receive, my appetite, and the dishes placed before me, in order to connect to the host. I have no way to connect to the host except through these dishes and through my hunger."

The same limit, he says, applies to what we can know about the Creator at all — only what reaches us through our own vessels, never anything beyond them.

> "I can only talk about what I receive from him, what I feel from him — good things, bad things, shame, pleasure. That's it. I understand within my vessels of reception, and never above my vessels. To emerge from the vessels means abstract light — there's no created being there."

## Part 4 — Spiritual Motion Is Entirely Equivalence of Form

Rav Laitman describes spiritual location and movement using the image of a soul inside the Creator's field, positioned entirely by how closely its qualities match His.

> "The Creator is the center of the spiritual force. From him, waves of the spiritual field emerge, and you as a soul are within that field. According to the potential in your soul — how similar you are to him — you get closer; the more distant in qualities, the further you move away. That's how you determine spiritual motion... We measure our initial distance as one hundred and twenty-five degrees from the barrier to him. How does one make a movement? According to equivalence of form. I need to be a little more like him than I was before — that means making a movement."

He adds a firm reassurance about the nature of apparent setbacks:

> "You will never be in a worse state than now. We always only advance. Sometimes it may appear that you're regressing, but this is only seemingly so — in order to give you the opportunity to feel new discernments."

## Part 5 — The Creator Already Relates to Us as Corrected

Asked what it means that the Creator relates to a person as though he is already at the end of correction, Rav Laitman explains that felt distance from the Creator is entirely one-sided — an illusion produced by concealment, not a real change in an already-complete reality.

> "When I advance, getting closer to the Creator, I discover that from his perspective there's no such thing as close or far — I'm simply always in him. From his perspective, I'm at the end of correction, and he relates to me from there. But I feel that his attitude toward me is negative to the extent that I'm not corrected... We truly are with him in the best, most perfect state, but this state is concealed from us behind one hundred and twenty-five covers. It follows that for him I am whole, I am perfect — it's only from my own perspective that I'm corrupted. The end of correction is the only state that actually exists. If it appears that we are outside of it, that's an illusion, coming from the dullness of our senses."

He compares the process to someone slowly regaining consciousness, until the state a person is actually in and the state he perceives himself to be in finally become one and the same. He also offers an image for why the Creator's guidance can feel like pressure rather than kindness: a demanding mother pushing her child toward excellence — not out of malice, but because his resistance is itself what calls forth more of her insistence.

## Part 6 — Starting Small, and Turning a Sandwich Into Gratitude

Asked which desire a person should actually choose to work on first, Rav Laitman recommends starting deliberately small — and then shows what makes even a trivial desire usable.

> "Take the smallest desire — it's safest, you won't fail... You could make a sandwich, feel it as pleasure, and relate that pleasure to the Creator — that he prepared this whole world for you, brought you to a state where you're enjoying this sandwich, and you want to show him the pleasure you derive from it. It's not really from the sandwich — it's from the feeling that he prepared it for you. You come to a state where what you need is to feel him. Start from that, and you will see that he will be revealed."

He explains why the Creator doesn't simply hand over correction directly, using a parable of two old friends, one grown rich and one poor:

> "The rich one takes the poor one in, gives him everything, with pure goodness. But the poor friend says: there is one flaw — it's not me, it's you who did this. If you truly want to benefit me, give me the opportunity to reach what you have on my own. And that's what the Creator does with us."

## Part 7 — Staying Connected: Points of Contact, and a Ten That Checks on Its Own

Asked how to stay attentive to the friends while also focused on study and on the Creator, Rav Laitman advises against trying to consciously juggle all three at once.

> "The solution is: don't tie them together. The main thing is to study, and during the study, demand that this — the Creator — be inside of you. Everything else is already there as preparation."

He describes the heaviness that typically follows a good Shabbat as new, not-yet-usable capacity rather than a setback, and explains that real acceleration comes from shortening the gaps between moments of remembering the Creator throughout the day.

> "Acceleration in progress means remembering to come back into contact with him every time. The less time remaining empty between entries — these are called points of contact with him... What's not good is that the time until you come back into connection can be a few hours, maybe a few days. That is your work."

He closes with a concrete, practical instruction for the group itself: since a large room can't track who has quietly drifted away, a Ten needs its own written agreement to check on every member.

> "Gather the friends, take a piece of paper, and make an agreement between you: every half hour, we check where everyone is. If someone ran away, we bring him back... This is why we split into groups — with a hundred people, you can't see who's missing. Ten is preferable. This agreement is mandatory. In writing it's very effective — it's serious, it affects a person psychologically."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Holiness is measured entirely by intention, never by the will itself or by action; "bestowing in order to bestow" (Galgalta veEinayim, coarseness levels one and two) only becomes "receiving in order to bestow" once the will is actually felt as pleasure (AHP, coarseness levels three and four).
2. The real spiritual action is the calculation of intention made "in the Rosh," the head; once that calculation genuinely settles, the action that follows it happens automatically.
3. A created being can never literally bestow the way the Creator does — he can only aim his will to receive at the Creator's pleasure rather than his own; correspondingly, we can never know the Creator as He is, only what reaches us through our own vessels.
4. Spiritual motion is governed entirely by equivalence of form, like a soul positioned inside the Creator's field; the 125-degree distance is felt, not literal, and real regression is impossible — apparent descents exist only to reveal new discernments.
5. The Creator already relates to a person as though he is at the end of correction; every feeling of unworthiness or distance is an illusion produced by concealment, not a change in the only state that actually exists.
6. The path forward starts with the smallest workable desire, deliberately connected back to the Creator — even gratitude for a sandwich; the Creator withholds direct correction not from severity but so a person can build the connection himself, the way a rich friend refuses to simply hand a poor friend his wealth outright.
7. Spiritual acceleration comes from shortening the gaps between "points of contact" with the Creator throughout the day; practically, a written, half-hourly check on every member of a Ten — not a larger group — is what actually keeps people from quietly drifting away.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Rosh / Guf (Head / Body) | The two parts of a Partzuf's structure; the Rosh is where the calculation of intention (how much pleasure can be received in order to bestow) is made — per this lesson, that calculation is itself the real spiritual "action," with the Guf's action following automatically. |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes" — in this lesson, the part of the soul worked on during Ibur and Katnut (smallness), at the will to receive's lowest, not-yet-felt coarseness levels (roots one and two). |
| AHP | Vessels of reception at coarseness levels three and four, where pleasure and shame are actually felt; per this lesson, the part of the soul worked on during Gadlut (greatness), where "receiving in order to bestow" becomes possible for the first time. |
| Aveyut (Coarseness) | The thickness/intensity of the will to receive, measured in degrees (roots one through four); determines whether work with a given desire counts as "bestowing in order to bestow" or "receiving in order to bestow." |
| Machsom (Barrier) | The point marking a permanent shift toward spiritual over corporeal preference; in this lesson, the reference point from which the 125-degree distance to the Creator is measured. |
| 125 Degrees | The full concealment structure between a person and his corrected state (five worlds × five parts × five Sefirot); per this lesson, a felt rather than literal distance, since a person is already, from the Creator's perspective, at the end of correction. |
| Points of Contact | This lesson's term for moments of remembering and returning attention to the Creator throughout the day; spiritual acceleration is described as simply shortening the empty gaps between them. |
