### Opening

The lesson opens with a puzzle built into the structure of growth itself: a higher spiritual degree can never look attractive from where a person currently stands, because from below it can only be imagined in terms of the current degree's own values — more comfort, more for me — never in terms of what it actually is, which is more giving. Working outward from Rabash's article on the spies, Rav Laitman and an extended Q&A trace why that gap is a mercy rather than a flaw, why the very forces that seem to sabotage a person's progress turn out, in hindsight, to be exactly what built it, and why real vessels — the capacity to actually hold and enjoy a higher state — always have to be earned through struggle rather than granted in advance.

## Part 1 — Why the Higher Degree Never Looks Attractive From Below

Rav Laitman names the two poles directly: what a person currently knows and feels is called Da'at (knowledge), while anything genuinely above the current degree can only be called faith, because it hasn't been attained yet:

> "Where I am now is called Da'at, knowledge. My knowledge, my opinion, my feeling, my understanding... But meanwhile, from the perspective of the lower degree, the higher degree is called faith."

The catch is that a person can only imagine a higher degree using his current degree's own categories — which is exactly why concealment is protective, not cruel:

> "It never seems to me that a higher degree means giving up more, working harder, investing more... It always seems to me that a higher degree means more comfort, more convenience, more for me... I can thank the Creator that we're in a concealment and we're not allowed to see the higher degree. Otherwise we'd all run away from it."

## Part 2 — Needing the Creator's Help, Not Just More Understanding

Rav Laitman is precise about what actually changes between degrees — it isn't simply more comprehension, but a real shift toward the Creator's own quality:

> "It's not that I understand more, control more, or acquire more, nor is it merely me giving up more and more. Rather, it's that I become more similar to the Emanator, to the Creator, to the upper force. That is why it is called an upper degree — because in it I attain greater equivalence with godliness."

## Part 3 — The Spies in Hindsight: How the Forces of Separation Turn Out to Help

Only after actually reaching a higher state, Rav Laitman says, does a person see what the spies — the forces of doubt and separation — were really doing for him all along:

> "After they acquire that higher state, they see how all the forces of separation, which seemed to want to pull them away from the path and destroy all of their progress, how much these forces actually were beneficial and truly brought them to that higher state... Unless a person descends into that sin and overcomes it, there is nothing he can do. Without it, he cannot conquer the land of Israel."

He defines "conquering the land" concretely: not acquiring new territory, but converting the seven basic desires already present in a person from intentions to receive into intentions to bestow:

> "Conquering the land means transforming intentions for the sake of receiving into intentions for the sake of bestowal... There are the seven primary desires, from Chesed to Malchut... The person corrects those so that in their place there will now be the intention called the people of Israel for the sake of bestowal, instead of the seven nations which represent intentions for the sake of receiving."

## Part 4 — Fighting Blind: Concealment, and Why Precision Comes Only Later

Asked how a person can fight seventy internal "nations" without even knowing which one he's up against, Rav Laitman says precise self-knowledge simply isn't available yet at this stage — only effort in the available channels is:

> "We can never know more than what belongs to the state we are currently in... What we can do is exert effort in whatever is possible: choosing the environment, the books, investing effort into them, and connecting to the subject itself as much as possible."

He distinguishes the concealment a person is actually in from a deeper alternative, and from what would be no concealment at all:

> "Double concealment means that you already feel that you are in some kind of concealment, but you do not even know what it is that is concealed from you... A single concealment [is when] you feel that you are in concealment and that there is an upper force organizing all sorts of events in your life."

Between a person and his own corrected state, he adds, there are 125 degrees of concealment (five worlds times five parts times five Sefirot), each with an exact, individual path — so the only real task at this stage is simply to keep advancing: "we only need to press the accelerator pedal, so to speak."

## Part 5 — Who Is "Israel"? Abraham and the Choice Beneath the Label

Asked how a person can tell which "nation" he belongs to, Rav Laitman explains the structural split first — the purer part of a person, resembling the Creator's own qualities, versus the part that is simply the created being's own will to receive:

> "The people of Israel corresponds to Galgalta veEinayim... You could say that they are the first nine Sefirot, the qualities of the Creator that entered the created being... The nations of the world corresponds to AHAP... That is why it is written that the Creator went to all the nations and wanted to give them the Torah, but they did not want it... Israel, however, said, 'Bring it on.'"

He then locates this same split in a single biography — Abraham's — as proof that the label was never about ancestry:

> "Originally, Abraham was simply a Bedouin, like everyone else around him... He began to desire spirituality so much so that the desire became so strong that he left behind his goats and cattle that were no longer important, and only spirituality mattered. That is how he became a Jew. Jew, Yehudi, coming from the word Yichud, unity with the Creator."

The same test, he says, applies to absolutely anyone: "It could be a barber from Italy, a soccer player from Brazil, or even some Israeli mafioso... If he has a desire to unite with the Creator, he's called a Jew. If he does not have a desire to unite with the Creator, he is called a Gentile."

## Part 6 — Two Forces of Motivation, and Why a Higher Degree Needs Refined Discernment

A student asks why the spies describe the land as both wonderful and impossibly hard in the same breath. Rav Laitman explains this as two necessary, complementary forces of motivation:

> "We progress to anything we desire through two forces. The first is the negative force in the present state, that I no longer feel good where I am... And the second is the positive force in the future state, that there's something attractive there, something sparkling, something that draws me."

But arriving isn't enough on its own — a person also needs the refined discernment to actually appreciate what he's reached, which Rav Laitman illustrates with a story about a government minister who mixed a 200-year-old vintage wine with soda water, and with the contrast between a machinist who can feel the internal stress in a piece of metal and an outsider who feels nothing at all:

> "When you rise to a higher spiritual degree, you lack all those qualities that make the higher degree higher than the one you currently occupy. You need greater sensitivity... So the spies help you in exactly that way. They provide you with what you're lacking... By conquering those nations, by conquering the land, you conquer a new desire. Only in that vessel will you truly be able to enjoy those fruits."

## Part 7 — Why the Vessel Must Come Before the Light

Closing the lesson, Rav Laitman explains why spiritual pleasure has to work backwards from how pleasure normally works — the capacity to hold it (the vessel, built through a screen) has to exist before the pleasure itself arrives, or the pleasure destroys the very desire it fills:

> "The vessels must precede the lights. Otherwise, whatever light you acquire, you will acquire it for the sake of receiving, and you will descend into the Klipot, the shells... Pleasure that completely fills the desire causes the desire itself to disappear. And if there's no desire, I no longer enjoy it."

This, he says, is exactly why worldly pleasure always feels fleeting and "imaginary" — a person is really just living off the memory of a first taste — while pleasure received through a properly prepared vessel can remain constant rather than vanishing the moment it's felt.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. A higher spiritual degree can never look attractive from below, because it can only be imagined using the current degree's own categories (more comfort, not more giving) — which is exactly why concealment protects rather than punishes.
2. What actually changes between degrees isn't more understanding, but greater equivalence of quality with the Creator — which is why the Creator's help, not just personal effort, is required to reach it.
3. The forces that seem to sabotage progress (the "spies") are only recognized as beneficial in hindsight; the descent they cause is a necessary stage, not a detour, on the way to "conquering the land."
4. "Conquering the land" means converting the seven basic desires already present in a person (intentions to receive) into the intention to bestow (called "Israel"), not acquiring anything external.
5. Precise self-knowledge of which internal force a person is fighting isn't available at this stage — only effort in the available channels (environment, books, connection) is; deeper clarity comes only at higher degrees.
6. "Israel" and "the nations of the world" are not ethnic categories but a structural split present in everyone — proven by Abraham, who became "a Jew" purely by choosing to yearn for the Creator over his goats and cattle, a choice anyone, regardless of background, can make.
7. Spiritual pleasure requires the vessel (a screen) to exist before the light arrives — otherwise the pleasure extinguishes the very desire it fills, which is why worldly pleasure always feels brief while properly prepared pleasure can remain constant.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Da'at | "Knowledge" — what a person currently perceives, knows, and feels at his present degree; contrasted with faith, which is simply what the next, not-yet-attained degree is called from below. |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes" — the first nine Sefirot, the Creator's own qualities as present in the created being; identified in this lesson with "the people of Israel" within a person. |
| AHAP | Vessels of reception; identified in this lesson with "the nations of the world" within a person — the created being's own will to receive, prior to correction. |
| Emanator (Maatzil) | "The one who emanates" — what a person becomes more similar to upon reaching a higher degree; the real measure of ascent is equivalence with the Emanator, not increased understanding alone. |
| Klipot / Klipa | Shells — where light received before a vessel (screen) is ready ends up going, since an unprepared vessel cannot hold pleasure without being extinguished by it. |
| Double Concealment / Single Concealment | Two degrees of felt distance from the Creator; in double concealment a person doesn't even know what is concealed from him, while in single concealment he senses an upper force organizing his life without yet perceiving it directly. |
| 125 Degrees | The full concealment structure between a person and his corrected state (five worlds × five parts × five Sefirot), each with its own exact, individual path of advancement. |
