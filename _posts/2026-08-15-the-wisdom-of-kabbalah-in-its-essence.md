### Opening

The lesson opens by naming its subject directly: Hitkalelut — variously rendered "integration," "incorporation," "inclusion" — described as "a very special quality... very high, noble," developed in the will to receive only after many corrections. The puzzle the lesson works through is what that word actually means in practice: not simply coming closer to the friends, or even tolerating the friction of being close to them, but something further — actually being included inside another person's desires and deficiencies, and having him included inside one's own, until "each one is completely incorporated in the others and everyone becomes to be one."

## Part 1 — Baal HaSulam's Sources: What Integration Means

The first excerpt, from "The Wisdom of Kabbalah in Its Essence," calls integration "the greatest wonder about this wisdom": all the elements of the vast reality are incorporated in it until they come into a single thing, the Almighty and all of them together. Rav Laitman extended this beyond Kabbalah study to any act of learning — a person "includes himself" in what he learns by building the pattern of it inside himself, and the more patterns gathered from outside and combined within, the better he knows and can work with the world.

> "All the elements of the vast reality are incorporated in it until they come into a single thing, the Almighty and all of them together."

The second excerpt, from Baal HaSulam's Letter 17, ties integration to the number 620: since the soul, upon descending into this world, could no longer cling to its root as before, it must increase its stature 620 times over — the meaning behind the name **Keter** itself (Khetar), which numerically implies 620. That figure resolves into the 613 Mitzvot of the Torah plus the seven Rabbinic Mitzvot, which in turn correspond to the five properties of the soul, NRNHY. "This is all attained only by the integration."

A third excerpt, "600,000 Souls," addressed how something spiritual — indivisible by nature — could appear divided into 600,000 souls and their sparks. The answer: only one soul was ever created, Adam HaRishon's; what looks like division is an effect of separate bodies, each concealing the same underlying light until the body is purified by Torah and Mitzvot. The sign of a body's complete correction is when a person no longer feels his soul as a private organ but recognizes it as belonging to the whole of Israel — "for one depends on the other."

## Part 2 — Adam HaRishon, the Shattering, and Why Every Detail Must Be Corrected

Before the sin of the Tree of Knowledge, Adam HaRishon existed only in Katnut (smallness) — vessels of reception not yet activated, "existence without awareness of himself." The sin was adding the vessels of reception to a structure that until then held only vessels of bestowal; lacking the screen (Masach) needed to work with reception in order to bestow, the structure shattered, and the vessels of bestowal shattered along with it because they had already incorporated with the vessels of reception, each with all the others.

The correction runs in the same direction as the break: each individual detail of the will to receive has to be corrected one at a time, and in correcting any one detail, a person corrects the incorporation of all the other details along with it — "you can't even touch one grain of the will to receive... unless you connect along with it." This, Rav Laitman said, is why every rule of Kabbalah treats the whole as included in each part and each part as including the whole: reality only appears divisible because human perception is limited, but "the truth is it's all one whole integral world."

> "By correcting each detail or each individual, he's correcting the incorporation in all the other details."

## Part 3 — Q&A: How Do We Realize Integration Practically, in the Ten?

Asked how to realize integration in practice, Rav Laitman located the answer inside the Ten: because everything is already mutually included in nature, correcting the Ten corrects all of reality along with it — the Ten has to feel itself as "the first healthy cell" from which the corrected state can spread to the rest of humanity. Since the corrected state — full connection — already exists in nature, the work is to discover it, which happens by drawing the reforming light through participation with the Ten: connecting more, and by that changing not the world directly but oneself, since the Ten is not a natural cell but an artificial, constructed one — specifically built as a place for this work.

Practically, integration was defined as caring about the friends, becoming sensitive to their needs, and working to come out of oneself rather than sitting alone and "bestowing" in isolation. The Ten was described as a laboratory: a shared space where this exact work is realized together, likened to being part of one vine whose fruit is the connection between the friends themselves.

## Part 4 — Q&A: From Elementary Particles to Adam — Levels of Integration

A participant traced integration through the stages of nature: from the Big Bang's elementary particles, whose integration is held together by light alone, through the vegetative level (where integrated matter begins to act somewhat like the light itself — growing, giving birth, dying), to the animate level, where a greater degree of light produces a deficiency that draws particles toward more complex forms of connection. What distinguishes each level is not the fact of integration but the degree of light working within it, and the corresponding depth of deficiency it creates.

Rav Laitman placed the current moment on this same ladder: humanity is now shifting from the animate degree to the speaking degree (Adam), where the aim becomes resembling the Creator specifically through incorporating with others — something that, by definition, "cannot be less than a Ten." A person not yet incorporated with others, he said, still stands at the degree of the animate — "he is still as a beast, as the verse goes" — not as a judgment, but as a plain description of where connection has or hasn't yet reached.

## Part 5 — Q&A: Deficiency and Fulfillment Together, and the Path from Goy to Yehudi

The Moscow Ten on duty raised an apparent contradiction: integration is supposed to bring the fulfillment of the Creator's revelation, yet integration is also described as taking on the friends' deficiencies — how can both be true at once? Rav Laitman's answer: they coexist, not in sequence. "You can't have the light without the Kli," and specifically through integration — acquiring the deficiencies of others — a person comes to acquire deficiency itself, since before integration his only deficiency is his own, at the animate level.

A related question traced the individual's spiritual progression in Kabbalistic terms: a Goy is simply a person who recognizes himself as governed by his own egoistic will to receive; through study, regardless of background, he begins wanting to give, keeps learning, and becomes a Yehudi (from Yechud, unification) — someone who starts uniting with others and, as a result, with the Creator. "So these are the steps of evolution for each and every person in the world."

## Part 6 — Q&A: Books vs. Connection, and Building an Inner Partzuf From the Friend

One participant pushed back that advancement comes only through the words of authors and books, not through working on or exposing one's own ego in a group. Rav Laitman's answer: what actually changes a person's desire is the reforming light, and the light is drawn specifically by placing it in front of the ego through connection — the books of Kabbalah are themselves about connection, so reading them and working to connect with a friend are not competing paths but the same one, done together.

Later in the same thread, integration with a friend was defined more precisely: not canceling one's own views, intentions, or desires, but building "an additional Partzuf" inside oneself out of what belongs to the friend — requiring a real response from him in turn, since integration cannot be one-directional. Beyond the point in the heart, a person is said to have nothing of his own; everything else he builds is acquired from the nine friends, described as "the first nine, the upper nine... the essence of the light, of the Creator." Connecting to them is, in this framing, how a person discovers his own connection to the Creator.

> "Then I discovered that, except the point in the heart, I have nothing. And everything I acquire... I acquire from these nine friends."

## Part 7 — Arvut and the Covenant

Two further readings — Rabash's "Here You All Stand Today" (Article 3) and an excerpt from Likutei Halachot — grounded integration in Arvut, mutual guarantee. "All of you" entering the covenant, in the Torah's plural-then-singular phrasing, was read as meaning that every individual of Israel must be included with and comprised of every other — "the people encamped under the mount as one man, in one heart." Only once everyone is included together does the reforming light of the Torah actually correct the vessels and bring the revelation of the Creator; the covenant itself was defined as what allows the friends not to forget the Creator between them, and the goal, and the direction.

Likutei Halachot pressed the point further: it is impossible to keep Torah and Mitzvot except through Arvut, since the whole principle of the Torah concerns the will, and the will can only be corrected in unity — "all who desire to take upon themselves the burden of Torah and Mitzvot must be joined together... through a strong bond of unity." A covenant, Rav Laitman added, comes specifically from recognizing one's own weakness: those who believe themselves permanently strong feel no need for one, but experience teaches that a stronger desire will eventually come to tempt a person away, which is exactly what the covenant with the Ten is meant to hold against.

> "It is impossible to keep the Torah in Mitzvot except through Arvut, in which each one becomes responsible for his friend."

## Part 8 — Q&A: What Kind of Vessel Does Mutual Inclusion Create?

Asked what integration concretely gives that isolated effort cannot, Rav Laitman described it as a genuinely new Kli: each friend sets aside his own desires to take up the other's in his place, and is taken up in turn — "he has to be included with my desires, and instead of himself, he has to be instead of me in this role." The Creator, he said, is revealed precisely in this shared area between two people, not in either person alone; finding it requires actively searching for what is common between the friends' desires, since this cannot be assumed in advance.

The session's closing exchange framed this common area as the site of ordinary mutual concern as much as high spiritual attainment — the same kind of wholeness present when people simply share a meal together. "Everything starts from a common desire," and the obligation the friends take on is not only to build that shared area but to keep watch over it: "we're responsible that the friend doesn't fall."

## Part 9 — Closing: Toward the Regional Congress

Closing the session, Rav Laitman named the depth that had opened up in the topic — mutual responsibility, connection, and incorporation — and turned toward the near-term occasion for putting it into practice: an upcoming Regional Congress, framed as the next real test of the Ten's ability to extend this same work of integration outward, beyond a single group, together with friends gathering from across the region. The recording ends here, mid-transition into the Congress announcement.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Hitkalelut (integration/incorporation) is not simply closeness — it is being genuinely included inside another's desires and deficiencies, and having him included inside one's own, until distinct people function as one.
2. Correction runs the same path as the break: because the shattering incorporated every detail of the will to receive with every other, correcting any one detail corrects the incorporation of all the others along with it.
3. The Ten is the practical unit of this work — not a natural grouping but a deliberately constructed one, meant to function as "the first healthy cell" from which corrected connection can extend to the rest of humanity.
4. Integration exists at every level of nature (elementary particles, vegetative, animate, speaking); what changes between levels is the degree of light at work and the depth of deficiency it produces, not the underlying principle.
5. Deficiency and fulfillment through integration coexist rather than alternate — acquiring the friends' deficiencies is itself how a person begins to acquire deficiency capable of being filled by the light.
6. The individual's path from Goy to Yehudi is measured by unity, not origin: study leads a person from private egoistic will toward uniting with others and, through that, with the Creator.
7. Arvut (mutual guarantee) is the mechanism, and the covenant is its recognition: because a person's strength cannot be trusted to last on its own, the Ten commits collectively to keep the connection when it's tested.
8. The common area between two integrated friends — not either person individually — is specifically where Rav Laitman located the revelation of the Creator.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Hitkalelut (Integration/Incorporation) | The lesson's central term: mutual inclusion of one's qualities, desires, and deficiencies inside another's (and vice versa) within the Ten, described as an advanced state reached only after many corrections. |
| Arvut (Mutual Guarantee) | The bond of mutual responsibility that makes integration possible; per Likutei Halachot, Torah and Mitzvot cannot be kept without it, since the will can only be corrected in unity. |
| Keter | The highest of the five-part Partzuf structure; numerically implies 620, tied in this lesson to the soul's need to increase its stature 620 times upon descending into this world. |
| NRNHY (Naranchai) | Acronym for the five-part structure of the soul's light — Nefesh, Ruach, Neshama, Haya, Yechida — described here as attained only through integration. |
| Adam HaRishon | The single original soul-structure whose shattering scattered the vessels of bestowal and reception together; its correction is the ongoing work of integration described throughout the lesson. |
| Masach (Screen) | The corrective capacity Adam HaRishon lacked when the vessels of reception joined his structure, whose absence is what allowed the shattering to happen. |
| Goy / Yehudi | Two states on the same individual path: Goy names a person governed by his own egoistic will to receive; Yehudi (from Yechud, unification) names one who has begun uniting with others and, through that, with the Creator. |
| Dvekut (Vikut) | Adhesion between the friends, named in this lesson as the state reached to the extent integration succeeds, through which the general force — the Creator — begins to be discovered. |
