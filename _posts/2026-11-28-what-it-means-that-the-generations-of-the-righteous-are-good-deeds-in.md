### Opening

Rabash's article ends with three states (permanent, ascent and descent), with learning "from my teachers, my friends and my disciples," and with the instruction that in a descent a person must decide nothing and only pray. Rav Laitman starts from the article's title: who is a righteous person, and what are his "good deeds"? The Q&A then becomes a lesson on bad states. If no one knows his own next step, how can we help a friend without interfering? Why does a bad feeling never correct anything? What does it really mean to yearn for the Creator? It ends with why reading five pages in an hour does not cost us anything, since what we grasp depends on inner vessels, not on the intellect.

## Part 1 — The Righteous Justifies

- "A righteous is called one who justifies what is happening with him."
- He looks for the root, the Creator, and wants to know His providence in every detail: "see the purpose of creation... connect to it, research it, and all of that is to justify the Creator." It isn't to become smarter or to enjoy it for selfish reasons.
- It comes from the urge that unless he connects all of reality to the purpose of creation, he won't reveal it to the end and won't be able to justify the Creator.
- So all the actions such a person reveals and wants to do "come in order to justify the providence. This is called **good deeds**."

## Part 2 — Each One's Own Path

- **Q: Rabash writes that each person has his own precise path. How does each one know his path in the group?** "First of all, no one knows which path he needs to take. Have you ever seen a person who knows what's his next stage?" Something suddenly falls on you, and looking back you see you didn't move intelligently from one state to another.
- In spirituality, perhaps, there is a sequence: first the screen, reflected light and a coupling by striking in the **Rosh**, then the lights spread in the **Guf**. First a decision, then the action. But here with us, "seemingly not."
- Interfering with another's path means "making decisions in his place." Studying and laboring together, we bring each other data, and with greater force and speed the events we must go through happen to us.
- What each one sees in his state and how he receives it, "a direct personal connection with Godliness," is forbidden to reveal to another and forbidden to interfere with. "I just need to support my friend, help him. Give him conditions to advance." Whether his next stage is a descent, ascent, despair or joy, "that I can't determine."

#### Then why lift his spirits?

- **Q: If a bad state is part of his personal process, why should I put him in a good mood? Shouldn't he chew through it fully?** We are told that in any state you may give a friend high spirits, even distract him: "drag him to a bar to have a beer," or work together with him on something physical.
- Why is it permitted? First, because it is written. Second, because one helps another and "there's a correction of incorporation."
- The greater reason: "a person who's in a bad mood... doesn't matter what percent of bad state he has, he never corrects anything in that bad state." If we think a bad feeling leaves something we'll need later, "this is the **Sitra Achra** confusing us and telling us it's worthwhile to stay there."
- "If we could make our whole path only with joy, then absolutely that would be desirable."

## Part 3 — Entering the Left to Turn It Right

- The three states still have to be. "Without entering the left you won't be able to correct it. But you enter it only to take it and immediately turn it to the right. This is called **walking in joy**."
- Even the entry into the left is purposeful: "you hardly feel bad. Rather you only feel what you need to take from it and correct." If the group and the person work ideally, "just one touch of the bad" may be enough for the record (**Reshimo**) to emerge, and with it he works to turn it to good and to joy.
- **Q: Corrections advance a person. If a correction is done on me and I'm in a bad mood, why wouldn't that advance me?** "The states where we feel bad are not corrections. They're states where we are inside our will to receive." The **surrounding light** shines, and opposite it the will to receive feels more darkness. "I'm eating that darkness... I just sit and suffer."
- As Baal HaSulam writes in Shamati, it is the Sitra Achra that says "stay longer in the bad feeling... it's beneficial for you." "Therefore, the moment the person feels bad, the faster he can come out of it, the better."

#### Picking up the speed

- No bad state helps. "You will come out quickly to the good, and you will fall into the bad again, and you'll come out quickly to the good again, and by that you save the time... you increase the pace of ups and downs."
- To those in "gray states, nothing good, nothing bad": better "go watch some movie on the television. I'm saying seriously, just disconnect from everything completely... but don't feel bad."
- This isn't a remedy of running away from every bad state. Sometimes the depression is such that "any means is worthwhile to come out of it." In a bad state a person "curses the Creator," stops himself and doesn't advance, and the bitterness won't later turn into strength.

## Part 4 — How the Ego Feeds on Suffering

- We think the ego is nourished when a person feels good and enjoys for himself. But when he feels bad and is "willing to suffer the bad and be like that, disconnected. That is nourishment to the Sitra Achra... the I suffers. He enlarges this I by this."
- To come out from bad to good "you have to bend. You have to accept some other authority": to agree to the bad state and still want to feel good in it. "This is accepting the burden of the kingdom of heaven," and the ego won't let him. So the Sitra Achra prefers these states; through them its vessels grow.

#### Yearning for whom?

- **Q: A person who yearns for the Creator doesn't feel good from it.** "First of all, who told you that he yearns for the Creator? Who is the Creator as he pictures him?" If the Creator is the force of bestowal, "do you yearn for the force of bestowal? Or do you want to adhere to Him in order to receive from Him?"
- Drawing near in the corporeal sense, to receive more, is like the Sitra Achra, which also wants to draw near "to take from Him. So you're drawing near to the Sitra Achra and not to the Creator."
- Some people cry that they want the Creator so much and He disregards them. "They want to suckle from Him." That He doesn't bring them closer "is for their own good. He's waiting until they will have their force of correction."
- In spirituality there's no object one draws near to: "nearness means **equivalence of form**." So the feeling "I yearn for Him but haven't reached Him yet" can give joy, provided "for Him" means for His qualities.

## Part 5 — Bitter and Sweet, Truth and Falsehood

- **Q: How can a person who sees he isn't whole be happy?** Not about feeling bad, "rather he should be happy about something else besides the bad": the goal, a greater connection with the Creator, his advancement.
- "It is possible to feel bad in the vessels of reception and good in the awareness of where I am going and with whom I am connected... I can feel bitter and at the same time enjoy that I'm in the truth."
- "Bitter and sweet is what a beast feels, and nothing more." A human (**Adam**) acts according to truth and falsehood "despite a feeling of bitter or sweet." If you act only by bitter and sweet, "you are still considered a beast. But a beast which is advancing."
- The right reason for joy "is only the measure of equivalence of form with the Creator."

#### A different scale

- In my will to receive, the less I receive from the Creator the more I suffer. But "if I advance more towards the goal with the will to bestow, certainly the will to receive will suffer more." So I can't measure advancement in my will to receive.
- I must build "a completely different system within me, a system of truth and falsehood," by always thinking what is in order to bestow, what going out of myself means. For this "I don't yet have senses. I will only have senses for this beyond the barrier. But for this I have intellect."
- In life we do this: we pay a doctor for bitter medicine because reward and punishment are revealed. "You will never find a true medicine that is sweet." In spirituality it isn't revealed, "so what can we do? We need to demand revelation." Every correction is bitter.

## Part 6 — The Outcry Before the Barrier

- **Q: How does one cope with being shown that he is in falsehood?** "What the mind doesn't do, time does." As said at the start, a person can't determine his steps; he walks and bit by bit acquires understanding. "Whatever you're given, push on it."
- **Q: Do we need a shattering to reach a prayer, an outcry?** Before the barrier, a person checks every quality he has and is left with the impression that he is the opposite of holiness. Why? "Because the surrounding light shines for him with all its power from the outside," and he sees his whole soul "from the barrier down to the very bottom," how dark and helpless he is.
- On the other hand, the records of the preparation period build in him a desire for spirituality until "he really prefers to die than to live" without knowing the Creator. "Let me alone with all of this world. It all becomes worthless." Godliness still shines to him from afar, in order to receive.
- These two things together "gradually, bit by bit, create in him the sensation of the outcry. There's no such thing as an outcry, just burst out." It accumulates over many years. "We never pray with an intention that we're going to pray. We do preparations for it."

## Part 7 — Building Yearning and Greatness

- **Q: How do I escape the imagination that I want the Creator?** Yearning can be built from two things: "either because He is great and good, or because I feel bad and I'm running away from the bad." One pulls me forward, the other pushes me from behind; usually it's both.
- "Not to wait for the suffering, but to run because I know His greatness." If I pray to know His greatness in order to yearn for Him, "and to Him means in order to bestow," that prayer does everything.
- The good way to build His greatness "is simply to ask that He will be revealed as great." The bad way is through suffering: "every time He drops me I see that I'm small compared to Him."
- In the will to receive: if I receive something good from Him, "I begin to slight Him. If I get something bad from Him, I begin to fear Him and then I see that He's greater than me."

## Part 8 — Five Pages in an Hour

- **Q: We went over five pages in an hour. Aren't we missing something?** Say you missed half the lesson working in the kitchen, or slept, or the reader skipped a page. "So because of that you cannot reach the goal? No."
- Yesterday's test lesson succeeded not because it was explained slowly, but "because you advanced in your emotional preparation. You acquired an inner vessel and could understand in it." Even a day earlier "you still wouldn't understand it."
- "If there are inner vessels in a person, after that, he grasps. Without inner vessels, bring your ten Einsteins and they won't get anything." Without living what in order to bestow and in order to receive are, "for him it's all dry... two centimeters between them."
- "There's nothing to do here without emotional preparation. And it doesn't matter how fast you read the article." The old problem is "taking everything in the intellect." Analyze it slowly at home if you like; "I'm not dismissing this labor," but that isn't the way to spirituality.

#### Why summaries, then

- Rav asks students for summaries for two reasons. First, during the lesson a person stays awake and tries to be connected to the material, "to search himself for himself in the material." Second, with notes in the book he can return to it, and when he teaches, it reminds him what to convey. "It's not about the intellect."

#### Taste and see

- As intellectual as the Ten Sefirot seems, "it talks only about feelings." "Taste and see that the Lord is good": first taste, the most corporeal feeling; then see, which is wisdom. This is what we learn in TES: **Malchut** receives from the nine Sefirot and checks and sees that the light is good.
- In TES Part 12, item 203, Baal HaSulam again repeats Chabad within NHY and HGT within HGT. "We've heard it a thousand times." He repeats it because he says "that which operates on us for correction," not what we'd like to know. He speaks "only from within the lights, how to awaken them so they shine on us more forcefully."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The righteous justifies.** His good deeds are every action aimed at revealing providence in order to justify the Creator.
2. **No one knows his own next step.** Don't decide for a friend or interfere with his inner path; support him and give him conditions to advance.
3. **A bad state corrects nothing.** The voice saying "stay in it, it's good for you" is the Sitra Achra; come out quickly, by any means if needed, and pick up the pace of ups and downs.
4. **Enter the left only to turn it right.** That is walking in joy; a single touch of the bad can be enough for the record to emerge.
5. **Yearning means equivalence of form.** Wanting to draw near to receive is closeness to the Sitra Achra, not the Creator.
6. **Measure by truth and falsehood, not bitter and sweet.** One can feel bitter in the vessels of reception and rejoice at being in the truth.
7. **Inner vessels, not the intellect.** The outcry, the yearning and the understanding are built gradually by preparation; reading speed doesn't decide what we grasp.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Righteous (Tzadik)** | One who justifies what happens to him by seeking to reveal the Creator's providence in every detail. |
| **Good deeds** | Every action such a person does in order to justify the providence; Rabash's "generations of the righteous." |
| **Interfering with another's path** | Making decisions in a friend's place about his inner process; forbidden, while support and uplifting spirits are permitted. |
| **Walking in joy** | Entering the left only to take what must be corrected and immediately turning it to the right. |
| **Sitra Achra** | The other side, which tells a person to stay in his bad feeling and grows its vessels from his suffering. |
| **Accepting the kingdom of heaven** | Bending the ego to agree to the bad state and still want to feel good in it. |
| **Equivalence of form** | The only spiritual meaning of nearness: acquiring the Creator's quality of bestowal. |
| **Bitter and sweet / truth and falsehood** | The beast's scale of feeling versus the human scale of acting by what is true, despite the feeling. |
| **Outcry** | An inner state gradually accumulated before the barrier from seeing one's darkness and one's passion for spirituality together. |
| **Inner vessels** | Emotional preparation by which a person grasps the wisdom; without them the intellect gets nothing. |
| **Taste and see** | From tasting, the most corporeal sensation, one comes to seeing, wisdom: that the light is good. |
