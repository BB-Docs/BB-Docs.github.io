### Opening

The lesson tackles a single hard problem: bestowal — real, selfless love — runs completely against the grain of a nature built only to receive, so how does a person actually get there? Rav Laitman's answer is that it can't be done alone or by will-power; it requires "the upper force," and the only place that force can be drawn is through concrete work with a group of friends. The archival Krasnoyarsk lecture lays out the mechanism — transferring one's own ego into the desire of the friends until their fulfillment becomes one's own — and the long Q&A that follows tests that mechanism against a wide range of real situations: shame over the past, forgetfulness about daily intentions, when to expel someone from a group, teaching without full spiritual grounding, and more.

## Part 1 — Camouflaged Bestowal vs. Real Bestowal

Rav Laitman opens by naming the trap built into ordinary giving: it still runs on reception.

> "If we unconditionally would feel these kind of sensations and bestowal, then... we would be giving in order to receive — so then this would be like the camouflaged egoism that we're still receiving, and nothing new would be experienced in this. We would never come out of ourselves. People give in their life all the time in order to get more pleasure."

The only way out of that loop, he says, is help from outside one's own nature entirely:

> "What we need is actually the participation of this upper force — this force of bestowal and giving that's separate from our ego. It's the upper force. It's called the Creator. So this force would help us tear away from ourselves and start to feel both the cause and the result and the fulfillment externally to myself."

This is presented as the whole difference between an emotional gesture and an actual spiritual attainment: pleasure has to be felt *in the other*, not routed back to oneself.

## Part 2 — Transposing Yourself Into the Friends

The practical method Rav Laitman describes is transferring one's own desire, piece by piece, into the friends:

> "If I can, in this way, concentrate my work — all the time to exit myself, all the time to enter into them, all the time to transpose myself into them — I feel the joy that I am left alone, that my egoistic desire is left alone, and all of it's transferred into them. That's meant by building this external vessel, or this reflected light. And then, as a result, I start to feel in them this property of bestowal — because actually it's me that's creating it. With that, I create the Creator."

He ties the completion of this transfer to a specific threshold, borrowing a Talmudic unit of measure:

> "When I complete this transfer... and I reach this measure of Seah — this is like a measure, a unit, an ancient unit of like produce... then I get more pleasure, that I feel that they're enjoying it... And with that, I start to feel the first spiritual degree."

But the work doesn't stop at the first degree. Each new level of attainment comes with a correspondingly larger, more sophisticated ego to overcome:

> "We're given additional new egoism each time that are of a completely new quality — not like earthly, from the first degree that we needed to get up from, but now of spiritual egoism. And this is called Klipa."

He describes what that deepening actually feels like — not less friction with the friends, but more:

> "Now I'm starting to feel hatred, and I feel rejection, and all their actions, all their words... are just full of lies... it's just some theater. So I get all kinds of huge, very serious doubts — these that are real and justified in my rational thinking."

The instruction for handling this is what he calls faith above reason: continuing to act for the friends' benefit while deliberately not looking at one's own rational objections.

> "If a person can... truly close, really close his eyes, and not look inside himself, his egoism, his reason, and still continue to act like an idiot — then he wins, because that is faith above reason. I accept your game, he must say to the Creator. There's none else besides Him. He's the only one playing with me and putting all these obstacles in front of me."

## Part 3 — Why the Group Is Not Optional

Rav Laitman is direct that none of this is available to an individual working alone; the group is the only mechanism through which the force of bestowal can be revealed.

> "Without the group, it's not possible to realize even one spiritual action. A group doesn't mean we're just sitting here together — a group is that which realizes that which I just tried to express for you. That's why Kabbalists spend so much time and attention towards creation of the group."

He quotes Rabash directly on the paradox this requires of each member — nullifying oneself while also taking pride in the group's existence:

> "Rabash writes: love towards your friend is only possible by self-nullification. On one hand, one person needs to nullify themselves, and on another hand, needs to be proud of the fact that the Creator gave us an opportunity that we were able to unify in a group where each one of us has just one goal — for the Shechina to be between us."

Because the group carries this much weight, Rav Laitman is also specific — in the Q&A that follows — about what actually threatens it. Asked what to do about a friend who is domineering or difficult, he says correction should be indirect, not confrontational, and reserves direct expulsion for exactly two situations:

> "There are only two reasons to expel someone from the group. It is when he goes against our common direction, our sources, or our leader... or he goes against the common flow of the group direction, or he destroys its unity. Destroying the unity... the worst is when that person destroys families, causes division. Family... is holy — not because there's some lofty reason for this, but because the family is the initial point of a corrected society."

## Part 4 — Q&A: Two Struggles With the Self

Two questions in the Q&A dealt with the ordinary difficulty of staying honest with oneself over time.

Sergey, from the Perm group, asked how to handle shame over things he did in his youth. Rav Laitman's answer reframed the entire question of blame:

> "No shame — the Creator was doing this on your behalf, so that you understand it was Him, not you... You must recognize that this is all in the past, that the Creator did this with you. There's none else besides Him. That's enough. So stop eating yourself. If you blame yourself for the past, then you're actually blaming the Creator — because everything that's in the past is determined by Him. Only the present, and the future which is determined by the present, is yours."

Maxim, from Moscow, described a familiar cycle: waking each morning resolved to accomplish something significant, then finding by evening that nothing happened, forgetting, and starting over without real progress. Rav Laitman located the flaw not in the person's willpower but in the fact that the intention was private:

> "In the morning you start to focus on some of your own private spiritual interests that are of your own — but you need to engage into group ones, and then you won't be able to have excuses... Actions [are] only in the group, only in the group, and at least with one other person. Because if it's just myself, and I give myself some promise... all day I was thinking, or had the right intention — but that's zero. That's nothing, if it wasn't related to others... The spiritual work is only if we are in the group, at least two people together, but ideally more. And otherwise it's not spiritual."

## Part 5 — Q&A: The Group in Practice

The remaining questions pushed the same principle — that real spiritual work happens only through and with others — into a range of concrete situations.

Asked whether it's possible to feel real love toward the 99% of people who show no "point in the heart," Rav Laitman reframed the whole category: everyone external to oneself, in aggregate, is what will eventually be revealed as one's own soul.

> "Everything that's outside of me, external to me, in the form of seven billion people — that is called my soul. It's one unified desire. If I completely reveal it for myself, then I'll completely be able to attain a full correction... My task is to exit from within myself into others, and I will actually be able to feel, to sense, everyone that exists."

Asked whether it's acceptable for group members who don't attend the morning lesson regularly to teach others in a study program, he tied the answer to whether the person can honestly say what they're teaching:

> "If they're incapable, they can talk a lot, but they won't be able to know what they're talking about... It's important for these teachers to know what they need to relay, and they need to understand their boundaries... So let them talk to you and give you a report, and only after that, maybe they can go and teach."

Asked whether spiritual progress requires studying psychology, he distinguished the academic discipline from real self-knowledge:

> "Psychology on its own — there's nothing much to study there... A person cannot attain another person. To do that, you need to ascend. By studying Kabbalah, you can understand the psychology of a person, his soul — because everything comes from the soul."

Asked about the intense bond war veterans describe with each other, he located the resemblance precisely, while noting the limit:

> "Relationships develop between people when a person depends on others, where he can't save himself... this separation from the ego, when there's no place for it anymore... they, in a way, become a Kabbalistic group. It doesn't matter that they don't understand the spiritual method — but this self-sacrifice that appears through interdependence, that's true, that does exist."

Asked about the visible joy of a particular group known for its strong, encouraging spouses, he was direct about the weight a partner's confidence carries:

> "A wife, in relation to this, can do a lot more than the entire group. If a wife is a part of the man, and if she walks along with him, she can secure his success... you have huge influence on them. You can determine a man's success."

Asked, on behalf of a student, whether it was right to have chosen physical setup/teardown work at a congress over a sightseeing excursion, he affirmed the choice without hesitation, and described his own attitude toward those who do the least visible service work at large events:

> "People come from far away... and they walk into the kitchen and actually spend the entire congress in the kitchen working there... always the entire time in the kitchen. And I'm very envious of him."

Asked whether an "integral" teaching approach could be applied to something like a music class, he said the method only holds where the activity itself requires people to feel one another:

> "If it's an orchestra — I don't know, choir. A choir, of course. Let them start to try to feel each other... Music, it's actually an individual activity — so only if it's an orchestra or a choir. But if it's just one person playing... I don't understand how it can be integral."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Ordinary giving is still egoism in disguise if the pleasure comes back to the giver; real bestowal requires help from "the upper force" (the Creator), since human nature alone cannot produce selfless love.

2. The practical method is transposing oneself into the friends' desire until their fulfillment is felt as one's own — described as building an external vessel, or reflected light, through which the quality of bestowal (the Creator) is revealed.

3. Progress is measured in a specific unit ("the measure of Seah") but doesn't end at the first degree — each new level surfaces a correspondingly larger, more sophisticated ego (Klipa), so deeper attainment can feel like more friction with the friends, not less.

4. The instructed response to that friction is faith above reason: continuing to act toward the friends' benefit while deliberately setting aside one's own rational objections, since "there's none else besides Him."

5. The group is not optional scaffolding — it is the only place any of this can actually happen; correction of a difficult member should be indirect, and only two things justify expulsion: opposing the group's common direction/source/leader, or destroying family and unity.

6. Private, solo intentions don't count as spiritual work, no matter how sincere — the work only exists when carried out with at least one other person, which is why forgetfulness and self-justification dissolve good intentions made alone.

7. The same principle — needing others to attain anything real — shows up across very different situations in the Q&A: teaching requires honest grounding through group involvement, a spouse's confidence can carry a partner's success, and even a war-time bond of interdependence approximates real annulment of ego.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Bestowal (in order to bestow) | The intention to give rather than receive; the entire goal of the work, attainable only with the help of the Creator ("the upper force") since it runs opposite to natural egoistic wiring. |
| Klipa / Klipot | Shell(s) — here used specifically for the new, more sophisticated ego that surfaces at each higher spiritual degree, distinct from ordinary earthly ego. |
| Measure of Seah | An ancient unit of measure (originally for produce), used here as the threshold amount of transferred ego/desire that must accumulate before the first spiritual degree is felt. |
| Faith Above Reason | Continuing to act toward the goal (or toward a friend) while deliberately setting aside one's own rational objections and perceptions. |
| Shechina | Divine Presence; described here as the common quality of bestowal that a group's members exist to create and fill the space between them with. |
| Point in the Heart | The seed of spiritual desire in a person; reframed in the Q&A as ultimately extending, through the work, to all of external humanity, which together constitutes "my soul." |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes" — described here as the initial tool the group provides, through which a person prepares to eventually work with all of humanity. |
