### Opening

If the Creator's desire to benefit the created beings never changes, and every desire we feel is awakened by Him, what could an "awakening from below" possibly be? Even our being drawn to the group and to Kabbalah is "a very specific, very selective awakening from above." Rav Laitman's answer: something counts as ours only when we **build it ourselves between us**. The lesson then turns very practical: if the only action we have is to turn to the Creator, how do we pray for the friends, their troubles, and their deficiencies?

## Part 1 — What Is a Real Awakening from Below?

- Whatever a person feels individually is "a necessitated action by the Creator." Every individual desire comes from after the shattering, is egoistic, and "they're not even his own, they're designed by the Creator."
- Something is registered "in his account as his own" only from the point where he is in the group and realizes he must **establish a special desire for connection**, and from that common desire, a desire for the Creator.
- This collective awakening "doesn't come from above. That needs to be implemented, needs to be established, needs to be built. And it's not simple, it takes a lot of effort."
- Its true form is the cry in Egypt: "The children of Israel sighed from the work... and they cried out." Not crying for ourselves, but not wanting to be ruled by Pharaoh, the evil inclination, and wanting only to bring contentment to the Creator.
- When that outcry is established "in our collective heart," it is **MAN**, the wish to rise from **Malchut to Bina**. "Nothing in spirituality is attained if not by prayer," and "no individual can do it on his own."

## Part 2 — The Only Action: Turning to the Creator

> We have nothing to do in life at all besides always turning to the Creator... There's no action on our part besides the requests. And we need to get used to that.

- We ask Him to design our desire, help us connect, and give us "a strong and good desire, more accurate, more sharp." We ask, and we give thanks.
- This is why there is a public prayer and **Arvut**: "We want no one to let go of that request."
- The desire from above "is already standing by to bestow to us... it's just waiting for the right desire from below." By constantly addressing Him, the surrounding light works on us until we "make your desire as His desire," and, as in Matan Torah, receive the light that reforms.
- **Can a desire from below ever be real?** Yes, "as much as the Creator enables us." It's like what we demand from children: it fits our degree. The upper one who gives us these conditions also gives a correction that matches our response.

#### Where the group stands

- Rav Laitman sees friends becoming more open with each other, but warns against too much openness: until we are protected by screens over our ego, "we may harm each other. So everything has to be kind of balanced."
- Compared with earlier conventions, which were strong in intensity, he sees "very great sensitivity, very great anticipation towards the inner feeling."
- What's missing is only daily effort: more attention, sensitivity, mutual concern. He suggests **exercises**, such as everyone praising the greatness and uniqueness of the friends during the day.
- "You don't realize how many light years away you are" from others who seemingly engage in Kabbalah. "One's heart should be proud in the Creator's path."

## Part 3 — What Is a Whole Prayer?

- A **whole (complete) prayer** is one where the group awakens and adheres to "the whole Creator," adapting itself to Him: "make your desire as His desire."
- Baal HaSulam describes a seemingly simple state: a person despairs and turns to the Creator. But to despair *correctly*, from one's own strength, depends on the group and on "the series of lights that you need to receive."
- What do we want to attain? "None else besides Him. Good that does good." We reach it through the group, "as one man." And that's only the first degree.
- On the first degrees we aren't required to match our "senses" to what we'll receive. Later, "if you won't aim the vessels you won't discover the lights": the work becomes more meticulous and precise. "Now it's more like an embryo."

## Part 4 — Arvut and the Basket of Deficiencies

- **Arvut** is "the correct connection between us, that this deficiency that we establish is fit for the Creator." It is "the Kli that is in the right preparation for the answering of the prayer."
- Connection isn't "a million things." It's **deficiencies**, and we address them to the One who can correct and fill them.
- Each friend unites his personal lacks with the lack of the group so that "all in all, with one deficiency, we come to the Creator." One brings candy, one cake, one coffee, but the intention and action are one: "a basket with all the gifts."

#### Not fixing the friend

- We don't need to develop more sensitivity to the friends' desires, only enough to aim them all at the Creator. "Otherwise you'll be immersed in fixing the world."
- Trying to correct or fill the friend yourself "is idol worshiping." If we make that the goal instead of the means, "the Creator will corrupt these friends and give them such suffering" until we see only evil comes of it.
- If a friend is sick, we go to doctors and do everything possible ("the doctor has the permission to heal," Letter 16), but afterward we turn to the Creator and rely on Him.
- Turning only to the friend, with our own strength and scrutiny, and not to the Creator: "That is the shell. That's Klipa."

#### Preparing the vessels for the answer

- We prepare according to what we want to attain: none else besides Him, good that does good, which gives us the opportunity to bestow to Him. "Then it becomes one receives one," **Dvekut**.
- The answer to the prayer is the light that reforms shaping our desire to bestow above all other lacks: the light of the **613 counsels**. At a certain level of adhesion we are filled with the **613 deposits (Pikudin)**. "I am for my beloved and my beloved is for me."
- "The cow wants to feed more than the calf wants to suckle." The upper one's AHP is full of light for our lack. As long as our desire doesn't match His, "the gap is felt as darkness in us." As we match it, we reach the point of contact, and receiving becomes bestowing.

## Part 5 — How to Pray for the Friends' Troubles

- We raise to the Creator our common desire to receive strength from Him to bestow. The friend's corporeal lack can be included in this, "because it's my spiritual lack."
- Where do the friends' deficiencies come from? From the Creator: "Go to the craftsman who made me." Why? "So that we will unite. There's no other goal."
- The specific problem is "just an external clothing." Still, the necessities must be cared for: "If there's no flour, there's no Torah." We ask that the friends' lacks be **filled corporeally to the level of necessity and corrected spiritually**.
- Clothings are "corrected when you rise above them," not when the troubles vanish. The great Kabbalists suffered illnesses and hunger. Even on the first spiritual degree, the troubles don't disappear; they're seen as "more and more deep and spiritual." "We're in a transition phase."

| Question | Rav Laitman's answer |
|---|---|
| A friend has trouble with his wife. What do I pray for? | "You don't pray for what you think is good or bad for him." He's missing the Creator's help. "That's it, nothing else." |
| Should I ask for something specific? | "Don't direct the Creator to something specific." Over all problems there is one solution: the revelation of the Creator to the created being. |
| Then why does the detail matter? | It's sufficient to know he has a problem and, certainly, a lack for spirituality. Don't dig in with broken vessels. |
| What should we write to the friends? | "Test it out." Don't go "into each other's guts." Be somewhat impressed and integrated; we're still in egoistic vessels. "Move slowly." |
| Should we weigh whose pain is greater? | No. "One judges through his own flaws." We must be very far from such determinations. |
| What about sharing the friend's sorrow? | Crying with him is like hiring mourners, "an egoist who cries." Feel the pain, then raise it to the Creator. |

- Rav Laitman then stopped answering: "Some things you're not going to get from me, but only from your experience. If I'll say something in advance, it will become this kind of mantra." Right now is "a scrutiny phase."

## Part 6 — Taste, Coercion, and the Means as the Goal

- **The means becomes the goal.** First I decide my goal is adhesion and the group is a means. Delving into the means, "inside the means I find contact with the Creator." The vessel and the light become one, so there's "no difference between the means, the friends in the ten, and the Creator."
- **"Taste and see that the Lord is good."** Is seeking taste a Klipa? No. The Creator is the force of bestowal and love itself, not "some idol, some character" who also has that force. "Bestowal is permitted to enjoy, reception is not permitted to enjoy."
- **Coercion** means working against the will to enjoy reception, to "flip" one's inner program, thought, desire, and orientation into enjoying bestowal. It's done by the light that reforms, but "with my requests, I always think about that inversion of my foundations."
- **From the bottom of the heart** means from the fourth phase: "I want to completely correct it without any compromise."

#### Can't write my problems down

- A friend couldn't write his problems, and feared reading others' would make him judge them. Rav Laitman: "That's your work. It's because you're prideful." Don't write everything. "Just write a little bit... A drop. So it won't hurt your honor."
- **Judging the friends' qualities**: liar, stubborn, lazy, control freak. "These are qualities that the Creator put into that body," given to me "as a friend that I need to connect to above these qualities." They matter only where they disturb the work and dissemination.
- A friend without a job: send his CV *and* ask for the light. "We need to do both," but "God forbid to think that by that we're actually solving the problem."
- "You don't know how much a person needs to strip down of himself in the spiritual path."

#### What is bestowal?

- "Only the Creator can bestow. I can't bestow anything." I can receive in order to bestow; my bestowal is "making myself a conduit."
- Giving that the other has no prior deficiency for serves our own ego, like the nations bringing their culture to Africa. "The deficiency of the lower one needs to become prior and be suited to what the upper one wants to give." Then "there's no difference between receiving and giving."

## Part 7 — When a Friend Passes Away, and Entering the Congress

- When a friend passes away, the work continues. Accept it "as judgment from above and we move on," take in another tenth. "He's a friend for eternity."
- While he is very ill, pray until the last moment: "It depends on our prayer." After that, the customs (Shiva, 30 days, a year) are symbols, but friends return to study the next day. The same applies to the teacher.
- **At the Congress there are no personal disturbances.** No troubles, no news: "only the Congress and its topic." Detach from everything, arrange things so the family won't need you, and make sure there is a place for women and children so everyone who wants to can participate.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **An awakening from below is built, not felt.** Individual desires come from above; only the common desire for connection and for the Creator, established between us with effort, is ours.
2. **Our only action is turning to the Creator.** Requesting and thanking; Arvut exists so no one lets go of the request.
3. **Arvut is a vessel of deficiencies.** Each friend's lack goes into one "basket" raised to the Creator as a single deficiency.
4. **Don't try to fix the friend yourself.** Help with necessities, but turning only to the friend and not to the Creator is a Klipa, even idol worship.
5. **Pray for the Creator's help, not for a specific outcome.** Over every problem there is one solution: His revelation.
6. **The Creator is the force of bestowal itself.** Enjoying bestowal is permitted; the work of coercion is flipping our inner program toward it.
7. **Move slowly and learn by experience.** Open up a "drop," don't judge the friends' qualities, and at the Congress leave all personal disturbances behind.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Awakening from Below** | Not individual desire (which comes from above) but the common desire for connection and the Creator that the group builds by its own effort. |
| **Awakening from Above** | The Creator's unchanging will to benefit, which awakens every desire in us, including the call to Kabbalah. |
| **Lishma** | Work entirely to bestow; a gift from above in answer to a prayer from below. |
| **MAN** | The collective outcry raised to rise from Malchut to Bina. |
| **Whole (Complete) Prayer** | The group adhering to the "whole Creator," despairing correctly of its own strength and asking only for Him. |
| **Arvut** | The correct connection between friends; the Kli prepared for the answer to the prayer. |
| **Klipa** | Addressing only the friend, with one's own strength, without turning to the Creator. |
| **Counsels / Deposits** | The 613 lights that first shape the desire to bestow (counsels), then fill the vessel in adhesion (deposits, Pikudin). |
| **Dvekut** | Adhesion; "one receives one," where receiving in order to bestow matches the Creator's bestowal. |
| **Coercion** | Working against the will to enjoy reception, flipping one's inner program to enjoy bestowal. |
| **External Clothing** | The specific corporeal problem dressed over the real deficiency, the lack for the Creator. |
| **Conduit** | Our only form of bestowal: passing the Creator's abundance through, never generating it. |
