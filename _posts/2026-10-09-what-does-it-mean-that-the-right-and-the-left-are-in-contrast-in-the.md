### Opening

How can a person be humble and proud at once, live in his environment without standing out and still rise above it? Rav Laitman calls Rabash's article "very broad and general." From it he draws one working rule: **in every state there is an inner contradiction.** The lesson asks where this contradiction comes from and how to keep it from blurring our perception. It also asks how much of our advancement is in our own hands, given that we're carried from environment to environment like a charge in a field.

## Part 1 — The Stages of Lo Lishma

Rav Laitman sets out the states that precede Lishma, so that "we won't delude ourselves" about them:

- **Working for society.** Reward and punishment from people: honor, money, knowledge, even animal pleasures clothed through society. A person can become a great scientist, a great rabbi, "great" in anything, and it's all for the sake of the society that rewards him.
- **The commandments of people.** He begins to relate to an upper force, but only because he was taught to. He would keep the same actions even if there were no upper force: "It's simply a habit, his upbringing and education."
- **"For the sake of heaven."** He begins to feel by himself that there's an upper force he can influence: some actions will bring good, others punishment. It looks holy, but "it's not a holy state." The reward is simply expected from above instead of from people. **This is still Lo Lishma.**
- **Lishma** is "without any benefit for himself." No thought of reward and punishment, not even of deserving the Creator. "The person's I does not exist."

## Part 2 — Why Every State Holds a Contradiction

- **The right line** exists in a person "when he annuls himself" and takes on the Creator's qualities. **The left line** is when he engages his own "I" and pulls his pride and ego into the work.
- The root is in the vessel itself. We attain the light only through the vessel, and the vessel has two opposite sides:
  - **By nature**, it is the will to receive, opposite to the light.
  - **By action**, it is the will to bestow, equivalent to the light. To the extent of that equivalence, it receives the light and understands who the Creator is.
- We have no grasp of the Creator Himself, only of "our corrected qualities," which we call the Creator because they're corrected relative to Him. So in every discernment and every critique there will always be an inner contradiction: "We have to understand where it's coming from and not let it confuse us."

#### An example: strong groups and an open world

- Rav Laitman had written that groups should be built from healthy, strong people, and people objected: how can you turn others away when you say the whole world must study Kabbalah?
- Both are true. The whole world should come and study "en masse, without any limitation." But a group of leaders and teachers must be "very strong, very crystallized." There's no room in it for someone lazy or negligent, someone who "drills a hole in the boat," as Baal HaSulam writes in his speech for the completion of the Zohar.
- These are two directions, like **Keter**, the Rosh of the Partzuf, which leads, and **Malchut**, the Guf, which agrees to be led. "These things are in contradiction."

## Part 3 — Greatness and Humility on the Right

- A student said the right line means seeing the Creator's greatness. Rav Laitman corrected him: when you see His greatness, you also see His lowliness.
- A great person who wants to lead people shows them his power. **The Creator does the opposite.** "He deliberately humiliates himself so he won't be seen. He hides," and brings you closer "not according to his power, but according to your ability."
- So the right line already contains both sides: tremendous power and great humility. Whoever adheres to the right learns both qualities from Him.
- And the left line? "In the left line you have only cruelty. Like in a baby."

#### Why 23½ hours on the right and half an hour on the left

- Receiving the Creator's qualities and being impressed by Him is very difficult, so a person needs most of his time in that reception. In half an hour on the left, "I already derive all the benefit, negative benefit, that I can," then add it to the right and build the middle line.
- Is the number exact? Baal HaSulam has writings with precise calculations by the Sefirot (fractions of Netzach, Hod, and so on), "very scientific. It's a wonder." Rav Laitman wouldn't guess at the reasoning: "As a number, that's how it is."

## Part 4 — "If I Am Not for Me" and "There Is None Else Besides Him"

- A student asked how this fits with spending the morning in "If I am not for me, who is for me?" and only the evening in "There is none else besides Him."
- "If I am not for me" doesn't erase the Creator. "If you erase Him, you have nothing to do." It means being in connection with Him while taking an **active attitude**, as if the outcome depends on you.
- Afterward you discover "that none of that was you. The vessels are His, the fillings are His." Then you set it all in "There is none else besides Him." When the next degree comes, you start again with "If I am not for me."
- **So who am I?** Even the desire for wholeness is His. All your actions and thoughts come from Him.

> You are this small point that reaches the state of adhesion and says that there is none else besides Him. And other than me saying there's none else besides Him, everything else is Him.

- When the questions turned abstract, Rav Laitman stopped them: "This is becoming philosophy instead of Kabbalah."

## Part 5 — A Charge in an Electric Field

- A student back from army reserve duty asked how to live among people whose environment he doesn't want to be absorbed in, without standing out.
- Rav Laitman: **you can't resist an environment you're in.** "You have no power to resist it, none." We left our old environments only because we were pulled out from above, when the point in the heart awakened.
- **The analogy:** a charge in an electric field moves when its potential changes, until it finds its place. Over thousands of incarnations a person's inner potential is changed for him and he's led, slowly, toward the center: the end of correction. "And you had no hand in it."
- Then, in one incarnation, he's given the potential called **the point in the heart**. From here, advancement is conscious and comes "only by free choice." He has to replace his environment himself and take care of his own advancement.

#### Three possible states

Wherever you are, you're in one of three states:

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| The thought is gone | You don't think you can replace your environment, so you advance at everyone's pace, over thousands of years. |
| The thought is given | You recognize your environment is bad relative to the goal. "It's an excellent state," but be careful: soon it will be taken away. |
| The proper environment | You've been given the opportunity to work in the right environment. |

- "It's impossible to be in a bad environment, to remain there and to advance." Students who left and returned ten or fifteen years later found the thought renewed and the years wasted.
- **Can I change my own potential?** Yes. It depends on your feeling and your relation. By drawing the light you change your potential, "and then you move." It's all one rule: **equivalence of form**, from which every other rule derives. "You are the point of choice alone... Other than that, there is no you."

## Part 6 — Environments You Can't Choose

- What about places you have to be (the army reserve, work, relatives)? "An environment means that I determine for myself that I am in an environment." If I don't determine it as mine, I'm not in it.
- Rav Laitman described living on Rabbi Akiva Street in Bnei Brak, "the holiest place," without adhering to what goes on there. He does what's needed, "but I don't adhere to it. I don't listen, I don't look." To rest, he watches ten or fifteen minutes of a nature documentary, "some lions," and returns. "The rest for me is not an environment."
- From Baal HaSulam: a builder must not be among bad builders, who say "let's make it a bit crooked." A builder among shoemakers doesn't care what they say. The closer people are to your own profession, spirituality, the less contact you should have with those who'd spoil it.
- **Within the group**, the opposite: a beginner is "one hundred percent dependent on what you absorb from the environment." Make yourself small, consider everyone there great, "because the smaller one is, the more he receives." If you sit in the group like a student in a university, "you won't receive from the society. There won't be any inner change."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **"For the sake of heaven" is still Lo Lishma.** Expecting reward from the Creator instead of from people doesn't make it holy. Lishma has no benefit for oneself at all.
2. **Contradiction is built into the vessel.** It is will to receive by nature and will to bestow by action, so every state has two sides. Understand the source and don't let it confuse you.
3. **The right line holds both greatness and humility.** The Creator hides and lowers Himself to bring us closer according to our ability.
4. **Act as if it's up to you, then discover it was all Him.** The only "I" is the point that says there is none else besides Him.
5. **The point in the heart turns advancement into choice.** Before it, we're moved like a charge in a field; after it, we must change our environment ourselves.
6. **A good thought about your environment is an opportunity that won't last.** Act on it before it's taken away.
7. **Don't adhere to environments you didn't choose; annul yourself before the one you did.**

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Lo Lishma** | Work for one's own benefit, whether the reward is expected from society or from the Creator ("for the sake of heaven"). |
| **Lishma** | Work without any benefit for oneself, where the person's "I" does not exist. |
| **Commandments of People** | Actions kept because one was taught them, out of habit and upbringing, not because of the Creator. |
| **Right Line** | Self-annulment and taking on the Creator's qualities, which include both greatness and humility. |
| **Left Line** | Bringing one's own "I," pride, and ego into the work; needed only briefly ("half an hour"). |
| **Middle Line** | Built by adding what was gained on the left to the right. |
| **Keter / Malchut** | Here, the leading group (the Rosh) and those who agree to be led (the Guf). |
| **Point in the Heart** | The potential given in one incarnation that makes advancement conscious and dependent on free choice. |
| **Environment** | Only what a person determines for himself to be his environment; the main source of a beginner's change. |
| **Equivalence of Form** | The single rule from which all others derive; drawing light changes one's "potential" and so one's place. |
