### Opening

In this letter Rabash tells his students of a talk he gave before returning to Israel. Repentance means returning to one's root: man is the will to receive and the Creator is the giver, and a person knows he has repented when he is rewarded with the Creator's pleasantness, meaning he already works in order to bestow. Everything is attained by overcoming, and "many pennies join into a great amount": even one who knows from experience he will soon fall again should overcome now, because each moment joins the general account. Perhaps this is why the gates of tears are not locked. Like one who stands among eloquent scoffers and can only protest a little, it is still good that he disagrees. Rav Laitman explains the Gate of Tears as an inner state built within a person, why despair of one's own strength is the happiest state, how to examine whether our work in the society is bringing us to it, and what we should really cry over.

## Part 1 — Nothing Helps but Prayer

- The usual states below the barrier: a person feels on one hand that he has nothing, and on the other that he still yearns. "If he did not feel that he has nothing, he would not feel that he has an empty vessel."
- These states come without asking him, and from them he must learn "that nothing helps him except the power of prayer." Prayer means asking the Creator "for the desire to bestow," because what he lacks is love for the Creator, not filling in his vessels.
- Out of despair and effort, a different approach to prayer takes shape: "not give me, but give me the strength to bestow to You. Give me the strength to love, to connect." Among his ascents and descents he begins to ask not whether he feels bad or good, but whether he is asking for his own benefit or "to have something in common with the Creator." "Already in his Lo Lishma there is a little Lishma."

## Part 2 — The Gate of Tears Is an Inner State

- "The Gate of Tears is a person's inner state that is built. It's not that it's there in front of us." It is the vessel gradually built in him, suited for the light.
- These aren't the tears of a receiver. They come from his not belonging to the bestowing part of creation. "Tears are not just desires, but such desires that he has no desires": he understands he can't have a real desire for spirituality, and that changing the state isn't up to him. "Then a person cries... He needs help."
- It accumulates through a change of values, giving "spirituality" and "corporeality" a truer meaning each time. At first everyone thinks they want spirituality, then catch themselves: they don't, and can't, by themselves.
- In the upper worlds, too, there are tears: the upper Partzuf cries over the lower one, and the tears drip into the Great Sea, by which Malchut is corrected. Tears are what one receives not by the screen, "something we call mercy," and they complete the vessel.

#### Existence from absence

- Why can't we pass smoothly from in order to receive to in order to bestow? "The will to receive is existence from absence and the desire to bestow is existence from existence, and you can't leap over," because that is the difference between the Creator and the created being.
- The created being doesn't cry over being from absence; "he cries over not being able to become similar to existence." "It's forbidden to lock this gate, because it is exactly that gap," and when he truly cries over it, "the Creator brings him over to His side."
- "The moment you reach that state... it is immediately open. In spirituality there's no delay in terms of time": when all the conditions are there, the action happens.

## Part 3 — Effort Above Human Strength

- **Q: How do we get from crying over nothing to real crying?** A baby cries over a toy as if the world were doomed; a grown-up cries because he lost everything. "That's according to one's investment. Gradually build that vessel." You know you have it when you enter spirituality.
- How is it built? Only through exertion, in "an action above human strength." Everything in order to receive a person can eventually do, with humanity as his partners. "Above human strength means in order to bestow, the Creator's qualities. And if you try to do that and you discover that you can't... you try until you reach the Gate of Tears."
- As Baal HaSulam writes in his letters, there is no happier state than when a person despairs of his own strength, having done all he could. Then he is worthy of a wholehearted prayer. As long as he feels some strength of his own, his crying isn't true, and the evil inclination helps by pushing him to exert more and more.
- Where to try? "Different actions in order to bestow, and you have whom to do it toward: the society. In relation to the Creator you'll deceive yourself all the time."

#### Bestowing fuel

- **Q: What can I give the society without profit for myself?** If the friends are on the path with you, what they truly need "is uplifted spirits... to bestow upon the society as much energy and fuel as possible."
- You'll see profit in it, and that's true. After many actions together you will reach despair: "by my own efforts I can't do anything," and yet, because I invested, I really want it. That's the state of shedding tears.
- "Our work doesn't have to end in success on our part, but in that we obligate the Creator." "My sons have defeated Me." "He has the power and the upper light, not me. I always need to prepare the MAN, the vessel, the lack." So coming to tears "is a very blessed thing": it shows the inferiority of the will to receive and the merit of the degree I'm about to acquire.

## Part 4 — Take the Reward, Examine the Intention

- **Q: Why not do actions in the society that bring me no reward at all?** "Unless I get some kind of benefit from the society, I will not be able to work... I run on the fuel of the will to receive." If instead of a toast I got a negative sign, I wouldn't even start.
- The actions are correct, and so is the praise: "you're great, what a friend." "It's very good that I did one gram of work and from them I get support as if I did a hundred kilos." The only question: "why did I do it? To get those 100 kilos, or because of the importance of the Creator?"
- Society must reward one who gives, and oblige one who doesn't, "to the point that it throws him out. It's called drilling a hole in our boat."
- The friends don't talk about their desire for the Creator; "this is forbidden to discuss." They only reveal their contribution, and from it I see that they too want the upper reward. When everyone has given everything in this world to the friends and the study and still isn't connected to Him, "it is called despairing of one's own strength," because resembling Him only the upper light can give.
- If I calm down and see that maybe I can do a little more, as Baal HaSulam says, I haven't yet despaired: "he has more discernments which he hasn't scrutinized."

#### When is it the end?

- "Nobody knows how you finish it. When you finish it, you see that you finished it." Many times we think "this is it, the world is in ruins," and then calm down like a baby with another toy. The real end is "that the Creator answers."

## Part 5 — The Quality of the Exertion

- **Q: How does existence from absence relate to working with the group?** It explains why there's no smooth passage, why there must be a crisis before entering spirituality, "the tearing of the Red Sea." "Imagine how much importance I need to feel toward spirituality in order to bestow. And from where will I take it?" The surrounding light must bring it during the study.
- "I pledge to acquire the force of faith prior to the study." Not that I've received it: "I want to want to have it. That is what I expect of the surrounding light... that is called the quality of the exertion," as Baal HaSulam writes in item 18 of the Introduction to the Study of the Ten Sefirot. From then, "start counting three years," or five: degrees, a kind of slogan.
- Faith is the Creator's quality: the power of bestowal. If through study and work in the society I still haven't reached despair of my own strength, "this is a sign that I haven't invested enough," above all in the quality of the exertion.

## Part 6 — The Donkey and Its Rider

- **Q: In Lo Lishma there is filling; how is faith detached from it?** The good feeling with the society, the respect, the sense of advancing together "needs to be that way. It's mandatory. Without that my donkey will never advance."
- But after the hard work, the toast, the meal and a strong study: "Ultimately, do you feel that you received something from the Creator? Are you in equivalence of form with Him? Or do you simply have some kind of psychological compensation?" There are hundreds of parameters in the blood alone; did you examine the spiritual one? Swap out every gene "and it remains the same beast."
- "Maybe you should have been in a state of despair while being happy with the society." The rider asks: the road is nice, the donkey is satisfied, "but do I reach the goal by this?" If you're satisfied, "you're not working with the point in the heart."
- A person can't awaken this examination by himself; "he will always forget." It depends on the spirit of the society, whether it pushes each one to check after every action: am I advancing toward the Gate of Tears? "If not, it's just the other gates": money, respect, knowledge.

#### Keep the goal on fire

- "The answer for how to truly advance is not found with me, but in the society." The society must hold the idea of connection with the Creator at its head, constantly burning. "If not that, then it's disaster."
- Otherwise we'll sit here twenty or thirty years more, though not in vain: "every slight motion done in Lo Lishma remains... Lo Lishma is a great thing, Rabash writes." It accumulates, and in another incarnation you start from a higher point. What we're going to acquire is the worlds, something above this world.
- "With every action, whatever happens, first you turn to the Creator and then the action."

## Part 7 — Despair, Ascent and What to Cry Over

- Every time there have to be three states: "despair and then upliftment and again you try." I lock onto the Creator, then choose the society and strengthen it so it strengthens me. When after all of it I still haven't reached Him, "then I despair and I shout and I ask and I demand."
- And what's revealed after total despair is that it isn't total yet: there are some 613 degrees, and when I finish one, the AHP of the upper one awakens in me. "Every time it needs to be total despair. Otherwise it cannot be said that I completed the previous degree." Don't count; "this entire state is called concealment."
- **Q: If it's total despair, what brings me out?** "The coarseness that is not within that action yet. It's the evil inclination which says that again I need to try."
- True despair teaches "that I need to yearn for Lishma a little bit also." What bothers me: "I'm not similar to the Creator."

#### Tears only toward the Creator

- "Tears need to be directed toward the Creator alone. If you cry to someone else, it's not even called tears." Crying over your fallen donkey, or because you hoped Kabbalah would give you control over the world, "turn to psychologists, economists." "Only one thing relates to the Creator: if you want to advance to Him, He will help you."
- Crying over the pain caused to the Creator, the Shechina in exile, "let's wait with that. It's a bit early." That belongs to a deeper scrutiny within the desire to resemble Him, where one cries not because he feels bad, "but because by this you are doing bad to the upper one." And yes, the tears are a result of the despair.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Nothing helps but prayer.** From feeling empty yet yearning, one learns to ask not for filling but for the strength to bestow and love.
2. **The Gate of Tears is built inside.** It is a vessel of bitterness and despair over being unable to want bestowal, and when it is complete it opens immediately.
3. **Existence from absence can't be leapt over.** The created being cries over the gap between himself and the Creator, and the Creator Himself covers it.
4. **Despair of one's own strength is the happiest state.** It comes only after real effort above human strength, in actions of bestowal toward the society.
5. **Take the reward, check the intention.** The society's praise is necessary fuel for the donkey; the rider must ask whether it brings him closer to the Creator.
6. **The quality of the exertion.** Before the study, want the force of faith from the surrounding light; failing to reach despair means not investing enough.
7. **The society keeps the goal burning.** Despair, ascent and renewed effort repeat at every degree, and tears count only when directed to the Creator.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Repentance (Teshuva)** | Returning to one's root, the quality of the giver; its sign is being rewarded with the Creator's pleasantness, working in order to bestow. |
| **Many pennies join into a great amount** | Every moment of overcoming, even with certainty of falling again, joins the general account until the vessel is full. |
| **Gate of Tears** | An inner state built gradually: despair of ever wanting bestowal by oneself, which the Creator answers at once. |
| **Existence from absence** | The nature of the will to receive, which cannot leap to bestowal (existence from existence) by its own strength. |
| **Above human strength** | Actions in order to bestow, which one tries toward the society until discovering he cannot. |
| **Despair of one's own strength** | Having given everything to the friends and the study without attaining connection; the condition for a wholehearted prayer. |
| **Bestowing fuel** | Giving the friends uplifted spirits, energy and yearning, what they truly need on the path. |
| **Quality of the exertion** | Expecting from the surrounding light, before and during study, the force of faith (TES Introduction, item 18). |
| **Donkey and rider** | The body that needs rewards to move, and the intention that asks whether the journey leads to the Creator. |
| **Drilling a hole in our boat** | Failing to contribute to the society; the society must oblige its members, even to the point of removal. |
| **Tears directed to the Creator** | Only crying over the lack of equivalence with Him counts as tears; crying for worldly loss does not. |
