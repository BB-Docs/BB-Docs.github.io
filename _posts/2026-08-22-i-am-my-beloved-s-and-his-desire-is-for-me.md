### Opening

The lesson works through a sequence of short excerpts — from Rabash, from the Zohar, and from a third source describing "the one who knows the mysteries" — that all circle the same question from different angles: how does a group of people, none of whom can feel the Creator directly, actually reach Him? The recurring answer is that the Creator is never approached directly at all. He is reached only through the connection people build between themselves, a connection Rav Laitman describes, startlingly, as something the group itself constructs and "gives birth to." The Q&A tests this claim from many sides — through equivalence of form, through the order of purification and revelation, through what it means to listen for a voice that isn't heard directly, and through reassurance that the suffering of longing for Him carries no real danger of falling.

## Part 1 — Catching the Creator "Like a Fish in a Net"

The session opens mid-discussion on what it actually means to build connection toward the Creator through connection with friends. Rav Laitman describes the process as a mutual, gradual widening: each person accepting more and more forms of bestowal learned from the Creator, until the group reaches something like a shared hold on Him.

> "We really grab hold onto Him like in a net — just like we catch a fish in a net. And on this the Creator says: my sons have won Me over. We have succeeded in catching Him and holding Him between us."

He adds that this is not a single event but an accumulation — accepting more forms of bestowal, feeling them out in oneself, and slowly, gradually reaching equivalence of form: "this it is called, I to my beloved and my beloved to me."

## Part 2 — Reading With the Right Intention

Asked how to approach short, repetitive excerpts that seem to keep saying the same thing — connect between yourselves, toward the Creator — Rav Laitman locates the work not in the words themselves but in the reader's own inner state:

> "I want to get into the excerpt. I want to make the effort to be impressed, to be inspired, to be excited by what's written there, which was written by Kabbalists. I want to feel this inner tremble, this inner shake — that's how the Kabbalist felt when he wrote it. And this is how we should enter it, and gradually we'll reach things, and our awakenings will be in accordance with what's written."

## Part 3 — Item 6 (Rabash): What "Bow Before His Holy Mountain" Asks Of a Person

After the group reads Rabash's short piece on exalting the Creator and "bowing before His holy mountain," the Q&A works out what these terms mean in practice. Asked directly what "thoughts of Kedusha" are, Rav Laitman keeps the answer simple:

> "Thoughts of sanctity — not much, the intention to bestow. That's it. Just the opposite, the intention to receive... The intention to receive is this world, thoughts of impurity. The intention to bestow is the spiritual world, thoughts of purity, of sanctity. That's it."

On "holy" itself, he draws an analogy to a bride:

> "Sanctity means separate, opposite from me... Just as you tell the bride in Hebrew, you are separated from the whole world and you belong only to Me — that's called sanctified. Separated. Separate from reception, from our ego."

Asked how "bowing" — surrendering the body even while it disagrees — is actually done, he answers plainly: "In actions we can do this. In prayers we can do this. Until there comes from above some unique illumination and sanctifies us. And then we reach His holy mountain. Slowly we begin to climb it." A follow-up question clarifies that the "mountain" itself holds both qualities together — reception and bestowal — which is exactly why it is hard to climb:

> "It's very hard to climb over, but nonetheless, when we climb, we rise. Barely, barely, but we do rise. That's called: we reach His holy mountain. By this we reveal what is sanctified, what is separate from the will to receive — which is a quality of bestowal."

## Part 4 — Item 7 (Zohar): Purification Comes Before Revelation

The next excerpt states that divinity is not present with the wicked, and that only when a person comes to purify himself does divinity rest over him — "I am my beloved's" comes first, "and his desire is for me" follows. Asked what it actually means to "purify," Rav Laitman describes a concrete, ongoing effort rather than a single achievement:

> "I want to be in the quality of bestowal like Him, and I do all sorts of preparations for this — I want to be connected to the friends, I want to bestow — and from this I prepare myself. Then I'm turning to the Creator, and I ultimately receive some response from Him: I begin to feel how I'm starting to change, how much I'm capable of coming closer... Sometimes it can take a long time until a person really hears this, agrees with it, and then begins to realize it. But that's the process. That's the path."

## Part 5 — Two Stages, and a Concealed First Move

Pressed on whether "I am my beloved's" and "his desire is for me" describe two stages — building the vessel, then its fulfillment — Rav Laitman gives one of the session's longer answers. The Creator, he says, always acts first, but a person cannot feel this:

> "The truth is the Creator begins this, but we don't feel it. He awakens our heart and we begin to somehow yearn. But when a person awakens the Creator by all sorts of means — by connecting with the group — he doesn't know that it's the Creator who prepared this for him... And then what happens is called 'I to my beloved': the Creator is concealed, the Creator is always first. When a person acts, it then seems to him that this is how he awakened the Creator, and not that the Creator is acting in him. And then the Creator is revealed and seemingly agrees to connect with the person — this is called 'My sons have won Me over.' But all this is a game from above, just as we play with children. Actually all this is the Creator — I am the first and I am the last."

Asked why the Creator needs this initial, hidden action at all rather than simply revealing Himself, Rav Laitman ties it to free choice: the awakening comes through "messengers... called the Malachim, angels, or persons" — an opportunity, a book that falls into a person's hands, something overheard from somebody — precisely so the response remains the person's own free choice rather than something forced on him.

## Part 6 — Building the Creator Between Us

Asked directly how a felt connection between friends can become a connection with the Creator, Rav Laitman gives what is arguably the session's central answer — that the Creator is not approached at any location outside the group, but is located, and even formed, in the connection itself:

> "You need to summarize between yourselves that you're connecting all the desires, yearnings, intentions, and tendencies together, and aiming them together to the Creator... This is the strongest action that can come from the group's part. What does it mean to aim for the Creator in a more and more accurate way? This is by how much we connect between us... There is no Creator. There is no place, nowhere to aim yourselves — rather, specifically, to the center of the connection between you. There He is. You are building Him. You are establishing, forming this concept, this term, the Creator. He fills everything and does everything, everything, everything — but where? There isn't, rather only in the connection between you, that you establish and form. In the center of the connection between you, in the agreement between you, and your work between yourselves — there you locate, you detect this quality that you attain. He says: you have built Me, you have made Me. There you build Him and form Him and adhere to Him. It seemingly turns out that you're giving birth to Him."

## Part 7 — Item 8: The Knot That Will Not Crumble, and the Great Tekiah

The third excerpt, read twice for clarity, describes "the one who knows the mysteries," whose longing for the Creator grows and intensifies "until one's point in the heart is completed with complete will, in a tight knot that will not crumble" — and who is granted, in the end, "the return of the Hei to the Vav for eternity," described as "the meaning of the great Tekiah." Asked to explain this last phrase more simply, Rav Laitman gives a direct definition:

> "The Tekiah Gedolah is the first thing — that's the coupling between man and the Creator, in receiving in order to bestow, in the mutual highest form — the final correction of man."

Asked how a person reaches the "correct choice" at all, he returns to the session's throughline — that this is never solved alone:

> "Only by connection with the friends can we find out where we really are. Otherwise an individual can't determine the goal, where he is in relation to the goal, and how he aims toward it — he can't. We have to have at least two, or at least three points, to be properly [oriented]. A single individual can just go in any direction. So only if he is with the friends — the minimum is two, but desirably up to ten."

## Part 8 — Closing Q&A: Hearing the Voice, the Sorrow of the Shechina, and the Timing of the Work

A run of shorter questions closes the session. Asked what it means to "listen to the Creator's voice," Rav Laitman ties it to a specific degree: "That requires reaching the degree of Bina — Light of Chassadim. Then we will hear. Then the level of vision, sight — Light of Chochma." Asked about the excerpt's description of the Shechina "suffering" alongside the person, he names this directly: "This is called the sorrow of the Shechina — the sorrow of the Shechina is that the Creator suffers because the man receives all the means from Him to get closer, and he doesn't [use them]."

On what it means to purify one's heart, he insists the work is never purely private: "I say that we need to cleanse ourselves toward the Creator — then first I'm aiming myself toward the friends, because without this I can't adhere to Him. The Creator is the general, the common; I can't reach Him only by checking some private tendency — even that is not correct. Rather, all the things we check only if we connect with the friends, and with them to the Creator." Asked what to do when a person doesn't know how to connect or pray at all, his answer is characteristically spare: "Keep silent and listen. Simply do like that, and you'll see that slowly it changes — without complaints and criticisms toward anything and no one, neither the organization, the friends, the ten, nor the Creator. Just listen."

Asked whether there is a "special" quality to this particular period, tied to the line "I to my beloved, my beloved to me," Rav Laitman calls it "the month of awakening" — a suitable time for a person's heart to awaken to the Creator, adding that "there's importance to the time we study in... there's times for the bad and times for the good." Finally, asked whether the increasing "affliction" of longing described in the excerpts could actually cause a person to fall, he is unambiguous: "No. No, there's none." What the excerpt calls being "tested" by the one who knows the mysteries "that he shall not fall," he explains, simply means "that the Creator already knows that a person will go further and further on the straight path" — provided he keeps doing what was already described: staying connected to the friends.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. The Creator is not approached at a location outside the group — Rav Laitman describes Him as formed and located "in the center of the connection between you," a connection the group itself builds and, in his words, "gives birth to."
2. "Exalt" and "bow before His holy mountain" are unpacked concretely: exaltedness is known only through equivalence of form, and the "holy mountain" itself holds both reception and bestowal together, which is exactly why it is hard, but not impossible, to climb.
3. Kedusha (holiness) is defined simply as the intention to bestow, as opposed to the intention to receive; the bride analogy — separated from everything else, belonging only to the Creator — is used to explain what "sanctified" means.
4. Purification precedes revelation ("I am my beloved's" before "his desire is for me"): a person prepares through connecting with and bestowing to the friends, then receives a felt response from the Creator showing how he is changing.
5. The Creator's first move is always concealed — He awakens a person through hidden means (books, chance encounters, other people) precisely so that the person's response remains a matter of free choice, even though "all this is a game from above," and it is always the Creator who is first and last.
6. "The Tekiah Gedolah" is defined directly as the coupling between man and the Creator, in receiving in order to bestow, in the highest mutual form — described as the final correction of man.
7. No individual can determine his own spiritual position or direction alone; a minimum of two, and desirably up to ten, connected people are needed to locate "where we really are" and aim accurately toward the Creator.
8. The sorrow of the Shechina is defined as the Creator's own suffering — that a person has been given the means to draw closer and does not use them — and Rav Laitman explicitly rules out any real danger of "falling" from the affliction of longing itself, so long as connection with the friends is maintained.
