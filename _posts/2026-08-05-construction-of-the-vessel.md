### Opening

The lesson opens mid-teaching, with Rabash's discussion of how the Kli (vessel) is built, and quickly moves into an extended live Q&A. The organizing question underneath everything that follows: since the Creator's entire purpose is only to benefit the created being, why can't a person simply be handed a finished, corrected vessel outright — why must desire be built up gradually, made to feel worse before it can be corrected, and routed through concealment and darkness rather than clarity? Rav Laitman traces the answer from the earliest stirrings of desire for spirituality in Adam and Abraham, through Egypt as the necessary school for enlarging that desire, into the mechanics of surrounding light and why growth can feel like regression, and finally into why genuine spiritual connection dispenses with language altogether.

## Part 1 — From Adam to Abraham: The Birth of the Desire for Spirituality

Rav Laitman opens by distinguishing the human being, in the Kabbalistic sense, from the human body.

> "As long until we have that deficiency, we're not even talking about the human. We're talking about the human from the beginning of the desire of the human — Adam. And that's from the word *Adam*, to resemble the Upper One... In our world we see bodies, human bodies, which actually reincarnate for thousands and hundreds of thousands of years... but then in humans there were no greater desires than just for existence."

Only about fifty-seven hundred years ago — what the Hebrew calendar marks as the birth of Adam HaRishon — did a corporeal person in this world begin to form even the beginning of a desire for spirituality, and even then it was felt only by a handful of individuals: "Adam" and his descendants, down to Abraham. "And in Abraham, that desire towards godliness was felt in a more conspicuous way." Abraham discovered spirituality from within and was given not only the desire to reveal it but a method for doing so — a method that let him see all of creation and step outside a level of existence bound by time, death, and concealment.

## Part 2 — Why We Must Descend to Egypt: Building the Vessel Before Correcting It

Abraham wanted to reach closeness with the Creator directly, without waiting for revelation from above. The Creator's answer was Egypt.

> "The Creator said, I'll make it so that your descendants will enter Egypt. And then Abraham calmed down, because he understood that by entering Egypt, they'll acquire something good — something sufficient to reach the purpose of creation. Meaning the deficiencies, the vessels, the sense of enslavement to the will to receive... Therefore, we have to descend to Egypt and be there for four hundred years. Four hundred refers to a complete degree — it's the four phases of the will to receive."

The point is not a literal journey but a principle: a person cannot receive an immediate correction on a small desire, because even a desire doubled or grown tenfold is still nothing compared to the scale of spirituality — and a partial correction would simply leave a person content and unwilling to advance further. The vessel has to be built to its full magnitude first, and only then corrected. Rav Laitman notes that Baal HaSulam writes that the Israelites were actually in Egypt only two hundred and ten years, not four hundred — and that this shortfall, leaving the vessel incomplete, is what caused all the subsequent exiles.

## Part 3 — The Trap of Feeling Worse: Surrounding Light and the Widening Gap

As a person studies and begins to acquire a genuinely larger vessel, Rav Laitman warns, the felt experience is not progress but its opposite.

> "If we begin through the study to advance and acquire a greater vessel, we each time see ourselves as worse, coarser, more egoistic... Since when we study, we attract surrounding light — light that comes to us from our soul that already stands at the end of correction... we feel ourselves always towards that surrounding light. We don't feel it directly, we feel rather the gap between us and it — and if each time I feel worse and worse, then I feel farther and farther from the surrounding light."

Because the light shining on a person's current state is fixed at the end of correction, an enlarging (and therefore more keenly felt) ego reads, subjectively, as a widening gap rather than approach — and that feeling, left unexamined, "controls the person, and the person enters despair." The corrective isn't to stop feeling worse, but to understand what the feeling actually measures.

## Part 4 — Growing Without Limit: The Vessel Must Become In Order to Bestow

Rav Laitman then explains why an egoistic vessel is structurally incapable of receiving what the Creator wants to give.

> "The purpose of creation is not that you will be in order to bestow — the Creator doesn't need your altruism... but in the egoistic vessel you cannot receive much... because the egoistic vessel, as much as it receives, is blocked right away, and it doesn't want more. And if it doesn't want more, it can't grow more. So to make it that the Kli will grow each time without limitation... that's possible only with a vessel that each time grows. And growing is possible only by acquiring deficiencies from outside."

The mechanism he gives for acquiring those outside deficiencies is caring about others: "I think of the others... I acquire the vessels of others... my vessel is growing, and I can receive more, and bestow to him." Bestowal, in other words, isn't a moral add-on — it's the only structural way an unlimited vessel can keep expanding rather than saturating and stalling.

## Part 5 — Aiming True: Anchoring Every State to the End of Correction

Asked how to avoid wasting time on side-tracks and dead ends while enlarging one's deficiency, Rav Laitman gives a single organizing principle.

> "If wherever I am, whatever state I'm in, I'll imagine myself as though I'm in the final state — meaning that I have a deficiency, and I have a request that the deficiency will be great and corrected and filled with the Creator... and then I won't go wrong, I'll go in this exact direction and not toward some other goals or states different from the end of correction. And then we advance fast."

He extends the same logic to a separate question about Ein Sof (infinity): Malchut of Ein Sof "descends" into this world specifically to acquire independence from the Creator, and only from that independent position can she gradually build the intention to bestow and rise back to Ein Sof — now "as independent," not through self-annulment. "And that's the purpose of creation: to do good to His created beings, and you can't do this in any other way."

## Part 6 — Jew and Israel: Yearning Versus Attainment

A question about the meaning of "Jew" leads to a short etymological digression that Rav Laitman uses to distinguish two states of the same soul.

> "What is a Jew? A Jew is one who aspires to unite with the Creator. *Yehudi*, Jewish, comes from the word *Yehud* — unification... What's the difference between Jewish and Israel? Israel is already the attainment of that state. *Yashar-El* — straight towards God... Israel is Zeir Anpin in the state of *Gadlut*, greatness. During *Katnut*, smallness, Israel is called Yaakov — Jacob, small, still demanding corrections."

So "Jewish" names the yearning itself, and "Israel" names having reached the goal of that yearning — the same underlying soul-state, named according to whether it is still in smallness or has reached greatness. In the same exchange he adds that being called a "Kabbalist" works the same way: it names a person's aspiration and direction, not yet an attained revelation.

## Part 7 — Advancing in Darkness: Acting Like a Child

A student asks how to know which "direction" to aim internal desires in, given that there's no actual road or visible lane to follow. Rav Laitman's answer is to stop expecting clarity.

> "As you are now in the darkness, continue. Don't be any more wise than what's given to you from above. You need to adjust to the state you're in. And so, like a baby — he rises and falls, he gets up and falls, he tries to do something, he breaks something... The main thing is that you act. And that's why it's written, everything within your power to do, you should do... You need to act without the mind first, like children."

He calls this whole stretch — from below the barrier (Machsom) until it is reached — a period governed by concealment, where there is no external map, only internal desires to be aimed and combined. Waiting to become wise before acting, he says, guarantees a person never becomes wise at all.

## Part 8 — The Language of the Branches: Why Kabbalists Borrow Words From This World

Turning to how Kabbalistic books can be understood at all, Rav Laitman explains the origin of the terminology itself.

> "The Kabbalists took words from this world... what there is in this world — all the objects you see, still, vegetative, animal, and speaking — it all has a root in spirituality. We take a word here, the name of the thing, and we use this name to call the root, because the root has no name... they invented for us a language called the language of the branches."

A term like "the Holy Temple," read only as a building in Jerusalem, draws nothing; read as pointing to its actual spiritual root — a vessel in the degree of Bina — and yearned for as such, it draws the surrounding light connected to that root. This is why, he notes, people can study Torah "for thousands of years" and not draw light from it: they remain with the words (the *branch*, in this world) instead of reaching for what the words point to (the *root*, in spirituality).

## Part 9 — Naked Desires: Why Spirituality Needs No Words

Closing the lecture, Rav Laitman describes spiritual connection itself as something that has no need for language at all.

> "Spirituality is called naked desires... to be rid of the body means to throw off the ego that separates between us. If I think of myself, that's called egoism — I'm drawing to me, and you're drawing to you; by that we're distinguished from one another. If this intention falls, then we are one... 'Love your friend as yourself' means I join his desire to me, and his desire and my desire become one... Hence we need no words."

Words, in this account, exist only because of unresolved separation between people — "our deficiency, not... our development." Two Kabbalists in the same spiritual state, he says, don't need to speak; the closer two people actually come in bestowal, the less language is doing any of the real work between them.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. A desire for spirituality only enters human history in a serious way with Abraham; before him, "Adam" (from a word meaning to resemble the Upper One) names only a handful of individuals across a much longer history of desire limited to bare existence.
2. A vessel cannot be corrected until it is first built up to its full magnitude; Egypt — four hundred years, the four phases of the will to receive — models how a deficiency must be maximally enlarged before it can receive real, lasting correction and fulfillment.
3. Growth in this work paradoxically feels like regression: because surrounding light is anchored to the fixed end of correction, an enlarging ego is felt as a widening gap rather than approach, which can tip into despair if the mechanism isn't understood.
4. The vessel must become "in order to bestow" not because the Creator needs altruism, but structurally: only a bestowing vessel — one that keeps acquiring "deficiencies from outside" by caring about others — can keep growing without blocking itself.
5. To avoid wasted motion between states, a person should aim every present deficiency directly at the end state, imagining himself already united with the Creator, rather than pursuing intermediate or arbitrary goals.
6. "Jewish" (Yehudi, from Yehud/unification) names the yearning to unite with the Creator; "Israel" (Yashar-El) names having attained that state — the same soul-condition, named differently according to whether it is in Katnut (Yaakov) or Gadlut (Israel).
7. Kabbalists write using ordinary words from this world because every object here is the "branch" of a root in spirituality; real spiritual connection itself needs no words at all, since language exists only to bridge the separation created by ego.

| Machsom | "Barrier" — in this lesson, the boundary a person crosses only by acting without full understanding, "like a baby," through a period of concealment described as darkness with no visible road. |
| Yehudi | "Jewish," from the word *Yehud* (unification) — per this lesson, one who yearns to unite with the Creator, as distinguished from Israel (Yashar-El), who has already attained that unity. |

| Surrounding Light (Ohr Makif) | Light from a person's own soul already at the end of correction, shining on his current state; in this lesson, the mechanism by which a growing ego is felt as a widening — not narrowing — gap from spirituality. |
| EINSOF (Ein Sof) | "Infinity" — in this lesson, discussed through Malchut of Ein Sof's descent into this world to acquire independence, a necessary step before she can build the intention to bestow and rise back "as independent." |
| Kelim / Kli | Vessel(s) — the desires through which delight is received; the central subject of this lesson, whose defining problem is how to make it grow without limit rather than settle and stall. |
