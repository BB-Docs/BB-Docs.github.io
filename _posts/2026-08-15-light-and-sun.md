### Opening

The moderator frames this as a special, extra-long session — two hours, "many questions and many scrutinies" — because the topic, incorporation (Hitkalelut), is what the group's upcoming regional Congress will center on. Rather than one article, Rav Laitman works through a chain of short excerpts on the same theme, stopping after nearly every one to take live questions from Tens around the world. The throughline he keeps returning to: incorporation is not simply getting along or feeling connected — it is a specific, advanced correction in which each person actually takes on the friend's deficiency as his own, and it is the only thing that makes it possible to draw the "light that reforms" and reveal the Creator.

## Part 1 — What Incorporation Actually Is

Rav Laitman opens by distinguishing incorporation from the earlier, more basic stages of connection. After the shattering, desires are scattered and opposed to each other "according to the equivalence or non-equivalence of form." The work of correction starts simply with coming closer — sitting together, tolerating the discomfort of it — and only later moves toward actual contact.

> "Until we start touching each other... in this touch we can feel greater resistance, like the nuts in the bag."

True incorporation (Hitkalelut) goes further than closeness or even contact:

> "Hitkalelut is when we reach states in spirituality when we're included in the qualities that each one has, each one acquires it, and then ultimately each one has to incorporate with the whole world — until each one is completely incorporated in the others and everyone becomes one."

He is explicit that this is an advanced state, not a starting point — "on the one hand" a person is meant to reach for it as a distant, high correction; "on the other hand," precisely because "the final deed is in the preliminary thought," beginners have to start practicing it immediately, even while only managing to come a little closer to one another.

## Part 2 — Why Incorporation Is Necessary: 620 and the Shattering

Working from Baal HaSulam's letter on the soul's need to grow "620 times more" than its original root, Rav Laitman explains that this expansion — corresponding to the 613 Torah commandments plus the seven rabbinic ones — is only attainable through incorporation. He connects it directly to Adam HaRishon and the shattering: the sin of the Tree of Knowledge was adding the will to receive without a screen to work with it in order to bestow, which broke both the vessels of reception and, because they had already mingled together, the vessels of bestowal along with them.

> "That's the result of the shattering. That's the good thing about it, the nice thing about it — that now each and every detail has to be corrected. And by correcting each detail, he's correcting the incorporation in all the other details."

The practical consequence: no part of the will to receive can be corrected in isolation.

> "You can't even touch one grain of the will to receive, the smallest thing, unless you connect along with it... You're correcting the entire integration and the entire creation."

He ties this to the rule that everything in the wisdom of Kabbalah is learned "out of ten" — the creation is a single whole, and it only looks divisible into separate pieces because of the limits of human perception and language. "We don't have the language to talk about this wholeness."

## Part 3 — Realizing It in Practice: The Ten as an Artificial Cell

Asked directly how to realize incorporation practically, Rav Laitman locates the entire answer in the Ten. A Ten has to become, in his words, "the first healthy cell in the whole of humanity" — and from the incorporation reached inside it, the group reaches out to incorporate with the rest of humanity.

> "The ten is not a natural cell, it's an artificial cell. It's a place for work. That's why by creating connection in the ten we create the conditions for equivalence of form with the Creator."

He is careful to keep the scope narrow: when a participant asked about extending this work to "the ninety-nine percent" (the wider world), Rav Laitman redirected firmly — "we're only talking about our friends in the ten, and between the tens, and no more." What incorporation actually requires day to day, he says, is not grand action but ordinary sensitivity: caring about the friends, becoming attuned to their needs, working to develop connection and "come out of myself" rather than sitting alone and imagining that bestowal happens in isolation.

## Part 4 — From Mineral to Speaking: Incorporation Across All of Nature

A long Q&A traces incorporation as a general law of nature, not something unique to human beings. Rav Laitman describes still (mineral), vegetative, animate, and speaking levels as different degrees of the same phenomenon — parts connecting because of the light working within them.

> "The light keeps them together, nothing else... The existence of light in these elementary particles calls for a deficiency in them, in a higher form. They feel themselves connected by a certain force, and they are attracted to this force — to the fulfillment."

What changes across the levels is only the depth of the deficiency and the intensity of the connection the light requires. Humanity's own present shift, he says, is exactly this kind of transition — from the animate to the speaking level, "where it wants to resemble the Creator" — a process ultimately carried out by the light, which "shakes the vessels" until each part is willing to nullify itself for the sake of a new, larger whole.

## Part 5 — Touching, Friction, and Where the Real Work Happens

Several questioners press on what "touching" actually feels like. Rav Laitman answers bluntly: to touch another person means the friend genuinely starts disturbing you — "at least feel that you want to tell him, move away." Real incorporation, he says, cannot be reached without going through the friction this produces.

> "Specifically there, specifically there — when we start having negative emotions, there we have to connect, there we have to work, and not run away from it... Through the connection between us, we scrutinize below it all of the disturbances, the obstructions, the contradictions between us... By colliding with one another we have the coarseness — and in a connection above that, we have the hardness of the screen. This is how we build ourselves."

When a friend seems to react badly to your effort to influence him for the good, Rav Laitman reframes the whole encounter: it isn't really about the friend at all.

> "There's no friend here. What is here is a demonstration of your internality... I'm going to attach to myself parts of my soul. It's not nice words, it's truly this way."

Asked what to do once an example has been set and connection achieved, his answer is equally direct: "You don't need to keep anything. You just have to keep going forward as if nothing happened before. Never try to keep anything."

## Part 6 — Corporeal vs. Spiritual Incorporation, and Why It Must Be Mutual

Asked whether incorporation comes from the Ten or is one's own individual action, Rav Laitman draws a sharp line between corporeal and spiritual annulment. A mother annuls herself naturally toward her baby — that kind of incorporation can happen one-sidedly. Spiritual incorporation cannot.

> "Corporeal incorporation comes by me annulling towards the other... But the spiritual annulment can be only in a mutual manner. Therefore we have to nevertheless awaken the hearts of the friends towards connection."

This mutuality is why "he who is not incorporated in others is not Adam — he is still as a beast." A single desire, or even two, isn't enough to count as spirituality at all; only in the overlapping area created when two people each annul themselves toward the other does anything spiritual appear. Rav Laitman describes building "an additional partzuf" inside himself out of what he receives from the nine friends, since — apart from the point in the heart — a person has nothing else of his own to build a spiritual vessel from.

## Part 7 — Sorting Out the Vocabulary: Incorporation, Adhesion, Arvut, Love

Late in the lesson, Rav Laitman turns the group's own confusion about overlapping terms — incorporation, Arvut (mutual guarantee), Dvekut (adhesion), love, connection — into a direct question, and lets several Tens attempt an answer before adding his own.

> "I think what's unique in incorporating is that each one is trying to annul themselves and go into the other, into the togetherness... Incorporation is this exact stage — to go from the state where everyone is with himself, to us being together, building this common area between us through annulment and inclusion."

One participant proposes a sequence — incorporation, then mutual responsibility, then connection, then love of the Creator — which Rav Laitman does not contradict. The distinction that emerges: Arvut is a real, binding "being in" that mutual guarantee; incorporation is the active work of exchanging places, taking on the friend's desire instead of one's own; and only in that shared, un-locatable "common area" between two people — "that place you can't put your finger on, but it's between us" — is the Creator actually revealed.

## Part 8 — The Covenant (Brit): Making Incorporation Hold

Reading from Rabash's "Here you all stand today," Rav Laitman explains the Covenant as what keeps incorporation from dissolving once it's reached. A covenant is made specifically out of the recognition of weakness — not strength.

> "The covenant is what connects us, what allows us not to forget — not to forget the friends, not to forget the Creator who is between us, not to forget the goal... This is why you have friends around you to constantly remind you. And with them, you make the covenant."

> "The covenant comes from recognition of our weakness. Those who feel that they are strong don't need to make a covenant... but there will always be a stronger desire that will tempt us, and something will happen for sure. So we need a covenant to keep each other in the cooperation even after that strong desire comes."

## Part 9 — Closing: What the Congress Is For

In the final exchange, participants return again and again to the same point: none of this can be reached alone. "Nobody can do it on his own... the goal is to just be responsible that we'll all be in the same tendency, that we're together now before the Creator." Rav Laitman closes the lesson by naming what has been opened — the depth inside a single word, incorporation, that turns out to hold mutual responsibility, connection, Arvut, and the Covenant all at once — and points the group forward: this is the preparation for the coming regional Congress, where the group will try to bring this same incorporation to a much larger circle of friends.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Incorporation (Hitkalelut) is not mere closeness or agreement — it is the advanced state of actually taking on a friend's deficiency and working with it as one's own, and it is the mechanism by which the light that reforms can be drawn.
2. The soul's need to grow "620 times" beyond its root, and the correction of the shattering itself, cannot happen piecemeal — correcting any single part of the will to receive necessarily corrects, and requires, the whole.
3. The Ten is deliberately called "an artificial cell... a place for work" — the entire, deliberately narrow arena in which incorporation is meant to be practiced, not the wider world.
4. Incorporation is a general law of nature, appearing at every level (mineral, vegetative, animate, speaking) as different depths of the same connecting force of light; humanity's own present shift is from the animate to the speaking level.
5. Real incorporation requires touching, and touching produces friction and negative emotion — that friction is not a sign of failure but the exact place where the work (and the coarseness/screen distinction) happens.
6. Spiritual incorporation, unlike corporeal self-annulment (like a mother toward a baby), can only be mutual — one person's effort alone cannot create it.
7. Incorporation, Arvut (mutual guarantee), Dvekut (adhesion), and love are related but distinct stages/aspects of the same overall path toward connection, with the Creator specifically revealed in the shared "common area" incorporation creates.
8. The Covenant (Brit) is what keeps a reached state of incorporation from being lost — made not from strength but from the honest recognition that any one person will eventually be tempted away, and that only the friends can catch him.
