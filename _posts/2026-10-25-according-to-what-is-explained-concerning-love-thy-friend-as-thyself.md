### Opening

If the group is simply the tool through which I reach the Creator, why must I also *love* the friends? Wouldn't it be enough to use them in a businesslike way, being impressed by them and influencing them? Rav Laitman opens with the three stages every person passes through, self-love, love of others, and love of God, and shows that the group is the springboard for all of them. From there the lesson moves through being both small and great toward the society, why the Creator relates to me only through my love for the whole, how the "factory" of shared work must be kept free of emotion, and how the stages of love map onto the path from Lo Lishma to love of the Creator.

## Part 1 — Three Stages: Self-Love, Love of Others, Love of God

- This is one of the first articles Rabash wrote (1984), for the first group of young students Rav Laitman brought from Tel Aviv. The first articles were about the society and "love thy friend as thyself."
- Every person travels a path of three stages, from the beginning of creation to its end:

| Stage | Where it happens |
|---|---|
| **Self-love** | Our state in this world |
| **Love of others** | Acquired on emerging through the barrier (**Machsom**) into the spiritual world |
| **Love of God** | Acquired on passing above the **Parsa** |

- It is impossible to reach the purpose of creation, love of God, without going through all three.
- The names seem disparate: **end of correction**, **equivalence of form**, acquiring the upper **NRNHY**, love of the Creator. "We don't understand that it's the very same thing." It is all one station, called the end of correction.

## Part 2 — The Group as the Springboard

- The means to achieve this is the **group**: how much force a person receives from it to advance, and how much he uses that force toward it.
- "Only the small can receive from the great." The more a person sees himself with many zeros in relation to the society, the smallest in it, willing to be impressed by it, the more it suffices him as **fuel** to do everything.

#### The problem is not fuel, it's impression

- People are ready to do anything, even to die, for an ideal they consider great enough. "There is no problem with the fuel."
- "You can derive anything from impression, and the problem is how to be impressed." Like a little child who wants to be like the adults, all of our work is how much we are impressed by the society, how small we are in relation to it.

#### Both small and great

- Making myself small is not enough. I must also ask: what will the society provide me with? So I must also be **great** toward the society, influencing it with the right goal so that the goal is "at the helm."
- "A person needs to build the society and then to receive from the society that which he builds in it." This back-and-forth, mutual work is how he builds himself.
- He builds two points in himself, **Keter** and **Malchut**: how great he is toward the society and how small he is toward it. "That is his level."
- Once he has chosen to be under the society's influence, as Baal HaSulam writes in the article on free choice, "he has no more free choice"; that decision determines his future.

## Part 3 — Why Love, and Not Just Use?

- The question: why not treat the friends "very businesslike," as a means, a system for magnifying the good in me and orienting me toward the goal?
- "Love thy friend as thyself" is "the key with which I open the door to the Creator's hall."
- Picture it: I am in a group, the group stands before the Creator, and "the Creator loves me to the extent that I love the group when we are together, because He does not relate to individuals apart. He relates to everyone together, collectively."

> If I acquire love for everyone, they're all mine, parts of my body and soul, and by that I acquire the Creator's relation to creation, to Adam HaRishon.

- If I don't love the others, I feel the Creator's relation to creation in an opposite way, and this helps me recognize my state as evil and change it.
- As **Rabbi Elazar, son of Rabbi Shimon** says, "love thy friend as thyself" must extend to the entire world. The Creator is revealed to me to the extent that I am connected in love to His created beings, because everything follows **equivalence of form**, and His relation to creation is that of **the Good Who Does Good**.

#### Absolute laws

- These spiritual laws are "absolute and unchanging." There is no sudden change from the Creator due to cries, prayers or "protection." "I, **HaVaYaH**, did not change."
- Our requests and prayers prepare the vessel and change *us*; the Creator relates to us according to conditions set in advance. "It's completely deterministic."
- So stop looking for "patents" to circumvent the situation. As Baal HaSulam writes, nature "never gave up on a single gram of exertion."

## Part 4 — How to Annul Correctly

- **Q: How do I give the society greatness, and also receive the greatness of the goal?** Connection "cannot be one way, it has to be bidirectional," just as with two people who love each other.
- I bestow upon the society as much upliftment and sense of the goal as I can. But once that is absorbed and I have chosen this society, "I need to really lower my head and receive everything from the society."
- **Lowering my head** means bowing to the ideal in the society, its goal. Choosing the society is choosing my thoughts, desires and who I will be tomorrow: "Who are you? You are like your friends."
- **Q: When friends argue, how do we examine the goal?** "According to what our teachers write to us, and not according to what the friends or what you think." The society's goal is clear: to connect mutually and show each other love and the importance of the Creator, the goal, and the friends. "A person is where his thoughts are."
- **Q: And in practical matters, like organizing a lecture?** Whatever the friends tell me, I try to listen. Making myself small "deliberately, by force, through exertion," their best thoughts flow into me "like from a high place to a lower place." If they say go clean or work in the kitchen, I do it. "What am I here for, actually? It's only for that."

#### The danger of standing out

- The more opportunities a person has to be inferior in the society, the better. A position where he stands out can be "a great curse," spoiling him so he can no longer be small and receive the goal; "then he'll be just some manager, like in a factory."
- Only those who are truly corrected should be managers, as with the rule of inviting the greatest people to perform a necessary violation of Shabbat, "because they have screens."
- **Q: What if the friends look down on me for annulling?** "That might be a good thing. It gives me the ability to truly submit myself and not think that I'm just acting."
- **Q: What if I receive too much respect?** "That fear itself already protects the person." And "even when I give someone honor, I try to spoil it immediately."

#### Small and great at the same time

- **Q: How can I hold my own opinion in my role and still be small?** Toward everyone I am great in wanting to give them inspiration from the goal, and small in wanting to receive it from them. "In bestowal, I'm greater than the society as much as possible, and in reception, I'm the smallest in the society, with no connection to the factory."

## Part 5 — From Easy to Difficult

- **Q: Love toward the whole society, or toward one friend?** Some people are easier to feel and connect with; others are "some kind of blank spot." You don't need to break yourself to penetrate them.
- Choose 10 or 15 people with whom you share a common language and build connection with them, "from easy to difficult. We learn that all corrections take place this way."
- But never discard the others: "the goal's the entire world." Limiting yourself turns it into "corporeal love, according to what is convenient for me."
- Practicing with those close to me is only training. "Where I truly come out of myself, it's with the people who I don't feel so much."
- Shouting "he's my friend" about one person is fine for a week or two; after that, "it indicates that you're lagging." Love of others means "someone other than me, outside of me": going outside my nature.

## Part 6 — The Factory and the Friends

- **Q: Love of friends clothes onto the factory; if someone doesn't do his job...** "The factory has nothing to do with love of friends, don't confuse the two."
- The **factory** means the business relationships between the friends in the shared work of dissemination. There they are workers, not friends.
- Emotional connection belongs above this framework, "as though disconnected from it." "Start bringing all kinds of feelings into it, and everything will collapse." In corporeal actions "we bring no emotion, but only intellect. Emotion is aimed at the goal, at the Creator."
- The framework is a **still level of holiness** (Domem de Kedusha). It doesn't give great acceleration, but it keeps a person's life oriented toward spirituality.
- A religious society survives for thousands of years precisely because "nothing changes" and observance is without emotion: "The Lord commanded, and I do it." "So we need the same thing. First of all, a framework."
- **Q: Should I justify a friend inwardly while acting correctly outwardly?** Only if you are certain it won't disrupt the framework. Suddenly forgiving a friend's mistake at work "because he's weak today" damages the business relationship, and "if you're damaging the framework, tomorrow you'll have no relationship with the Creator."
- Only to the extent that real love of others is attained will there be no difference between business and emotional relationships. "You are not yet corrected enough."

## Part 7 — From Love of Others to Love of the Creator

- **Q: How do we move from love of others to love of God?** At the barrier we begin to build love of others: **bestowing in order to bestow**, the correction of creation, while I still don't feel the Creator within me.
- Feeling the Creator comes only in **receiving in order to bestow**. The **Ta'amim** (flavors), the inner light filling the vessel, are the measure of adhesion and of the mutual love between us.
- Bestowing in order to bestow gives only the measure of **Hesed**, emerging from self-love to **Bina**, **Chafetz Chesed**, which is "still something relatively cold."

| Stage | Schematic | Path |
|---|---|---|
| Self-love | **Malchut**, receiving for myself | Double and single concealment, **Lo Lishma** |
| Love of others | **Bina**, bestowing in order to bestow | Crossing the barrier into **Lishma**; reward and punishment, intentional transgressions become unintentional |
| Love of God | **Keter**, receiving in order to bestow | Eternal love; all transgressions become merits |

- Merits means all the transgressions become corrected vessels, "vessels in which the mutual love between us and the Creator becomes revealed."
- **Q: What happens to love of others at the degree of love of God?** All the previous degrees rise to that degree and connect. As Rabbi Elazar says, love of the world: "it is in the collective vessel of the whole world that you attain eternal love." You acquire the whole vessel of **Adam HaRishon**.

## Part 8 — Suffering, War, and Uniting

- **Q: We are all traveling toward the Creator; why don't we unite in such a difficult time?** When people suffer, they are ready to connect: a large vessel can change the state, the prayer is stronger, and suffering brings faster **recognition of evil**.
- "I feel suffering in my ego, so it's easy for me to concede my ego." But when the troubles pass, everyone withdraws into himself again. The only correction is for the Creator to reveal Himself "so that we will look at Him and not within ourselves," as in the exodus from Egypt.
- **Q: So should we wait?** "If we wait, that's the path of suffering... Instead, we need to act."
- Every state will be different; that is how the world advances toward the end of correction. "Why do I need to be in sorrows in order to begin connecting with others?"
- Baal HaSulam writes there could be a third and fourth world war, leaving only a small handful who would carry out the general correction for all the souls.
- **Q: Sometimes bad times push us apart, sometimes together?** It depends where the good or evil is clothed. If things are bad outside, I run to the society; if the good is clothed in the society, I run toward it.

## Part 9 — Short Answers

- **Why "light"?** It resembles light in our world, a flash of insight, something good, "but there is no connection between our light and spiritual light."
- **Must I keep rebuilding Keter and Malchut toward the group?** "That's correct. A person needs to do this all the time."
- **Is the exchange of desires like connected water tanks?** Yes, on every level. Physical influence falls with the square of the distance; in spirituality, in desires and equivalence of form, the influence of remoteness is far greater.
- **Does receiving from the group increase my ego?** "Did anyone say it's bad if my ego is bigger?" The hardness of desire toward the right goal is what opens the barrier. "Let me receive from the society a very powerful desire toward the Creator... In this, let my ego grow without any boundaries."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Three stages, one goal.** Self-love, love of others (at the barrier), and love of God (above the Parsa) are the path to the single station called the end of correction.
2. **Impression is everything.** There is no shortage of fuel; the work is how much I am impressed by the society, which depends on how small I make myself.
3. **Small and great at once.** Great in giving the society the goal, small in receiving it back: Keter and Malchut, the measure of my level.
4. **Love is the key to the Creator.** He relates to the collective, so He loves me to the extent that I love the group, and ultimately the whole world.
5. **The laws are absolute.** The Creator doesn't change; prayer changes us. Nature never gives up a single gram of exertion.
6. **Keep the factory free of emotion.** Shared work is a stable framework, a still level of holiness; love of friends is built above it until we are corrected enough to join them.
7. **Act, don't wait for suffering.** Suffering unites temporarily; deliberate connection, from easy to difficult, is the good path.
