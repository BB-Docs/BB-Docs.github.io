### Opening

Why is it forbidden to hear a good thing from a bad person? Because the evil inclination often comes in a beautiful garment while its inside is evil, and the garment pulls a person off the path. Rav Laitman uses this to explain the whole period of preparation: why we can't tell true from false while we are still ruled by bitter and sweet, why each of us must fall again and again in a particular area until we hate it, and why the society, through its examples, is the only thing strong enough to change us. Along the way he defines real exertion, the "two hundred kilos" we can't lift, which only the Creator can move.

## Part 1 — The Garment and the Inside

- With every desire and thought that comes to a person there is "an obstacle in the beginning and an obstacle at the end."
- If the evil inclination's garment and content are identical, a person can see from the start that it seduces and weakens him. But if the garment is sweet and only the inside is evil, "that thing will become apparent at the end." The inside "is always evil if it's the evil inclination."
- **Ishmael** is the example: his outer garment comes from the father, Abraham, while the evil inclination inside him comes from the mother. They can be together "so long as the evil inclination is not yet developed." Then **Sarah**, the good inclination, the holy **Nukva** opposite the impure Nukva, says they must separate.

#### Bitter and sweet versus true and false

- A person who hasn't mastered the scrutiny of **true and false** is still governed by **bitter and sweet**. When the sweet comes, "he becomes addicted to it," following good-sounding advice that is ultimately false.
- "We have no control over it." We can withstand it only above reason, or with the help of the society. "He can't really rely on himself." So the path is full of failures and obstacles until we gradually acquire that scrutiny.
- This holds "almost up to the entrance into Kedusha." After that, "a wise disciple needs to be like iron": bitter and sweet no longer affect him.
- "A thousand times will the righteous fall and rise." He must fall with each discernment, see how evil it is and how powerless he is, rise, correct it, and continue, until the clear discernments gather into "a shout, a prayer, a complete desire to rise above his nature."

## Part 2 — Falling Until You Hate It

- **Q: What is the discernment of true and false in the period of preparation?** It comes only from "everything that a person went through, and not what's ahead of him." For what's ahead, "Don't believe in yourself until the day you die." In a second you can be confused again and run "like a little child... after some doll or a bubble."
- The experience is reinforced from fall to fall until a person gathers enough impressions according to the root of his soul. "Then it's over."
- The purpose is to have **records** for later, in holiness, using all these faults to justify the Creator and turn iniquities into merits.

#### Each one's particular obstacle

- Each one has a set number of obstacles according to the root of his soul, and they come "in a special way specific to him." One is made to fail with lust, another with money, another with pride and control. Usually it's two or three in some combination.
- He is given it again and again and falls again and again, until he learns from experience that this quality is evil, begins to fear it, and truly hates it, "because hatred is distance in spirituality."
- This can take ten, twelve, fifteen years, depending on the pace. "But he must go through that."
- In the society you go through "the same obstacles, but with different clothing."

#### Why experience seems to stop working

- **Q: What makes a person reject an experience he went through?** "A person can never reject any experience." It's simply that "a stronger record comes." I was caught stealing ten shekels and felt the bitter punishment, "but now I have a million shekels in front of me," and the pleasure quenches the memory.
- There's always a justification: I'll steal so I won't have to work and can devote all my time to Kabbalah; I'll make a million and then go to spirituality; I'll pay the tithe. "It can be painted in such a way... I need it on the path. Without that I won't succeed."
- **You learn only through failure,** all the way through: with money, "he needs to actually go bankrupt," or make a profit and see it's of no benefit. Then the corporeal troubles are replaced for him with spiritual ones, and he begins to be sorry for his spirituality.

#### Only those who invest

- "These things happen only to those who invest a lot of power, a lot of energy." Go at it half in earnest and you get small problems, "a little bit of work, a little bit of money, a little bit of illness, like a regular family."
- Even those aren't random: they collect negative impressions so a person will finally ask about the purpose of life.
- So "it's impossible to just see and judge with your eyes to tell who is more advanced."

## Part 3 — Incorporation in the Society

- **Q: How does one devoted to the society go through these obstacles?** Being incorporated with the friends, "he already has a different possibility to arrange the records."
- To the extent I invest in the others, "I subconsciously absorb" their impressions. "A person doesn't feel I contributed twenty kilos effort to him, I received from him impressions of a thousand different cases," but that is how it passes.
- **Q: If no one in the group falls, where do we learn from?** Then we learn from "the torments of love": "how much I don't love the other, how much I fail with the other." The failures move to a spiritual level, the level of our relationships.

#### One friend or many?

- "We're told that in fact one is enough. Two is already a group." But that isn't realistic; with one, a person will confuse himself.
- In one example, five people could invest in one another, but at fifteen they lost the connection, "a sign that there was no connection."
- The test is whether the group stands before you as one body, "as one man with one heart." Then it doesn't matter if it's two or twenty. Otherwise, work with a limited number, or begin with a larger number of people who are ready for it.

#### Work and results

- **Q: Is working in the kitchen enough, or do I need friends physically beside me?** What matters is the feeling that "I'm doing something for the whole group." There are many kinds of work, general and individual.
- Some invest great strength and heaven doesn't let them see a result. One friend prepared to sell about a hundred sets of TES and sold one or two. "But all that doesn't go to waste... The result is never in a person's hands."

## Part 4 — Real Exertion: The Two Hundred Kilos

- Rabash proposes a test: the true path is where there is more exertion, since the evil inclination always pulls you toward what's convenient. **Q: But without a true guide, how do I know?**
- Going toward more effort means "you don't see any payment for it," and then you need above reason.
- Lifting twenty kilos, even if it nearly kills me, "is just called work." **Exertion** in spirituality is when, after all my efforts and calculations, something weighing two hundred kilos lies on my path and "I'm not capable."
- It must be important to me "like life and death," like a terminally ill person who must have the medicine. Then it's revealed that only the upper force can help, and I turn to Him with all that necessity.
- How He does it, whether He lets me lift it, dresses in me, or does it while I watch, "doesn't matter." Reaching the point where the Creator does it "means that I exerted above reason." What is demanded is "this final scrutiny that I'm incapable of doing it, and that I demand of the Creator."
- Not every two hundred kilos, "only those two hundred kilos that are standing in the way." Unlike the gym, where lifting is the goal, here "my goal is to pass through... and removing the obstacle is a means."

## Part 5 — Friends as Examples, Not Hidden Righteous

- **Q: If external results don't reflect effort, why do we choose role models by externals?** "The eyes see," and we can't do it otherwise. "This is what the Rabash writes."
- "I don't need hidden righteous here to sit on the side someplace." I need friends who help me "as an example so that I myself will improve."
- A friend who studies diligently at home helps the group, "but he can't be an example for me." He needs to show his love of friends, so I'll want to do it too. If we all become hidden righteous, each one will only justify whatever the other does.
- "On the contrary, I want each one to show me even more than what he perhaps is doing."

#### The power of the environment

- Rav Laitman recalls entering a place where smoking was forbidden. He felt such contempt and hatred for the habit from the people there that "the body didn't demand it," although medically it still needed it. "The hatred for it that I absorbed from them suppressed all my craving."
- "Society can do to you whatever it wants." The difference is which society I let myself be impressed by.
- **Q: What if they're only acting?** "Why should I care if they're acting?" My body accepts the act as true. Better to pretend to be in **Atzilut** than to claim to be a hidden righteous who does nothing.
- If they show love of friends, even externally, I'll be impressed and will want to do things I never dreamed I could, with joy and without calculation. "It won't cost me any effort to do such good deeds."
- The example must be conspicuous and respected. A newcomer can be kept, but someone who dismisses such examples "should simply be thrown out."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Beware the sweet garment.** The evil inclination's inside is always evil; when its clothing is pleasant, the evil shows only at the end.
2. **Bitter and sweet rule us until near Kedusha.** Only above reason and with the society can we withstand them, until we become "like iron."
3. **Each soul has its own obstacles.** Lust, money or pride come again and again until we fall fully, hate the quality, and gather records to justify the Creator later.
4. **A stronger pleasure isn't stopped by old experience.** Every fall comes with a justification; we learn only through failure that goes all the way.
5. **Incorporation multiplies impressions.** Investing in friends brings their experience into us; in a good group, we learn from the torments of love.
6. **Exertion is the weight you can't lift.** Real effort is reaching the necessity that only the Creator can move the obstacle on the path.
7. **Be a visible example.** The group needs friends who show love of friends, even by acting, not hidden righteous.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Garment and internality** | The outer form of a thought or advice and its true content; with the evil inclination the inside is always evil. |
| **Bitter and sweet / True and false** | The early scrutiny by pleasure, which misleads, and the later scrutiny by truth, acquired gradually. |
| **Ishmael and Sarah** | Ishmael's good garment from Abraham hides an evil inside; Sarah, the holy Nukva, demands separation. |
| **Wise disciple like iron** | One for whom bitter and sweet no longer have any effect. |
| **Records (Reshimot)** | Impressions from each fall, needed later to justify the Creator and turn sins into merits. |
| **Root of the soul** | Sets each person's particular obstacles and how many falls he needs. |
| **Torments of love** | Learning from how much I fail to love the friend, instead of from corporeal blows. |
| **Exertion (Yegia)** | Effort that reaches the necessity of the Creator's help to move an obstacle beyond one's strength. |
| **Two hundred kilos** | Rav Laitman's image of an obstacle on the path that a person cannot move himself. |
| **Hidden righteous** | A friend who keeps his efforts private and therefore gives others no example. |
| **Environment (Sviva)** | The society whose examples can suppress cravings and give the desire for good deeds. |
