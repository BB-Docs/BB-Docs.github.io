### Opening

Every disturbance in the work boils down to two questions from the body: "What is this work for you?" and "Who is the Lord that I should obey His voice?" Together, **Mi** and **Ma** ("Who" and "What") make **Mayim**, the waters of the flood that kill a person's spiritual life. Rav Laitman asks how a person survives this flood: not by answering the questions, but by entering the "ark" of **Bina**, faith above reason, building it first as a foundation, and only afterwards returning to the disturbances to correct them. Along the way he explains how to relate to the friends, to society, to one's parents, and why "the light is what corrects."

## Part 1 — The Ark: Building the Force of Bina First

- The articles of the portion of Noah, as explained by the Zohar, Baal HaSulam and Rabash, all speak about the **ark**: the power of **Bina**, of **faith**, which a person acquires at the start of his spiritual development, "without which he cannot advance."
- It is "like the fundamental pillar" he must build, around which he can then scrutinize and correct all the other discernments.
- This force must be built inside oneself and around oneself, in the group and in all of humanity. It is the force called **Arvut** (mutual guarantee).
- It is acquired by working with the disturbances, the questions of "who" and "what." The force of faith saves him: he moves away from the disturbances of the will to receive and "cleaves only to the force of faith, faith above reason."

#### Two stages of the work

| Stage | What a person does |
|---|---|
| **Foundation (Bina, GE)** | Rides above the disturbances without entering into them; justifies the Creator; acquires the vessels of bestowal, **Galgalta veEinayim**. "A righteous shall live by his faith"; "the righteous is the foundation (**Yesod**) of the world." |
| **AHP of ascent** | Only on top of that foundation does he begin to take the vessels of reception, the disturbances themselves, and find in them the ability to use them in order to bestow. "This is already a different kind of work." |

- At the beginning "not to take it into consideration, not to go into the scrutinies and disturbances themselves," but to see that the group and the world are corrected and that the disturbances come from the Creator so that I will justify Him.

> As if I don't drown in the waters of the flood, but to the contrary, I want to be saved. I lock myself, close myself.

#### Toward the broad group

- The same approach applies not only to our small group but to the whole broader group: whatever their preparation or depth of understanding, "I must accept them as partners on the path and not criticize them," and relate to everyone equally.
- If we look at beginners through our uncorrected view, we fall into "the waters of strife," which lead to nothing beneficial. Built correctly, we become a pillar to which all who aspire to the Creator will come and cleave.

## Part 2 — The Two Questions: "What" and "Who"

- There are only two questions, and all disturbances are included in them. They correspond to the **double concealment** a person is in.
- **Pharaoh** is "the generality of our will to receive," and he asks only these two things.

#### "What is this work for you?"

- Against the will to receive "you have nothing to answer... You're right." In this world the will to receive rules by reason, power and the laws of nature: "Show me that it's worthwhile. I'll join, no problem."
- Rav Laitman recalls starting at about twenty-nine or thirty with a successful business, a wife and children and a penthouse, and closing everything to go "to some old man" in **Bnei Brak**. His mother asked, "What do you need it for?" When he put on a kippah, people asked whether someone had died. "I had nothing to say to my mom, really."
- The answer exists only for the **speaking** part in a person, which "is not evident to the eye" and demands a different environment to live in: a spiritual world. This is why "the view of Torah is opposite to the view of landlords" (**Baalei Batim**).
- Toward this question inside, "you have to blunt his teeth": "You live like a beast, you'll receive like a beast."

#### The healthy beast

- A beast never eats more than it needs. If we gave the still, vegetative and animate in us only what is necessary, it would not suffer; "you're not robbing it."
- The excess comes when a person pours his human desires (money, honor, knowledge) into his beastly life and wants to swallow the world. "That's where all the destruction in the world comes from."
- If people kept a simple, secure animal life and directed money, honor and knowledge toward spirituality, "everybody today would have bread and everything they need."
- Science increasingly shows that the approach of filling the will to receive "doesn't satisfy us," which makes it "a lot easier to hit the ego in the teeth."

#### "Who is the Lord that I should obey Him?"

- This is "already about the essence, the driver, the purpose," a more complex question. Why am I drawn to spirituality: curiosity, fascination, reward in this world and the next? Or adhesion to the Creator, being His servant, "where He is the one who obligates"?
- The two questions are clarified gradually and "go hand in hand." As long as a person is between the **Klipa** and **Kedusha**, both remain: one against the will to receive, one against the Creator.

## Part 3 — Disturbances Point to Where Bina Is Missing

- Correct appeal to the Creator means not setting conditions, "give me Your greatness and then I'll do something," and not determining the form of His help.
- It means relating to every disturbance as beneficial: it shows "little places" in my will to receive that I have not yet covered with faith.
- If I feel something is not good, it came from the Creator. So in that place I am saying He is not righteous, which means "in this place I'm not righteous... I'm still lacking faith, the correction of **Chafetz Chesed**."

#### The complete righteous and the penitent

- A **complete righteous** is one whose entire will to receive has been revealed and over all of it he has the covering of Bina: he has a large **AHP**, restricts it, and uses only **Galgalta veEinayim**, "like the state of Adam HaRishon before the sin."
- Then begins the work of the **penitent** (Baal Teshuva): taking the AHP and correcting it in order to bestow, bringing it back gradually. "Where the penitents stand, the complete righteous cannot stand."
- Until I see the world as "there is none else besides Him... the good that does good," I have not corrected my faith.
- As in item 17 of the *Introduction to the Study of the Ten Sefirot*, a person must commit to strengthen his faith, seeing the Creator managing the world in the best way and attributing what seems bad to "my corrupted vision."

## Part 4 — Living by Faith: The Force of the Society

- The strength comes especially during study: being inspired together so that it "stains you" for longer and longer, and you don't forget it in regular life.
- A large society creates a force that makes you demand this fulfillment, until "we won't be able to exist" without feeling our belonging to the yearning for spirituality, like a hungry or ill person.
- "That's called the righteous lives in his faith. He lives in it, otherwise he doesn't feel he's alive. He feels like he's choking."
- **Q: Isn't this an escape from the bad?** No. We understand that the evil is beneficial; we don't run from it but "build a different relation... above it." "If you step on it, we rise up." The world, by contrast, dwells in the evil and repeats the same failures from generation to generation.
- Restriction of the will to receive happens naturally: if I yearn for spirituality, I use everything else only as necessary. But for dissemination and correction, "we're gonna have to use everything": billions, the best technology, thousands of workers.

## Part 5 — Q&A: An Uncorrected Society, Parents, and Seeing Correctly

#### How can an uncorrected society direct me?

- "An uncorrected society gives you a direction because it's not corrected." If it seemed corrected you could not see your own corruption; "you grow through the shells. They feed you, they nourish you."
- "If I'm righteous, they're not. If they're righteous, I'm not. One of the two. So you decide what your approach is."
- **Q: So society can direct me more correctly than the Rav?** "For sure, no doubt." The Rav stands aside "like a kindergarten teacher... play nicely, kids." The rest you learn from playing with one another.

#### Kabbalah or my parents?

- "Honor your father and mother" is a commandment, but everything comes from the question, "What is the meaning of my life?" If one can silence it and live by his relatives' desires, let him. If he has outgrown it, that is a previous degree and he should move on.
- Ninety percent of what we do our parents don't agree with: whom we marry, our profession, where we live. "That's the problem of fathers and sons."
- **Respect and support them on the beastly level.** "They gave you life as a beast." On the human level every generation adds to the previous one. "The parents are our previous degree."
- His mother, now in her mid-eighties, still says, "If you lived in Rehovot... you'd be healthy and calm. Why do you need all this?"

#### How to change my natural view

- By nature "a person always feels himself as righteous" and ascribes what is wrong to the outside, and ultimately blames the Creator.
- To read the picture correctly, "outside of me everything is perfect and the end of correction, and all the corruption is in me," only society can remind me, as an idea "burning in our air," so each recalls it several times a day.
- This applies to the friends who walk with me in the same direction, not yet to the rest of the world. "Later on, we'll have the same approach to them too."
- Trying this, I begin to feel that this is how the Creator relates to us, and "I'm already living on a different level of connection."

## Part 6 — The Light Corrects; Faith Above Reason

- The result of this attitude is not "devouring myself" but always searching how to be in an uplifted state. Then I resemble the surrounding light and it works on me; dwelling in "I'm horrible" moves me away from it.
- "I don't have good or bad desires." The difference between good and bad deeds is the **intention**: what I adhere to.

> Do the maximum you can to come close to the light and it will do the job.

- **What does the light correct?** "It gives you a feeling of importance," the importance of bestowal over your vessels, which lets you ride above them. Building GE above the AHP is not stacking blocks: "The values you have toward the Galgalta veEinayim are greater than what you have toward the AHP."

#### Above reason vs. below reason

| | Faith below reason (the masses) | Faith above reason (Kabbalah) |
|---|---|---|
| **The will to receive** | Erased; not allowed to ask or look at disturbances | Grows constantly and is fully seen |
| **The method** | Shut the eyes, hold to a dogma, "scream something" | "I see all my flaws... but along with that, I adhere to the Creator" |
| **The result** | Respect for bestowal, as in every religion | GE built over a restricted AHP, held in awareness |

- "Faith doesn't mean that you're going with your eyes shut... Faith is a force that rides upon all the will to receive when you take it into account."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Mi + Ma = Mayim.** The questions "Who" and "What" are the floodwaters that take a person's spiritual life; all disturbances are included in them.
2. **First the ark, then the AHP.** Build the foundation of Bina, faith above reason, without entering the disturbances; only then return to correct the vessels of reception (AHP of ascent).
3. **No conditions on the Creator.** Don't ask for greatness first; treat every disturbance as showing where faith and Chafetz Chesed are still missing, and ask only for strength.
4. **Outside of me everything is corrected.** The friends' apparent flaws are my uncorrected qualities; only the society can keep reminding me of this.
5. **Give the beast what it needs.** Keep a simple animal life, honor parents on that level, and direct the human desires toward spirituality.
6. **The light corrects, not self-criticism.** Resemble the surrounding light by adhering to the good; it brings the importance of bestowal.
7. **Faith above reason rides on the will to receive.** Unlike faith below reason, it sees every flaw and still adheres to bestowal.
