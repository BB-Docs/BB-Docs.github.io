### Opening

Continuing directly from the morning session, Rav Laitman frames the whole of Elul as a single, sustained act of awakening from below: coming "as one man with one heart" to ask the Creator for the quality of bestowal, without waiting for any external occasion — not the upcoming Regional Congress, not anything else — to begin. The session moves between two short Baal HaSulam excerpts on self-annulment and on evoking the Creator's own desire, and a long, wide-ranging Q&A that keeps returning to one practical question: since a person cannot reach the Creator directly, what actually has to happen between people for that connection to become real?

## Part 1 — Awakening From Below, All Through Elul

Rav Laitman opens by naming the arc that the whole month is meant to trace:

> "We want to awaken from below, we want to come as one man with one heart to the Creator, to the quality of bestowal, and say we want to be like You. We demand it as one man with one heart, just like at the foot of Mount Sinai... during the lesson we should try not to wait for the convention, not to wait for anything. Right now we're beginning to awaken the common prayer from below."

Asked in effect what this month is actually for, he ties it directly to the acronym behind its name: "I to my beloved and my beloved to me... is our work, our disposition to discover such an attitude towards the Creator that in our attitude we will discover the Creator's relation to us... in particular we have to develop during this time, during this month, in the approaching Rosh Hashanah, that we check the relations between us and our relations with the Creator, and how much we are correctly aimed towards connection, towards adhesion."

## Part 2 — Annulling the Will to Receive Before the Will to Bestow

The group reads a short Baal HaSulam excerpt on annulling one's will before the Creator's, glossed as the meaning of dvekut. Rav Laitman then unpacks what this actually involves in practice:

> "With the desire itself, you can do anything — you can only make changes over the intention, how you use the desire, but not the desire itself. So over your whole desire, you make a restriction so that you don't use it for yourself, but rather you want to use your desire as much as possible for the benefit of the Creator... And then, according to the degree in which you are in equivalence of form with Him, the feeling that is in the Creator, the upper light, passes on to you and you are filled with it. How much feeling will you receive? That determines your spiritual degree."

Asked how to stay happy through the restriction itself rather than experience it as loss, he answers: "It depends what you're going towards. If I restrict my will to receive... and I begin to open up my vessels in a different way in bestowal — meaning in connection with the upper force, with the vessels of the Creator — by that I am in joy, and I'm advancing. As much as I am able to connect my will to receive and the intentions to bestow, this is how I advance."

## Part 3 — What "I to My Beloved" Actually Asks a Person to Track

Pressed on the phrase itself, Rav Laitman relocates the whole question away from the person's own enjoyment:

> "The important thing is: what will the Creator receive from me? How does He enjoy from me? In what form? So in that same form that He enjoys maximally, I'm willing to stay... I no longer think about how much do I enjoy, but rather how much, in my state, I'm delighting the Creator. This is called I to my beloved. And then, to the extent that I disconnect from myself and think about how much I'm directed towards the Creator, to that extent the Creator can already bestow to me."

Asked why the excerpts speak of a "beloved" rather than a master, king, or teacher, he traces the word to its source: "This comes from the Song of Songs... it indicates a specific connection" — a different, more intimate register than the language of authority used elsewhere.

## Part 4 — One Work in Two Parts: The Ten and the Creator

Asked whether working within the group of friends and working directly toward the Creator are really two separate tracks, Rav Laitman says they only look that way at first:

> "Only in the beginning it appears, it seems to us that these are two different things. Later on it seems like these two are one after the other — first we have to connect with friends, be told by them, and then reach the feeling of the Creator, and bestow to Him through the friends. And when we get closer and closer to it, it turns out that it truly becomes one work, even though it includes two parts."

This is developed further later in the session, where he describes how connection with friends builds the actual capacity to perceive anything beyond oneself: "As much as he connects with the friends, he by that develops vessels actually for the revelation of this alien force... This rule, from the love of people to the love of the Creator, is a formula of physics — a scientific formula. It's impossible in any other way to develop vessels for the perception of a force which is higher than us."

## Part 5 — Evoking a Desire From Above (Shamati 57)

The group reads Baal HaSulam's Shamati excerpt on the Creator's own need to be "awakened": *"We must evoke a desire from above to impart below. It is not enough that we have a desire, but there has to be a goodwill on the part of the giver too... he still waits for our desire to awaken his desire. In other words, if we are unable to evoke his desire, it is a sign that the desire on the part of the receiver is still incomplete."* Asked plainly what this means in practice, Rav Laitman answers:

> "The Creator has a desire to bestow to the created beings 100%. If you want to have a special relation to you, that the Creator will treat you with a special treatment, correcting you, bringing you closer, transforming you to a corrected vessel — then you have to awaken his desire... The Creator likes when you love the friends, when you help them, when you come closer to them, when you disseminate the knowledge of Him in the world and influence the world to move towards connection, adhesion. If you do that, then you start getting His attention. And then you ask for correction."

## Part 6 — Seeing the Friend as Great: the "Two Floors"

In one of the session's longer exchanges, a participant objects that she cannot picture the Creator as a "friend" — He is too high above her. Rav Laitman's answer builds directly on Part 4's theme, that the friends are the only available ladder:

> "It's very comfortable to distinguish between 'I'm with the Creator'... That's exactly our work. That's the building with the two floors. Until we reach the first floor — to see the friends as great, the way it seems to you the Creator is — there's no revelation of the Creator. There isn't. The Creator, we can't bring down to ourselves. Nothing will help. He won't come down to us. He's above. We want to reach Him? We have to make the friends greater."

He adds, from earlier in the same lesson, that this is the literal meaning of the session's opening claim: "As much as I am for my beloved, I'll discover that my beloved is for me... meaning we're just waiting for our work towards the friends. We work correctly towards the friends, the Creator will be revealed."

## Part 7 — The One Deficiency Nature Never Gives Us

Asked what specific "actions" are to the Creator's liking, Rav Laitman narrows it to a single, deliberately unnatural desire:

> "We don't have such a deficiency. Naturally, such a deficiency doesn't exist in nature. Every other deficiency but this exists — we want food, sex, family, money, honor, knowledge, everything we want, besides wanting to bestow like Him. That we need to create in our laboratory, called the Ten, in our environment, in our general society... so that we will perform actions like our Rav, like Rabash, like Baal HaSulam, who know how to ask for this desire."

This connects to a separate answer, later in the session, on how a person even recognizes that this deficiency has arrived: "We say about the will to receive that the wicked receive 100, 1,200, until their dying day — meaning until the will to receive dies. When does the will to receive die? Precisely when a person discovers that he is the wicked one... and then he says, I want to put this mode of work to death. This is the right deficiency. When he has that deficiency, he receives a desire to bestow."

## Part 8 — Why the Environment Is the Only Way In

Asked whether a person can build this desire to bestow alone, through private effort, Rav Laitman is direct: "A person can[not] solve this on his own. The only thing a person can do is to enter himself into an environment that needs to constantly... give him desires that he doesn't have naturally, and he even resists." He returns once more to the "making the friends greater" principle from Part 6, this time framed as the actual mechanism of influence:

> "If I don't see the friend as great, he won't bestow upon me. He can talk endlessly, it won't impact me. I diminish him — the moment I make him smaller, I don't receive a thing from him, not an impression, not greatness. It all depends on my way of relating to him... The greater I make them, the more importance and greatness I'll receive from them."

## Part 9 — Closing: Telling Real Awakening From an Enlarged Ego

A participant, describing an unwillingness to fall back into how he felt before this teaching struck him, asks what protects a person from mistaking a growing ego for real spiritual feeling. Rav Laitman does not promise that the will to receive will stop growing — only that it must be matched:

> "The will to receive will grow for sure. It is not that it's wrong for the will to receive to grow. The question is, if we correspondingly increase the point in the heart and develop it — these two things need to work in parallel. You can't advance in spirituality without increasing the will to receive... If the will to receive grows, but correspondingly the demand for the correcting light grows so as to correct us, it's a positive process... We always work with two parallel forces in the wisdom of Kabbalah. You can't advance on one foot."

He closes the session by returning once more to what that demand actually builds toward: "With the Ten, I try to build a connection that is called the Creator. The Creator is not an image — it's the system of connection we build between us, opposite which the Creator can clothe... Let's try to use this time to awaken ourselves. Let's help each other. We already understand that alone we don't want to wake up, but together it can be more successful."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Elul is framed not as a calendar observance but as a sustained act of awakening from below — checking, all month, how correctly a person is aimed toward connection and adhesion with the Creator, ahead of Rosh Hashanah.
2. Annulling the will to receive does not mean discarding desire itself — only the intention behind it. The same desire, redirected toward bestowal, becomes the vessel through which a person is filled with light.
3. "I to my beloved" reorients the whole question away from a person's own enjoyment and toward a single concern: what does the Creator receive, and how much, from this state?
4. Working within the Ten and working directly toward the Creator are not two separate tracks but one work in two parts — connection with friends is what builds the actual capacity to perceive anything beyond oneself.
5. The Creator is described as waiting to be "awakened" by a person's own actions — specifically, loving the friends, helping them, and disseminating — rather than acting unilaterally.
6. Nature gives a person every desire except the desire to bestow like the Creator; that one deficiency has to be manufactured deliberately, in the "laboratory" of the Ten.
7. A person cannot build the desire to bestow alone. The environment is described as the only mechanism available — and it only works in proportion to how much greatness a person is willing to attribute to the friends.
8. Growth in the will to receive is not itself a danger; the danger is letting it grow without a matching growth in the demand for correction. Kabbalah is described as always requiring these two parallel forces at once.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Elul | The Hebrew month in which this lesson is set, whose name Rav Laitman reads as an acronym of "Ani LeDodi VeDodi Li" ("I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine"); described in this lesson as a dedicated period for checking one's relations with the Creator ahead of Rosh Hashanah. |
| Bitul (Annulment) | Self-nullification — here, annulling the will to receive before the will to bestow; this lesson frames it as a redirection of intention over the existing desire, not a suppression of desire itself. |
| Dvekut | Adhesion with the Creator; this lesson names it as the direct result of annulling one's will before the Creator's, reached in the same "receiving in order to bestow" excerpt as Bitul. |
| Achoraim (Posterior) | The concealed mode of guidance received before "the disclosure of the face" (Revelation of the Face); this lesson's Q&A treats accepting the posterior as important as if the disclosure itself had already been received. |
| Revelation of the Face (Gilui Panim) | The open disclosure of the Creator's face/Providence, granted, per this lesson's excerpt, only once the vessels of reception ("Yud-Heh") have been purified. |
| Shamati | Baal HaSulam's collection of private talks compiled by Rabash; source of this lesson's Article 57, "We Will Bring Him a Burnt Offering to His Will," read and reread twice in this session. |
| Baal HaSulam | Rabbi Yehuda Ashlag, author of the Sulam commentary on the Zohar; author of both excerpts read in this session. |
| Arvut | Mutual guarantee between friends; invoked in this lesson's closing Q&A as the covenant that lets a group "help one another as much as we can... in order to help one another attain that which none of us has." |
