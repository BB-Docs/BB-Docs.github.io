### Opening

Each soul is its own desire, developing in its own way, with its own connection to the Creator. So how can there be a "prayer of many"? How can a person feel someone else, take on his deficiencies, and be sorry with the friend's sorrow? Rav Laitman answers that the body, the very thing that seems to separate us, is what lets the soul connect to all the other souls. From there the lesson moves to a wider question: why we need corporeal actions at all, and what it really means to add an intention to them.

## Part 1 — How a Point Becomes a General Soul

#### The point and the body

- The soul descends from a special place where it is adhered to the Creator, as a root. It comes to this world and receives an addition of desires, the **Guf** (body). Rav Laitman stressed this is a spiritual concept, "not the beastly body," but the clothing the Creator places on the point of the soul.
- A person works against this body so that it will not be a partition between him and the Creator. By returning to adhesion despite the body's resistance, he acquires the force of adhesion, of nearness and acquaintance with the Creator.
- He does not only restrict the body. He places it under the authority of the soul and uses all its coarseness to **receive in order to bestow**.

> "Specifically the body enables the soul to connect with the rest of the souls."

#### A point does not grow by itself

- Before this, the soul is adhered to the Creator on its own and feels itself as one point, a seed (**Nekuda Achat**).
- "The point with which a person is born remains a point." There is nowhere to take additional desires from if they are not there.
- Through working against the body, it begins to connect with the other points. This gives it volume, an addition of deficiencies, until each soul is included of 600,000 deficiencies. "On its own, this point does not grow. It only grows at the expense of her connection with all the rest of the points."

#### What makes it a prayer of many

- As the soul is incorporated with many "seemingly foreign deficiencies" as her own, her demand is called the **prayer of many**.
- In the **final correction**, each soul feels all the other souls connected with it without limit, without partitions of bodies, with one common deficiency.
- On the way there, the more a person connects with others' deficiencies, the more his prayer, his lack and his appeal to the source of fulfillment, grows. He is immersed in a greater deficiency and also receives a greater fulfillment. "It is only in such a way that he advances."

## Part 2 — Serving a Great Person

- **Q: If the collective lacks vessels, how can his prayer be full of their deficiencies?** It is a matter of how much the collective can supply the individual.
- Rav Laitman's example: suppose there is a great Kabbalist, and all you know how to do is sew his clothes. In those clothes he will do his spiritual work, and you hope that because you helped him, something spiritual will come to you.
- "That's a prayer. And that is spiritual work." You relate to him as to an upper **Partzuf**. By raising your **MAN** to him through these low, practical actions in this world, you are rewarded with a reply. "This is the whole matter of serving a great man," on condition that you really have that intention.
- There also has to be a demand from the collective: an understanding that they are doing important work because it is tied to spiritual development.

## Part 3 — Why Tie Intention to a Corporeal Action

#### Spirituality is eternal, our states are not

- In spirituality, once you have risen to a degree and acquired it, it is yours. You descend from it only in order to ascend higher. "We're raised in holiness, not lowered."
- In our world, below the barrier, our intentions and longings are not permanent. People come, study, then fall away and disappear. Something remains in them, "but these are not things that are speaking in them."
- So we need support so as not to go down: a framework, a group, a Rav, books, an alarm clock, maybe a wife who reminds me about spirituality.

#### The shoeshine example

- On the positive side: suppose once a month, for five minutes, I have a real passion for spirituality, and then it disappears.
- If in those five minutes I go to a great Kabbalist and shine his shoes, wanting to advance with him by this, "this becomes eternal." My thought is not eternal yet, but "I made an action that does not disappear. You can't say that I didn't. The shoes are polished. And I adhered my intention to them."
- This is what Baal HaSulam explains in the **Article for the Conclusion of the Zohar**: a student who serves his Rav can be rewarded from the Rav's being in the upper world. Through his service he passes on his passion. "If he has no passion, what is there to use?"
- The same applies toward the Creator. If we join an intention to an action, the intention remains.

#### Without intention: the still of Kedusha

- An action without intention is the **still of Kedusha** (Domem de Kedusha). It exists, "and no more." It is a foundation from which nothing yet grows.
- To the extent I join my intention to it, the action becomes a foundation on which something begins to grow. Today's intention passes, but because I attached it to an action, it stays.
- This is why one needs to do the **Mitzvot**, but not like "those who are learned," who were taught as children in the **Cheder** and repeat the same act their whole life. Do them with an intention that keeps changing toward the Creator, in order to bestow.

#### Ninety-nine percent intention

- Baal HaSulam says to give 99% of the weight and importance to the intention, to what you want to reach, and 1% to the external execution, which "really doesn't demand more than that."
- External people are very meticulous about the action and leave the inner matter completely, "which can be studied only from Kabbalah." The **Creator's work** is only in Kabbalah.
- "Prayer is the Creator's work. What is prayer? Raising MAN." The correct appeal, asking for the screen, is the end of the whole matter, and it takes much preparation.
- The error began with the destruction of the Temple, when the intention to bestow was lost and the same actions continued without intention, or in order to receive. Regarding the action, the Kabbalists say "don't add and don't detract": don't turn it into an elaborate ritual. The intention, though, must be refined and updated every time.

## Part 4 — What Is an Intention?

#### Asking for the collective

- **Q: What does it mean in our state to ask for the collective?** "I can't tell you. It's something that's either in a person or not." Asking for it to be explained in the mind is not the same as having the inner deficiency.
- A better question is how to reach a state in which I have that request. We can help ourselves toward loftier deficiencies, not "a movie or a steak." From a certain level of development, a person starts caring for the collective. It comes through the **surrounding light** drawn during study with intention, which brings the **grace of Kedusha** (Chen): a sense of what is beyond my body and the five senses through which I serve my ego.

#### Intention is the work of the screen

- **Q: Is there a specific intention for each commandment?** The intention is keeping that commandment spiritually.
- The **Tefillin**, for example: spiritually it means drawing a certain illumination in **Zeir Anpin** of Atzilut in **Gadlut**, when he is in a coupling with **Nukva**, with an illumination from behind and from the front.
- "By tying this box on my hand, I think how to gain something. That's not intention." True intention is being on the degree where the spiritual action happens: screen, reflected light, direct light, receiving in order to bestow.

> "Intention is exactly your effort now in your heart. How are you going to receive in order to bestow, to bestow in order to bestow? In short, the calculation of the screen is called the intention."

- If you do it together with an action in this world, "that's wholeness, both of them together." You have done the act in two worlds.

#### Two intentions

- Studying with the intention to reach spirituality is the intention of the commandment of studying Torah. It is not the same as performing the other commandments: washing the hands, the blessing over food, the Tefillin, the prayer shawl.
- Through such acts a person draws the **light of correction** on himself. The first intention is "I wish to be corrected," for the upper force to come and correct me.
- After he has received a certain force of the screen from above, the intention changes: now he does the act in order to bestow. "These are two intentions."
- Books like the prayer book of the **RASHASH** and the **Shulchan Aruch** speak about the work of the screens, not the preparatory corrections. The letters and letter combinations in the RASHASH are like musical notes for the soul, while the Shulchan Aruch describes the same act for the body: how to hold it, where to place it, how many times to turn it. "It's the same record," done once in the soul (spirituality) and once in the body (corporeality).

## Part 5 — Commandments Have No Root in This World

- **Q: If every corporeal action has a spiritual root, why the commandments?** Look at them from outside and none is rational. Rav Laitman listed attempts to justify them: digesting before the blessing over food, health reasons for kashrut. "It's all abstract, has nothing to do with reality." His sharpest example: the orphan's Kaddish. "My father has passed away, and I'm saying praise the Lord, how great you are."
- The same goes for the **Four Kinds**, sitting in the Sukkah, Shabbat, tithing. "No commandment has its root in this world, a rational root."
- The Kabbalists, from **Abraham** on, attained the upper roots and began to keep them in corporeality accordingly, as it is written, "our fathers kept the entire Torah," out of their attainment of the upper world.

#### Commandments vs. ordinary actions

| | Ordinary actions | Commandments |
|---|---|---|
| **Spiritual root** | Yes, every action in this world has one | Yes |
| **Branch in this world** | Grows naturally from the root (eating, sleeping, working) | None. "Does the Tefillin grow on a tree?" Kabbalists took it from the spiritual world and brought it into ours |
| **Function above** | Sustains a soul at its current degree | An act by which you rise to a new degree |
| **Intention** | Only a general intention: I live in order to reach the purpose of creation | A specific spiritual act, a degree |

- "I'm driving a car in order to bestow," there's no such thing: there is no driving a car in spirituality. "In order to bestow" can only be placed where a spiritual desire exists to perform the act on.
- A commandment is an act by which I acquire a spiritual degree. There are 620 degrees, so 620 acts: the **613 commandments plus the seven** of the sages. Doing them in this world, "I sanctify all my actions."
- **Q: What is an eternal action?** An action to which an intention was attached; an act in the spiritual world. In the corporeal world, matter only changes form and the previous form disappears. In spirituality, once you acquired a screen and built a Partzuf, it remains, "like a chain."
- Kotzk saying: what intention should there be in eating? "Chew." Natural acts like food don't need correction; the commandment is the **blessing**, not the eating.
- **Q: Why is there no blessing over marital relations?** Any contact of light and desire in spirituality is a **Zivug** (coupling). Rav Laitman gave a first thought, that it may be because it is a general pleasure that contains all others, and said he would need to check before saying more.

## Part 6 — Correcting What Cannot Be Corrected

- The will to receive below the barrier is a "bulk to receive" on which you cannot place an intention to bestow. The upper light does not reach it, so it cannot feel shame or that there is a landlord. "Tell your cat, why did you steal this salami from me? Shame on you." It doesn't respond.
- It can be corrected only by joining it to spirituality: doing the act with the body and making the intention in the soul. "This is why man consists of both parts," this world and the spiritual world. A person who has both intention and action connects the two worlds, and correction reaches even this desire.
- Our five senses are a projection of the five degrees of coarseness and five degrees of the screen, but without a screen. If we left this region as it is, "we won't get to the end of correction."

#### The nations and the stony heart

- By root and branch, the nations of the world belong to a part of the will to receive that cannot be corrected directly. Once the Jews are corrected in **Galgalta veEinayim**, the nations can join, as in the Temple, which had a place for the Kohen, Levite, Israel, women, and the nations.
- The part that cannot join is corrected by not using it. "The stony heart is corrected. How? By not using it." Someone who restrains himself from stealing has made his correction.
- "With respect to the correction, they are equal": one may be a great Kabbalist and one refrains only from his evil, and both corrected themselves 100%. "You don't judge the level, you judge the measure of correction." At the end of correction there is no difference in level between Gentiles and Jews.
- A Gentile observing Shabbat "deserves death" means it is impossible: observing Shabbat means rising with the will to receive to Atzilut, and "you'll burn yourself."

#### A warning

- Corporeal observance helps spiritual advancement only on condition that through it a person wants to think and add an intention. Without that, it can do the opposite: he becomes satisfied, righteous, overdoes it, prefers externality to internality, and damages himself, the nation, and the world.
- "A commandment without an intention is like a body without a soul. It's dead." Know and regret that it is dead, and begin reviving it. The first intention is "I want life." The sorrow over it is already a prayer.
- In prayer it is different: your heart may be saying something other than the text. "Don't force your heart."

## Part 7 — The Big Wheel

- Even inventions like a screw or air conditioning have a root above; no thought could come without it. Such things serve to sustain the body at a given level.
- Rav Laitman's illustration: at Netzach of Yetzira I live in a field with animals. To rise to Tiferet of Yetzira I perform a new commandment; now I live in a city with a car and a bus. At Gevura I suddenly need a refrigerator and a washing machine, "and then I seemingly invent them."
- Technological development comes to us from higher spiritual degrees, from the "big wheel" turning at the pace of the six thousand years. Our inner, emotional development lags behind, still in Assiya. "This is why all my inventions work against me." Two hundred years ago people worked ten to twelve hours a day; now some work fifteen. "What have we gained?"
- Whoever wants to advance in such an environment has a big problem, "but it's the plight of many."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The body lets the soul connect.** A point on its own stays a point; it grows only by connecting with all the other souls and taking on their deficiencies as its own. That is the prayer of many.
2. **A spiritual impulse attached to a physical act becomes permanent.** Shining a great Kabbalist's shoes in a moment of passion turns a passing intention into an eternal action.
3. **Ninety-nine percent intention, one percent execution.** The act is a minimal frame; the intention must be refined and updated every time.
4. **Intention is the calculation of the screen.** It is the spiritual act itself on its degree, first asking to be corrected, then acting in order to bestow.
5. **Commandments have no rational root in this world.** Kabbalists brought them down from the spiritual roots they attained; they are the acts that raise a person from degree to degree.
6. **The uncorrectable will to receive is corrected through us.** A person who joins intention and action connects the two worlds; what cannot join is corrected by not being used.
7. **Our technology outruns our hearts.** Inventions come from higher degrees, but without inner development they turn against us.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Prayer of Many (Tefilat Rabim)** | The demand of a soul that has taken on the deficiencies of many other souls as its own; fully realized at the final correction. |
| **Nekuda (Point)** | The soul as it is before connecting with others; it does not grow by itself. |
| **Guf (Body)** | The addition of desires clothed on the point of the soul; both the partition and the means of connecting with other souls. |
| **Serving a Great Man** | Practical service to a great Kabbalist done with a spiritual intention, which raises MAN and brings a reply from above. |
| **Eternal Action** | An action to which an intention was attached; an act in the spiritual world, which remains. |
| **Still of Kedusha** | Observance without intention: it exists and sustains the foundation, but nothing grows from it. |
| **Intention (Kavana)** | The calculation of the screen: receiving or bestowing in order to bestow on the degree of the spiritual act. |
| **Light of Correction** | The light drawn through the commandments when the intention is "I want to be corrected." |
| **Tefillin** | Spiritually, drawing an illumination in Zeir Anpin of Atzilut in Gadlut, in a coupling with Nukva. |
| **620 Commandments** | The 613 commandments plus seven: the acts by which a soul rises through the 620 degrees. |
| **Stony Heart** | The part of the will to receive that cannot be corrected; its correction is not using it. |
| **The Big Wheel** | The pace of development over six thousand years, bringing technology from higher degrees faster than our inner development. |
