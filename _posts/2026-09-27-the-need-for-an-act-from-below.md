### Opening

Can we only "leave Egypt" after we've already overcome our nature? In a short clip, Rav Laitman answers yes. That is exactly what leaving Egypt means. But he locates the whole of a person's part in it somewhere unexpected: not in overcoming the ego by his own strength, but in **the request**.

## Part 1 — Egypt and the Land of Israel Are Two Kinds of Control

> Going out of Egypt is coming out of the control of the desire to receive over a person. That's the control of Egypt. The control of the desire to bestow is called the Land of Israel.

- For now, we are in Egypt.
- We aren't even aware of how completely the **desire to receive in order to receive** rules over us. That is something we still have to discover.

## Part 2 — Discovering Egypt Through Trying to Bestow

- The discovery comes **to the extent that we try to build relations of bestowal between us**. The more we try, the more we see that we're not in bestowal, and that "qualities of reception are being revealed between us constantly."
- A person fights this and can't win, until he starts to recognize "some bad force that is constantly pressuring me and entering my mind and heart."
- Then he cries out. That is the verse **"the children of Israel sighed from the work."**

## Part 3 — What Depends on Us: Only the Request

- The outcry rises to the upper force, the Creator, who then starts to arrange for us the **forces of bestowal**. "The taste of bestowal comes from above."
- There is **one upper force**. It gives either the evil inclination (egoistic) or the good inclination (love and bestowal), and which one depends entirely on our request.

> What depends on us is how we convince Him to give us the good force instead of the bad force.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Egypt and Israel are two forms of control.** Egypt is the rule of the desire to receive over a person. The Land of Israel is the rule of the desire to bestow.
2. **We don't yet know how deep Egypt goes.** It is revealed only by actively trying to build bestowal between the friends.
3. **Failure leads to the outcry.** The struggle we can't win produces the cry "from the work," and that cry is what reaches the Creator.
4. **Bestowal comes from above.** A person can't produce it. There is one force, and it gives according to the request.
5. **Our whole part is the prayer.** Convincing the Creator to give the good force instead of the bad is the only act from below.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Egypt (Mitzrayim)** | The control of the desire to receive in order to receive over a person. |
| **Land of Israel** | The control of the desire to bestow. |
| **Exodus (Yetziat Mitzrayim)** | Coming out from under the rule of the will to receive. |
| **Outcry ("sighed from the work")** | The cry that comes from failing to reach bestowal on one's own. It rises to the Creator. |
| **Act from Below (Awakening from Below)** | Discovering that one wants the force of bestowal and asking the Creator for it. According to Rabash, this is a person's only choice. |
| **One Upper Force** | The single force that gives either the evil or the good inclination, depending on the request. |
