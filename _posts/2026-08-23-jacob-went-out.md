### Opening

The archival lecture opens directly from Rashi's comment on the verse "Jacob went out" — that a righteous man's departure from a place leaves an impression — and reads it as the pattern of a person's entire spiritual climb: leaving one degree in order to reach the next, always by first descending to check his footing on the "earth" below him. From there the lesson and its long Q&A circle a single, sharpened question: if spirituality is supposed to be pure good, why is progress built out of repeated falls? The discussion moves from the mechanics of ascent and descent, through why the fall itself is unavoidable even for someone who wants to obey the spiritual law completely, to the practical machinery — environment, nighttime study, even paying for the lesson — that a person needs in order to keep discovering new lack without simply collapsing under it.

## Part 1 — Little Jacob, the Ladder Set Toward Earth, and Becoming an Angel of the Creator

Rav Laitman opens the lecture proper by returning to the verse itself: "little Jacob" names a person who has only a small desire for spirituality, and in order to reach the goal he needs to leave his place.

> "The ladder is always set toward the earth... every time he wants to change his state from one degree to another, he needs to descend to the earth, to the lowest degree, and check whether he is standing upon corporeality — whether his desires, from the coarsest desire to the highest desire he now wants to reach and correct, are all found on that same ladder of degrees."

The image of angels ascending and descending (*malachim olim veyordim*) becomes the definition of full spiritual alignment — what it means to become "an angel of the Creator":

> "All his movements, all his thoughts, all his steps will be in accordance with what is in the thought of creation... to such an extent that even if there were no plan, he himself would create and program it — meaning that his steps, thoughts, and actions would even be faster than his receiving commands from above. And that person is called an angel of the Creator."

Even that angelic state doesn't exempt a person from descent — "the greater the ascents, the deeper the descents corresponding to them." Progress through this cycle is what carries Jacob to Israel: the Gadlut, the greatness of the state of Jacob, at the top of the ladder.

## Part 2 — Why Descend at All? Every Degree Is a Measure of Ein Sof

A student presses the obvious question: why does a person need to descend at all, if he simply hasn't yet appreciated the importance of the degree he's on? Rav Laitman's answer starts from what perfection would actually mean if it had no built-in lack:

> "If I'm in perfection, and spirituality is perfection, and in every state I am in spirituality I need to feel really good — eternity, no lack whatsoever — then the state is dead, and the person cannot grow from it. The smallest degree in spirituality would finish him off. It would bury him, and that would be it."

The resolution is that no degree is actually final — every degree, however small, already contains the whole structure of correction in miniature:

> "Every degree, every spiritual state, is some small measure of Ein Sof, of the end of correction. Whether one-hundredth or one-tenth, it doesn't matter — it's a measure of the final degree, because every degree has Ten Sefirot, has all the discernments from Keter to Malchut. It only depends on how he discerns them, how he feels them — either somewhat through a fog, or more and more clearly, until with all the details he enters the end of correction."

Movement beyond a degree that already feels like perfection comes only from above: greater light is shone in from a still higher degree, and the person begins to feel — not his own deficiency, but that spirituality itself is "much more exalted... much more important than he had previously thought." That revised sense of scale is what starts him moving again.

## Part 3 — Two Forces: Suffering That Pushes, Pleasure That Pulls

Rav Laitman names two forces that act on a person on this path:

> "One force acts by revealing to us our lacks, our deficiencies, how flawed we are — this force pushes us from behind, through suffering. And the second force pulls us forward through pleasures, by revealing to us how much more important spirituality is, how much more special and good the right line is than what had previously been revealed to us."

He states a clear preference between them — "it's better to progress through the force that pulls us toward pleasures... rather than the force that pushes us from behind" — and describes what it looks like when a person manages the substitution: instead of feeling his own deficiencies as failure, he reframes them entirely as unmet longing to bestow, what Rav Laitman calls "the sufferings of love":

> "He replaces the suffering that pushes him from behind with... how much I am still incapable of bestowing. And then he exchanges one for the other, and certainly advances in the desired way."

Even in the Creator's own eyes, he adds, a person drawn forward is preferable to one who is merely "compelled and pricked from behind."

## Part 4 — Humanity's Widening Lag

Rav Laitman detours briefly into an observation about ordinary human development, using it as a mirror for the spiritual lag just described:

> "You see how it is in this world? We're constantly lagging behind. We develop our means, we develop our intellect, we develop in science — but we don't develop accordingly in how to use these tools. So we constantly lag behind. And this lag accumulates. And you see that from one generation to the next, people find themselves more and more frustrated, in proportion to how they seemingly create more opportunities for themselves, yet are less and less capable of enjoying them."

The diagnosis: ordinary people simply don't know how to use the law of development correctly — a lag that compounds across generations rather than resolving itself.

## Part 5 — Sudden Awakening, and Why We Need Each Other to Keep Moving

Asked why a fall from a good state always feels sudden, Rav Laitman traces the sequence a person actually goes through. A vessel must first be filled, then emptied, before a person can truly feel what state he was in — and along the way, something specific gets lost:

> "A person feels himself good... because he wants spirituality, to bestow. And then afterward, what happens is that he starts to forget that he wants to bestow, and that this is spirituality, and instead he begins simply to enjoy his state, that he feels good... From the moment he feels only the good, they begin working on him to lower him from the degree. But he still feels the good. And then, after some time, he suddenly discovers that he is no longer in spirituality at all... and only then does he awaken."

The remedy is to never let the feeling replace the goal:

> "You always need to come up with additional deficiencies for yourself, so as not to stop, but to constantly advance. There can be no stopping in spirituality, either forward or backward."

This is where the group becomes practically necessary, not just emotionally helpful:

> "If a person is in a group where other people around him are working, and he becomes incorporated in them, then even if he forgets, they are working — so it's as though he is traveling together with them... But if he's not in a group, if he's alone, everything really depends on him. The moment he forgets about the goal and clings only to the feeling, the descent immediately begins."

Being surrounded by "another hundred people" gives a person a built-in damper — "a brake... a certain cushion" that keeps a lapse from becoming a full fall, though Rav Laitman is careful to note this cushion still requires the person's own work.

## Part 6 — Descending Without Descending

Returning to the verse's own image, Rav Laitman states the law plainly: spirituality allows no flat, unchanging state — only ascent, or the descent that provokes the next ascent, "therefore he writes that angels of God ascend and descend." A student notices the tension this creates for someone trying to be fully obedient, and Rav Laitman offers a way through it:

> "So he says, no — you can descend without descending. How? If each time you find new deficiencies in your state, through which you can rise higher, then the new deficiency that you find already serves you in place of a descent. And then you don't need to descend... This descent, which you immediately discover within yourself and which you yourself want to find, you attach to the ascent."

This, he says, is the meaning of the article's image of the righteous one who "walks with his head bent down, in order to search for deficiencies" — actively hunting for the next lack rather than waiting to be ambushed by it.

## Part 7 — Beyond the Barrier: Two Natures, and Rabbi Shimon in the Marketplace

A student asks what a descent means for someone who has already crossed the Machsom: does he lose the feeling of the Creator entirely, or only the ability to bestow? Rav Laitman's answer locates the difference in two natures a person comes to hold simultaneously:

> "He knows that he descends. But nevertheless he cannot do anything... There are two kinds of nature he has to find: he has the force of the Creator, and he has the force of the created being. Then he is no longer only within his animate nature, his beastly nature, but he has this divine part from which he can, on one side, see the other side."

That divine vantage point actually begins developing before the Machsom, as the point in the heart grows — what changes at the barrier itself is subtler:

> "Before the barrier, a person passes from double concealment to single concealment — meaning even in his falls, he knows that they apparently come from the Creator."

Beyond the barrier, descents continue as "departure of the Mochin, until he acquires the next degree." Rav Laitman illustrates the doubled awareness this produces with the classic image of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai:

> "So certainly he has states like Rabbi Shimon becoming Shimon from the market — when Rabbi Shimon felt that he was like Shimon from the market. But nevertheless, he felt that he was Rabbi Shimon who feels himself as Shimon from the market. It's not that this knowledge of who he is completely disappears... this comes in order to give you a sharper discernment between the two extremes where you can be — either adhered to the Creator or adhered to a market stall."

## Part 8 — There Is No Real Descent — Only Growing the Vessel, Naturally or by Achishena

A student offers a reframing: since spirituality has no true descents, is what feels like a fall actually just the first sign of reaching for the next degree? Rav Laitman confirms it and sharpens the language:

> "There is no descent, no actual descent. Rather, each descent is the acquisition of a new force of the left [line], which is still not corrected... It's impossible to call it a descent — it's an ascent, an ascent in the left line, which is called a descent... As you rise in holiness, you have to have the same revelation of the force of evil. Otherwise, what do you use in order to correct the vessel?"

He walks through the mechanics with a concrete measure: a corrected vessel of "ten grams" reveals a certain amount of spiritual reality; reaching the next degree means discovering another ten grams of will within oneself to correct. There are two ways to discover it:

> "I can discover it by being drawn towards spirituality... or I can discover it by having it revealed to me from above — but then it is revealed to me as a bad thing, that I want corporeality... This whole process can take place over a very extended duration, until I discover that it's to my detriment... until I switch this direction to that direction, in terms of how I use the desire."

The second route — waiting for suffering to force the discovery — is the slow way. The alternative is Achishena, hastening the time:

> "This whole method teaches you how to discover a lack — not in the natural way, in its own time, the way that it happens and takes a long time — but rather through Achishena, hastening it: you preempt the enlargement of the vessel before it's revealed to you from above... It's only by drawing to yourself more and more of the recognition of the greatness of the Creator... And if you discover it that way, then you don't need descents or suffering — and you save a lot of time and a lot of suffering."

## Part 9 — Society as the Deterministic Lever, and the Conditions for Advancement

Asked how a person can build a steady pull toward spirituality rather than relying on being pushed, Rav Laitman locates the entire answer outside the person:

> "This can be done only through society, because all the qualities in me, and the phenomena outside of me, work on me in a deterministic fashion. I was born with a certain nature — I have nothing to do with it... So by what can I change? I'm being changed from above. So how can I determine those changes? Only by determining your society. If you know that you need to find a society that would reveal to you the importance of the Creator, the importance of spirituality, then look for a society that will awaken in you those sensations. That is your choice — all of it."

Choosing well, he says, saves a person from the slower path: "you save yourself from the path of suffering, and bring yourself to the path of the Torah — the path of light, which pulls you forward... In any case, you'll get there. It's just a question of how many more years you'll suffer for each discernment."

This leads into a broader list of conditions for advancement that a person keeps without fully knowing why: studying at night, and specifically around Chatzot (midnight) — explained by root and branch as the hour when surrounding lights begin their own daily corrections, so that a person studying then draws those same lights more strongly; love of friends; and, more surprisingly, Baal HaSulam's own condition that a person pay for the lesson, including the Maaser, the tithe. Rav Laitman shares a personal memory of not understanding this requirement at first:

> "Baal HaSulam, for example, had a condition where if a person doesn't pay for the lesson, he can't come to the lesson... And in addition, he says, here you have to bring the tithe... I didn't understand the principle of it. But after two years or so — a year and a half after coming and studying, and becoming [Rabash's] assistant, I started managing his bank account, and then I saw what the tithe was."

He closes the point simply: "group, society, studying at night — with that, without that, it's simple. Without that, you don't meet the conditions for advancing at all."

## Part 10 — The Screen: Intention to Bestow, Not a Fixed Measure

The closing exchange asks whether the screen (Masach) is something constantly available, or whether it depends on the state a person is in. Rav Laitman first clears up what the screen actually is:

> "It's the intention towards the Creator — my thoughts, my desires, everything is aimed towards Him. That is called the screen... It's an emotional thing, an emotional intention. It can't be a measuring thing — 'I need to give Him this much, let's measure it out' — that's not a screen."

As for availability, the screen tracks the vessel a person has already corrected up to that point:

> "You developed in this world up to the biggest will to receive that can exist in this world, and over that you acquire a screen... it cannot be that instead of spirituality, you look at corporeality anymore. This is called making a restriction over the pleasures of this world."

Beyond that restriction, Rav Laitman describes two ways a person can meet the far greater pleasures — eternity, perfection — that begin to appear once he is past this world's frame:

> "If you start falling into these pleasures in order to receive, this is called a shell (Klipa). But if you gain the power to relate to that with the intention to bestow, that is called holiness... Both are above this world, both the shell and holiness. It's not that you no longer belong to this world — you can still discern various pleasures here — but it's not that you will ever prefer corporeality to spirituality. That can no longer happen. You're above that choice already, for certain."

The transcript closes as the group turns to summarize its impressions from the lesson and prepare for the workshop, cutting off mid-exchange.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. "Jacob went out" is read as the pattern of the entire climb: a small, "little Jacob"-sized desire leaves its place, checks its footing on the "ladder set toward earth," and through repeated ascent and descent eventually becomes Israel — the Gadlut, greatness, at the top of the ladder.
2. No degree can be a true final resting point, even one that feels perfect: every degree already contains a full measure of Ein Sof, the end of correction, discerned more or less clearly — so continued progress depends on greater light revealing that the degree isn't actually the ceiling.
3. Two forces move a person forward — suffering pushing from behind, or pleasure (the revealed greatness of spirituality) pulling from ahead — and being drawn is explicitly preferred, both practically and "in the eyes of the Creator," over being driven by deficiency alone.
4. A fall always feels sudden because a person quietly stops tracking the goal and starts simply enjoying a good state; the fix is to keep manufacturing new deficiencies rather than settle into the feeling, and the group functions as a literal brake against sudden falls for anyone traveling alone.
5. Even someone who wants full obedience to spiritual law cannot avoid descent altogether — but he can "descend without descending" by actively hunting for the next deficiency himself, so the discovery of lack becomes part of the ascent rather than a separate fall.
6. Past the Machsom, the same structure continues: a person holds two natures at once (the Creator's force and the created being's), and even Rabbi Shimon's states of feeling like "Shimon from the market" don't erase his deeper self-knowledge — they sharpen the discernment between the two.
7. Properly understood, there is no such thing as an actual descent — every "fall" is the ascent of the left line, revealing a new, uncorrected force to work with; the resulting growth in the vessel can be discovered slowly through natural suffering, or hastened (Achishena) by deliberately drawing in the Creator's greatness instead.
8. The environment is described as the one deterministic lever a person actually controls, and the lesson extends this into concrete conditions for advancement — nighttime study timed to the surrounding lights, love of friends, and even Baal HaSulam's requirement of paying for the lesson (including the tithe).
9. The screen is defined strictly as emotional intention to bestow, not a calculable measure, and it scales with a person's already-corrected vessel; past that point, the same great pleasures (eternity, perfection) can be met either as a shell, by receiving for oneself, or as holiness, by receiving with the intention to bestow.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Katnut / Gadlut | Smallness and greatness. This lesson opens on "little Jacob" — Katnut, a small desire for spirituality — and traces his progress up the ladder until "Jacob becomes Israel," the Gadlut at the top. |
| Ein Sof | Infinity / the end of correction. Cited here for the idea that every degree, however small, is already "some small measure of Ein Sof" — containing all Ten Sefirot from Keter to Malchut — so no degree can be a true final state. |
| Achishena (Hastening the Time) | Advancing by deliberately drawing in the recognition of the Creator's greatness, so a new degree of vessel is discovered before it would otherwise be revealed through suffering — explicitly preferred in this lesson as the way to "save a lot of time and a lot of suffering." |
| Screen (Masach) | Defined here strictly as emotional intention to bestow ("everything is aimed towards Him"), not a measurable quantity; its extent tracks the biggest will to receive a person has already corrected in this world. |
| Machsom | The barrier. This lesson describes what changes at it (the shift from double to single concealment) and what continues past it (ongoing descents as "departure of the Mochin" toward the next degree). |
| Kelim / Kli | Vessel(s). Illustrated in this lesson with a concrete measure — a "ten-gram" corrected vessel needing to grow to "twenty grams" to reveal spiritual reality twice over — discovered either naturally or through Achishena. |
| Maaser (Tithe) | The tenth given as part of Baal HaSulam's condition that a person pay for the lesson; Rav Laitman recounts not understanding the requirement until he began managing Rabash's bank account as his assistant. |
| Rabash | Rabbi Baruch Shalom Ashlag; author of this lesson's article, "Jacob Went Out" (Article No. 10, 1985), and the teacher Rav Laitman recalls assisting and managing finances for in the lesson's closing anecdote. |
