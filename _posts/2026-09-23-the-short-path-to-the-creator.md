### Opening

This is the opening talk of a multi-day Congress, and Rav Laitman uses it to set the aim for the whole gathering. Why bring hundreds of people from a dozen countries together? Not to create good feelings between them, he says, but to look for **the Creator in the relationships between us**. The lesson lays out what that search looks like, why it is called the **short path** to the Creator, how it differs from the **long path**, and one firm rule: a person's relationship with the Creator is never discussed with friends.

## Part 1 — The Goal: Finding the Creator Between Us

Rav Laitman opens by saying the materials prepared for the Congress have one purpose: building a shared set of qualities and feelings so that, inwardly, the participants come to sense each other and, through that, the Creator.

- The Creator is described as a force that is revealed in a person's **altruistic quality** and intention.
- A person does not connect with another *for the sake of connecting*, but so that **within that attitude he feels the Creator**.
- The effort must be total, "desperate, so to speak, to the max."

> The primary goal is to find the Creator between us. If that is not our goal, we immerse ourselves in even greater ego, and all our efforts are doomed to fail, as with communism, the kibbutzim, and all the other earlier attempts.

He adds that this applies only among those who share the aspiration to reveal the Creator, the group engaged in this study, and not to people in general.

#### Egoism is the starting point, not an obstacle

- If a person looks honestly inside, he will find he is an egoist doing everything for himself. "That doesn't matter. This is how we were created. This is how we must begin this work."
- Until the Creator is revealed in the connection, a person **cannot even imagine** what it means to come out of himself, which is what Baal HaSulam calls altruism.
- So a person should not give up because of his present qualities and say "it's not for me." It truly *is* impossible by our own strength. Only the Creator's revealed attitude toward us makes it possible.

> As soon as we begin to feel the Creator, that feeling will suddenly give us such a force, such an understanding of the picture, that it brings us to a level where we relate to each other altruistically.

#### Rebuilding the one Kli

The Light created a single common **Kli**, which it fills. If we recreate that Kli between us, it is as though we are restoring it, and we will feel it *between* us. Rav Laitman stresses the difference: "We're not looking for good relations toward one another. We're looking for the Creator's manifestation in the attitude between us."

## Part 2 — Practical Conditions for the Congress

- **Help each other** so that relating to one another becomes easier, but without imposing outward displays of "love" on each other. The work happens at an **inner point** each person finds in himself, where "we catch the Creator" in our inner attitudes toward others.
- Treat the days together as a kind of intensive inner training. If the effort is made, it will succeed.
- **Modesty between men and women.** Keep contact to a minimum so the men are not distracted. Rav Laitman asks the women to support each other in this so the men can focus inward. "We understand our nature, our weakness, and must help each other move forward in this."
- All the Congress guidelines exist only to point everyone toward the state they need to reach.

## Part 3 — The Short Path and the Long Path

Rav Laitman cites Baal HaSulam and an article of Rabash: there are **two paths** to the Creator, and the difference shows clearly even among beginners.

| | The Short Path (Path of Hastening) | The Long Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Aim** | Reveal the Creator in the relationships inside the group | Moves toward the goal, but without taking this aim as the core of the work |
| **Method** | Uses the environment on purpose, applying one's qualities toward others to find the Creator in them | Engages more with the technical study, surface-level group activity, practical tasks |
| **Time** | Traditionally 3–5 years; Baal HaSulam speaks of 2–3 years of adaptation | 20–25 years, even when moving correctly |

#### How the Creator begins to be revealed

A person pictures the Creator as a point beneath the whole structure of the group, gluing it together like a cup holding its contents. As he tries to relate to others the way the Creator does:

- He starts to feel how the Creator relates to the whole world and to him.
- The Creator is revealed first as **the governing force** behind everyone and all their relationships.
- When a person's desire aligns with the Creator's, he sees that the people he thinks about act in line with that intention. They are not commanded; this is simply how the Creator's control shows itself.
- This gradual revelation, the measure of the Creator "clothing" in this world, is what is called **the worlds**. It grows as a person steps further outside himself against greater and greater **disturbances**.

#### Shame as a springboard

Rav Laitman asks participants to become aware that they want to *use* each other for their own spiritual ascent, and to feel **shame** about it. That shame should not be suppressed. It should work "like a springboard," giving momentum toward the right intention toward one another.

## Part 4 — Why Kabbalists Wrote Their Books

Rav Laitman refers to Baal HaSulam's *Introduction to Talmud Eser Sefirot*, items 154–155. Kabbalists wrote their books for one another, for those already **beyond the Machsom**, so they could share how to relate, how to feel the Creator more, and how to refine their intentions, and so climb the **125 degrees** faster. The Machsom is only the entrance.

So why should a beginner study them? The study draws the **Light that Reforms**:

> Like little children, like little monkeys, we imitate what the adult does without understanding it. We don't understand how the Kabbalists work with the Masach, with Rosh, Toch, Sof, the Partzuf, the Gematria, but we try.

- Once one arrives, he sees that all those terms describe **inner work** with a person's own states, ego, and natural inclinations, arranged in the best possible way. That becomes "the formula of the interrelation with others," through which the Creator is formed.
- So we imitate a Kabbalist's intention: forming our intentions toward others (only within the group, never toward outsiders) in the likeness of what the Kabbalists describe.
- What a Kabbalist receives as **Inner Light (Ohr Pnimi)** in his Kelim, we receive for now as **Surrounding Light (Ohr Makif)**. How strongly it acts depends on how closely we try to resemble their work. It then draws a person across the Machsom into the Inner Light.
- Baal HaSulam's commentary on the Ari in TES is itself called *Ohr Pnimi*, "Inner Light." It sets out the whole path for beginners so they can receive that Light.

## Part 5 — A Unique Time: Everything Prepared from Above

- Any correct path starts with a period of **adaptation**, like a child who "doesn't grow in one day." Baal HaSulam writes of 2–3 years. The Talmud speaks of 3–5 years to break into the upper world.
- Today's prepared materials and media make that adaptation shorter than Baal HaSulam described.
- Rav Laitman expects the group to grow in variety: more English, Spanish, and German speakers, people "from Australia to Canada, Siberia," more than ten languages among two or three hundred people. That variety comes to resemble the single Kli that holds all its different parts.
- Past groups of Kabbalists who tried to gather "didn't have permission from above." He gives the example of the Ari, who called his students to go up to Jerusalem, and they did not come for reasons as small as "my wife didn't let me."

> We exist in a time where everything is already prepared from above. We are ready in the eyes of the Creator for this mission, and everything else is in our hands.

#### Swinging back and forth at the Machsom

Rav Laitman compares the approach to the Machsom to a person standing at the surface of water, **leaning in and out**, forward over the barrier and back. These states repeat many times so a person can practice being in both and work through all his **Reshimot**.

- Don't mind that sensations come and go. "This is good."
- **Never regret the past and never try to go back to it.** Learn from it, but in each new state look for something new, the way a child learns new skills.

## Part 6 — Two Types of People, Both Commendable

Following Baal HaSulam's article "The Freedom," the short path belongs to a person who understands that he **needs the environment** and uses it on purpose to reveal the Creator, first in a small group, then spreading to the whole world.

Some people cannot take this in. They cannot yet grasp the breaking of the Kli and the ego that now stands between us. They are "too thick, egoistically speaking." For them, progress looks different:

- They may be drawn to the technical side of the wisdom, to certain outward parts of group work, or to **service**: organizing things, running activities.
- "We can't say it's better or worse." Some souls take the shorter path and some the longer one. Everyone should aspire to the short path, but if a person loses his connection to the group when he tries, he should reconnect by *doing* something for it.
- "Everything in your power to do, do." Whatever abilities a person has are there to be put to use.

Rav Laitman points to the group's own practice. It avoids outside contractors and brings as much work as possible inside the group: kitchen, construction, translation, its own publishing house, even ten thousand liters of its own wine that year. Each shared effort "creates new points of connection between us."

## Part 7 — Dissemination: The Most Concentrated Correction

> The most effective joint action that can bring us to the Creator is dissemination.

Why it meets all the conditions at once:

- You spread the wisdom of the Creator.
- You do it **toward other people**, bringing them into the common Kli.
- It passes *through* you. You connect those people personally and show your own attitude toward them, so as to reveal the Creator to them too.

Serving one's own group, and then other groups, comes next, "on a lower level." Rav Laitman notes that nearly all of the group's budget and people go into dissemination: radio, television, video, audio, books, translations. He says Kabbalistic groups that keep to themselves have not advanced the same way, "mainly for this reason."

## Part 8 — The Rule: Never Share Your Relationship with the Creator

The best way to connect is to talk about **the material that unites us**, never about personal spiritual feelings.

> One's own personal relationship with the Creator cannot be disclosed to anyone else. Must not be.

**Why:** we are all uncorrected egoists. Until a person has fully corrected himself, with no reaction left to any disturbance, and risen to the world of Atzilut, a friend's ego will **automatically** react to what he hears, and that brings a **double harm**:

1. **To me:** the friend's automatic egoistic conclusions about my relationship with the Creator disturb it and bring me down from my level.
2. **To my friend:** without meaning to, I pull him into an egoistic connection focused on me, which brings *him* down.

So friends talk about the group, how to relate to the friends, the articles, and how the study applies to this work, "but not me and the Creator." Singing together as a choir is fine. The songs express a shared aspiration.

## Part 9 — Q&A: "There Is None Else Besides Him," Revealed Through Others

**Question:** More concretely, what should we be doing inside ourselves toward the group, and how do we get across the Machsom?

Rav Laitman points to Baal HaSulam's "There Is None Else Besides Him," which he calls the first and most basic article. It describes a person's constant outlook: the Creator alone exists, and He determines and controls everything around me and in me, including my thoughts and actions. My job is only to reveal this.

- That revelation does not come from sitting and doing inner exercises. "I will come to some silly meditations." It comes **only through others**. "The Creator shows himself through others."
- **Light and Kli:** there is a Light that no one feels in itself and a vessel that feels it. Everything else (Tzimtzum, the different ways of wanting to feel the Light without shame) is only the vessel's changing attitude toward the Light. "Nothing changes. There is Light and there is a vessel."
- The Creator shows Himself only in **the general Kli**. A person makes himself a point that builds that Kli within itself. A small group is enough, because through it a person extends his attitude to the whole world.
- **The degrees are clarity, not size.** Each degree of attainment is the *whole* Kli seen more clearly. "The body is general, but I'm feeling it as one 125th." Rosh, Toch, Sof exist from the very beginning.
- The group gathers people who are as different as possible, from Dagestan, Estonia, Asia, and Toronto, as if representing every layer of the Kli. In practice, 10–15 people right around you are enough. For now the work is men with men and women with women.

## Part 10 — Q&A: Two Points on a Line: the Creator at Start and End

**Question:** You spoke of a tiny point, a thread we need to catch in our attitude toward the Creator. Can you say more?

Rav Laitman describes setting a course, like a ship or plane that has lost its north. A person has to **start with the Creator**, "there is none else besides Him." If the Creator is not at both the beginning and the end of the effort, the effort is off course. He cites Baal HaSulam's letter in *Pri Chacham* (around p. 53 or 63), which draws the Creator between two points of the movement.

- **The Creator is the endpoint; the group is the means.**
- The group is really **my own qualities**, the 620 properties of the soul, seen as if they were outside me: 248 (Ramach), corresponding to Galgalta veEinayim, and 365 (Shasah), plus 7, making 620. The general and the particular are equal. However it is divided, any part again contains ten.
- **The first point** is saying "I am aligned to the Creator." But that is only something I've pictured in words after reading an article. It isn't attained yet.
- **The second point** is actually living that unity. Reaching it means passing through every inner state along the line, revealing the Creator's qualities by **practicing them on others**.

#### Relating to the Kli as He does

> I have no other manifestation of the Creator other than the way He manifests to the entire Kli. We say "Good who does good." That is how He manifests to the common Kli. Outside of the Kli, we don't know Him. That is Atzmuto.

- In the Creator's eyes, all people are already in **Gmar Tikkun**, complete correction. In my eyes they are separate egoists, because the Kli is broken *in my eyes*.
- So I look for how the Creator relates to them and relate to them the same way. By doing so I become like Him, which is **Dvekut** (adhesion), the final goal.
- "The Creator is not nearby. Doctors say that no matter how many surgeries they had, they couldn't find Him within me either." He is found only in the relationship to the Kli.
- **How does a person know he is being corrected?** Only by his attitude toward the group. Even in Kabbalah, Light cannot be measured in itself, "only as the Kli changes." That gives the degrees of NRNHY and millions of others.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The purpose of connection is the Creator, not good relations.** Friendship pursued for its own sake falls back into ego, as with communism and the kibbutzim. The goal is to find the Creator's manifestation *in* the relationships between us.
2. **Start as the egoist you are.** Seeing your own egoism is no reason to stop. Altruism can't be imagined until the Creator is revealed, and only that revelation makes it possible.
3. **The short path uses the environment on purpose.** Taking up the group as the place to reveal the Creator cuts the path from 20–25 years to a few. The long path and service-oriented participation are still legitimate. Everyone should do what they can.
4. **Imitate the Kabbalists like children.** Without understanding the terms, forming our intentions toward the friends in the likeness of their inner work draws the Surrounding Light that carries a person across the Machsom.
5. **Dissemination is the most concentrated correction.** It spreads the wisdom, brings others into the common Kli, and passes through the person himself.
6. **Keep your relationship with the Creator private.** Talking about it harms both you and your friend. Talk instead about the group, the study, and the work.
7. **The Creator at both ends, the group in between.** Begin and end with "there is none else besides Him." The group is the means, and relating to others as the Creator relates to them is what brings adhesion.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Short Path / Path of Hastening** | Intentionally using the group to reveal the Creator in the connection between friends. Traditionally 3–5 years (Baal HaSulam: 2–3 years of adaptation), shortened further by today's materials. |
| **Long Path** | Progress toward the goal without making the group the place where the Creator is revealed. Takes 20–25 years even when moving correctly. |
| **Kli (Common Vessel)** | The single vessel the Light created, broken into many souls. Rebuilding it between the friends is where the Creator is felt. |
| **Machsom** | The barrier between this world and spirituality. Only the entrance to the 125 degrees. |
| **Surrounding Light (Ohr Makif)** | The Light that reaches those not yet across the Machsom from outside. Drawn by imitating the Kabbalists' work, it reforms a person and pulls him across. |
| **Inner Light (Ohr Pnimi)** | Light received inside the Kelim through the Masach. Also the name of Baal HaSulam's commentary in Talmud Eser Sefirot. |
| **Dissemination** | Spreading the wisdom to others. The most effective joint action, and "the most concentrated form of correction of the general Kli." |
| **Shame (as springboard)** | Feeling ashamed of wanting to use the friends for one's own ascent, turned into momentum toward the right intention. |
| **620 Properties** | The parts of the soul: 248 (Ramach) + 365 (Shasah) + 7. Seen as the friends "outside" oneself. |
| **Ein Od Milvado** | "There is none else besides Him." The first point a person sets his course by. The Creator is the sole ruler, revealed only through others. |
| **Gmar Tikkun** | The end of correction. How the Creator already sees all people. |
| **Atzmuto** | The Creator's essence outside the Kli, which no one can feel or understand. |
| **Dvekut (Adhesion)** | Equivalence with the Creator, reached by relating to the Kli exactly as He does. The final goal. |
| **Reshimot** | Impressions a person has to work through, which is why states repeat as he swings back and forth at the Machsom. |
