### Opening

The archival lecture opens from the article's reading on Vaetchanan — fear out of love, above reason versus within reason — and moves into a long, doctrinally dense Q&A built around a single reframing: what looks like sin, punishment, and concealment from a person's own vantage point is, from the Creator's, preparation, correction, and care. The session then pivots sharply into a live, present-day workshop on structuring the group's "dissemination factory" — departments, discipline, small versus large groups — which turns out to be organized by exactly the same principle: build the practical vessel first, and only then clothe it with spirituality. The lecture closes on Rav Laitman's own summary of the night's real message: choose a better environment, and don't wait for things to happen.

## Part 1 — Reframing Sin, Punishment, and Correction

Rav Laitman opens on the question of why Adam HaRishon's act is called a sin at all, given that it was necessary for correction to become possible:

> "This is actually something that has been prepared for him in advance... this act was an act that has been prepared for him from the very purpose of creation, from the beginning, before he was even born. If he wouldn't have done it, then it would have been impossible to reach the desired correction. If so, how can we say about him that he had sinned?"

He resolves it by relocating blame entirely off the person:

> "Rabash says that the sin itself, where a person sins — it's not that it was his fault that he performed this act, but rather it is orchestrated from above, this deed, because they want to bring him down to a certain particular degree, so that from this degree onward he has to start the correction. Which means that what seems to us as a sin is actually not a sin, it's a punishment — and the punishment doesn't come specifically because the person before that was guilty of something. That's it — they lower you in your degree. This is called punishment."

Even "punishment" turns out to be the wrong word once the purpose is in view:

> "Because Adam HaRishon fell into self-love, with all kinds of difficult states in which he truly suffers and does work with great labor — then it's already not punishment, that's his correction. This means that we look according to the emotional state; it seems to us as punishment. But we don't look at it according to the purpose of the whole thing... Therefore, when we come to evaluate states, we should always relate to them with the spiritual, purposeful definition — the way they seem in the eyes of the Creator — then instead of punishment we will see reward; instead of a sin, we will see the determination of a new state that has been prepared in advance and does not depend on the person at all."

## Part 2 — Why the Wisdom Was Concealed, and Why It No Longer Needs to Be

Rav Laitman connects this reframing directly to why Kabbalah was historically kept hidden. Someone who mistakes correction for arbitrary punishment, without the inner preparation to hold the reframe, is at real risk:

> "He reaches a state where — then what should I labor for? Why should I do anything? He just despairs and throws everything away... We see that for us it hardly ever happens. But rather if it does happen, it happens with a person who doesn't yet have the inner preparation."

This is why revelation of the wisdom was always calibrated to the person, not given as abstract information:

> "It's not that Kabbalah in and of itself is the wisdom of the hidden — we're always talking with respect to the person, not with respect to something that exists outside the person... If the person is ready, you can reveal the wisdom. If not, it's not worthwhile to reveal it, because otherwise you might harm him... A person who is not ready for it can take these definitions incorrectly — they won't do what they need to, because they don't have the mind, and the soul is not ready yet to digest the true definitions of reality."

He locates the shift in generational readiness at a specific point: "this ultimately is the reason for the concealment of the wisdom in previous generations, until 1995... until our days, where people already, in large numbers, are altogether sufficiently developed so that they can hear things and not be harmed. This is why now there is nothing to hide about Kabbalah."

## Part 3 — Above Reason, Within Reason, and the Break in Faith

Turning to Adam HaRishon's specific failure, Rav Laitman locates it in a break between faith and knowing:

> "He couldn't hold onto faith above reason because the force of Bina wasn't there. Faith is the force of Bina. The force of Bina couldn't penetrate into this point of the stony heart — the correction that he wanted to make specifically in that point. Certainly he fell from faith to knowing. Only through the power of Bina can we hold onto above reason, to the upper one, to spirituality."

He then defines the three terms precisely, tying "reason" to a specific light:

> "When I receive, and I judge what I receive, and accordingly I am — this means I actually operate according to my nature, according to my mind, according to my reason. This is called operating according to knowledge. Knowledge is the light of Chochma, intellect... Because the light of Chochma expands in the vessels of reception, I get some knowledge about my situation... This is called reason, because reason is the highest degree from all the degrees of reception — it means I work according to my vessels of reception."

## Part 4 — AHP of the Upper One: Every New Ego Is Just the Next Degree's Raw Material

Rav Laitman explains the mechanics of working "above reason" through the relationship between Galgalta veEinayim (GE) and AHP, using a homely image:

> "This is like — you have some dough, and inside the dough you have these chocolate crumbs. These chocolate crumbs are the Galgalta veEinayim, the parts inside the dough itself, which is called the AHP. The light can expand only inside these crumbs, and if they are organized correctly, then out of them the system of the worlds of BYA is constructed... Adam HaRishon, to begin with, is this AHP."

A person's own reason and will, on this picture, are simply the dough — not the working material:

> "A person who works not with his AHP, this mass of the dough, but rather in his Galgalta veEinayim that are incorporated in it as a result of the sin of Adam HaRishon — it is considered that he's working above his reason, above his knowledge, above his will... because the reason, the intellect, his desire, that's the dough, not these crumbs. This is considered working in faith above reason: he tramples on, he steps on the AHP, he makes a restriction against it."

Asked what this means concretely as a person advances, he collapses "the upper one" into the person's own next degree:

> "The AHP of the upper one — who's the upper one? It's me, on a higher degree. This means what's revealed in me, that I need the AHP of the upper one, is to accept it above my own reason, because now I have to arrange vessels of bestowal against this new bulk to receive that's revealed. All these AHPs, on all the degrees from the lowest to the highest, that are revealed in me degree after degree, is me — my own ego, my own bulk, revealed more and more. This is called the AHP of the upper one relative to me."

## Part 5 — What Is Love? The Baby Analogy and the Mechanics of Restriction

Asked directly to define love, Rav Laitman starts from ordinary shared feeling and pushes toward something harder:

> "Love means that there's someone, and whenever he's feeling any pleasure, I am feeling pleasure too... Now the question is: what is the cause and what is the consequence? Either my pleasure comes from the fact that he's enjoying himself — so I check myself: even when I feel bad, if he's having a good time, I'm feeling pleasure. How can I restrict myself first, and then work in order to bestow? This is called true love — unconditional, not dependent on what I'm getting out of it."

The group presses on whether such a split — suffering in one's own vessel while feeling pleasure in another's — is even possible, and Rav Laitman answers with an ordinary example:

> "We see that even in our world there is such a thing — the mother may be suffering, but she feels pleasure because her baby feels pleasure. So there are two types of vessels: what she feels for herself, and what she feels for the baby that's incorporated in her... one of them is that the mother doesn't take the baby to be the reason for her suffering."

He then explains what restriction actually accomplishes, since spirituality allows no gap between wanting and receiving the way corporeal willpower does:

> "In spirituality there's no vessel for action and a vessel for feeling — if I want something, it means I'm already swallowing it. So how do I provide a solution to my will to receive? What do I fill it with, so it will be considered restricted, and given reward, satisfaction? This is called Chassadim."

## Part 6 — From Ein Sof to This World: Restriction in Billionths

Asked why the correction of Chafetz Chesed (delight in mercy) can possibly be strong enough to overcome the will to receive, Rav Laitman locates the answer in how the will to receive itself was built:

> "It corresponds to the Creator — all of the intensity, the power, everything the Creator has, against that He created the will to receive. Otherwise the will to receive wouldn't be able to swallow, to receive all of the Creator, all the light that's revealed. Inside the will to receive there is tremendous capability, exactly like the Creator."

He describes correction as necessarily gradual, arriving only in fragments of the whole:

> "This distancing from the state of Ein Sof to the smallest state that can be from Ein Sof — a person begins to restrict a part of a billionth of Ein Sof, and does an action of receiving in order to bestow on one portion — a portion out of a billion, two, three parts out of a billion, and so on. This means he approaches himself on the ladder of degrees — each degree has to restrict itself, just like Ein Sof itself... That's why we need the time of preparation — if not, you would be able to restrict the great vessel all at once."

## Part 7 — Build the Factory First, Clothe It With Spirituality Later

The session pivots into a live workshop on the group's dissemination enterprise, and a participant asks why spiritual intention should be set aside while the practical organization is built. Rav Laitman defends the sequence by analogy to how the worlds themselves are structured:

> "Anything can be real if it has a corresponding example in nature... for the time being we see there's a Creator, there's a created being — one's above, one's below, one controls, one is controlled — that's how things exist. Meaning, if we won't have a strong organization built according to healthy rationale, as in any business — a manager, and under him heads of departments, and then managers of different kinds, people who perform things — then we won't be able to realize our vessels, our abilities."

The practical structure, he insists, has to be built and functioning before spiritual meaning is layered onto it:

> "Before the desire to receive, before the evil is revealed in a person, we don't talk about corrections, we don't talk about why you need corrections. So we first need to build a system, a good, healthy system — as if you have no business with spirituality whatsoever — and then you'll clothe it with spirituality, after everything functions and you have profit."

Once that stage is reached, the deeper purpose reasserts itself: "the final product in this factory is a spiritual product, and it's for that product that I'm building this factory... the next stage after the structure is presented is already the human stage — we begin to really scrutinize the relations between the workers... these are already things that have to do with internal work."

## Part 8 — Love of Friends Works Better in Small Groups Than Large Ones

A participant reports a concrete finding from trying to build love of friends at scale: "when we tried to present that to a hundred people, it didn't work. Then we tried it in a smaller framework — I got the impression that it's much more effective to try it in a small framework." Rav Laitman confirms the direction and sharpens what makes a small group actually work:

> "We're returning actually to smaller groups — groups that will have a connection, not because it seems to you that someone is attractive by their face or characteristics, but for you to have a connection with them — a connection with the physical work — so that you'll also be able to build, upon that, an additional spiritual connection. And the groups will also be relatively smaller... so you'll nevertheless have a connection with them, and the work will obligate you to constantly return to that feeling: why am I with them, what am I working for, what am I doing with them?"

He contrasts this with the prior, looser groupings, which ran on a subtler form of ego:

> "In the small groups we had before, that was a pretty random, loose connection — these were groups that exist more because each group wanted to stand out over others, and that's where the group ego worked on each and every group. But here you have a connection with each and every one that is much more natural, for the sake of the work — and this will obligate you to connect, and also obligate you to relate, maybe also in a spiritual manner, toward a friend... this is much healthier and less egoistic than the connection we had before."

## Part 9 — Don't Force Structure — Let It Emerge, and Don't Suppress Nature

As the group works out departments, attendance, and a "human resources"-style role for caring for one another, a participant worries the process is taking too long. Rav Laitman is unconcerned:

> "I don't care about a few months, if within these months you're truly discussing each one — where it's worthwhile, what's good for him to work on, and convincing him that it is good... I see nothing wrong with this — you're already, in some sense, in some spiritual work, and the corporeal work, for the timing, it's going somehow."

Asked whether people who resist a role or assignment should be pushed, he refuses on principle:

> "No one is going to go and break things that, out of nature, already exist, because to go against nature is simply — you need to be stupid... each one, altogether in the society, takes the place that, according to his character and his general qualities, he'll find his place, and it won't help anything [to force it]. On the contrary, you should not suppress anything, any desire or inclination in a person — let him develop."

The organizing principle instead is participation: "let's just decide together what is worthwhile for the benefit of society, for each of us to stand — that's why everyone should participate."

## Part 10 — Closing: Choose a Better Environment

The lesson ends with the group sharing impressions, and one participant's summary becomes the night's headline takeaway:

> "The end was very powerful... don't wait for things to happen — come, stand up, make it better. There's nothing that can withstand the desire, and if I have the opportunity to work together, to improve on what we have, that's a good way to connect with people... choose a better environment — for me, that was the key message he shared."

The transcript closes on a related, unfinished thought about why setbacks matter: "if a person doesn't receive descents in the work, then he is probably static, and that's not good, because the descents are an opportunity for ascent and development, and if we don't have them..."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. What looks like sin and punishment from a person's own vantage point is, from the Creator's, orchestrated preparation for correction — Adam HaRishon's act was "prepared for him from the very purpose of creation," and evaluating states by the "spiritual, purposeful definition" turns punishment into reward.
2. Kabbalah's historical concealment was calibrated to readiness, not secrecy for its own sake: revealing the true nature of sin, punishment, and correction to someone without inner preparation risks despair rather than growth, which is why the wisdom stayed hidden until enough people were ready to receive it without harm.
3. Adam HaRishon's fall is defined as a break from faith to knowing — the force of Bina couldn't penetrate the "stony heart," so what should have been held above reason collapsed into ordinary knowledge, the light of Chochma operating in the vessels of reception.
4. AHP names a person's own ego and will — the "dough," not the working material — while Galgalta veEinayim are the crumbs within it through which light can actually expand; each new degree of ego revealed is simply "the AHP of the upper one," meaning a person's own next degree.
5. True love is defined as unconditional pleasure in another's pleasure, illustrated by a mother's non-contradictory suffering and joy over her baby — a split only possible once restriction has separated "wanting" from automatically "receiving."
6. Correction proceeds by billionths, not all at once, because a will to receive built to match the Creator's own intensity is too vast to restrict except in gradual, degree-by-degree portions — which is the entire purpose of the preparation period.
7. The group's practical dissemination work follows the same doctrine in miniature: build a healthy, functioning organizational structure first, "as if you have no business with spirituality whatsoever," and only then clothe it with spiritual purpose.
8. Love of friends measurably failed at the scale of a hundred people but succeeded in smaller groups built around real shared work — a connection that is "healthier and less egoistic" than groups formed around mutual attraction or competing for status.
9. Rav Laitman refuses to impose structure on the group from above, insisting that nature and personal inclination not be suppressed and that participation, not command, should determine where each person lands — closing on the reminder to "choose a better environment" and not wait for things to happen.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Faith Above Reason / Within Reason / Below Reason | This lesson defines "within reason" as judging by what one's own mind (the light of Chochma) reports; "above reason" as trampling on that judgment to hold onto faith (the force of Bina) instead — a capacity Adam HaRishon lost when Bina couldn't penetrate his "stony heart." |
| AHP | Acronym for Awzen-Hotem-Peh; here glossed as the "dough" — a person's own reason, ego, and will — as opposed to Galgalta veEinayim, the "crumbs" within it where light can actually expand; each new degree of AHP revealed is described as "the AHP of the upper one," i.e., one's own next degree. |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes"; together with AHP, the two components of a Partzuf. In this lesson's dough analogy, GE are the chocolate crumbs — the only part of the mixture the light can actually enter and organize into the worlds of BYA. |
| Bina | The force of faith above reason; this lesson attributes Adam HaRishon's fall specifically to Bina's force failing to penetrate the "stony heart," breaking his connection to faith and dropping him into ordinary knowing. |
| Tzimtzum (Restriction) | The mechanism that separates wanting from automatically receiving, making bestowal possible; illustrated here as proceeding gradually, "a portion out of a billion" of Ein Sof at a time, rather than all at once. |
| Chafetz Chesed | "Delight in Mercy" — the corrective force powerful enough to counter the will to receive, described in this lesson as matched in intensity to the Creator's own power, since the will to receive was built to that same scale. |
| Ahavat Chaverim (Love of Friends) | The cultivation of connection between friends; this lesson reports a direct practical finding that it works in small groups built around real shared work, but failed when attempted across a hundred people at once. |
| Rabash | Rabbi Baruch Shalom Ashlag; author of this lesson's article, "When a Person Knows What Is Fear of the Creator" (Article No. 35, 1985), and the source cited for reframing Adam HaRishon's sin as preordained correction rather than fault. |
