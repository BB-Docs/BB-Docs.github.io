### Opening

If the concealed belongs to the Creator and only the revealed belongs to us, what exactly is left for a person to actually do? The session opens with a short teaching about Moses and the menorah that states the division plainly — action is ours, intention is His — before the archival 2002 lecture takes up the article's real question: why wasn't reality simply created already corrected? The answer runs through the whole session: a vessel that only feels, discerns, and eventually unites with the Creator has to be built in stages, out of a gap that is revealed to a person only as fast as he can actually bear it.

## Part 1 — You Do the Revealed Part, I Give the Concealed

Before turning to the day's main article, a short teaching about the menorah sets up the whole session's theme:

> "Making the menorah was difficult for Moses, until the Creator showed him with his finger, as it is written, 'This is the work of the menorah.' We should interpret this in the work: the light of the Creator will shine, yet this requires vessels of bestowal... The Creator showed him with his finger and said to him, 'This is the work' — that is, you will do the work, and I will give the intention. This is considered that you will do the revealed part, and I the concealed part."

The point is reinforced with a reminder about the previous session: "Only from Lo Lishma can we reach Lishma, and we should never forget that — our will to receive still needs to receive something."

## Part 2 — Why Reality Wasn't Created Already Corrected

Turning to the archival lecture proper, Rav Laitman frames the article's governing question directly from the verse it's built on:

> "Rabash wants to answer a question here: why was reality created in such a way where we are not at the end of correction, not in terms of our powers, our abilities, our attainment? ... This is called 'the concealed things are for the Lord our God' — meaning the intention in order to bestow requires the corrected state, the correction. And 'the revealed are for us and our children' means that gradually, slowly, it is revealed to us."

The reason it must unfold this way rather than all at once is that a vessel can't simply be handed over complete: "It would have been impossible to create this vessel that will feel the end of correction in any other way, if not like us — these vessels which begin to be fashioned and improved from zero, more and more, through to the corrected state. And it's not that we can do something other than to recognize the necessity of correction, and who it depends on." The alternative — total absence of understanding — is named without softening: "It's real confusion, it's complete, absolute detachment from all the causes and the forces and understanding. And this is all in order for us to gain the corrected state."

## Part 3 — Two Opposite Points: The Empty Vessel and the Point That Can Do Everything

Every state a person passes through, Rav Laitman explains, is defined by discovering two opposite points at once:

> "His vessel is completely empty, and he has no spiritual awareness, consciousness — nothing from his own side. This is called the point of Malchut. And he also discovers, on the other side, the point of Keter — the Creator, who can do everything, is omnipotent, can do anything, is good. That is the highest, most desirable goal. When a person discovers these two things, which are opposite to one another in form, this means that he is worthy of that degree in practice, and then the Creator does those mysteries."

Discovering both points isn't automatic; it requires a specific kind of readiness: "A person needs to display some readiness for that. And readiness, or preparation, means to discover these two opposite points — the importance of the Creator, as we call it, and the lowliness of the created being. In that gap we have the vessel that receives the purpose."

## Part 4 — Torah as Light That Corrects and Fills; Records and the "Request"

Asked why the Creator doesn't simply give a person the power to change his own intention outright, Rav Laitman locates the answer in what Torah actually is:

> "Torah is the force that corrects the person and the force that fills the person. The Torah is light — it comes as the light that corrects the vessels and the light that fills the vessels. It's all called the Torah. What the person does, he makes preparations — that is called a commandment. The light that fills him as a result is called Torah."

The mechanism by which a person actually draws that light is reframed away from a literal transaction: "This is what we call the records [Reshimot] — the sum total of details and discernments and feelings and experience all together, that awaken the upper one to assist the lower one. And it's not that the upper one doesn't want to — the upper one is a higher degree, and if your desire is to awaken that higher degree, then it's an indication that you can be part of it. And that is the issue of the request. It's not a request — it's simply compatibility with the vessel of the upper one. The upper one feels your vessel, and then, because of that feeling, you're equal to the upper one — that means you've risen to its degree."

## Part 5 — Suffering in Bearable Doses: Why the Path Runs 125 Degrees

Asked why the will to receive isn't simply revealed to a person all at once, Rav Laitman explains that a person's capacity to tolerate the gap between himself and the Creator is the actual limiting factor:

> "The will to receive that's revealed in the person brings a person suffering, because it's revealed with no feeling — only a lack. So the will to receive cannot be felt by a person if it brings only suffering; the person cannot tolerate it. If my will to receive was revealed from my degree all the way through to Ein Sof, infinity, it would be a suffering that's completely incomprehensible — the illumination of the terrible black Malchut. But right now it's revealed to me only a little bit of that gap... Let's say I feel Ein Sof through a distance of 125 degrees maximum, and then it doesn't appear to me that my state is so severe, and I can tolerate it. The person is shown the measure of his oppositeness to the good by his readiness to suffer it, and that readiness is very limited — that's why this scale, the path from us to Ein Sof, is divided into 125 degrees."

He adds that each new degree first looks impassable before it doesn't: "When the will to receive is revealed for the first time on each degree, it appears to us simply as the Red Sea — impossible to cross. Later, bit by bit, we begin to see that it's okay, it's possible somehow... illumination simply comes from above, gradually, the surrounding light, and it gives us the feeling that it's not so terrible." Not everyone continues: "There are people who fill that gap and they run. And there are those who continue a bit further, and by the study and their persistence, they see that it's not so bad."

## Part 6 — From the Gap to the Vessel to Turning to Him

The gap itself, once discovered, is what actually becomes a vessel:

> "To the extent that I reveal this gap between myself and Ein Sof — between me and the degree above myself, which appears to me to be the goal right now — to that extent I am awarded with a vessel. To be awarded with a vessel there are many conditions, because a vessel is a lack that is worthy of receiving spiritual fulfillment, a lack worthy of the Creator coming to fill it, to dwell in it. This lack needs to be suitable to the Creator — adapted to the Creator, meaning with the same intention, which is in order to bestow."

Recognizing that the lack doesn't yet match the Creator's own intention is itself what turns a person outward: "I discover that I'm not in order to bestow, and so the Creator cannot unite with me. Then comes the recognition that I must correct myself. I can't correct myself either, so then I begin to understand that I need to turn to Him. And then it's revealed to me that this was all prepared by Him from the start. By turning to Him, I begin to unite with Him already, to connect to Him. And that connection is the goal."

## Part 7 — Reality Doesn't Change; Only the Intention Does

Asked directly what actually separates a Kabbalist from someone still in preparation, Rav Laitman rules out anything external:

> "It doesn't matter if it's in the beastly world or the spiritual world, between angels or beasts — we're talking about the person himself, his attitude towards reality. That's the difference: intention. What is reality — cars, buses, people, houses — does that change or not, if I'm below the barrier or above it? Reality doesn't change. The bus and the house and the children and the books and the groups — they remain the same. I change from the intention in order to receive to the intention in order to bestow. Now here we learn what 'the concealed is for the Lord God' means — the difference between this world and the world to come is the difference of inner intention."

He extends this to perception itself: "When you begin to see through the reflected lights, reality appears to you completely differently — meaning how it appears, not what it is. You don't see the house suddenly as being something different, but rather its essence, in the intention and purpose of creation, you see differently. You don't even know why, for what reason, but here you discover in everything a purpose. The intention changes your perception, but that is all that changes — externally, nothing changes."

## Part 8 — When Intention Determines the Action Itself

Pressed on whether correction changes anything a person can actually see — including suffering in this world — Rav Laitman gives a careful, hedged answer that the intention in order to bestow, not the corporeal action itself, is what is actually being judged:

> "Everything is tested according to the intention in order to bestow contentment to the Creator. The intention in order to bestow is the determining factor, and the action — call it slaughtering, call it stealing, whatever you say — it doesn't matter. If such a thing could be, I don't know, yes or no. But if there is a true intention in order to bestow that necessitates such a thing to happen, it will happen. I don't want to say more here, and I don't even want to think about the action. I'm saying that the intention in order to bestow will determine the actions we would do."

Asked separately about a line describing someone who won't accept correction being "banned from society without mercy," Rav Laitman is similarly careful and uncertain about how literally to take it, noting only that such a person's exclusion — from the group, the city, or further — would itself be treated as his correction if nothing else reaches him, and observing that even in the "last generation" some people accept correction only because they're willing to follow without understanding it, not because they've attained it themselves — "they will be corrected, but through us, not through themselves."

## Part 9 — Closing: Shame as a Springboard, Correcting All Souls, and What Makes a Connection "Holy"

Asked how to relate to shame and to one's own past, Rav Laitman distinguishes between shame that paralyzes and shame that moves a person forward:

> "When we cross the barrier, we feel shame with respect to the giver of the pleasure. If I feel that the shame is something He sends me purposefully, that it comes from Him, then it becomes a springboard. Whoever thinks about the past is like the wife of Lot — it becomes like a lump of salt that cannot be dissolved; you can't eat it, you can't be without it, but only in a way that you add it to food. Soon, I promise you, you'll see that the past for you will have no importance whatsoever — only for the sake of the present will you need it."

The session closes with what full correction actually looks like. Reaching it, Rav Laitman says, must happen in this world — "a person who is not in this world cannot reach the final correction; the final correction is a state that we reach here, while we're living here" — and it isn't private: "I can reach my personal final correction only if I connect with all the souls on my behalf — by being sorry with their sorrow, taking their sorrow, absorbing it within me, raising it to the Creator, and in such a way I correct my personal part that is incorporated in each and every soul." He illustrates with a theoretical case of ninety-nine souls already corrected and one still uncorrected: she doesn't feel the other ninety-nine percent as her own, "because she has nothing to feel it by — her point in the heart is still black and dead. But the moment it starts to work on that point, it will immediately begin to feel that everything is ready for her." Asked what actually makes a connection between people "holy," Rav Laitman roots it in the word's own meaning: "Holy is that which is differentiated, distinguished, or separated — because we have in creation only two parts, in order to receive and in order to bestow. I devote this, dedicate this, to Him — that's called a sanctification to someone specific." The transcript's live captioning ends mid-thought, on the last named preparation: "the preparation to fill the gap between me and the Creator is only in the desire to want to know Him... we're still confused, it seems to us that all those things are different things — the spiritual world and the Creator and the world—"

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. The verse "the concealed is for the Lord, the revealed is for us" is read as a division of labor: a person supplies the revealed, corporeal action; the Creator supplies the concealed intention — action and intention are not the same contribution.
2. Reality wasn't created already corrected because a vessel can't be handed over complete; it has to be "fashioned and improved from zero," built out of the very gap between a person's emptiness (Malchut) and the Creator's completeness (Keter).
3. Torah is described as light in two functions at once — correcting the vessel and filling it — drawn not by literal petition but by "records" (Reshimot) whose compatibility with a higher degree is itself the "request."
4. The will to receive is revealed gradually, across 125 degrees, specifically because a person's readiness to suffer the full gap between himself and Ein Sof is limited; each new degree looks like an impassable Red Sea before it doesn't.
5. Discovering that one's own lack doesn't yet match the Creator's own intention (bestowal) is what turns a person to ask for correction rather than for fulfillment — the same "concealed/revealed" split as Part 1's menorah teaching.
6. Reality itself never changes between a beginner and a Kabbalist — the same buses, houses, and people remain; only the intention behind a person's relationship to that reality changes, which is what "the difference between this world and the world to come" actually means.
7. Correction is framed as something that must be reached while embodied in this world, and as inherently collective: a person's own final correction depends on absorbing and correcting his "part" incorporated in every other soul, not on private attainment.
8. A connection between people is called "holy" specifically when it is directed toward bestowal rather than reception — the same root sense of "differentiated, distinguished, set apart for a purpose" that defines holiness elsewhere in the tradition.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Shlavei HaSulam ("Rungs of the Ladder") | The published collection of Rabash's articles; this lesson's source, cited from Volume 3, Torah portion Nitzavim, built around the verse "the concealed is for the Lord our God, and the revealed for us and our children." |
| Malchut | The tenth Sefira, "Kingdom" — the receiving vessel; this lesson names it as "the point of Malchut," a person's complete emptiness and lack of spiritual awareness, discovered opposite the point of Keter. |
| Keter | "Crown," the highest of the five-part Partzuf structure; this lesson names it as "the point of Keter" — the Creator, who "can do everything, is omnipotent... the highest, most desirable goal" — discovered opposite the emptiness of Malchut. |
| Reshimot | "Records" — retained impressions from a soul's prior states; this lesson describes them as what "awakens the upper one to assist the lower one," reframing prayer/request as compatibility of vessel rather than literal petition. |
| EINSOF (Ein Sof) | "Infinity"; this lesson uses it as the endpoint of a 125-degree scale — the full, incomprehensible gap between a person and the Creator, revealed only in bearable measures matched to what a person can actually tolerate. |
| Kedusha | Holiness, the quality of bestowal; this lesson glosses the word itself as "that which is differentiated, distinguished, or separated" — a person's dedication of something specifically toward the Creator, out of only two possible directions, reception or bestowal. |
