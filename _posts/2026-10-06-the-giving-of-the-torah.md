### Opening

The Congress is closing, and Rav Laitman steps back to the widest picture: all of reality is a single **thought of creation**, the desire to do good to His creations, and our task is to reach adhesion with it. But we are like a ship lost in the ocean, unable to see the goal or know which of our forces to use. What steers the ship? His answer is the group, built on **Arvut**, mutual guarantee. The lesson moves from the structure of reality, to the group as compass and engine, to what "giving myself to the group" means, and ends with the descent everyone is about to feel and how a world group can keep itself from ever falling.

## Part 1 — Reality Is One Thought

- Reality is built in layers. Above them all is **Atzmuto**, which we don't know. Kabbalists who reach the topmost layer say what they find there is a single thought, the thought of benefiting His creations, also called the **Creator**.
- This thought "starts to actualize itself": it creates created beings and descends stage by stage, through Partzufim, worlds and Sefirot, until it reaches our lowest degree. "There is none lower than that."
- Our task is the reverse: rise through these levels until we feel this thought "as some kind of a field that is simply passing through us and operating in our whole world."
- **Adhesion** comes by **equivalence of form**, by how much we make ourselves similar to that thought. Inside it we feel good, wholeness, eternity; what is outside it comes to us as murkiness, a "gray" or "black" covering called concealment, felt as suffering.
- The thought acts on us gradually, revealing more and more of the gap between us and it, felt as growing suffering, until we search for a way out. Later we want to unite with it because it is good; later still, "not because it evokes within us good feelings, but because this is how it is."

#### From bitter and sweet to true and false

- For now we only have vessels for "tasty, sweet" and "bitter suffering." An additional sense will come, called **true and false**, which does not depend on the will to receive.
- When we begin to live by true and false instead of bitter and sweet, "this new discernment will be called our existence in the upper worlds." Life is redefined: not filling the will to receive but existing inside the thought of creation, "without any distinction of ourselves."
- The work divides into heart and mind: an effort to feel and an effort to understand. Understanding here means the data that come from feeling, the **recognition of evil**: feeling dead in the heart and knowing in the mind why.

## Part 2 — The Ship, the Compass, and the Engine

> The person inside of the group is like a ship in the ocean that doesn't know where it is and which way it should advance.

- Our state is even worse than a lost ship: we can't see the goal or the way, and we don't know which of our qualities are good or bad, or how to use them.
- The **group** is a platform, a foundation under the ship that turns it in the right direction. "The group is truly like a compass and an engine." If I am inside it, the compass directs me and the engine drives me toward the goal in the optimal way. (He ties this to Baal HaSulam's article on free choice.)
- The group also gives a diagnosis that is "correct, true, measurable": not only my own self-monitoring, but feedback from outside on whether I'm on the path, and whether I'm investing all my forces or only a quarter.
- **Only one effort.** "They say that I have to make only one effort with myself: to give myself to the group." After that, the group operates me. "It's not that I operate myself, but rather it will operate me."

## Part 3 — The Giving of the Torah and the Arvut

- "The Giving of the Torah" explains what the method of correction is for; "The Arvut" explains the condition for receiving it. Without an external system in which I'm incorporated as an inseparable part, "where there is no I, but rather there is we," the method can't be received.
- The method of correction is the **screen and reflected light**. If we build a system where each one is a guarantor for the others, "we will receive from above the reforming light," the light of AB-SAG, the light of Torah. Until then, we are in the time of preparation, "building our collective ship, our flying carpet."

#### What Arvut does to the will to receive

- We are built of a will to receive, the "murkiness" in which the thought of creation is clothed, and it must be filled in every state.
- If a person is certain he will lack nothing, and that this will always be so, the will to receive lets him rise above it and care only for others. In the upper worlds this is the force of **Bina**, the light of **Hassadim**, which gives the vessel the correction called **Chafetz Chesed**: it wants nothing more for itself, and from there it can begin to bestow.
- Baal HaSulam calls this a psychological force. All our lives we look for exactly this: a guarantee for old age, illness, whatever may come. "I'm always concerned about the next moment."
- **"Who are these friends, a few beggars like me?"** It doesn't matter. What matters is a person's inspiration from his small society, and "in the multitude of people is the king's glory."
- **Isn't this just a calculation?** No. The reason I want confidence is different: I'm built of the will to receive and must rise above it. I come to the group so it will "brainwash" me, influence me so I'm ashamed to use my will to receive and feel it is filled enough, and then I can "throw myself into the group": not to fall on their hands so they care for me, but to pass all my forces to our common goal.

#### Moses, Aaron, Joseph, David

- Moses (Netzach) and Aaron (Hod) received the Torah only because after them came Yesod and Malchut. Joseph, a minister in Egypt, took into himself all of Egypt's will to receive and so became Yesod, "the righteous one, foundation of the world," and David is Malchut.
- Then the nation had to be large, representing all the souls of Adam HaRishon with the small coarseness of that time. Today our coarseness is much greater, so a small group is enough to be impressed by. Still, the group should keep expanding, and each one in it will feel more confidence and responsibility.

## Part 4 — Building the Arvut, Step by Step

- **What comes before Arvut?** Its "posterior": a deficiency for Arvut, a need to connect with the Creator, who is revealed only inside the Kli of Arvut.
- The practical step is **mutual advertisement**: every group to its friends, and all the groups to one another, raising the **greatness of the goal**. Rabash calls this the **greatness of the Creator**. When the society supplies each one with enough of it, "no world around can disturb us."
- **What Arvut requires.** It is a consequence of building the correct environment, one that supplies:
  - the greatness of the Creator and of the mission of adhesion;
  - confidence that we can reach it, and the necessity of reaching it ("there's no other way of existence");
  - friends who care for him more than he cares for himself;
  - recognition of evil (we can't live this way), recognition of good (only in adhesion is there life), and confidence that the leap is possible.
- **The stream.** When the desire is aimed correctly, "we immediately enter a certain flow" that draws us from one degree to the next, "it's called the Messiah." We don't make the ascents ourselves; "the light does that." We only build the Kli, the **MAN**, the deficiency, and that deficiency comes only from the group, reinforced by the world Kli.
- "There cannot be any success for anyone or any group if it won't be a general success for the whole world group." There must be a **critical mass** of correctly aimed deficiency.
- **Since the Congress,** a person should think of the greatness of the Creator not only in his small group but in the whole world group. We have one goal, one bylaw, a common foundation (*Yesod*). Still work in the small group, where feedback is immediate, and toward the big group and eventually all of humanity.
- The world itself helps: from the end of the twentieth century, humanity is discovering that its life is at a dead end, and even physicists now speak of one thought operating all of matter. Arvut comes to us "from the outside world, from your internal despair, from the group."

## Part 5 — One Kli, No Great or Small

- **New students vs. veterans.** Don't calculate by country, size, or seniority. Students who came a few weeks or months ago grasp these matters quickly, because what's needed is not knowledge of Talmud Eser Sefirot but **discernments**, which "are received very quickly."
- **Like kneading dough.** Each new ingredient has to be mixed in; soon the differences between groups become minute. "In one Kli there's no great and small. It's not that these are nice words; this is how the vessel functions." Coarseness below and refinement above complete each other, direct and reflected light, upper and lower triangles.
- **Is there an individual factor?** We're connected by qualities, not physically. The inner **HaVaYaH** (the four phases of direct light) gives forth branches, all divided into Keter, Hochma, Bina, ZA, Malchut. Keter in one group connects with all the Keters in the world group, Hochma with all the Hochmas. That's why the group is "like a mash that all materials have been mixed in."
- **Work in the group, not on yourself.** "Not to work on himself — to work in the group, for the group." That is called **bestowal**. If you think "I study, I want, I," you enter the path of suffering: years later the surrounding light shows you that you went in the opposite direction. That's why we need the group to "obligate me... not allow me to go in the opposite direction," not by blows but by showing its importance.
- Advancing to the Creator is like advancing to the center of the group. In the center, the "I" disappears, and "that's where the building of the first line begins."
- **"I came to the group, what do I know?"** A person can't build himself; he needs a model. Entering the group, the will to receive enters a mold, "and from it comes out the image of the Creator."

## Part 6 — The Worlds Are Degrees of Giving Myself to the Group

- To the extent a person can immerse himself in the group with all his desires, he has entered the feeling of the spiritual world.
- Giving myself partially, twenty or thirty percent, is some degree of the worlds: "the world is my partial relation to the group." Giving myself one hundred percent is **Gmar Tikkun**. Giving even "one gram" to the friends or humanity is the first feeling of spirituality.
- The worlds aren't "up there." That's only how we depict them. A person enters "from himself inwards, into this thought that is revealed to him all over the world."
- **The changing yardstick.** Ascent and descent aren't feeling good or bad. An ascent can be being closer to the Creator while feeling worse. What changes is the *quality* of the scale a person weighs good and bad with, not the quantity. Things worthless to the will to receive, like an act of giving, love, or incorporation, gradually become important. That's why the Sukkah's covering is made from the waste of the barn and winery: "where you find the greatness of the Creator, there you find His humbleness."
- **Root coarseness and the screen.** The vessel begins when I restrict all my desire relative to the importance of the group and of bestowal, "not choking myself forcefully, but with a correction against the desire." From that restriction, the **screen** begins to be built, and with it I can use my will to receive for incorporation with the society. Outwardly life continues ("we will still have cups and napkins and tables"); only our vessels change.

#### Wicked and righteous

- Before the thought of creation is revealed in a person, all his actions are incorrect. Whoever cannot obey this field with his own recognition is called **wicked**, "even if he does it without any awareness." Those in double or single concealment are complete or incomplete wicked.
- Once it is revealed in our senses and we act from it, we are called **righteous**, incomplete or complete, by the measure of its revelation. Above the barrier, the upper force controls us and we can't act incorrectly.
- "Try never to leave the domain of holiness": if I feel this thought, it determines my thoughts and actions. Outwardly my actions look like anyone's; inside there's a different feeling.

## Part 7 — The Creator, the Group, and Me

> The society and the Creator become as one, because the power of bestowal is bestowal.

- If the society is in bestowal, I'm in bestowal, and the Creator is in bestowal, "what's the difference between them?" To the extent I'm incorporated in the group, I develop forces of bestowal, and by equivalence of form I feel the Creator. "Where do we meet Him? We meet Him in the group."
- So "I cannot advance more than the group, the group cannot advance more than each of its individuals, and the Creator cannot be revealed within us more than we are." Within certain bounds, one can be a little beyond the others according to his contribution, but there must be a general yearning from which one can soar.
- **One teacher, one source.** A world group must receive from one source at the same time, or at least the exact same lesson. Groups that study the same material from someone else "will not be groups." Beginners' introductory lessons are an exception for the first ten or fifteen lessons only.
- **How a small group helps the big one.** Through one organization: shared plans, activities, mutual help, publications, and material help. "But it's still external things. The important thing is the work." An embrace doesn't have to be physical, and a hug can hide a knife.

## Part 8 — The Coming Descent, and a Group That Never Falls

- The groups have felt an ascent and are starting to feel small descents. In a week or two everyone will feel the coarseness: "how bad it is, how dark it is... leave us alone." He waits for it without fear, though he remembers descents where "you feel death." Rabash used to say he wished there were a sleeping pill to take from the beginning of a descent to its end.
- **One person vs. a group.** Rabash said a single Kabbalist would shut himself in a room and dance and sing to pull himself out. A group never has to be in a descent, because "when one jumps up, the other one is down, and altogether they maintain their general level." Your descent is covered by my ascent if you're incorporated in me.
- **Responsibility and shame.** The fear that "someone can fall because of me" saves us. Don't stay in a descent more than a minute; at the first sense of falling, reconnect, and the connection becomes stronger. Rabbi Zusha had hundreds of descents and ascents in ten minutes.
- **The promise.** "If we do this in the world group, I guarantee everyone a life like in paradise," with no harm, physical or spiritual, because all the bad comes from concealment of the light. Kabbalists, Baal HaSulam among them, became sick because they took on the deficiencies of humanity they were incorporated with.
- **A group can't all fall at once.** Like a body with healthy and sick organs, a group connected by its Reshimot from Adam HaRishon supports itself unconsciously.
- **How to prepare for a descent.** Don't come down from the general feeling of the world group, the thought of it, the concern for it. That concern is the **MAN**, "actually a plea to the Creator to guard us."
- **Leaving the Congress.** In earlier years, groups cried when leaving; once he came out of a goodbye with the Moscow group in a wet shirt. This time they just got on the bus, a sign they didn't feel they were separating, just as one who enters the upper world doesn't feel the body's death as separation.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Reality is one thought.** The thought of doing good to His creations descends to us; our work is to rise into it by equivalence of form.
2. **From bitter/sweet to true/false.** Living by true and false, independent of the will to receive, is existence in the upper worlds.
3. **The group is compass and engine.** Make one effort, to give yourself to the group, and it steers and drives you.
4. **Arvut frees us from the will to receive.** Confidence from the friends lets a person stop caring for himself and begin to bestow.
5. **Build Arvut through the greatness of the goal.** Mutual advertisement among all the groups creates the deficiency that draws the stream from degree to degree.
6. **The Creator, the group, and I meet in bestowal.** None can be revealed beyond the others; the worlds are degrees of giving oneself to the group.
7. **A world group never has to fall.** Mutual responsibility turns individual descents into a cycle that keeps the whole group rising.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Thought of Creation** | The single thought of benefiting His creations, the topmost layer of reality and the force operating all of it; also called the Creator. |
| **Arvut** | Mutual guarantee: each member's certainty that the group will provide for him, which frees him from his will to receive so he can bestow. |
| **Greatness of the Creator / Goal** | The importance of adhesion, which the society must supply to each member through mutual advertisement. |
| **True and False** | A new sense, independent of the will to receive, replacing "bitter and sweet"; living by it is existence in the upper worlds. |
| **Chafetz Chesed** | The correction from the light of Hassadim (Bina) in which the vessel wants nothing for itself. |
| **Equivalence of Form** | Becoming similar to the thought of creation; the measure of adhesion and of revelation. |
| **MAN** | The deficiency, built only through the group, that draws the light; concern for the world group is MAN. |
| **Screen** | The force, built from the restriction of desire relative to the group's importance, that lets the will to receive be used for bestowal. |
| **Wicked / Righteous** | Those who cannot yet consciously follow the thought of creation, versus those in whom it is revealed and who act from it. |
