### Opening

The lesson opens with a paradox from the article: the ashes of the red heifer purify the impure but defile the pure priest who prepares them. Rav Laitman uses this as the doorway into a single, central question — what actually happens to a person the moment he takes on faith above reason? The surprising answer is that beginning this work does not make a person feel more righteous, but less: a person who felt himself pure while working for reward discovers, the moment he tries to work in order to bestow, that he is in fact steeped in self-love. From there, the lecture and an extended Q&A trace what faith above, within, and below reason actually mean, why growth in this work is only possible outside the will to receive, and how a person is meant to relate to the discernments the "upper one" hands him — even when they flatly contradict what his own senses report.

## Part 1 — The Paradox: Defiling the Pure, Purifying the Impure

The article's opening image is the ash of the burned red cow — a residue of something cancelled, annulled. Rav Laitman ties this directly to what happens the instant a person accepts faith above reason.

> "Before he began this work, when he worked in order to receive reward... he felt that he was righteous. He saw that he was observing the mitzvot and did not find within him any flaw... But when he begins the work of bestowal, and to believe in the Creator above reason, he sees the truth: that he is immersed in self-love and is devoid of faith. It follows that now he has become impure. This is called 'to defile the pure.' But afterwards he is rewarded with 'to purify the impure.'"

The apparent contradiction — how the same subject can be called both pure and impure — is resolved by recognizing these as two different points in the same process: contentment with reward-based observance looks like purity only because it hasn't yet been tested against the standard of bestowal.

## Part 2 — Why Creation Requires the Opposite of Perfection

Turning to the article proper, Rav Laitman lays out why the created being cannot simply be handed perfection and adhesion outright, even though the Creator's purpose is only to do good to His creations.

> "The creator cannot perform an action which is below his level... And so when he created the created being, he created him exactly in adhesion, perfection, eternality, which is equal to the Creator's own degree. And for the created being to reach that feeling — who he is, what he is, where he is, who he belongs to — he needs to recognize this state, to know it in full. And he needs to come to know it from within his nature, meaning from the opposite: from something from nothing, existence from absence."

Full recognition, in other words, requires passing through the opposite of eternality and perfection — in actual experience, not just in thought — so that the difference between "existence from absence" (the created being's own state) and "existence from existence" (the Creator's) becomes something felt, not assumed. Only through that contrast does a person come to recognize evil correctly: not as feeling bad, but as being in a state inferior to the Creator's.

## Part 3 — Settling for a Little: Why the Will to Receive Can Never Reach Perfection

Rav Laitman explains why perfection is structurally impossible from within the will to receive alone.

> "Within the will to receive, there cannot be any perfection. Because the will to receive is enclosed within itself. It feels what's within it, it attains what's within it, and therefore it is limited in quality as well as quantity... Settling for a little means that each time he attains something, what he attains suffices for him, and he doesn't want to expand it — because expanding the border means he now needs to suffer from a lack."

Left to itself, the will to receive would always settle and freeze in whatever comfortable state it reached — food, warmth, rest — with no motive to suffer for something more. Growth only happens because "the upper providence" keeps adding more will to receive, pressuring a person forward through society and the books. True, unlimited fulfillment is only possible by emerging from the will to receive entirely — living not in order to receive but in order to bestow, with the intention aimed outside of oneself. Love of others, he notes, is not the goal itself but the means: the environment and the exercises given "here in this world towards others" accelerate the recognition of just how limited the self-enclosed self really is.

## Part 4 — Below Reason, Within Reason, and Above Reason

The bulk of the Q&A turns on distinguishing three ways a person can act on the information he receives, illustrated with a scenario of being shortchanged at a bank counter.

> "Whatever I feel, whatever I understand, what I accept as fact — that is called reason. And if I act according to that... it is called faith within reason... There's faith below reason as well, where I don't know anything about what I'm being told... I'm not activating my senses, my reason, anything... Usually we call that fanaticism."

Within reason and below reason, he stresses, are not limited to religious contexts — most of humanity's decisions, in some proportion of the two, work this way. Above reason is different in kind:

> "If I stand at the bank and I ask for a thousand dollars, and it's counted for me, and I see that he's counting nine hundred and ninety-nine — what should I do? ... I can look at it and see: wait, this is nine hundred ninety-nine. And to act according to what I see — that's called below reason, no criticism... And that is called going above reason: I need to emerge, to exit myself, to concede, relinquish my reason, and to accept his reason. It's not simple. It's not some inner trick you can just perform."

If what's missing is not one dollar but nearly all of it, and a person is still asked to behave, in mind and heart, as though he received the full amount — that requires more than external agreement or acting; it requires actually filling the lack with real inner fulfillment, which he says is impossible without genuine spiritual attainment. It cannot be willed into being as a decision or a game played with oneself.

## Part 5 — Above Reason Belongs Only to the Sixth Sense

Asked whether above reason applies to the corporeal versus the spiritual generally, Rav Laitman narrows the claim considerably.

> "Above reason is not corporeal against spiritual... We don't have, in any of our corporeal desires, our five senses — we cannot act above reason. Above reason applies only to the spiritual vessel, the sixth sense towards the upper light, towards the Creator... The vessels remain the same vessels, the beastly ones. It doesn't change... It's only our sixth sense that we talk about. It changes."

The body keeps feeling pain and pleasure exactly as before — "it can be a great Kabbalist; if you poke him, stab him, he'll cry" — because above reason is a law that governs only the point in the heart, the sixth sense, which is the sole point of contact with the light and the Creator.

## Part 6 — Calibration: Faith Above Reason as Equivalence of Form

Returning to why the examination between above and below reason matters, Rav Laitman reframes the "test" not as an exam but as a calibration.

> "By this I'm checking what kind of regard I have for divinity — meaning, am I perceiving reality correctly?... For me to know what exists without, I need to correct my sixth sense so that it is the same as what exists outside. So how? I accept these corrections on me... that gives me equivalence of form between the sixth sense and the external world."

He extends the bank illustration into a calibration metaphor: if the cashier calls something white that looks black to me, and I want to feel correctly, I have to "update my reception device" — like a scale or radio dial that must be calibrated before it can read what's actually there. Above reason is what lets a person accept the upper one's discernments as fact even when they directly contradict his own five senses, and it requires an established connection and trust in the upper one — it isn't a technique that can simply be tried out to "see what I profit."

## Part 7 — Growing Above Reason: From Embryo to Birth

Closing the main lecture, Rav Laitman maps the stages of spiritual growth (Ibur, embryo) directly onto the logic of faith above reason.

> "You are willing to cancel everything you have, to give everything up that you have, in order to be adhered to the upper one without the intellect, without reason... You adhere to him like a seed inside the womb. That's called an embryo. That's where you start growing from."

As the embryo develops — flesh (desire to receive) is added, along with doubts and "shells" (klipot) that work against the upper one — the person must keep annulling himself and staying adhered despite them. At forty days, corresponding to Bina, a first faint likeness to the upper one appears; after nine months, birth brings dependency on the upper world for sustenance, just as an infant depends on milk. Every stage, he stresses, runs on the same law: growth happens only outside the self, through forces added against the upper one that a person must keep choosing to emerge from.

## Part 8 — On Recordings and Repetition

In the closing Q&A, a student asks whether listening to recordings conflicts with wanting only the surrounding lights during study. Rav Laitman answers from his own history as a student of Rabash.

> "When I came to Rabash I studied before that three, four years, searching where I could study Kabbalah... The second time I already had a tape recorder... I started to record from the second lesson... I don't think it's possible without that... What I'm building here in these lessons is a bridge between a modern human being and the books of Baal HaSulam."

He recommends going over recordings repeatedly — even passively, in the background, at work or half-asleep — since the material is absorbed regardless of conscious attention, and describes traveling long commutes with recordings in his ear as part of his own practice.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Taking on faith above reason does not make a person feel more righteous — it does the opposite at first: someone content in reward-based observance discovers, only once he tries to bestow, that he was steeped in self-love all along. This is "defiling the pure" on the way to "purifying the impure."
2. The created being must pass through the actual opposite of perfection and eternality — not just understand it abstractly — in order to fully recognize his own state and correctly identify evil as inferiority to the Creator, not as feeling bad.
3. The will to receive can never reach perfection on its own; it is self-enclosed and will always settle for whatever it has. Growth only happens under outside pressure, and true unlimited fulfillment requires emerging from the will to receive entirely, aiming intention outward.
4. There are three modes of acting on information: within reason (accepting what your own senses/understanding tell you), below reason (accepting something on no criticism at all, i.e. fanaticism), and above reason (consciously overriding what your own reason reports in favor of the "upper one's" reason) — and everyone operates on some mix of the first two.
5. Above reason applies only to the sixth sense — the spiritual vessel, the point in the heart — never to the five corporeal senses or ordinary bodily pain and pleasure, which remain unchanged regardless of spiritual attainment.
6. Above reason functions as a calibration of one's own perception (equivalence of form) against what genuinely exists outside oneself, not as a self-administered test; it requires trust in and connection to the upper one, and cannot be adopted as a trial technique.
7. Spiritual growth follows the stages of embryonic development (Ibur) — total self-annulment, then the gradual addition of desire and doubt (klipot) that must be worked through by staying adhered — culminating in a birth that remains, just as in this world, dependent on the upper world for sustenance.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Faith Above Reason | Consciously accepting and acting on the "upper one's" discernment even when it directly contradicts what a person's own reason or senses report; per this lesson, a law that applies only to the sixth sense, requiring genuine connection to the upper one rather than being a technique one can simply try. |
| Faith Within Reason | Acting according to whatever a person accepts as fact through his own understanding, regardless of whether that "fact" is actually true; per this lesson, how most of humanity's everyday decisions are made. |
| Below Reason | Accepting something with no criticism or examination at all — described in this lesson as fanaticism, whether in religious or entirely secular contexts. |
| Ibur (Embryo) | The first, most preparatory stage of spiritual growth: total adherence to and annulment before the upper one, "like a seed inside the womb," from which flesh (desire to receive) and doubt gradually accumulate. |
| Equivalence of Form | The alignment of a person's inner sense with what genuinely exists outside himself; in this lesson, described through the metaphor of calibrating a receiving instrument (a scale, a radio dial) so it reads reality correctly rather than one's own distorted sense of it. |
| Tzadik (Saddiq) | "Righteous" — in this lesson, the state a person mistakenly believes he's in while working for reward and finding no fault in himself, prior to discovering, once he attempts to bestow, that he is actually immersed in self-love. |
| Bina | The sefira of understanding/bestowal; in this lesson, the degree reached at "forty days" of embryonic growth, marking the first faint likeness between the growing soul and the upper one. |
