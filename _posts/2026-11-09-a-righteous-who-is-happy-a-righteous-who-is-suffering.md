### Opening

A person who goes out to the work of the Creator passes through changing states, and no state is the same as the one before. How should we understand being "wicked" or "righteous," feeling good or feeling bad? Rav Laitman explains that these are not moral labels. They measure how far a person adapts himself to Providence, and a person can truly call the Creator good only to the extent that he has acquired that quality himself. From there the lesson asks why spirituality looks attractive to us at all, and answers with a simple picture: a "box" of concealment behind our senses, the only place where we ever work.

## Part 1 — Four States, Measured by Equivalence

- We need to get used to change and accept every change "with a purposeful understanding that this is how it has to be."
- A wicked who feels good, a wicked who feels bad, a righteous who feels bad and a righteous who feels good are stages set by the measure of correction a person goes through.
- He is measured by how far he agrees with the Providence working on him, with the goal, and with the forces compelling him to advance.
- We have a will to receive, a will to bestow, an intention to receive and an intention to bestow. Accordingly "we have four degrees of our attitude towards the Creator's Providence."

#### You can only justify what you have attained

- To adapt to Providence is to equalize form with the Creator, and "to that extent he can justify the Creator."
- "I can't say you are good if within me this quality of good that I point to does not exist." Saying "it is good" theoretically, out of regard or schooling, "is not regarded in spirituality."

> Either according to our measure of equivalence of form, we justify the Creator, or according to the disparity of form, we curse Him. There's nothing in the middle. If there is something in the middle, it's called concealment.

## Part 2 — Above and Below the Barrier

- **Q: Do these four states also exist in the time of preparation?** In preparation we have neither equivalence nor oppositeness of form. Only above the barrier are there the forces of the **Klipot**, the left line, and the qualities of the Creator, the right line.
- "Both Kedusha and the Klipa are above the barrier. We haven't reached it yet, therefore we're not yet wicked, and all the more so we're not yet righteous."
- **Wicked and righteous are two states on the same degree**, and every degree has both.
- On the left line a person is immersed in his uncorrected vessels. "He must feel it entirely, and then he is called wicked." For the state to be true, the right must be completely concealed from him.
- Above the barrier these are concrete states, clearly inscribed, because a person carries them out according to the **Reshimot**. As far as he implements them, he becomes similar to the Creator and takes Providence into his own hands on that degree.
- Below the barrier we have something corresponding, but foggy and not under our control: "as in a dream." The closer a person comes to the barrier, the more he justifies the Creator and is drawn to the true Creator, "not the way he depicted to himself."

#### The wicked who feels good

- This state can occur even to very advanced people. A person sees that right now he doesn't care about the goal or the Creator, only about feeling good and gaining something from the study, perhaps honor.
- He looks at himself from the side and determines: this is my state, and I don't even want it to change.
- "There are no degrees in spirituality" as things that exist in and of themselves: "the degree is what you determine." After you determine your true state, your name is set accordingly.
- **Q: Do the states change gradually?** Yes, but the process includes quick flips, ups and downs, so that a person gets impressions of the gaps between states.

## Part 3 — The Curtain and the Box

- **Q: In reality the wicked do feel good. And if the righteous feel good, why doesn't everyone run to spirituality?** In this world the wicked manages well, because "this world is wicked, so it fits the wicked." In spirituality, the righteous does.
- But no one is drawn to spirituality as it really is. "What we're told, we translate to what feels good to us." We hang a nice curtain over spirituality, paint on it what we wish to have, and run toward it.
- Then the work begins. Blows and changes come, I see the curtain a little differently, and I accept my next state as good. Each time "we are being corrected to see spirituality in a truer perspective."
- If it opened to us now, "there would be nothing more despicable and hateful," and the Creator would appear as a source of evil. Even the closest upper degree must stay hidden: "You can't bear it."

#### Concealment is inside me

- **Concealment** is "a kind of internal system within us that knows the whole truth" and brings it down through all the degrees, transforming it to fit my state so that it seems good to me. "That whole mechanism is called concealment. Or we can call it the five worlds."
- I perceive Ein Sof, the complete state, in all my senses. But behind the senses a box deciphers that data for my will to receive and gives me the form I can now see and feel.

> Just draw the five senses, then they come through wires to a box, and from the box they reach a place called the will to receive.

- "We're only working on that box." Its intentions to receive should gradually be corrected to in order to bestow. Then what enters the box and what comes out will be similar. "Nothing changes other than my perception."
- In one who wants to correct himself, a point awakens in that box. Through it he corrects the whole program of perception, and he is given the chance to see himself from the side, objectively.

## Part 4 — Energy Techniques Are Not Spirituality

- **Q (from the internet): What's wrong with methods of leaving the body, observing it from the side, meditating?** Psychologists also speak of observing oneself, Rav Laitman notes, "but it's not the same thing."
- He describes a Tibetan doctor who explains the body through medicine and through energies. Each organ is also a source that takes in and emits energy. "These are physiological things... it's all below the barrier."
- Someone who observes his thoughts accumulates an energy he can direct for good or bad. "These are corporeal things." Less egoistic peoples simply kept control of forces of nature that we lost by developing in a more egoistic direction.
- Using it is like anything physical: engage in it for healing and you do good, for harm and you do bad. A Kabbalist with a headache can take a pill, get a massage or use guided imagery. "It's the same energy" at different levels, not the light revealed above the barrier.

#### Where the danger lies

- "People can make an idol from anything and detain themselves in their spiritual development," and we often don't know the boundary between this and that.
- The goal of such a practice is harmony with the universe, peace and wholeness. "That's not a deficiency for equivalence of form, for seeking godliness. It's a deficiency for equivalence of energy to the universe."
- If such a person receives a **point in the heart**, he will come, "but this technique does not lead to a deficiency."

## Part 5 — What It Means to Agree with Providence

- **Q: What does it mean to agree with Providence?** "Everything I feel in my world is something perfect because it comes from the upper force." Not because I feel good or bad, but "for a simple reason, because it comes from the Creator." This is the degree of a righteous.
- It can include horrible things, and a person still says it is whole and perfect. As an example he recalls Rabbi Akiva, whose skin was being torn from him as he read the Shema: "We're talking about the greatest shell compared to the greatest sanctity, and they're connecting on the highest degree." We can't understand how this happens.
- **Q: How do you disagree with the situation yet agree with Providence?** I don't agree with the state of being immersed in the nature of my will to receive, and I agree with the feeling that I'm correcting that state. "That's the left line and the right line on the same degree."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Righteous and wicked are not ethics.** They measure how far a person adapts himself to Providence, and both exist on every degree.
2. **Justifying requires equivalence.** We can truly call the Creator good only to the extent we have acquired that quality; anything else is words.
3. **The real states are above the barrier.** Below it we have only foggy counterparts, and the closer we come, the more we justify the Creator.
4. **The degree is what you determine.** A person must objectively define his own true state, even "wicked who feels good."
5. **We chase a painted curtain.** Spirituality as it truly is would be unbearable to us now; each correction shows it more truly.
6. **All the work is on the box.** Concealment lies behind the senses, and correcting its intention changes nothing but perception.
7. **Wholeness in this world is not the goal.** Energy methods are corporeal and useful as medicine, but they don't create a deficiency for equivalence of form.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Wicked / Righteous** | Two states on the same degree: immersion in uncorrected vessels on the left line, and justifying Providence on the right. |
| **Four states** | Wicked or righteous who feels good or bad, the stages of a person's attitude to Providence. |
| **Providence (Hashgacha)** | The upper force acting on a person; agreeing with it means seeing all that comes as perfect because it comes from the Creator. |
| **Equivalence of form** | Acquiring the Creator's quality, the only basis for truly justifying Him. |
| **Disparity of form** | Oppositeness to the Creator, by whose measure a person curses Him. |
| **Barrier (Machsom)** | The boundary above which Kedusha, Klipa and clearly defined degrees exist. |
| **Reshimot** | The records a person implements to make degrees clear and inscribed in him. |
| **Concealment** | An inner mechanism, also called the five worlds, that converts the whole truth into what I can currently perceive. |
| **The box** | Rav Laitman's picture of what lies between the senses and the will to receive; the only place we correct. |
| **Point in the heart** | The desire for the Creator, which energy techniques do not produce; in the box, the point from which correction begins. |
| **Left line / Right line** | Disagreeing with one's state and agreeing with Providence, held together on the same degree. |
