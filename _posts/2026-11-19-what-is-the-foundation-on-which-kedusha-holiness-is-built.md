### Opening

The second half of the article explains the three wells. Over the first, Esek, the herdsmen of Gerar quarreled with the right line, telling Isaac's servants their small work wasn't worth rejoicing over. Over the second, Sitna, they slandered the left line so that the servants couldn't pray. Over the third, Rehovot, the middle line given by the Creator, there was no quarrel. Rav Laitman picks up from there: why the right line is the line of truth and the middle line a compromise, when the Creator enters the picture, why He must stay hidden, what the greatness of the Creator actually gives a person, and why the work on the greatness of the friend is the work itself.

## Part 1 — The Right Line Is Truth

- "The right line is always called the line of truth." We say we walk in the middle line, but "the middle line is not truth. The middle line is a compromise": truth according to my strengths for the time being, "but it's not called the absolute truth."
- "If I could become one hundred percent similar to the right line with all my left line, that would be absolute truth. But I'm not able to." So I work in order to bestow as much as I'm able, and the middle line "is still a result of the effort," of the corrections.
- At the end of correction the left and right lines "literally become one line. It's not the middle line but one line," as in Adam Kadmon before the lines, when BYA rises above the Tabur to the root. "The created being, without any calculations... is incorporated in the Creator, is similar to Him and becomes like Him. This is truly truth."
- After that comes the correction of the stony heart, where there are no lines at all. Malchut of Ein Sof returns to the form of a circle, but now "there's no boundary on the part of the vessel," because there is a screen over all the qualities of the created being. Not the square form of one line, not the triangle of the second restriction: "everything returns to no boundary."

## Part 2 — When the Creator Enters the Picture

- **Q: Which lines do we build through the group, and when does the Creator enter?** A person performs all kinds of actions, in the study, in the group, wherever he can, to shift himself toward bestowal. As a result "he begins to enter darkness," with an occasional illumination.
- After double concealment comes single concealment. Now "he no longer hears what he is told." He begins to understand by himself how he's being worked on, sees the Creator a little, and sees his own nature: "how much he nevertheless does not agree with what the Creator does to him."
- From holiness's side he sees what must be done; from his own side he sees he can't do it. "This is a very uncomfortable state, but a juicy one. He truly sees both sides, and cannot decide in favor of holiness." He knows it's forbidden "and still he does it."
- He demands strength from the books, from the group, from everything, trying to cling to the right line, which means acting "not according to reason... but above it. It is a foolish act, as it were." A long time passes "until truly all the bitterness takes shape inside of him." This is faith above reason in the preparation period: body, mind and the whole world say one thing, "and right next to it you see that godliness is not like that. Step over these calculations."

#### The cry for help

- Through these struggles he reaches a decision that he must ask the Creator for help. It doesn't end with success: "the more he succeeds, the more he comes to a state where he does not succeed."
- Here a person can do foolish things: go with faith below reason, think he's doing the deeds of a complete righteous one, and act foolishly in ordinary life. "So here truly intensive care is needed."
- Gradually everything is arranged from above, until "he cries out to the Creator, not to someone else, and then he receives help."

## Part 3 — Why the Creator Must Be Hidden

- The will to receive "can be corrected... only if the will to receive feels dependent." If I want something and you won't give it, I'm dependent on you and you can set conditions. That's how I go to work for an employer: "We subdue the will to receive."
- If the Creator were revealed, "it would be like everything else in life." We'd see He is great, take off our hats, and run to work for Him for the best things. Even a professor will trade his science for good food: "ask him to develop a bomb for me and he does it."
- "To reach in order to bestow, it is precisely necessary that He be hidden." If He's revealed, "I'm already addicted to the pleasure that comes from Him. I need to be addicted to the idea itself... to the very idea of in order to bestow."
- We must come to where "I say, bring me all the pleasures. No, I want corrections." Since wanting pleasure is our nature, "this is possible only through suffering": the vessels of reception are given suffering until it's better not to use them, and pleasure and suffering are also given in the intentions.
- There are two systems, bitter and sweet, and truth and falsehood, and both must go through a reversal, until we "demand only the truth, in order to bestow."

## Part 4 — What the Greatness of the Creator Gives

- "What does the greatness of the Creator give me? Well, He's great. What do I care whether He's great or small?" The will to receive is built so that connection with the upper one gives it pleasure and confidence. So recognizing greatness can serve me "as pleasure without receiving from Him."
- Bring a great person a cup of tea and you enjoy it: is there enough sugar, is it dark enough? You get your sustenance from giving to him. Whether I receive pleasure from giving him tea or from taking a hundred shekels, "the body received the same excitement." The difference is "that this is something eternal, something unlimited," and beyond that, the giver gains "the standing of being the bestower like the Creator."

#### Annulment as a means, or annulment for him

- **Q: If I annul myself before a great one, where do I get the desire to be like him?** From annulling yourself you won't get that desire. "If you crave pleasures, you annul yourself before him, receive pleasures. That's the end of it."
- "But if you annul yourself because you want him to have it good, then you begin to study him, to know him, know the God of your father and serve him."
- When you absorb his deficiencies, "you absorb deficiencies of bestowal... You're already replacing your inner software." What does He lack? He wants me to have it good. Connecting to His deficiency to bestow, "I acquire new vessels from Him, truly of bestowal... a different nature": the first nine Sefirot joined to the black point of Malchut, and then "I can be like Him."
- "It cannot be clear. For that we need to cross the barrier. It's a parting of the Red Sea. A miracle." "All my words are just to soften the ear."

## Part 5 — There Are No Great Ones

- **Q: If giving to a great one gives the same pleasure, why does the will to receive resist it?** "Giving to a great one gives unlimited pleasure. The problem is that there are no great ones next to you. Rather, you feel yourself great."
- "Great means that in your understanding, your feeling, he can fill you." Give billions of people such a feeling and they'll cling to someone, "like chicks under their mother." "The problem is that there are no great ones. That is why the common people are miserable." Whomever you think is great turns out to be "an inflated balloon."
- Even people released from a labor camp sometimes want to stay under its regime: it's a great one for them, and they're at peace. "It doesn't matter what it rules over... if we really accept it as great." The feeling of the root automatically brings us its qualities.
- The difference between the greatness of the Creator and of a friend? "The friend, if you make him great, is no longer a friend, he's already a Rav." Anyone greater than you is called a Rav, whether the Creator, a teacher or a friend: "whomever you choose as great."

## Part 6 — The Greatness You Depict Is What You Acquire

- Looking around the room, Rav Laitman sees only one friend doing good work, sitting and laughing and trying to awaken a good mood in the others, whether or not he succeeds. "I haven't seen any other face here... someone laughing, happy that he's sitting here and he was given from heaven such privileges." Instead, "beaten-down, miserable people who were simply gathered up, lifted out of bed and brought here."
- "This is not what we said we need to do toward one another." If you can't decide together, perhaps each should sit quietly in his corner and think about what is really demanded of him. "Maybe you talk too much."

#### The thousand-shekel example

- If I serve a friend who is as messed up as I am, what quality can I receive? "If you think that he's greater than you... then precisely that same importance you gave him by him being greater than you, you will acquire that same quality."
- Suppose I don't steal below a hundred shekels, and I think you don't steal below a thousand. "That's greatness. I'm speaking seriously." Your limit is ten times mine, so you think about the Creator ten times more, are devoted ten times more. "I made you great in my eyes and by this I already begin to acquire this quality, whether it's in you or not." You don't need to teach me. "You can be a liar and steal whatever you see. That's not important."
- Work this way with a friend and with the Creator, since you don't see Him either.

#### Still Lo Lishma

- But this way you won't acquire in order to bestow. You've only changed your calculation: "spirituality is more important and great than corporeality... Better sustenance. The bigger Lo Lishma is there."
- "To move from this Lo Lishma to Lishma, you must have Him revealed," to acquire His real quality and not what you imagine from the will to receive.
- Where does the desire for His revelation come from? "Because you will feel that the surrounding light that's coming to you, that what you have is not real, that it's not Lishma." The surrounding light adds to your Lo Lishma "a grace of Kedusha, so that you will want to do something completely disconnected from yourself."

## Part 7 — Questions: Inputs, the "I," and Taming the Beast

#### We don't remember or forget

- **Q: In blows a person feels the greatness of the Creator; when things improve he forgets. How does he keep it?** "We don't forget and we don't remember and we don't do anything. We are given in our mind, in our heart, conditions from above."
- Picture the "machine called an Adam": in the heart he felt blows, in the mind that there is a Creator. Then he's given another state where he feels good without the Creator. What discernment is asked of him? Compare precisely what was put in and where. "We'll study cybernetics... the bodily systems work in such a very simple way."

#### Depicting the friend as great

- **Q: Is the best way to the greatness of the Creator through the greatness of the friend? And then can I relate to everyone equally?** You don't recognize greatness in the friends; "you need to depict to yourself that your friend is great. To imagine each and every one." By intellect and feelings, "everyone is zeros."
- That's how the "I" relates to the whole world, "the godly part from above, the I in us." Like a dream of walking into a huge city and there's no one there: "It's I alone. That's actually what a person is." Even the Creator isn't greater than me, unless He'll give me. This "I" is the stony heart, which changes only at the final correction.
- When I really do see someone as great, a problem comes at once: either I cling to him so his greatness becomes mine, or I lower him. "The work upon the greatness of a friend is work. And the more you depict him as great, you will be impressed by your imagination."
- Besides the inner work, "that everyone's great and I'm small," play an external game toward the others, "so that everyone will be impressed from you, and by that it will return to you their impression, and you'll receive it in truth. Because what you feel from others, your body is impressed."

#### Feeding the beast

- The world has lost its past and future, and from morning to night it's troubles, so it runs on entertainment. In the Great Depression people bought a movie ticket instead of bread to watch the rich enjoy life, and felt satiated. "That's what a person lives from, that lie. So why don't you want to use this?"
- Relate to your body "as a person to your beast, from above downwards. This beast is tormenting my life. How can I make this beast serve me? So I have no choice. I have to simply feed it lies," like the carrot ahead of the runner, or the lure in front of racing dogs.
- This isn't fantasy: "That's called a serious attitude, the correct and mature way to the beastly body... and how to truly operate it. You tame a dog."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Truth is the right line.** The middle line is a compromise according to our strengths; at the end of correction the lines become one, and finally return to a boundless circle.
2. **Single concealment is uncomfortable but juicy.** Seeing both holiness and one's own nature, and being unable to choose, builds the bitterness that ends in a true cry to the Creator alone.
3. **Concealment is a must for bestowal.** If the Creator were revealed we'd serve Him for pleasure; only through suffering, in the vessels and the intentions, do we come to want corrections instead of pleasures.
4. **Greatness lets us enjoy giving.** Giving to a great one gives the same pleasure as receiving, but unlimited and eternal; annulling oneself so that He will have it good opens His deficiencies and gives new vessels.
5. **There are no great ones around us.** We feel ourselves great; "great" means one who can fill us, and whoever we choose as great becomes our Rav.
6. **The greatness you depict is what you acquire.** Imagining the friend as greater passes his quality to me, though by itself this is a bigger Lo Lishma; the surrounding light brings the need for revelation and Lishma.
7. **Use the imagination to tame the beast.** Inwardly see everyone as great and myself small, outwardly play that game for the friends, and feed the body the "carrot" that makes it serve the goal.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Three wells (Esek, Sitna, Rehovot)** | Isaac's diggings: Esek (right line), quarreled over by the herdsmen of Gerar; Sitna (left line), from "Satan," slandered so no prayer could form; Rehovot (middle line), given by the Creator, with no quarrel. |
| **Right line (line of truth)** | Wholeness and gratitude above reason for even a small grip on the work; called the line of truth. |
| **Middle line** | A compromise: bestowing as much as one is able; still a result of the effort and the corrections. |
| **One line / circle** | At the end of correction the lines merge as in Adam Kadmon; after the correction of the stony heart, the vessel has no boundary at all. |
| **Single concealment** | A state of inner struggle where one sees the Creator a little and his own opposite nature, but cannot decide in favor of holiness. |
| **Greatness of the Creator** | What lets the will to receive enjoy giving without receiving from Him; pleasure that is eternal and unlimited. |
| **Rav** | Anyone a person chooses to regard as greater than himself: the Creator, a teacher or a friend. |
| **Greatness of the friend** | The quality one depicts in a friend, which one then acquires, whether or not it is in him. |
| **Lo Lishma / Lishma** | Serving because spirituality is a "better sustenance" (Lo Lishma) versus acquiring His actual quality of bestowal, which requires His revelation (Lishma). |
| **Grace of Kedusha** | What the surrounding light adds to Lo Lishma: the wish to do something entirely disconnected from oneself. |
| **The beast** | A person's body and will to receive, to be operated from above, fed with the "carrot" of greatness so that it serves the goal. |
