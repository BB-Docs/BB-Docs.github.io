### Opening

If a person can't even feel what he's missing, how can he honestly pray for it? The session opens with the article's own puzzle — that in corporeal matters, prayer is simple: name the lack, ask for it to be filled. But in spiritual matters, a person by nature has no felt deficiency for bestowal at all; the desire to give doesn't exist in the will to receive he was born with. The archival 2002 lecture and the live Q&A that follows both circle the same question from different angles: if the one thing worth praying for is a vessel you don't actually want yet, where does the wanting come from, and what is actually left for a person to do?

## Part 1 — Work and Reward Are the Same Thing, Felt Differently

Rav Laitman reframes the ordinary opposition between labor and payment before turning to the article itself:

> "We always get confused: what is the work and what is the reward? But the purpose of creation is to benefit his creations, and the state of the end of the work is rest... actually everything depends on the receivers. And the work could be the reward, and the work could be felt as rest. And the exertion depends only on how we relate to it. Do we feel it is labor, or do we feel it is pleasure?"

He brings Baal HaSulam's image from the letters (page 146) to explain why exertion feels like suffering at all: a store owner who pockets two shekels the instant he makes each sale never experiences the sale as labor, because the payment arrives with no delay. "All of the suffering comes from the distancing — the distancing of the payment for the work." The conclusion is that the raw material never changes: "Our work is only to correct the vessels — the feeling, our inner definition of what for us will be a reward, labor, payment. And then eventually we reach a state where there is only good and pleasure, and this is all only because we corrected our vessels" — the same abundance that was always being poured out is simply felt, in the corrected vessel, "with a new meaning."

## Part 2 — The Deficiency Has to Be for a Vessel, Not for Fulfillment

The article's title supplies the session's central distinction, which Rav Laitman states plainly:

> "In corporality we want fulfillment. And in spirituality there needs to be a deficiency for having a vessel. Without a deficiency it's impossible. There has to be a vessel. The question is, what is the deficiency? What is a vessel? What are you yearning for? According to what you're yearning for, that will be a reward for you."

Naming the reward correctly changes everything: "If the reward for you is to bestow — an opportunity to bestow — and you enter a state where it is possible, you can do it, then we are done. Then you reached rest and joy." But that particular deficiency, he stresses, can't be self-generated: "A deficiency to bestow, you can get it only from the Creator, because he seemingly has this deficiency to bestow. So what does he say — that we have to pray? One has to pray for this primary deficiency. So go ahead and pray. It's all written in the title, right? The main deficiency for which one should pray."

## Part 3 — The Trick of Concealment: Praying for What You Don't Actually Want

Asked how a person could ever reach a state where his prayer is only for this, Rav Laitman is blunt about the obstacle:

> "This is against our nature. This is called 'I labored and I found.' We have no possibility, in some place within us or in our existence, to find such a deficiency. Let's not delude ourselves — we don't have it."

The way through is not honesty but concealment: "If now you could see what it means to bestow, you wouldn't ask for it. So they shut it away from you, and you are seemingly willing to ask... Even though you're asking from afar, that you don't have a true desire — from Lolishma you come to Lishma." What makes the asking possible at all is a leftover spark: "Because you have some kind of a desire to bestow, however it's corrupted — it's in the captivity of the will to receive from the shattering of the vessels, you have this spark. So you can seemingly say that you want to bestow, and you feel suffering. And this gives you the possibility to make some kind of preparation until they give you this power. And this is called that you cross the barrier." The concealment itself, he adds, is necessary precisely because what's being asked for is so contrary to a person's nature: "Without it we would never — I mean, what am I, crazy, asking to truly die? ...the worst, most contradictory thing — how would I be able to ask for it? But as it is, the concealment allows me to do so." And the "lie" of this preparatory period has its own purpose: "It's not that we will seemingly make some efforts and then transition into the truth, but rather because in these states, in this lie, we make transgressions and mistakes, and we need those later... If you have enough iniquities, the Creator sees that you made enough labor, then he takes you over the barrier."

## Part 4 — Where the Deficiency Actually Comes From, and What's Left for the Person

A sharper worry surfaces: if you pray for something you don't understand, could the Creator simply grant it and cause harm?

> "Can you pray for something you don't know? ... And the Creator will give you what you're asking for, right? And you will get something that you don't need. This thing can actually be harmful — if they give it to you from above, it could be harmful."

The resolution turns on what a real prayer actually is: "First of all, how can there be a prayer if it's not an inner feeling in the heart? The prayer is truly what the heart feels within itself — not what I heard now in some advertisement and I wanted it. This means a true deficiency that my vessel begets truly from within it. And from within it, the vessel can beget a deficiency only through the light that influences the vessel from above." That reframes who is actually doing the asking: "This means the Creator actually creates this deficiency, and not me... So where am I here? I am the one who only feels that his actions are being done on him." What remains for the person is narrow but real: "What is upon me is only to try all the time to be as sincere as possible with what I feel, and tie it to him as the only source that performs all the actions... He truly wants to check it all the time — good or bad, doesn't matter, true, false, everything — but maximum in an open manner: throw away all the shells, all the confusions, all the formulas, all the habits, everything that he was taught, and connect there inside with the Creator."

## Part 5 — Finding the Grip Point: The "Uncle Shmuel" Analogy

Asked what the practical starting point actually looks like, given all the fogginess described above, Rav Laitman offers an everyday comparison:

> "Practically, the effort starts with this society. Because when you search within — where can I find, can I feel some point where I can have a connection with the Creator? — as, let's say now, you can't remember some uncle you have. I remind you that you have Uncle Shmuel. You begin to establish his character — it's like this, like that, connected to this and that, he lives over there, he has this kind of a face — you start to seemingly connect to him, to find that point that you have in your memory, in your heart, towards him."

The same mechanism, he says, is how a person reaches toward the Creator: "So it's the same thing — you go through all the events of this world, suddenly you remember, ah, the Creator. So now you're searching: what is that point? What data do I have about him? Where is his file? You find his file, meaning now you remember who he is, what he is — you search precisely for that gripping point. A point of connection where you and him connect. It's in the mind and in the heart. If you don't have it yet, then open that file and start writing down the things that you can accumulate, that you can collect." He specifies what kind of "picture" this is — "meaning an impression... mostly his inner image, his inner character, how he is, what he is, what is his personality" — so that a person can "start to talk gradually about your connection with the Creator, not as a beastly personality, God forbid, but about your impressions," tying it to the verse that the paths of Torah and the Creator are one: a person yearns for such a grip and holds it through the very actions the Creator advises.

## Part 6 — Two Models of a Society: The Kotsk Disciples and This Group

A question about outward behavior toward the group draws out a contrast between two legitimate but opposite strategies:

> "There are two forms of behavior towards the society. Either the behavior as it was amongst the great ones who had a burning fire in their hearts, and on the outside they could show neglect — like Kotsk, the disciples of Kotsk... These are people who were in high degrees, in yearning and in true work, internal endless work and efforts — and on the outside they demonstrated complete disparagement towards everything, to the point that it looked like they were complete Gentiles, each and every one of them... on purpose, so that there will be no grip to the internal work that they had. And this is out of seriousness, absolute seriousness."

This group, he explains, deliberately does the opposite, because it is not yet at that level of internal certainty and needs the society's outward impression to build its own: "Each of us needs to receive the impression for our spirituality from the society — otherwise, what will there be? ... If everyone will be neglecting and disparaging right now, then I'll operate only according to the externality. I won't have the possibility to see into the hearts of the friends. So I too will start not taking things seriously, like them." So instead: "We behave in laughter, non-serious way — we act as if we really want spirituality, and as if we love one another, and as if we're willing to do everything for one another. When we do that, nevertheless, it's true that we're lying — no doubt about it, and everyone understands. But the body understands what it sees, what it hears — that's what it believes, and then he's impressed by it." He compares this to marital advice from psychologists — "you need every day to tell your wife that you love her... because that's what's heard" — and closes the point: "That's why we need to really show this, talk about it, and that's how we will hold on."

## Part 7 — Closing: The Group's Own Fogginess, and What They Take From the Lesson

In the closing round, the live participants apply the lesson directly to themselves. One names the same blurriness the lecture described — "I'm foggy. I need you to help me get out of this fogginess" — and is answered in exactly the terms Part 6 laid out: "We will help you, Ronnie. We will demonstrate love of friends to you. Connection is important, bestowal is important." Another participant singles out the Kotsk disciples as worth emulating specifically for their seriousness, even though their outward form is the opposite of this group's own practice — "how important it was for them so that there would be no grip to the internal work that they do... it was very important for them to preserve the most internal, intimate thing a person has in his work." A third brings in David's point that revealing one's own failures and transgressions is itself part of the work: "In accordance with the ones that we reveal, the Creator sees that we put forth our labor and gives us the forces to advance" — paired with a caution to guard against the ego quietly taking credit, "as if I'm doing something" of one's own accord.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Work and reward are the same raw material, felt differently — suffering comes specifically from the *distance* between exertion and payment, not from the exertion itself; a corrected vessel feels the very same abundance "with a new meaning."
2. Corporeal prayer asks for fulfillment; spiritual prayer must first ask for a vessel — a deficiency for bestowal — since the desire to bestow doesn't exist in a person's given nature.
3. Concealment is what makes this prayer possible at all: if a person could see what bestowal really means, he wouldn't want it, so the desire is kept hidden while a leftover "spark" from the shattering lets him seemingly ask anyway.
4. A prayer only counts as real if it rises from a genuine inner deficiency — and even that deficiency is created in the vessel by the Creator's light from above, not manufactured by the person; what's left for a person is sincerity and attaching everything he feels back to the Creator as its only source.
5. The starting point for feeling any connection to the Creator is compared to reconstructing the image of a half-forgotten relative — searching within for a "grip point," a genuine impression, rather than reasoning about an abstraction.
6. Two opposite but legitimate models of group behavior exist: the Kotsk disciples hid a burning internal fire behind outward neglect because they were already certain; this group instead shows outward enthusiasm and love deliberately, because the body believes what it sees and hears, and the group still needs that impression to build its own inner conviction.
7. Revealing one's own failures and transgressions to the group, and to oneself, is treated as part of the actual work — evidence of labor that lets the Creator "take a person over the barrier."

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Kelim / Kli | Vessel(s) — the desires/capacities through which delight and pleasure are received; this lesson's whole argument turns on the difference between a vessel for reception (already given at creation) and the still-missing vessel for bestowal, which alone is worth praying for. |
| Lo Lishma / Lishma | Not for Her sake / for Her sake; this lesson describes the path from Lolishma to Lishma as itself enabled by concealment — a person asks "from afar," without a true desire yet, using the leftover spark of bestowal left in him from the shattering. |
| Machsom | The barrier; per this lesson, a person is taken over it once the Creator sees he has made "enough labor" through the transgressions and mistakes of the preparatory, concealed period. |
| Klipot / Klipa | Shells; referenced here as what a person must throw away — "all the shells, all the confusions, all the formulas, all the habits" — in order to connect sincerely and directly with the Creator. |
| Arich Anpin (AA) | One of the five Partzufim; this lesson cites the Sefira Bina's departure from "Rosh de AA" (the Head of Arich Anpin) during the second restriction as an example of something not felt as a deficiency because it isn't yet needed — illustrating that lack is only real when it is actually needed and its absence causes pain. |
| Environment (Sviva) | The society/group; this lesson locates the entire practical starting point for feeling any connection to the Creator in the impression received from the environment, and frames the group's outward shows of enthusiasm and love as deliberately built to impress the body, since it cannot yet believe anything it doesn't see or hear. |
