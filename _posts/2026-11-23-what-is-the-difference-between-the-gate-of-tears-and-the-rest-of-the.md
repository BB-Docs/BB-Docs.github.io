### Opening

Our sages said that since the ruin of the Temple the gates of prayer have been locked, but the gates of tears have not. Then why are gates needed at all? Rabash answers that the gate of tears is locked before one who cries over luxuries, like a child squabbling over a toy or the rabbi who wailed for a diamond ring for his wife. It opens only for one who cries over necessity: to be simply a Jew who loves the Creator and isn't immersed in self-love, and who, having tried every other gate and found it locked, cries for his life. So the first prayer is to know what to pray for. Rav Laitman explains what makes a desire a true desire, why the Gate of Tears is the first spiritual degree, how necessity is acquired, and why we need an advertising department aimed at ourselves.

## Part 1 — A Desire That Doesn't Change

- The other gates "are not gates that definitely open for us an entrance to the goal... Rather, they merely help us reach the Gate of Tears." The tears are "a measure showing that what I want, I truly want it."
- A child cries and in five minutes laughs: "his pain is not true pain and his joy is not true joy." A person is capable of great things "only if his desire does not change": when all desires join into one that seizes him entirely and "simply conquers the person forever."
- "Crying is a sign of smallness." The desire is fierce but doesn't give him the strength to attain what he wants, and besides the desire he must know to whom to direct it. "This is called a broken heart."
- This is "the recognition of the breaking of the vessels," not from the side of receiving in order to receive, but recognizing how incapable he is, and that only in the Creator is the light that can correct him. "Then I realize the first spiritual record. I raise my first MAN, and this means that I've reached the Gate of Tears," like "one who stands at the door holding out his hand."

## Part 2 — Necessity and Luxury

- **Q: How does a person check what's necessary?** At first the desire for spirituality seems a luxury: we labor for the necessities of this world and give spirituality an hour or two. If a person goes through the stages of preparation, "spirituality is a necessity for him and all the rest is luxury. He inverts the whole scale of values on its head."
- Then he cries out of weakness, unable to carry it out, "because he certainly has nothing else to cry about." He has passed all the gates, examined them, used them to build his deficiency, "and saw that truly there is only one gate for entering spirituality." "That is the Gate of Tears; we build it, this state within us."
- Before necessity there are calculations, "I still have something else to do in life," until "I already live out of the soul, and I want to feel only it."

#### World, year, soul

- **Q: Why is this tied to Yom Kippur?** To come into contact with the Creator, three conditions are needed: world, year and soul, like the high priest in the Holy of Holies on Yom Kippur. On it "I discover all my empty vessels corresponding to the five afflictions, the five phases in a vessel."
- At the closing prayer (Ne'ila), after all the prayers, "I see that even my prayers do not help." "The Creator does not help because you cry, rather through all these things, the point of unification is formed... beyond the barrier." It doesn't come as a direct result: "I labored, and I found."
- The whole year is preparation: all of one's desires (world) over the whole time (year) concentrated, in the highest surge of the soul, into one point.

## Part 3 — The First Prayer: To Know What to Ask

- **Q: Why must one first pray to know what he lacks?** "Many are the thoughts in a man's heart, but only the counsel of the Lord." Until a person crosses into spirituality, he truly doesn't know what it is, thrown from desire to desire.
- Bring a child into a toy store and "he will go crazy, he doesn't know what to grab." Rav Laitman recalls watching his little daughter at Disney World: "we're exactly like this in this world," thrown among food, home, sex, money, honor and knowledge, each endless, with the point in the heart hidden among them.
- "Therefore, the first prayer is for scrutiny; to know exactly what to ask for." Ask a child what present he truly wants and you can confuse him at once. "Only the light can scrutinize it." What we haven't attained lies beyond the barrier, so we pray for "a deficiency for the right thing without seeing the right thing... give me the ability to want what is truly worth wanting without knowing what it is."
- It's like standing on a highway with no roads, not knowing which way Jerusalem is, and asking "be my guide." "This concealment is in fact the conditions under which alone we can draw closer to the Creator," so that the goal won't turn into some other place or status, "and gradually I understand that the goal is He, Himself."

## Part 4 — Starting From "I Lack Nothing"

- **Q: What can I ask for when I feel good in corporeality and spiritually?** A person raised religiously, blessed in his study, business and family, hears that there is something more but it doesn't speak to him. "This knowledge is the starting point, we all started this way."
- Rav Laitman lacked nothing; he only wanted, like a scientist, to know what we live for. That point was clothed coarsely in him, more delicately in others. He went to many old sages in search of what Judaism wasn't telling anyone, until he saw "if I poke, I see the air coming out of him, like an inflated puppet... and that the whole world is like that as well."
- Then everything depends on the society. In corporeality a child falls instinctively into his parents' hands. "In spirituality, we have to on our own generate that state." A child raised in an orphanage by people merely obliged to care for him gets "some simple profession, and that's it." To the extent I connect to a group, a Rav and books "like devoted parents," I receive better treatment. "Here, it's free choice."

#### Advance in balance

- Before the first gate you can't shout like one at the last gate: "this artificial lie won't help." Structure life "in a normal, balanced way": each stage changes my work, family and attitude by itself. "Not that I'll just go artificially now, throw away my job, my family... On each stage that comes, we simply yearn for the goal."

## Part 5 — I, the Goal and the Creator

- **Q: What happens when he has inverted his values but can't carry them out?** After all the gates comes the Gate of Tears: "he has nothing more in life other than to attain that thing," and understands he cannot attain it on his own. "The greatest sorrow with which I cry out to the Creator."
- "Here I have three things: me, the goal I want to attain, and the Creator. But they're still not connected together, and now by my outcry, the Creator will connect them... That is called the first spiritual state." Only through the light, "suddenly I discover that the Creator is the goal."
- **Q: How do I know the next step, if I can't trust my own vessels?** "Difficult." A student jokes that this is why the Jews cry at all the gates. Rav Laitman: some prayer books for Yom Kippur say "here you cry." Rabash, half joking, advised that if you can't cry on Yom Kippur, go to the women's section and see how they cry, "and then you will cry also!"

#### Adhesion

- **Q: How do I connect my I, the path and the Creator?** In spirituality this is the drop of adhesion, "connection with no limits." The vessel receives light through the screen only when it is compatible with it, "and in that, the revelation of the Creator happens towards the vessel."
- Malchut of Ein Sof gathers all her corrections and screens into one, and the Creator, already there, is revealed in it. "Adhesion is already beyond that, it's like in the simple light, where there's no difference at all." It can't be explained or conveyed.
- A person doesn't always know if he's in the vessel, holding the Creator, or in corrections. "The closer you get to the end, the more confusions come," as in the darkness of Egypt just before the exodus, when he can't imagine that this is the state just before redemption.

## Part 6 — Buy Yourself a Friend

- **Q: How can a person accelerate to necessity?** "You're asking: how can I acquire desire for something that I don't want? For that, only the environment exists, nothing else."
- "You can take people, pay them money to work on you." An advertising agency doesn't care about green tea; they sit with psychologists and plan how to make people want it. "So I'll build spirituality for you, just pay... That's why it's written, Buy yourself a friend. It's an acquisition."
- You'll be inspired "like a beast" even knowing you paid for it: watch films about butterflies and you'll start loving them. "It all depends on you... it's so simple, and we don't want it." "Where is our advertising department? For ourselves, not to advertise to others."

#### Advertising aimed outward

- **Q: Isn't what we do here one big billboard?** "What you do here is for the sake of advertising outwards. Not for yourself." Other movements, even with more abstract ideas, influence the world with great devotion, "like a commando unit that wants to rule the world with their opinion."
- **Q: Why am I not impressed?** "Because you're working on the whole world, not on yourselves." "All your actions are aimed outwards, and if some echo turns a response inwards, it's truly on the fringe of things."
- Our friends don't know how to make advertisements; "take experts on the side." Rav Laitman doesn't come home saying "listen what I heard, oh boy." "The society doesn't feel that it's obliging in itself the development from itself."

## Part 7 — Wrapping and Message

- **Q: Shouldn't we use beauty, an amazing building, to impress?** "Completely not." Newcomers would be more impressed by simplicity. Lavish synagogues invest "so much in the externality instead of the internality"; a synagogue should be simple, "like a little temporary house."
- Advertising uses the wrapping only to catch attention "so that they can plant their thoughts in you... there has to be an inner message." "The whole problem is to know the psychology of the beast."
- For an introductory lecture, clothe the message in what they can hear, sometimes shaking things up to get attention, but "their ear was opened only by pleasant and understood things." Create normal conditions, not glitter; a famous name may bring people the first time. "Pour upon the whole world your message in the broadest possible way... But later, the point in the heart works."
- Being hypnotized by an opinion we build ourselves is something we resist: "you have an intellect... You want someone to enter into your head with their thoughts?" Only when we feel "truly sick and messed up" do we reach the necessity and begin to wash our own minds.
- **Q: Then what is a person's work?** "Our work is only an advertisement, in choosing each time a better environment," one that tells me with greater might about the purpose of creation. As the Study of the Ten Sefirot, Part 8, puts it, a person receives his whole spiritual attainment "from the vessels of his friends, which he connected to himself."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **True desire doesn't change.** Tears measure a desire that has conquered the whole person, joined with the recognition that he cannot fulfill it alone.
2. **The Gate of Tears opens only for necessity.** Crying over luxuries, additions to what one already has, leaves it locked; crying to be simply one who loves the Creator opens it.
3. **All the other gates serve this one.** Trying and exhausting them builds the deficiency; the Gate of Tears is the first spiritual record, the first MAN.
4. **First pray to know what to ask.** We are like children in a toy store; only the light can give a deficiency for the true thing without seeing it.
5. **Necessity comes only from the environment.** Like devoted parents, a group, a Rav and books raise us, and choosing them is free choice.
6. **Buy yourself a friend.** We can be "advertised to" even knowingly; what's missing is an advertising department aimed at ourselves.
7. **Message over wrapping.** Outreach needs pleasant, understandable clothing, but our real work is choosing an ever better environment, receiving attainment from the friends' vessels.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Gate of Tears** | The state after all other gates are locked: one desire for the Creator with the recognition that one cannot attain it alone; the first spiritual degree. |
| **Necessity / luxury** | Crying for life itself, to love the Creator, versus crying for additions to one's completeness. |
| **Broken heart** | A fierce desire that cannot carry itself out; the recognition of the breaking of the vessels from below upward. |
| **First prayer** | Praying to know what to pray for, a deficiency for the true thing without seeing it. |
| **"Open a gate for us when a gate is locked"** | The Ne'ila prayer: real tears come once one has learned from above what he truly lacks. |
| **World, year, soul** | The conditions for contact with the Creator, concentrated on Yom Kippur in the Holy of Holies by the high priest. |
| **"The wicked in their lives are called dead"** | Being under the will to receive is separation from the Life of Lives; hence the cry for life. |
| **Drop of adhesion** | The point where I, the path and the Creator connect without limit. |
| **Buy yourself a friend** | Acquiring an environment that implants the importance of the goal, even by payment, as advertising does. |
| **Advertising department for ourselves** | The society working on its own members' impression of the goal, not only outward. |
