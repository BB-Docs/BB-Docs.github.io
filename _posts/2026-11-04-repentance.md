### Opening

"Everything depends on importance." What a person considers important decides whether his effort is a burden he wants to escape or something he already enjoys. When the goal is important enough, the labor and the reward are in the same place. Rav Laitman applies this to the work of the Creator, and from there to the central puzzle of the lesson: how can "**If I am not for me, who is for me?**" and "**There is none else besides Him**" both be true? His answer is that neither cancels the other. The Creator didn't create man in order to erase him; adhesion is two partners in one coupling, and the self must stand fully against Him to become like Him.

## Part 1 — When the Labor and the Reward Are One

- Whatever a person is focused on determines everything. If it's money, everything in life is for money; if it's honor, "I'm willing to pay millions for it, to work from dawn to dusk."
- Usually the **means** (efforts, time, stress) and the **reward** are two separate things. If I get what I wanted, it's a reward; if not, a punishment, and I regret all the side efforts.
- Some people are happy because they're immersed in their profession: "It's everything for them... He can come from dawn to dusk, work and enjoy that." For them there's no separation between labor and goal.
- If connection with the Creator, coming close to Him and feeling Him, became our whole engagement, "then right there and then I'll have the effort and the reward and the punishment all in the same subject," and I'd never feel I'm missing something.
- Even someone who does everything correctly toward a goal isn't "immersed in goodness" while the work itself isn't the goal. When he brings the goal close enough that he already has some of it, "there's no separation to him between the labor and the goal."
- Our problem is that "we can't skip the concealment." If we valued the engagement He gave us, wanting Him, searching for Him, with infinite importance, we would already be in happiness now.

## Part 2 — Importance Comes from the Society

- On one hand there is none else besides Him; on the other, "If I am not for me, who is for me?" What is missing is "only a vessel, a deficiency" to truly feel that there's none else besides Him.
- That deficiency is gathered by effort, through **free choice**, from the friends, from myself, from the books and the authors. "If I am not for me" refers to the effort of mustering the longing from the society.
- What I need from the society is **the importance of the Creator**, so that it builds a deficiency in me that becomes a prayer (MAN), and He comes, corrects me, and fills me. "Prayer before prayer. Deficiency for a deficiency."
- The same two attitudes apply toward the friends: "If I am not for me" means I invest in them as much as I can; "none else besides Him" means He arranges that I will truly be inspired by them, and that inspiration becomes my springboard to the Creator. "In each and every subject I have in life, I have to have these two attitudes."

## Part 3 — Concealment, and a Shield Against Evil

- **Q: If concealment is our fuel, why do we yearn to cancel it?** We don't really aim to cancel it; we lack the recognition of what is dangerous.
- Rav Laitman describes places with radiation warning signs, where people wear protection and check everything they've been exposed to. "Who doesn't want some defense... against such a dangerous matter?"
- But "we don't feel that all our troubles come from receiving in order to receive. So I don't care. Give me a million, give me two."
- People ask him for protection against the evil eye. What's really missing is "sensitivity to what evil means," that is, **recognition of evil**. Know exactly what makes you feel bad, "and corresponding to the evil, you'll have a shield, shield and sword."

#### The diet example

- **Q: We act only from some importance of the Creator, and in concealment it isn't true importance. How can we work correctly?** Pleasure you aren't ready to receive harms you, like scratching an itchy rash. You need a force of resistance and the recognition of good and bad.
- "There are people who can maintain a diet, and I can't." They join circles, hear things, "get brainwashed... and then they acquire a screen against all these temptations." Through recognition they gained a shield.

## Part 4 — "You Don't Need the Revelation of the Creator for This"

- "What's important is the brainwashing I get from the society." Whole societies organize their lives around ideas no one has verified, and people give their lives for them. "Does it matter if it's true or not?... The society has this power to compel a person to think a certain way."
- "So there is no reason to complain about the Creator not being revealed... Receive power from the society." If you don't want the group, "that's your business. It's written: either fellowship or death." Baal HaSulam, the Zohar and Rabash all write it.
- "The Creator will not be revealed. Forget about it... Maybe He doesn't exist." That is exactly why you say "If I am not for me, who is for me?" It all depends on you, "so stand on that and until you pass this point nothing will help. And don't run from it."
- It's like an exam you must pass before anything else: organize a society around you so that the importance of the Creator appears to you "in an infinite way."
- The Creator appears to you now **as this world**: "the walls, the houses, the people, everything... it's Him." He can't skip a degree for you, because "the Creator is the general law of the whole of reality."

## Part 5 — "If I Am Not for Me" and "There Is None Else Besides Him"

#### The motive is always the connection

- Every act of "If I am not for me" must already be based on "there is none else besides Him": the reason I do anything is the connection with the upper force. Rabash writes that a person waking up in his bed must say, "If I am not for me, who is for me?" "If it's with no connection to Godliness, even for just a moment, it's an act of a beast."
- Both sayings are in the negative because each must be proven from its opposite. Acting "for myself" without the Creator in mind proves nothing.

#### Not cancellation but partnership

- We think the second saying cancels the first: first I act, then I discover "it was all the Creator, I was mistaken." No: "I do actually have free choice, and in the end, ultimately I connect these two threads together," the Creator and I "on equal footing in a coupling like two partners."
- "The Creator didn't come to cancel out the person. The correction of the vessels isn't cancellation."
- Light that fills a vessel without a screen quenches the desire, and the person feels almost nothing. With a **screen and reflected light** he becomes like the Creator: "They dwell together, they are in charge together, they rule together," like the **horse and rider**, "where you can't say who comes first."
- If the vessel is annulled, only **Nefesh** remains. It must have full power and all its overcomings, "by that it becomes true man."

#### Why only in Kabbalah

- If "the Lord is always before me" cancels my self, "it's bad. It prevents work." The Torah can be a potion of life or of death.
- The true work of the Creator is only the wisdom of Kabbalah, "because in Kabbalah you work with the left line, with yourself." Popular religion doesn't awaken "If I am not for me," and rightly so: the general public lacks the strength. "I'm not against it... It's nature."
- In Shamati's still, vegetative, animate and speaking, the **speaking** is the one who goes against the Creator, "as if cancelling the presence of the Creator and acting by himself... I myself want to determine my nature." This is **ZON** "face to face... in one embrace," a **coupling by striking**.
- So a Kabbalist seems to rebel, even toward heresy. If a person doesn't give up his independence before the Creator, this is "**My sons have defeated Me**," and every father wants his son to surpass him. "If I don't rebel against Him, then there won't be a place where I exist, a self."
- The general public is "like the seed within the womb"; they annul themselves and are led by nature, like a horse that does exactly what the rider wants. That too is coarseness of the root of holiness. But one with a greater desire must develop it with independence and guidance, "and that's why it takes years. Because this desire is so dangerous." It can't be coerced.
- "A person on the path is never satisfied and never calm. And he does want more, and that's a satisfaction."

#### The unchanging law

- **Q: If the law never changes, how does anything happen?** The law toward us is one: **equivalence of form**. "To the extent that I'm equivalent to the Creator, that equivalence of form determines my state, my place," and everything else follows from it.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Everything depends on importance.** When the goal is important enough, the labor and the reward are in the same place and the work itself is the joy.
2. **The society supplies the importance.** It builds the deficiency for a deficiency, the prayer before the prayer, through which the Creator corrects and fills us.
3. **Recognize the evil to want the shield.** We don't seek protection because we don't feel that receiving for ourselves is the source of all our troubles.
4. **Don't wait for revelation.** The Creator appears now as this world; "brainwashing" from the society is enough to act, and "either fellowship or death."
5. **Both sayings together.** "If I am not for me" must rest on "none else besides Him," and together they become a partnership, not a cancellation of the self.
6. **The self must stand opposite Him.** The speaking degree works in the left line and goes against the Creator to become like Him: "My sons have defeated Me."
7. **One law: equivalence of form.** It determines every state and place.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Importance** | What a person focuses on and values; it decides whether effort is labor or already reward. |
| **Labor and reward in one place** | The state where the work itself is the goal, so there is no separate punishment or regret. |
| **If I am not for me, who is for me?** | The person's own effort and free choice, especially gathering longing from the society. |
| **There is none else besides Him** | The Creator as sole operator: the motive of every act and the recognition after it. |
| **Deficiency for a deficiency** | The prayer before prayer: wanting the desire to ask, received from the society's sense of the Creator's importance. |
| **Recognition of evil** | Sensing that receiving for oneself causes all suffering, which creates the wish for a shield (a screen). |
| **Screen and reflected light** | What lets a vessel receive without being quenched, making it like the Creator. |
| **Horse and rider** | The partnership of man and Creator in adhesion, where neither comes first. |
| **Left line** | Working with oneself and one's independence, unique to the work in Kabbalah. |
| **My sons have defeated Me** | The Creator's joy when man keeps his independence and rises through opposing Him. |
| **Either fellowship or death** | The saying that without a society a person has no spiritual life. |
| **Law of equivalence of form** | The single unchanging law that determines each person's state and place. |
