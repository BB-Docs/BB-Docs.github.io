### Opening

If the Creator already wants to give more than we want to receive, what reward are we working for? Rav Laitman's answer is that the reward is not a filling but a **vessel of bestowal**. The problem is that we don't feel why we'd want it: we have no records of Ein Sof, and when we think about bestowal honestly, we may even hate it. The lesson traces how that hatred turns, through the society and years of effort, into a **"grace of holiness,"** an upper layer of esteem for bestowal built over our will to receive.

## Part 1 — Imagine Being Pushed Away from Ein Sof

- Rav Laitman suggests an exercise: imagine we were "completely entirely like the Creator," in the desire to bestow, equal to Him. Then a force called the evil inclination, concealment, the will to receive, settles on us and pushes us "to the lowest place, farthest from the Creator."
- If we felt that weight, the reward would obviously be to throw it off and return: "to return the vessel of bestowal, the reflected light, this is what we need."
- **The problem:** "This picture is imaginary, not emotional." We have no records of Ein Sof. If we did, reward, punishment, and exertion "would be clear to me."

#### Concealment keeps us from being a Klipa

- If we felt the records of spirituality while under the evil inclination, "I would want to attain Ein Sof inside of the will to receive," moving away from it by my own desire. "This is already called a Klipa, a shell."
- So "the reason why we have concealment is that concealment doesn't allow us to be a shell." At least I'm not drawn to receive spirituality in vessels of reception.
- "First I have to work on the vessels and then I attain the feelings." The reward is the vessels.

## Part 2 — A Vessel of Bestowal Is Never Empty

- **Q: If every reward is another vessel, another deficiency, don't I just feel more empty?** No. In a vessel of bestowal "there's no deficiency... the lack is only to bestow," and on bestowal there was never a restriction.
- "The act of bestowal is the feeling of the vessel of bestowal." A mother feeding a healthy child who eats everything: "What else does she need?" Wanting to give more is simply an additional vessel.
- "The vessel is the light." The light is pleasure, and if my whole desire is to give, "from that my desire is full." This refers to bestowing in order to bestow, "not to bestow in order to receive."

## Part 3 — From Hating Bestowal to the Grace of Holiness

- **Q: Every time bestowal comes close in feeling, there's a rejection that burns.** "Feeling is feeling. You can't say about the feeling that it's true or false." A real disgust for bestowal "is already a determination of truth": my nature and bestowal are opposites.
- That feeling is itself a result of study and effort. As effort increases, the definition of the state changes. Rav Laitman walks through the stages:

| Stage | What a person feels |
|---|---|
| **Hatred** | "I only want to receive. And for bestowal, I don't want a thing." |
| **Necessity** | "Maybe I don't want it, but it's probably a necessary thing." The real world may be built on it. |
| **Recognition of evil** | "If I were in vessels of bestowal, I would be free like a bird." My nature is bad. |
| **Refuge** | Running to the thought of the Creator "like a shelter," a ray of light through the cracks, as Baal HaSulam writes. |
| **Importance** | "I begin to respect this quality... to love it in and of its own," though I don't have it. |
| **Grace of holiness** | An inner drive to do something for the Creator; the act of bestowal itself becomes precious. |

- "It all takes years. It's all years, I'm telling you. So, if you have no time, you can get off the train right now."

## Part 4 — Two Layers: Lo Lishma That Brings to Lishma

- All of this is still depicted "in vessels of reception... before the barrier," with no screen or reflected light. That's why it's **Lo Lishma**, "but it's already Lo Lishma, from which we come to Lishma."
- Deep down there's always a calculation of benefit, "if there weren't that, I would not be able to think about this at all." But "before my eyes... I love Him and I want Him."

> I have a two-layer existence. On the bottom layer, of course, there are calculations for myself and there's nothing I can do. But through my labor, the work with the society, with dissemination, with bestowal as much as I can... I created like a top layer.

- Contrast this with **bestowing in order to receive**: "Let's do some business." Giving the Creator gifts to get more back creates no top layer; I don't value bestowal above reception.
- **Q: Do I work on the bottom or top layer?** "I don't work on the bottom layer. My bottom layer is my will to receive. I work only on the importance of bestowal." As Rabash writes, habit becomes second nature and what the public says becomes important to me.
- **In ascents and descents:** in an ascent, a person is attached to the top layer and works to increase the desire to bestow. In a fall, he's attached to the bottom layer and examines "how much the will to receive is against him, how it closes his eyes."

## Part 5 — Just Press the Gas

- **Q: Must the upper layer be as clear to me as the lower one?** "A person cannot determine his states." So we're told, "Whatever you can do, do."
- "Just press on the gas. The wheel is not in your hands. We're like a train. We are on the tracks."
- The only thing we change is our relation and attention, the necessity we get through the society. Increasing the speed changes the feeling: "either you're pushed by the negative force, or you yearn for what is desirable."
- "Make the society give you as much inspiration as possible... You don't need more than inspiration." Be like one lovesick, "mindless."
- Before the barrier "I have nothing in my hands, no tool with which to check." So "our work should be to receive the power of bestowal, period." Receiving it is "really a gift... the giving of the Torah."

## Part 6 — Building Importance Through the Society

- **Q: I see only actions, not intentions. How can I be impressed by friends?** Rav Laitman points to a friend who drove people home until 11 PM and got yelled at at home, then worked until 10:30 the next night: "Either he's an idiot or he doesn't care... This must be important to him." Another came from Australia.
- **Quality vs. quantity:** a million Jews "making all the noise" next to billions. "There is gravel and there are diamonds." Bring a primitive tribe a diamond and a shard of glass; the glass shines more. Importance "depends on the person."
- "This is called **advertising**. This is why I need the society." I bring something small; the society must talk only about it and appreciate only it, until its appreciation changes mine.
- **Free choice** is taking something worthless in my world, bestowal ("it's even embarrassing to talk about"), and nurturing it until it's great in my eyes. "The biggest lie is our life, that it's got any value" if it isn't connected to the goal.
- Twenty years earlier you couldn't speak about this without being called insane; "today, there begins to be some recognition of it, a little bit."

## Part 7 — Why the Creator Hides, and the Mechanic Who Stops Fixing Cars

- Concealment is like Rabash's thief who runs ahead shouting, "catch the thief": if the Creator were revealed, we'd want to bestow only to receive more. (Sending a gift to Shimon Peres and getting a catalog back.)
- Because He's hidden, "we are purportedly independent from His greatness... I can determine my relation to Him, not to the pleasures that are in Him. And until I determine my relation to Him, I will not discover that there are pleasures in Him. It is salvation."

#### The mechanic

- A friend fixes everyone's cars. Do you want to be valued because you're useful, or "as a human being"? So "stop fixing cars, let's see how people will change." Soon: "What do we need him for?"
- With friends we can "play with the will to receive" by withholding the pleasure and see what we really value. "With the Creator, if it were revealed, there we wouldn't be able to emerge from a calculation of the will to receive ever." This is restriction first, then screen and reflected light.

#### The path of joy vs. the path of suffering

- **Q: Bestowal is good for me because it saves me from troubles. Isn't that egoistic?** Yes, but from it I start to value bestowal above reception.
- If I value bestowal at "10 kilos" because it offsets 10 kilos of trouble, why value it at 20? "If I don't appreciate it now... then I'm given 20 kilos of troubles," and I'll value it against my will. Doing it myself "is called the path of joy, not the path of suffering."
- Without the troubles, "only the society can do this for you."
- Rav Laitman ended the exchange by noting the questioner was speaking theoretically, "not from experience... But it'll come."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The reward is the vessel of bestowal,** not a filling; the Creator already wants to give more than we want to receive.
2. **Concealment protects us** from wanting spirituality in vessels of reception, which would make us a Klipa.
3. **A vessel of bestowal is never empty;** the act of giving is its filling.
4. **Hatred of bestowal is a true stage.** Through effort it turns into necessity, recognition of evil, refuge, importance, and the grace of holiness.
5. **Lo Lishma that brings to Lishma has two layers:** we don't work on the bottom layer (the will to receive), only on raising the importance of bestowal above it.
6. **We can't steer, only press the gas.** The society supplies the inspiration and "advertising" that raise bestowal's importance, the path of joy instead of suffering.
7. **The Creator hides so we can value Him, not His pleasures.**

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Power of Bestowal** | The reward for labor in Torah and Mitzvot: the Kli that lets us receive the pleasure already waiting. |
| **Vessel of Bestowal** | A desire to give; never empty, since the act of giving is its filling. |
| **Reflected Light** | The root of reward: the desire to bestow born from the Zivug de Hakaa. |
| **Records (Reshimot)** | Impressions of past states; we lack records of Ein Sof, so the reward isn't felt. |
| **Klipa** | Wanting spirituality inside the will to receive; concealment prevents it. |
| **Grace of Holiness (Chen de Kedusha)** | Esteem and love for bestowal itself, before having its power. |
| **Lo Lishma / Lishma** | Working for a reward until the light reforms; the reward's name changes, not the reward. |
| **Two Layers** | The bottom layer of natural reception and the top layer of importance for bestowal built by labor. |
| **Path of Joy / Path of Suffering** | Raising bestowal's value through the society, or being pushed by troubles. |
| **Advertising** | The society's role in raising the importance of the goal. |
| **Bestowing in Order to Receive** | "Doing business" with the Creator; creates no top layer. |
