### Opening

The Zohar says that "there was a flood and the saboteur was sitting inside," and Baal HaSulam asked what difference it makes whether the flood or the saboteur puts a person to death. Rav Laitman explains: very difficult states can come until a person feels the Creator has departed from his mind, knowledge and sensations. Even if one small thought remains, "maybe the Creator exists," he must not despair of mercy. The lesson defines the soul as the vessel in which we feel the Creator and explains why good states can be saboteurs too. It shows how the flood is mercy without a limit, and why the saboteur, whose only work is to cut our connection, is actually our guide.

## Part 1 — Do Not Despair of Mercy

- A flood can come without the saboteur, or with the saboteur in it. "It is not the flood that puts him to death and harms, but the saboteur in it."
- States can become so hard that a person feels the Creator "is completely concealed from him, departing from him, his mind, his knowledge, his sensations, everything."
- "And even if nothing is left to man except one small thought, maybe the Creator exists," it's a sign that he still feels the godly part in him. If even that is gone, "he is already a beast." From this thought he must begin to justify the Creator's providence, that it is for his good.
- This is "even if He takes his soul," for the soul means the sensation of the Creator. "Nevertheless, do not despair of mercy," and work to bring better states closer.
- Do not despair of mercy means that in all these states you should see "mercy greater than before. Because before it was mercy like for babies." Now the Creator wants to bring you closer than before. A person not ready to accept this falls from the degree and discovers afterward that the saboteur was inside the flood.

## Part 2 — The Soul and the Language of Branches

- **What is a soul?** "It is a vessel in which we feel the upper light, the Creator." Man is built of qualities that correspond exactly to the light: similar to the Creator or opposite to Him.
- This whole vessel is "the shared vessel between the person's sensation and the Creator's presence," and the measures of the Creator's sensation in it are called **worlds**, degrees. In each degree something is shared between the vessel and the light: the **screen** that connects them.
- The external vessel over the soul is our five senses, and what we feel in it is called this world. "This world too is a copy of the soul." So a Kabbalist takes words from this external impression to tell of a more internal one, the **language of branches**.
- "One who feels the soul understands" what he speaks of; one who doesn't attributes the Kabbalists' words to external things.
- So "a person's whole effort should be to attach himself to the most internal sensation possible," justifying Him and loving Him in good and in bad, whatever passes over that sensation. One whose screen keeps him in adhesion through all those forms "has been rewarded with complete love."

## Part 3 — Why Not Work with the Good Feelings?

- **Q: The article describes nearing the Creator through good too, like being healed of an illness. Why do we work only with suffering?** "The Creator does not treat us with good things and bad things." From His side comes one light that does good and reforms, filling all of reality, in both correction and fulfillment.
- "Everything depends on the state the vessel is in, and it does not depend on the light at all." The light acts on you "from its end of correction," not from the states revealed along the way. "The Creator did not create any state other than that state."
- To the extent the vessel isn't in **equivalence of form**, it feels the light's action as impatience and suffering instead of pleasure.

#### The simple mechanics

- Two things are revealed in the vessel, one after the other. First, the extent of its equivalence of form: say twenty percent yes and eighty percent no, which determines its spiritual degree. Second, the sensation of the Creator that results from it.
- "The mechanics are very simple." Detail them by every process and "we get stuck in all kinds of complicated orders." The complication is turning nature, opposite to the light, into something similar by the **intention**. "These are two things that contradict each other, intention and nature," and they must work together in one vessel.
- "On the Creator's part, there are no actions. He simply does not act, He does not change." Merciful, gracious, the throne of judgment or of mercy: "all these descriptions are in fact on the person's side."
- Through red glass the world is red; through green, green. "Is the world colored, or did I put these filters on my vessel?" Expand the vessels and you'd hear frequencies from zero to infinity. "Everything we have is only inside the vessel. Anyone who disqualifies disqualifies with his own flaw."

#### Joining the feeling to the purpose

- We do join good feelings to the work. "You cannot advance only from the bad, certainly from the good as well." But good and bad depend on what you determine they are.
- Feeling good and saying "thank God," because I earned well, "but what of it?" If I feel it as good before examining it against the goal, "then it is beastliness. It certainly does not advance me."
- If I receive a feeling, good or bad, and connect it to the purpose, "then it also doesn't matter what I feel afterwards," even if I then curse everything, the source included. "Still I received it. I connected it. That's an advancement."
- Connecting to the purpose means simply "together with the feeling to feel the Creator. There is none else besides Him. I received it from Him." If I can hold the very worst feeling together with His presence in my awareness, "that state is excellent."
- "The work is that the Creator should not depart from a person's awareness even for a moment," even only in the mind, without inspiration. Thoughts land and rattle me, "but it still hasn't disappeared from my awareness, fine. That is the main thing."
- If even in the middle of the flood I am together with the Creator, "then the flood turns into the most beneficial thing."

## Part 4 — What the Saboteur Does

- The saboteur, the **Sitra Achra**, "does not work by making you feel bad or good. On the contrary, they want to bring you good things." They work only against the Creator's presence in a person's awareness. "That is the worst thing."
- **Q: If everything comes from the Creator, the vessel and its filling alike, what is left of the creature?** The vessel is existence from absence and its filling existence from existence, both from Him. "Then who am I? I'm the one who feels," at most the one who feels that vessel and feeling both come from the Creator. "That is all of man."
- The Creator sends the saboteur within the flood "and wants to show you where you are." There's nothing you can do about the flood. What you can do is exert yourself so the saboteur won't put you to death.
- "Death means that you lose the soul," the connection with the Creator. When you receive more connection, "the soul returns to you, having become bigger." So He teaches you how to be in constant connection and strengthen it.

## Part 5 — Greatness Through Its Opposite

- **Q: Is the saboteur like the evil inclination, and how do I see the Creator as great?** "The greatness of the Creator is attained through its opposite." Where you see His humility, there you discover His greatness: from His concealing Himself, making Himself weak, "making Himself a zero," willing to suffer everything and give man complete freedom of action.
- Like our parents, who gave in to us on everything. We thought it was weakness, that we were big and they small. "When I grow up, I begin to understand that it was actually a matter of greatness and boundless love on their part."
- Without feeling He is great, "I cannot build my work." But if I see Him as great, then as in this world I either try to defeat Him or join Him, "my desire to receive acting according to nature." So that we connect to Him above nature, "He cannot show us His greatness. On the contrary, He must constantly diminish Himself."
- A person attains His greatness "not in sensation, he attains it from experience" of negative things: from blows he can't escape, from being turned this way and that while confused. "From this, a sensation accumulates in me that He is truly above everything, because I see with what sophistication He handles me."
- "If He appeared great to me in an embrace, saying, come, let's take a walk, let's have a good time, I would disdain Him. That is how we are built."

#### A flaw in greatness

- **Q: Why would there be a flaw in sensing the Creator's greatness?** If it's revealed in vessels of reception. "If I see Him as great, then I subdue myself because of the desire to receive. I need to subdue myself because of the will to bestow."
- First I must want to bestow and be unable, then call on Him to help, and receive from His greatness the forces to bestow. "What do I need His greatness for if I've not yet come out of the will to receive?" Only once I have a restriction and a screen.
- Honestly, as a will to receive, "I know that now if the Creator is revealed to me as good, I stop thinking about the goal." Those pleasures would buy me. "So I don't even want these pleasures. I'm afraid they will confuse me." Let Him hold me in balance, "not either in the very bad or in the very good."

## Part 6 — A Good State Can Be a Saboteur

- **Q: What do I feel when something good is revealed, and it's from Him?** "There is no deficiency at all," neither toward His greatness nor toward advancing, and no opening to make a movement of your own. "It is not a vessel for advancement."
- So be like an old man bending down to search for something lost: look for deficiencies in that state until it no longer seems so good. "If you do not bend down, then the state is to your detriment. You lose it and fall." Bending, you use it as a degree from which you rise, "not from which you will fall and only afterwards rise."
- "If the vessel has no deficiency at all, that is a big problem." All day and no night exists only at the end of correction. Even beyond the barrier, with adhesion and a screen, a person still yearns further and descends and ascends.
- **Q: So even a good state can be a saboteur?** "Yes, a good state is certainly a saboteur too." It wants to cut off your connection with the Creator, "and it is much easier to do that through a good state" than a bad one.

#### The malicious waters

- "It is not for nothing that they are called malicious waters. Water is **Hassadim**, and it is precisely from water that the flood is made," from an excess of mercy, goodness, abundance and care. Rabash writes in a letter that if parents reveal love to their children without any limit, the children begin to hate them.
- "The flood comes from mercy, but mercy without a limit is not good." A person must add a deficiency coming from himself. Otherwise it's water that puts to death.
- Everything, every correction and filling, comes from **Bina**, which acts upon and fills **Malchut**. The GAR of Bina delights in mercy, wanting nothing; the thought of creation is revealed through her. And all the sufferings, down to the very worst and up to death, "also come from Bina," embedded within the Hassadim.
- "In all the very worst things that exist in reality, you need to reveal the mercy," in accordance with the vessel of Malchut. A person can't attach the Holocaust to mercy, or even his small daily problems, "that is our problem, but it all comes from mercy."

## Part 7 — The Saboteur as Guide

- **Q: What strength should a person ask for?** "Only not to disconnect from the Creator." Not from the knowledge of Him, the feeling, the connection.
- The saboteur "works against the intention. What's the intention? Connection between the giver and the receiver." Even depicting Him in my imagination is already the beginning of the intention: "I'm relating to Him already."
- "Without this saboteur we won't be able to advance." If he didn't show us the disconnection, there'd be no way to build the connection, and all the desire, abundance and processes exist to build it.
- If the Creator were revealed only as bad, He'd be forgotten; only as good, "He'll also be forgotten, because it's something we'll take for granted."
- So "my attitude to the saboteur needs to be a purposeful one," effective and beneficial. "I want to be connected with this saboteur, so that he will constantly sabotage," and from it grow wiser and learn his sophistication, reinforcing the connection where he shows me "I'm not connected yet."
- On the path between the peaks and the abyss, he wants to drop me a little, and by that shows me where I'm not okay, "and I immediately find the counter forces in connection with the Creator. That's the saboteur. That's the good force."
- Those who can't invert this toward advancement constantly suffer. Those who also suffer but turn it into advancement enjoy: "It's fruitful work. It gives that feeling of satisfaction."

#### The two beastly states

- **Q: In a good state, how do we find the saboteur?** In a good state there's no finding him. The very bad and the very good "are two beastly states that are given to a person so that he'll be afraid of them," where only feeling operates, not the intellect.
- A person who feels he's in such a state needs to add deeds: come to study, work for the society, all kinds of things.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The saboteur, not the flood, kills.** Hard states are mercy greater than before; one small remaining thought of the Creator is enough to begin justifying Him.
2. **The soul is where we feel the Creator.** Its measures are worlds; losing the connection is losing the soul, and regaining it returns a bigger soul.
3. **One light, many filters.** The Creator doesn't act or change; good and bad are the vessel's degree of equivalence of form.
4. **Join every feeling to the purpose.** Good or bad, hold it together with the Creator's presence; the work is that He never leaves our awareness.
5. **Greatness through its opposite.** He conceals and diminishes Himself so we can connect above nature; we learn His greatness from experience, not sensation.
6. **Good states can sabotage.** Mercy without a limit is the flood; bend down to find the deficiency, or the good state becomes a fall.
7. **Make the saboteur your guide.** He only shows where we're not yet connected; ask only for the strength not to disconnect, and turn each blow into advancement.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Flood (Mabul)** | Mercy and abundance without limit, the "malicious waters" of Hassadim, which can bring death. |
| **Saboteur (Mashchit)** | The force within the flood that works only against the Creator's presence in a person's awareness. |
| **Putting to death** | Losing the soul, the connection with the Creator. |
| **Soul (Neshama)** | The vessel in which a person feels the Creator; the shared vessel between his sensation and the Creator's presence. |
| **Worlds** | The measures of the Creator's sensation in the soul. |
| **Language of branches** | Words taken from the five senses to describe inner impressions of the soul. |
| **Equivalence of form** | The degree to which the vessel is similar to the light, which determines whether the light is felt as suffering or pleasure. |
| **Intention and nature** | Two contradictory things that must work together in one vessel. |
| **Greatness through its opposite** | Discovering the Creator's greatness from His humility, concealment and the experience of negative states. |
| **Hassadim** | Mercy, water; without a limit it becomes the flood. |
| **GAR of Bina** | Bina delighting in mercy, wanting nothing; the source of all corrections, fillings and sufferings. |
| **Two beastly states** | The very good and the very bad, where only feeling operates; to be feared and met with added deeds. |
