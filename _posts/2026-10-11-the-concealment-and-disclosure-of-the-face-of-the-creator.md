### Opening

If the Creator is "the good who does good" and never changes, where do concealment and suffering come from, and why does one person keep connecting suffering to the Creator while another loses Him altogether? Rav Laitman's answer: front and back, concealment and revelation, are not two sides of the Creator but **our own perception** of Him. The decisive question is what happens to the image of "the host" when the pleasures disappear, and whether a person, through **preparation in the group**, can keep justifying the Creator in every state.

## Part 1 — Face and Back Are in Our Perception

- The Creator is "the good who does good." If a person feels good and ties it to the Creator, "it means that he knows the face, the anterior."
- If a person suffers but still feels the suffering comes from the Creator, he feels Him "through the posterior," the **back** (Achoraim).
- Is the Creator "two-faced," good from the front and bad from the back? No. "Front and back, we create those in our perception, our sensation of the Creator."

> When I'm in equivalence of form with him... he and I are face to face. He is in bestowal and I'm in bestowal.

- If I feel suffering from Him, "I'm not in vessels of bestowal. If I were in vessels of bestowal above my will to receive, I would never have felt anything negative coming from him ever."
- So "the Creator is always with his face towards me. It's only I who am now in a state of posterior towards him."
- But a person doesn't attribute the state to himself. He looks at the reality that comes to him and decides whether the Creator is acting toward him "through the good side or the bad side."
- Rav Laitman also notes the version of the article being read "has been edited," shortened from Baal HaSulam's original.

## Part 2 — Double Concealment: When the Root Vanishes

- In **double concealment** a person doesn't feel even the Creator's back. He feels abandoned and attributes his suffering to "fate and blind nature." The suffering is so great "that they confuse the person. He loses connection with the Creator altogether."
- **Nature** means "anything other than the Creator." Since there is none else besides Him, if that root vanishes, "a thousand and one factors" appear as causes of my states.

#### Why does pleasure or suffering conceal the Creator?

- The answer is rooted in the **four phases of direct light**. In phase one, besides feeling pleasure, the vessel begins to feel **the root**, "who it is that's filling her": the landlord, or host.
- This is already imprinted in us: the will to receive feels the root as more exalted and begins to yearn for it, until resembling and connecting to the root is a greater pleasure than the raw filling itself.
- **The central question:** if the pleasures diminish and vanish, does the image of the host as the good who does good also vanish from me? Or can I, by effort, stay with the recognition of the host's goodness and say, "I am not willing to part from the host"?
- One can even want it this way, to examine "that I'm not dependent on pleasures. Let them vanish."

#### Independence from the feeling

- Becoming independent of the sensation of pleasure or suffering ("for me it's the same thing") means "I put to death my will to receive... I've acquired the quality of **Bina**, faith, overcoming."
- After that, a person can "go back" and "play with my will to receive," toward minus pleasures or plus pleasures, "but only ever for the sake of the host." If He wants me delighted, "I'll be delighted."
- Then "I begin to be free, like him," and see that everything the host created, He created for me, to make of it all a means of bestowing upon Him.

#### Who is in double concealment?

- Not everyone on earth. It is "a person who was in some state of revelation," with records of earlier impressions of the Creator, who is then thrown into a state emptied of sensing the source.
- While in it, "he is unconscious." When he takes a book or receives a reminder, he can decipher his state, and "as soon as he begins discerning that, it will no longer be concealment."

#### The description in the article

- In double concealment, prayer and good deeds bring no answer, or the opposite. When he leaves the study and the society, he seems to succeed. When he overcomes and tries to enter the society with all his might, "success turns its back to him, and he's repelled with no mercy."
- Those on the Creator's path seem poor, sick, uncultured, and hypocritical; those who aren't seem successful, wise, and secure. Rav Laitman recalls a former student who began measuring the group this way near the end of his path: "They're not men of philosophy, science and culture... they don't excel in anything humanity usually respects."
- Every person before the barrier is "in parts" of his state also in double concealment, and when Providence awakens this feeling in a person, "it's a very important thing."
- **Losing faith** means ceasing to feel that the Creator watches over the created beings. Citing Shamati, "faith is the sensation of the Creator."

## Part 3 — Q&A: Normal vs. Double Concealment

| Question | Rav Laitman's answer |
|---|---|
| Is double concealment a high state that gives an opportunity to cling to the Creator? | "No, that is not true." In single (normal) concealment I still believe in the host and attribute the confusions to Him; in double concealment that connection is gone. |
| What is normal concealment? | I don't see Providence clearly, but understand that it comes from Him: He sends disturbances so I'll rise above my will to receive. "I'm nonetheless justifying him." |
| What separates the two? | "The magnitude of the troubles." Zero; minus one is normal concealment; "minus two is double concealment," when the suffering is so great I can't justify the Creator in any way. |
| Can great pleasures also bring double concealment? | Yes. "I see a million dollars, I forget about the creator and steal." Or I lose a million and forget Him. What matters is "the intensity of the impression," positive or negative, its "absolute value." |
| What moves a person between the two? | There are only two things, "the filling and the one who fills." "The measure of a person's correction is how independent he is of the feeling." |

#### Bestowing in suffering vs. in pleasure

- First one becomes independent of the feeling, "called acquiring **Galgalta veEinayim**, vessels of bestowal." Afterward one uses the feeling, negative or even positive, "which is very difficult."
- Which is the greater work: bestowing when the vessel is completely empty, or when it is filled with infinite pleasures?
- The key: "We were not created with a desire to escape suffering. We were created as a desire to enjoy." The absence of pleasure is felt as suffering, so "the true work in order to bestow is done from within pleasures."
- Rising above suffering to adhere to the Creator "is only halfway... a sign of the correction of the vessels. But I have nothing to give and nothing to bestow."

## Part 4 — Normal Concealment: Punishment or Help?

- "The name Creator means good and doing good. There is no other definition." If He seems to be the source of suffering, "something's distorting it." Since there is only He and I, "I'm still corrupted in my qualities."
- **The bitter medicine.** A sick child thinks his mother is bad for giving him medicine; "he lacks an understanding of his mother." So we think about the Creator only through what is revealed in our vessels.

#### There is no punishment from above

- "Punishments exist only in our world and these punishments never correct anything."
- What looks like punishment is help: if I did something bad and don't see it, I may be given "an even worse feeling" within it, so that I feel it and correct it. "Nothing can come to us from above except corrections," felt as punishment because of our uncorrected state.

#### The turning point

- The article says the suffering comes "either as punishment for his actions, or to lead him toward the good." Here is the turning point:
  - Seeing it as **punishment** ("the Creator pays no attention to me, even more troubles") moves a person further from justifying until he falls into double concealment.
  - Seeing it as **help** ("this comes to strengthen me... to awaken me") sends him to the group, the friends, and the study.
- **Can he decide in the moment?** No. "This depends on preparation." A person who has arranged the society and study around him, the morning lesson, an alarm clock, calls from friends during the day, receives the group's force.
- The society influences him "to the extent that he contributes to the society," and then instead of punishment he says, "this comes to save me from the descent."
- The description of normal concealment (insufficient livelihood, illness, disrespect, no rest, yet he *knows* the Creator watches over him) "is the state of most of those who are before the barrier. And it's a state that we need to battle against."
- "A person needs to feel that he is always at a decisive point," deciding to justify himself and the whole world as receiving good from the Creator.

## Part 5 — From Animal Feeling to Inner Work

- Ordinary people feel only pleasure or suffering, "called an animal or a beast," disconnected from thinking about the Creator. This includes religious people, who in our terminology also lack a connection to His Providence, "which can come only through coming closer to resembling him."
- Only when, through the wisdom of Kabbalah, we start looking for the source of good and bad can we speak of revelation, concealment, double concealment, and normal concealment: "a person's inner work."
- Deeper analysis of these states will come later, when "we're a little more immersed in this."

## Part 6 — Toward the Revelation of the Face

- The conclusion: it depends on us "how much we yearn throughout the day," the **entries and exits**, to justify the Creator in everything that happens and to remember Him as the one who brings about everything in me. "There's none else besides him... This is called standing on the decision that leads us to the revelation."
- As Baal HaSulam describes in the letters (Pri Chacham, p. 70), one who yearns for the source of his love "until it doesn't let him sleep" and **fills his quota**, tying every situation to the Creator in mind and heart, gradually comes to the revelation.
- Strengthening in faith during concealment brings a person to the books, where he receives the light of correction, until the Creator "opens himself to a person."
- This isn't about wanting to feel good: "He begins to feel that he doesn't want to slander the Creator. That he wants to love him," and the bad feeling obstructs him.
- In revelation a person senses the Creator as the source of the good, "and after that he can never feel bad." He crosses the barrier into a world "that is all good," though there too are ascents, descents, lines, and shells, "already different states."
- Next lesson: the description of the revelation of the face.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Face and back are in us, not in the Creator.** He always faces us as the good who does good; suffering means we are in disparity of form, "with my back to him."
2. **Concealment is measured by the intensity of the impression.** Pleasure or suffering that exceeds my sensation of the host disconnects me from Him.
3. **The real test:** when the pleasures vanish, does the host vanish too, or can I hold on to His goodness?
4. **The measure of correction is independence from the feeling,** first in suffering (GE, half the way), then by bestowing from within pleasures.
5. **There is no punishment from above, only corrections** that feel like punishment while we are uncorrected.
6. **Whether suffering is felt as punishment or help depends on prior preparation** in the society, to the extent a person contributes to it.
7. **Constant yearning to justify the Creator** "fills the quota" and leads to the revelation of the face.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Hester Panim (Concealment of the Face)** | The Creator's guidance not felt as the good who does good, due to our disparity of form. |
| **Normal (Single) Concealment** | Feeling suffering yet still knowing it comes from the Creator and justifying Him. |
| **Double Concealment** | Concealment within concealment: troubles or pleasures so intense the Creator vanishes and everything is attributed to fate and nature. |
| **Gilui Panim (Revelation of the Face)** | Sensing the Creator as the source of the good; after it, a person "can never feel bad." |
| **Achoraim (Back / Posterior)** | Feeling the Creator through suffering; a reflection of our state of reception, not of Him. |
| **Nature / Fate** | "Anything other than the Creator," seen as the cause of states once the root is lost. |
| **The Host (Root)** | The Creator felt within the pleasure as its source, from phase one of the four phases of direct light. |
| **Galgalta veEinayim** | Vessels of bestowal: the first stage of becoming independent of the feeling. |
| **Faith** | The sensation of the Creator; that He watches over the created beings. |
| **Machsom (Barrier)** | The threshold crossed with revelation, entering a world "that is all good." |
| **Filling the Quota** | Accumulating constant efforts to tie every state to the Creator until He opens Himself. |
