### Opening

The Creator has to hide. If He were revealed, we would simply be slaves to His delight. But if He is hidden, where do we get an example of what to become? Rav Laitman's answer: the Creator shattered the common soul and gave us a rule, "treat the other souls as I treat them." So everything we need (strength, memory, values, the image of the Creator) is outside us, in the group. The Q&A then turns to the lesson's central claim: in our time no one crosses the **Machsom** alone. A group has to reach a **critical mass** first.

## Part 1 — Why the Creator Hides, and What We Build From

- If the Creator were revealed, "we would be automatically absorbing His sensations." To make us **free and equal to Himself**, He conceals Himself.
- He gave us a starting point, the **point in the heart**, the point of **Bina**. Our whole ego, in its "scope, power, strength, and depth," is equal to the Creator, with the opposite sign.
- The method: place the ego under the point of Bina and use it only to bestow, the way the Creator uses Himself toward us. That raises **Malchut** through Bina to **Keter**.
- For an example to copy, the Creator shattered the common soul into many parts. A **small group** is a miniature model of the common soul of Adam. There I learn from them, they learn from me, and it is "more reliable, more joyful."

## Part 2 — Everything Is Outside Me

> Whether he wants to or not, the person is still under the influence of the society. Therefore, the choice of the surrounding society is ultimately my only free choice.

- A person forgets that his desires, moment by moment, are set not by himself but by the Creator, or by his ego, "which is one and the same thing" until he's corrected.
- "I have no strength of my own, I must ask for it from above." Nothing is within a person except the starting point of Bina. Everything else comes from the society or from the Creator.
- **Don't blame yourself for being what you are.** "Why am I not like this? But I cannot be any different." The only thing to reproach is **forgetting** that my good thoughts and strength have external sources.
- The environment is a **repository**: my memory, my guidance, my instructor, parent, educator, and doctor. "I wake up and it must remind me where I am, what I am, which direction I should move." It can do this because I already invested all of this into the friends.
- People who fall out of the group lose the sense of being on the path. Several **Reshimot** will spin through before they feel the difference and are pushed back.

#### Egoistic dependence becomes concern for the friends

- If my whole future, in this life and the next, is in their hands, "it becomes terrifying." Why aren't they worried? I start to demand from them and to care about their spiritual state, like a seriously ill person who depends entirely on those around him.
- This starts from an egoistic point, **Lo Lishmah**, but it produces the urgent need to gather, write a charter, organize, and constantly support the shared aspiration.

## Part 3 — The Group Sets My First Nine Sefirot

- **Malchut** is simply the will to enjoy, "from Coca-Cola, food, sex, honor." The first nine **Sefirot** are all the other souls. I only determine *that* I want to yearn somewhere. The group determines my **vector**: what connection, altruism, and the form of the Creator are.
- So no one can be greater than the group. A member can be smaller, but "never can a person's imagination about the Creator, his attainment, be greater than what the group presents to him."
- A group can face the right direction and still be too low to let anyone past it.

## Part 4 — Critical Mass and the Individual Leap

- Rav Laitman says dozens of people in Bnei Baruch and groups abroad (Moscow, Odessa, Kaliningrad) are individually ready to cross the Machsom. "If they lived with my Rav, 15 or 20 years ago, they would have passed." What's missing is the group's level.
- In past generations, five or six people around a Kabbalist were enough. Today "we need a huge group and a correct group, in quantity and in quality."
- **Two dependencies**, which he admits he's struggling to put into words:

| The group's part | The individual's part |
|---|---|
| A **critical level**: complete agreement, mutual responsibility, each working so that everyone reaches the goal | An additional investment from himself, beyond the group's level |
| A "blurry," lower potential barrier, the step everyone stands on | Wanting to break away from all his egoism, even if not yet genuine, and tuning to the **Ohr Makif** |
| Provides the base to jump from | The jump itself, which is individual, but only from that base |

- There is a general Ohr Makif and a personal Ohr Makif, and both help with the leap.
- The priority is **unifying the groups** more than raising any one group's level.

#### Why the rules changed

> We are at the threshold of the general correction, not the particular correction that happened in past centuries.

- Kabbalists of earlier generations, in bodies or not, reached personal Gmar Tikkun and act as **conduits** for **Ohr Pnimi** into our world. There's now enough of it. Individual Kabbalists aren't needed anymore; that inner light awakens the Reshimot of the masses.
- Since about the end of the 20th century, "the world is related to from above as one common creation. There are no individuals." So the work has to aim outward, at more people, while raising quality from within.
- A group of 100 or even 300 won't be enough unless it's connected with groups of different mentality, geography, and social conditions, "a miniature model of humanity." He adds that he can't predict the future, since "none of this has ever been realized before."
- A small group relates to the large group exactly as a person relates to his group: complete acceptance and dependence. "The general and the particular are absolutely alike."
- Once such a system is launched it becomes **self-correcting**: people wobble, but the whole returns them to a more corrected state. The general Ohr Makif treats it as a single organism.

## Part 5 — Q&A: Providence, Israel, and the Kabbalists

**Is personal providence (Hashgacha Pratit) abolished?** No. General providence works through the Ohr Makif; personal providence is the Ohr Pnimi to each soul, which we don't sense. But now the whole world must absorb the Ohr Makif as Ohr Pnimi, so what counts is how a person relates to the whole, not what he did alone.

**How does this relate to the Jewish people?**

- Rabbi Elazar, Rabbi Shimon's son and "the next degree," always speaks of **humanity's** elevation. "We follow Rabbi Elazar more than Rabbi Shimon."
- Kabbalah was always meant for the whole world. He cites Abulafia (who, he says, taught Kabbalah to the Pope) and later groups of non-Jews in Europe.
- The group Abraham created later called itself a people "without any biological reason." Its job is to pass the method to the world. The midrash about the nations refusing the Torah means no egoistic desire accepts self-correction on its own; the quality of **Bina** has to be inserted. The Jews are a **conduit**, "a necessary intermediary."
- Until about the 18th century (he mentions the Shlah), every author of Jewish religious books had real attainment. His own Rav, Rabash, was "the last of the Mohicans," the last link in a thousand-year chain.
- Asked about the destruction of European Jewry, he says it follows from delaying the **Reshimot** that must be carried out with respect to the rest of the world, and "the same awaits us in the future if we don't do what needs to be done."

## Part 6 — Q&A: The Machsom and the Red Sea

- **What the crossing is:** "When you sense one single force that envelops the entire world, and you merge with it, and your self disappears."
- Before it, there are illusions of "I'm already in the world of Infinity." When it happens, "you'll find out about it yourself."
- The last states before the Machsom are chaotic, like the Exodus: Pharaoh's army behind, the sea in front, no idea where you're going, only that you can't stay.
- **Nachshon** is a quality in us that jumps into the sea ready to drown just to escape the ego. Then the waters part. The **Gevurah** (strict but fair judgment) in Bina is the barrier, and it disappears, letting people into Bina's mercy. **Yam Suf**, "the end sea," is where egoism ends and bestowal begins.
- Only after the Machsom, once a real **Partzuf** forms with its **Rosh** (Mocha and Liba), does a person start to understand and organize what he went through.
- **Machsom** means complete dissolving of the self in the Creator through the group: "What does it mean to pass the Machsom if not to annul your I?"

## Part 7 — Q&A: The Individual's Role and Practical Connection

- **The individual's role** is only how much he merges with the group and serves it. The "L'Chaim" toasts at meals aren't just credit; they lift up qualities to learn from.
- **Knowledge without action is dead weight.** Knowing the articles or the **Talmud Eser Sefirot** without using it in group work is "Hochmato meruba mi-ma'asav," wisdom greater than deeds. At death, memory disappears "like a computer." Only corrected qualities and aspirations go on.
- **The test of a lesson:** "If you walk out feeling more responsibility to connect to the group, the lesson was good. If not, it's as if the lesson didn't happen." The group is the means, never the goal.
- **Joint work:** a group that isn't connected enough should invent shared activities. He recalls folding cardboard boxes together for a few shekels, "even more important than study," though "you need a bit of both."
- **No private spiritual advice.** He'll help with a stomach ache or a problem with your wife, but spiritual questions everyone has get answered in lessons, because "the Creator doesn't see us point blank. He sees us all," in our role toward humanity.
- **Between groups:** a shared "bulletin board" of daily actions and a direct online connection. Small events count, because "we can't weigh them in spirituality."
- **The group's critical level:** each member is ready to give his own Makif to anyone in the group. Beyond knowing my destiny is in their hands, I reach the point where I *agree* to it and would choose it even if I didn't depend on them. "I approve of the structure of the Creator."

## Part 8 — Q&A: Family Roles, Customs, and Daily Life

- **Men and women:** in a normal family, women lead upbringing and men lead the "hunting," the active dissemination. In the group, the women's part supports the men's. He adds that much of the work closest to spirituality (publishing, editing, translation, audio and video) is done by women, and that he prefers working with them because they're more diligent.
- **The kippah** is a custom, a sign of modesty before the Creator, not required. "We do not require any national or religious paraphernalia. Kabbalah is above all of these things." He cites Baal HaSulam in "The Last Generation": let every nation keep its own religion. "It's all about correcting the heart."
- **Symbols and the Masach:** the laws Moshe wrote came from spiritual attainment, and Talmud's agricultural and Temple details describe corrections up to Gmar Tikkun. We don't need them for spiritual work. How to build a Masach on each Reshimo (Ibur, Yenika, Mochin; Aviut Shoresh through Dalet) is studied in the Talmud Eser Sefirot.
- **A full work life:** whatever obligatory work a person does counts as serving humanity. If all he can give is thirty minutes a day, filled with intention toward the Creator, "that's enough." The question is only "where is his heart?"

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The Creator hides to make us free.** We build His image from our ego, used for bestowal under the point of Bina.
2. **Choosing the environment is the only free choice.** Strength, memory, and values come from outside, from the group or from above.
3. **Reproach forgetting, not yourself.** The mistake isn't being who you are; it's forgetting where your good thoughts come from.
4. **The group sets my first nine Sefirot.** No one can rise above what his group presents.
5. **No crossing without critical mass.** The jump is individual, but only from the level the whole group provides, and unifying groups matters more than raising any one.
6. **The era of general correction.** Since the 20th century, the Creator relates to humanity as one creation, so the work turns outward.
7. **The Machsom is annulling the self in the group.** Crossing means sensing one force and merging with it.
