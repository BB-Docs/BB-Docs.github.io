### Opening

The session opens with a question posed directly by whoever is leading the reading: "We need to reach that in which the Torah of our Rav will bring fruits, and the Torah of our Rabash will bring fruits, and the Torah of Baal HaSulam brings fruits — and our Torah, does it bring fruits?" That question, borrowed from the article's own image of a Torah that can be "barren," frames everything that follows in the archival 2002 lecture: light enters and withdraws from a person constantly, building his vessel through repeated "entries and exits" — but whether that shaking ever actually produces fruit depends on nothing the Creator withholds, only on the one thing left for a person to supply: his own attitude.

## Part 1 — Israel: The One Desire That Agreed to Correction

Rav Laitman opens with the Midrash behind the article's own theme: "The Creator, after having created all peoples and everyone on earth were settled, went to each and every one of them with the Torah — perhaps someone wants to receive it — other than Israel, nobody did." Asked what could possibly be unappealing about the Torah, he answers that the offer was never really about the nations at all:

> "There is only one desire in a person called Israel, and opposite that desire there is something called Torah. Israel — they need the Torah, meaning they're corrupted... The evil inclination seemingly exists only in Israel. In the nations of the world there is no evil inclination. Israel are the egoists — they go against the Creator, not the nations of the world. And so they need the Torah."

"Israel" here names a felt state, not a nation: "Everyone who feels that he lacks this corrective force is called Israel." Rav Laitman is careful to add that this lack "comes from above, of course" — a person doesn't manufacture the desire himself, but is required to actually feel what desire is already speaking in him and act through it: "not to remain passive, indifferent, but rather, as much as he can, take the means called Torah and utilize it." He extends the category outward rather than narrowing it: "Israel... includes the nations of the world also, who receive the aspect of Israel, and they are also corrected. It's not just Israel."

## Part 2 — Entries and Exits: How the Light Builds a Vessel

Rav Laitman describes spiritual development as light periodically shining into, then withdrawing from, a person's ordinary desires:

> "To begin with, there is the beastly will to receive. Then opposite that, a bit of spiritual light starts shining within the beastly desire, where he has desires for beastliness — money, respect, knowledge. Suddenly that desire shines to him... there's a light opposite that awakens him toward that."

The pattern accelerates rather than resolves: "each time more of the light comes, the light increases, shines to him more. It awakens then a screen over all the desires of the person, from a coarser discernment to a higher one." The alternation itself is what produces the craving: "When the light shines, the person wants spirituality. When it doesn't, he falls into corporality... until, by his exertion, he hastens this process. And ultimately, at the end of the process, he begins to want spirituality more than any corporeal thing. Being lovesick, it is called. And this comes to him as a result of the light constantly playing with him — a little less, a little more, a little less, a little more." He ties the image directly to the verse "From Zion the Torah will emerge" — read here as entries and exits themselves.

## Part 3 — Two Operations of the Light of Torah

Once a person has "passed the barrier" — decided spirituality is worth more than corporeality — the same light performs a second, different job:

> "The light of Torah performs two operations: each time it gives the person correction according to the lack, and before that, in order to have that lack, it awakens the person constantly... Enter and exit, they shake the vessel... and so a vessel begins to be formed. There's sensitivity toward light and darkness, good and evil — that's how that sense is built, and there's no other way."

The correction phase is named specifically: "the correction also comes as the light of Torah, but now as the light of AB-SAG... this is when it's clear to the person which discernments he wants to correct, and to what extent he's asking for it." Rav Laitman stresses that the number of these shaking cycles a person needs is fixed in advance, and that the task is to compress them: "A person sits alone, he doesn't do anything. You come to the society, you begin to study, you talk to someone — then you all start awakening, and you have these changes all the time. These changes help a person cross this path quicker." The standard he holds up is demanding: "Any state that lasts more than a moment is flawed... a constant, permanent desire doesn't exist in spirituality."

## Part 4 — Q&A: How the Creator Helps — and Why Your Attitude Is the Vessel

Asked directly how the Creator helps a person, Rav Laitman answers that the shaking described above is entirely His doing:

> "First of all, the Creator does everything. You have the will to receive, which is corrupted, opposite in form to the Creator... and through the lights he constantly shakes you, awakens you. The light enters the vessel — like in Galgalta, there's a process of development until the vessel removes the light. Then the same light shines again... it removes the light again. And so on. Always these entrances and exits exist, and from the Creator's perspective, this is constant pressure by which to bring a person to the purpose of creation."

But this total dependency doesn't erase a person's role — it relocates it: "The Creator does everything. And the person only determines his attitude — but by determining his attitude, the person determines everything, does everything. Why? Because your attitude is the vessel. It's not your desire that the Creator created, not his light which he sends to the desire — but your attitude, which appears as a result of your exertion." He is explicit that action performed only because the Creator forces it doesn't count: "Anywhere you don't add your own desire, opposite his desire, to become a partner with him in that action together, you will always feel pain and suffering... These are forces that will compel you to build the screen and reflected light in any case — it cannot be that you'll have any kind of screen without you wanting it."

## Part 5 — On Not Needing the Creator: A Little Child's Mistake

A participant's comment that feeling helped by the Creator might cancel one's own sense of independence draws a sharp correction:

> "The feeling that the Creator helps me annuls my feeling of self, of independence — that's not true. It seems to you that you're a big shot today — ah, what Creator, I don't need him. That's how a little child thinks. We are little children, not just you."

Rav Laitman locates this resistance specifically in men — "in men there is that: how can he be stronger than me? I need to discover a force that's greater than me, that determines everything to me, does everything to me, gives me everything" — and contrasts it with women's spiritual preparation, which he describes as not requiring the same direct correction, since the posture of "I will rule, I'm in control, I'm in charge" is, in his account, specifically "the full force of the shattering" as it shows up in men's will to receive.

## Part 6 — Q&A: "We Shall Do and We Shall Hear" — and Not Knowing What Water Tastes Like

Asked to explain the gap between "we shall do" and "we shall hear," Rav Laitman ties the two directly to action and correction:

> "'We shall do' means to perform the actions above reason. And 'we shall hear' means that we will already receive the correction... after the correction, we'll be able to hear — in the vessels of Bina, in the correction of Bina — what you, the Creator, truly want to tell us. Before the action, before the vessel, it cannot be that you receive light so that you'll understand what's being spoken about."

He illustrates why a person can't simply decide he wants spirituality with a homely image: "It's like you say, 'I want a glass of water,' but really you don't want real water — you want cola. You just don't know that it's called water. We don't know what 'in order to bestow' means. I want spirituality — spirituality means there is no me. My me, myself, is annulled completely... So don't say you want spirituality — say you want pleasures bigger than what you've felt until now." The "doing" itself is never really the person's own performance, only his exertion toward it: "You don't perform this action; certainly the Creator performs it. But you need to give a certain exertion... in return for that you receive something against your egoistic desire — not what you asked for, not what you wanted, not what you intended... it's simply that you yourself, at the end of the path, want the Creator to put you in order, because you no longer identify with your will to receive." Asked separately what "front and back" mean in a commandment, he answers plainly — "back is preparation, front is already the reception of the reward" — while a participant notes that this distinction has no clear parallel in an ordinary physical act like washing the hands, since "actions in spirituality are not recorded... spirituality is what the soul observes without the body."

## Part 7 — Why a Torah Can Be "Barren"

Returning to the article's own question, Rav Laitman locates the fault entirely in use, never in the Torah itself:

> "The Creator created the Torah as a remedy for the will to receive. And if the will to receive does not undergo correction through the Torah, then the Torah does not give birth to results from it... This is called the Torah being barren... The Torah itself is not at fault. The Torah is what a person takes from the Creator as a force to correct his will to receive, period. Whoever relates to the Torah in such a way, to the upper force, is considered to be receiving the Torah. If not, then not."

Misusing that same force doesn't leave a neutral vacuum — it flips the polarity entirely: "When it's not done in such a way, they take that same force in all kinds of other forms, and then the force of Torah disappears, and in its place becomes the Klipah — whatever you want, that is opposite that force. Klipah and Kedusha are one opposite the other. You don't have just a Klipah on its own — either you take the upper force as Kedusha, or you take it as Klipah. There's nothing in between, and neither exists separately."

## Part 8 — Growing Like a Seed Toward a Degree a Billion Times Greater

Asked how a person can possibly prepare for a spiritual degree he cannot yet see, Rav Laitman rules out the intellectual approach entirely:

> "Spirituality is a billion times greater than you... not in quantity — quantity is not the problem, but in quality. It's opposite to your degree... So you need to reach a state where you completely bury yourself, completely decay — and then, on the next degree, you begin to live. You begin to grow like a seed, from which a sprout begins to grow."

The only real preparation, he says, is working backward from the desired state through Recognition of Evil: "I need to be in this recognition of evil — that my current state is the worst compared to the upper degree, which is good, while I'm completely bad. Otherwise I won't want to get rid of it. Perform these calculations in reverse, and you'll see where to begin now." Growth even uses the very egoism it corrects, since a bigger desire is what makes the burial complete: "The more the egoism grows, the more it buries itself — because the will to receive in the seed is smaller than in the sprout. It's like a contradiction, but it helps you come out of the bad."

## Part 9 — Closing Workshop: Agreeing With What the Creator Does

In the closing workshop, one participant summarizes the lesson's own arc: "The Creator shakes a person, gives him a little light, a lot of light — and that way a person begins to feel, develops a sense... The Creator gives light, takes the light — and that's how he builds the Kli. The Kli is the deficiency — that I precisely know what I'm lacking, and then I turn to the Creator for Him to help me." That summary sharpens into the session's last live question: "If the Creator shakes up the vessels and does everything, what are we needed for here? What is up to us to do? Can we change anything?" Rav Laitman's answer returns the group to the same point Part 4 already reached: "We need first to agree with what the Creator does... The Creator wants us to be in action with Him constantly, connected with Him — and that is what we need to develop in us, in our common group. This will be the biggest deficiency — to turn to Him, and together we can build that." He closes by returning to Part 1's terms: "We are Israel, and the evil inclination exists only in Israel — the nations of the world don't have it. So all of our effort is to sustain each other in this state called Israel, and then we will understand what the Torah, the reforming light, is for." Asked to name the two ways a person can turn to the Creator, he begins, "One kind is: I want Him to correct my corrupted, spoiled desire..." — and the transcript's live captioning cuts off mid-sentence before the second kind is named.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. "Israel" names a felt state, not a nation: whoever feels he lacks the corrective force the Torah supplies is Israel, and the evil inclination this exposes exists only within that felt opposition to the Creator.
2. Spiritual growth runs on repeated "entries and exits" of light — shining in, then withdrawing — which shakes and gradually forms the vessel; the withdrawal is as necessary as the arrival, and the endpoint is being "lovesick" for the Creator specifically because of that alternation.
3. The light of Torah performs two distinct operations: first it awakens a lack, then, once the barrier is passed, it corrects that same lack as the light of AB-SAG.
4. The Creator does everything — the shaking, the light, the correction — but a person's attitude is the one thing that is genuinely his to supply; action performed only because it was forced, without a person's own added desire, brings pain rather than a real vessel.
5. Wanting "spirituality" honestly usually means not knowing what one is asking for at all — like asking for water but really wanting cola — which is why "we shall do" (action above reason) has to precede "we shall hear" (correction, then hearing in the vessels of Bina).
6. A Torah is called "barren" only through misuse, never through any fault of its own; the same force, redirected, becomes the Klipah rather than a neutral absence — Klipah and Kedusha are strictly one opposite the other, with nothing in between.
7. Growth into the next degree cannot be reached by intellect alone, since it is qualitatively, not just quantitatively, a billion times greater than the current one; it requires "burying" the present self and working backward from Recognition of Evil to see what to correct now.
8. Asked what is actually left to do if the Creator does everything, Rav Laitman's answer is to agree with what the Creator does and to develop that agreement together with the group — turning towards Him is named as the group's single biggest deficiency.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Yashar-El (Israel) | "Straight to God" — the felt state of whoever recognizes he lacks the Torah's corrective force; per this lesson, the evil inclination exists only within this state, not among "the nations of the world." |
| Midrash | Rabbinic exegetical teaching; cited here for the account that the Creator offered the Torah to every nation and only Israel — the one desire that felt itself opposite Him — agreed to receive it. |
| Torah | The corrective, reforming force a person draws from the Creator; per this lesson, called "barren" whenever it is not actually used to correct the will to receive, whatever form the misdirected force then takes. |
| Reforms (Reforming Light) | The light without "clothing" (money, honor, sex, knowledge) that shines into and withdraws from a person's ordinary desires, gradually producing the desire for spirituality itself ("lovesickness"). |
| AB-SAG (Ohr AB veSAG) | The light of Torah in its corrective (not merely awakening) capacity, drawn once a person has "passed the barrier" and determined that spirituality outweighs corporeality. |
| Screen (Masach) | The acquired capacity to receive in order to bestow; this lesson stresses it cannot exist unless a person wants it — the Creator only compels its necessity, never substitutes for the wanting. |
| Klipot / Klipa (Shell) | The force a person builds in place of the Torah's corrective force when it goes unused; framed here as strictly opposite Kedusha, with no neutral or independent middle state. |
| Recognition of Evil (Hakarat HaRa) | Seeing one's current state as the worst compared to the next, better degree; this lesson frames it as the only way to "perform the calculations in reverse" and know what to correct now. |
| Naaseh veNishma (We Shall Do and We Shall Hear) | The two-stage order of spiritual work: "we shall do" is action performed above reason, before correction; "we shall hear" is the resulting ability, once corrected in the vessels of Bina, to actually understand what is being asked. |
