### Opening

The session opens mid-thought, picking up the previous day's discussion of slander and the "law of life," before Rav Laitman explicitly moves the room into the next day's material: the continuation of Rabash's article on slander. The central puzzle carried over from the day before — that true slander is never really between one person and another, only ever a disturbance in a person's relationship to the Creator — now gets worked out concretely: what actually needs correcting in a person (the vessels of reception), what can't be corrected until the end of days, and what a person can do about any of it right now. The answer that runs through the whole lesson, and resurfaces almost word-for-word in the closing summary, is that a person cannot correct himself directly — he can only seek out the light that corrects, the way one calls an expert rather than trying to fix a broken washing machine alone.

## Part 1 — The Two Vessels: Bestowal and Reception, and How They're Corrected

Rav Laitman opens with the underlying structure of the soul, dividing it into two parts:

> "The soul is comprised of two parts: AHP, vessels of bestowal, called Israel; vessels of reception, called nations of the world."

He states the general rule for correction plainly: the vessels of reception, on their own, can never be used — not until the end of correction — and can only take part in the work by incorporating themselves into the vessels of bestowal:

- Vessels of reception on their own are called "Gentiles."
- Once they incorporate with the vessels of bestowal, that same material is called "proselytes."
- The vessels of bestowal themselves are called "Israel."

This split explains, in his account, the two different kinds of commandments Israel receives. Because the vessels of bestowal (Galgalta veEinayim) *can* reach adhesion with the Creator, they're given a positive commandment — "love thy friend as thyself," since "thy friend" here ultimately means the Creator. The vessels of reception, which cannot be corrected until the end of days, are instead given a restriction: "what is not good for you, don't do to others" — Hillel's formulation, as opposed to Rabbi Akiva's. Rav Laitman ties this directly to a distinction in language:

> "The tongue of holiness... is the tongue spoken in vessels of bestowal, ivrit, Hebrew — the face. And what is translation, which is still not the tongue of holiness, but the tongue with which AHP prepares themselves to later reach the correction to be like Galgalta veEinayim... therefore the Zohar was also written in translation, which indicates that it's a book that talks about the corrections."

## Part 2 — Necessity, Redundant Desire, and the "Religion of the Last Generation"

Asked how the article's talk of "evil tongue" connects to actual behavior between friends, Rav Laitman turns to an extended discussion of what a person genuinely needs versus what he only wants because of others. He starts from the animal level, using Rabash's own example:

> "Like the Rabash writes here, when the wolf catches or hunts the deer, it doesn't mean it causes evil to it. It's just nature... As much as we might look at it as horrible, it's not horrible, it's just nature."

He extends the same logic to habitual behaviors that no longer serve a real purpose — not blameworthy, just leftover nature — before drawing the line at the human level, where he says Baal HaSulam's essay "The Last Generation" proposes a striking measure for what counts as genuine necessity:

> "The religion that will be in the end of correction, in the last generation, is a kind of general religion for all nations... it's not to take from society more than the last straggler."

The moment a need is calculated against what others have rather than what the body actually requires, it stops being necessity and becomes what Rav Laitman calls a "redundant" desire — belonging to the four general human desires of money, honor, power, and knowledge, even when it wears the costume of food, home, or family. He illustrates this with a story Rabash told about his own life in Jerusalem:

> "At the time when they lived in Jerusalem... there was kind of a small table and we ate on it, just 30 centimeters, and everyone would just sit on the floor... Today, basically, he says they took the same room, they divided it into five rooms, and everyone has their own private corner."

| Necessity | Redundant Desire |
|---|---|
| Measured against the body's own requirement | Measured against what others have |
| Food, home, family in their plain form | The same needs re-shaped by comparison to the neighbor |
| "A basic quota" — what even a corrected society still provides for | Money, honor, power, knowledge — dressed up as necessity |

## Part 3 — Love of Friends as the Only Measurable Test

Rav Laitman is direct that spiritual progress can't be measured on its own terms — a person can never anticipate or picture a higher degree in advance — but there is one place a person actually can check himself: his attitude toward the friends.

> "If you wish to examine whether you're getting better, check whether your attitude to them has changed. If it has truly changed, fundamentally, even a little bit, it means you've made a big step in spirituality... Does it sting your eyes less?"

A student pushes back with what looks like a logical inversion: shouldn't love of the Creator come first, by reason, and only then produce love of the created beings — rather than the other way around? Rav Laitman's answer reframes the whole order of development:

> "Love of the Creator, according to what we learn, is the last degree of the development of desire, the correction of desire. The desire exists in double concealment, single concealment, reward and punishment, and love... eternal love being the greatest, last degree of love, meaning reaching the complete end of correction."

The friends and the group, in other words, are exactly where that long development plays out — "the whole reason for this world is that it burdens the soul... so that despite the disruptions I am given, I am trying to be at the end of correction." He adds that a person is, in a sense, already at the end of correction right now, only covered by "125 covers" that must be neutralized — and compares the disruptions themselves to electrical resistance, which is what makes any feeling of light possible at all:

> "If the electricity passes through a wire with no resistance, we're not going to feel anything. We place some resistance, and then by that resistance, we get throughput out of the electricity... it's the same thing with us and this burdening of the will to receive."

## Part 4 — Faith Above Reason and the Embryo of Every Degree

A student asks Rav Laitman to explain the relationship between faith and knowledge. His answer distinguishes two paths to the same goal — adhesion with the Creator, in equivalence of form:

> "We can't go on the path of knowledge, because the way of knowledge means receiving some kind of reward in corrected vessels already... So to reach that in spirituality, we can only [go] through faith, meaning through the correction of Chafetz Chesed, desiring mercy."

He identifies faith with the light of Hassadim (attained through the screen, working on coarseness roots one through four), and distinguishes it sharply from the popular misunderstanding of the word:

> "Don't think, like the general folk in our world think, that faith is called that I close my eyes, I become a fanatic person, whatever I'm told I do without activating my mind. That's called faith [to them]. In spiritual vessels it is certainly not that way — we need the head in order to work with the light of Hassadim. There is also a lot of sophistication and a lot of exertion. It's not erasing the desires, it's knowing them, and upon them building a relationship in which I go above them. That's called faith above reason."

Because everything a person does still rides on his own desire to receive — even work done at the level of pure bestowal is still built on top of some coarseness — Rav Laitman describes each degree of growth as having its own "embryo" (Ibur) stage, a fresh stage of self-nullification required at every level:

> "In every degree you have a degree called embryo, or Ibur... To be an embryo on the degree of, let's say, 100, is a lot more than being great in the degree of 99."

## Part 5 — Society Is the Means to Draw the Light, Not the Correction Itself

Asked how to keep the goal itself — rather than the means to it — from quietly becoming the real goal (since "love of friends is the means to reach the Creator"), Rav Laitman turns to the relationship between time, thought, and action, using an anecdote about Baal HaSulam and his own Rav:

> "He also says this in the article, in the speech for the completion of the Zohar, where he tells his Rav that he's not adhered to his Rav permanently, but now he brought him a cup of tea, and later he thought that by that he wanted to connect [to] spirituality... Nevertheless these two things work together, as if that was the reason why he brought the cup of tea. Time acts for our benefit — it connects all the labors that we cannot connect."

The practical instruction that follows is not to try to calculate one's place among all the souls (something no one can actually know), but simply to aim every action and every state toward one thing: adhesion with the Creator, understood as the felt connection to all the other souls in a corrected state. From there Rav Laitman returns to the question directly and gives the lesson's central image:

> "I don't do correction actions directly... Rather, I need to care for this handyman that will come and fix this and correct this... This is like a woman who sits at home and screams that her washing machine broke — some professional needs to come in with some part and put it on there and fix it. We need to look for this person, the person who knows how to do that. That's the upper light."

The means for drawing that light, he explains, is not private effort but the environment: without the society, a person's desire for the goal stays too small to draw the light at all. He warns against the alternative — dwelling on one's own faults instead of turning outward:

> "If you're not turning to the right direction, but you're looking into your own hole... you won't correct it. You need to seek the professional that will bring you the part and know how to install it."

A student then asks how adhesion with the friends is even possible when each person's perception of reality — and of the Creator — is different in every moment. Rav Laitman answers that connection was never meant to merge desires:

> "There's no matter of connecting the desires. Each one stays in their desire, the way they perceive reality, in the root of their soul. But we build a common area between us where the Creator can be revealed."

The lesson's own summary, read out before the closing song, restates the whole arc in miniature: because hatred of the friends is, at root, hatred of the Creator, the entire path comes down to refusing to agree with that hatred wherever it appears, and turning back toward love instead.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. The soul's two components — vessels of bestowal (AHP/Israel) and vessels of reception (the "nations of the world") — receive two different kinds of commandments precisely because only one of them can be corrected before the end of days; the other can only participate by incorporating itself into the first.
2. A need stops being genuine necessity the moment it's measured against what others have rather than what the body actually requires — Baal HaSulam's proposed "religion of the last generation" is simply not to take more from society than the last straggler.
3. Since spiritual attainment can never be measured directly or anticipated in advance, a real, felt shift in one's attitude toward the friends is the one honest gauge that progress is happening.
4. Love of the Creator is described as the *last* degree of desire's development, not the first — which is why the entire work happens through the friends and the group, the place where that long development is actually burdened and pressed forward.
5. Faith above reason is not switching off the mind; it is using the mind fully while choosing to work above one's own desire to receive, at whatever level of coarseness a person currently stands.
6. A person cannot correct his own vessels directly, any more than an untrained person can repair a broken washing machine — the real work is seeking out the "corrector," the reforming light drawn through study, the book, and the society, not tinkering with the vessel itself.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| AHP | Vessels of bestowal that make up part of the soul's structure (Israel); paired here with the vessels of reception ("nations of the world") that cannot be corrected until the end of days except by incorporating into AHP. |
| Galgalta veEinayim (GE) | "Skull and Eyes" — the vessels of bestowal capable of reaching adhesion with the Creator; given the positive commandment "love thy friend as thyself." |
| Lashon HaRa | "Evil tongue"/slander — in this lesson, ultimately only a disturbance in a person's relationship to the Creator, never truly a matter between one person and another. |
| Chafetz Chesed | "Desirer of kindness" — the corrected form of Bina; identified here with the path of faith, as opposed to the path of knowledge. |
| Faith Above Reason | Not blind belief, but full use of the mind combined with a deliberate choice to work above one's own desire to receive. |
| Ibur | "Embryo"/gestation — the self-nullifying starting stage present at every one of the 125 spiritual degrees, proportionally harder at higher degrees. |
| Reforming Light | The light through which the Creator's own qualities reach and change a person; what a person seeks through study and society rather than trying to correct himself directly. |
