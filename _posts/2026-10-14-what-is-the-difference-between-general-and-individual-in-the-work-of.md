### Opening

If the individual and the collective are built by the same laws, they must go through the same stages. The first stage, as the Rambam describes it, is to keep Torah and Mitzvot for self-benefit, and it's forbidden to reveal Lishma to people at that stage. Rav Laitman uses this to ask two questions: how do *we* grow a will to receive big enough to be worth correcting, and why should Kabbalah not be pushed on a public that isn't asking for it yet? Most of the lesson is Q&A about the Baal Shem Tov, the Gra, and how the wisdom of Kabbalah reaches people in each generation.

## Part 1 — One Law for the Particular and the General

- "All of reality is ten Sefirot, nothing besides that." Every element is made of ten, and each of those is made of ten, and the same laws govern the general and each detail.
- This is **"I the Lord do not change"**: HaVaYaH is always uniform and complete, and "there's no single little element of creation that will behave differently than the rest."
- HaVaYaH is the Kli that exists in the reflected light, the desire to bestow with a screen. The will to receive itself is "considered a point," and on it there is a different order: it feels the providence of the upper light as darkness and suffering.
- **In development, too:** an individual moves from initial excitement, to the recognition of evil, to doing good, then crosses the barrier into the real work. "The same stages are what the whole public, the whole collective has to go through."

## Part 2 — Stage One: Building the Kli

- The **order of the work** on p. 278 comes from the Rambam (Hilchot Teshuva, ch. 10): the public is taught Torah and Mitzvot "with the intention of self-love," and must not be taught Lishma.
- "This is basically what you are now being taught." We came to Kabbalah because life had lost its taste. Unlike the cow that chews grass all its life, we were given "a greater deficiency," but "it's the will to receive." From above we're given little illuminations, a little darkness, a little light, "in order to grow and nurture the will to receive."
- "That's stage one, to have a Kli."

#### The desire must grow before it can be corrected

- First the will to receive wanted money, honor, and knowledge. "Now we need to develop the desire to receive spirituality. I want to devour spirituality. I don't know what it is, but I want it to be mine."
- **The obstacle:** because spirituality is concealed, a person "settles for little," suddenly becomes righteous, and stops chasing it. This can last tens or hundreds of years.
- As Baal HaSulam writes in the Introduction to the Book of Zohar, the true will to receive doesn't stop at this world; it wants the next world too. "And then, only then, we begin correcting it. Otherwise, what do you need to correct?"
- Correcting beastly desires (diets, nice education, "let the animals live") is not our work. "A person who doesn't come to the completion of this vessel for spirituality is not given any beginning of correction."

#### Only the society can enlarge it

- This stage "can be crossed only on the condition that I receive an impression from the society": envy, lust, and honor toward friends who yearn for spirituality.
- "If the society is kind of sleepy, in a coma, then this society can put me to sleep. I must escape it or change it."
- Dissemination serves the same purpose: "in the multitude of people is the King's glory." The more others are impressed, the more I'm impressed.

> Therefore the whole work, dissemination, participation, the group, they ultimately need to bring me a big desire for spirituality, and if not... it's a sign that something's flawed.

## Part 3 — When to Teach Lishma: The Baal Shem Tov's Method

- **Q: When are children taught to perform Mitzvot in order to bestow?** Rav Laitman first questions the premise: the usual reality is a public educated as the **sacred still**, held in that form its whole life, which "can continue for millions of years," like other ancient cultures held together by a national philosophy.
- Spiritual education follows the law that **there is no coercion in spirituality**: "Educate a boy according to his own path." Only people who've developed through still, vegetative, and animate to the speaking level can be educated as speaking.

#### Hasidut as dissemination

- The Baal Shem Tov inserted "a little bit for everyone" of the internality: worlds, NRNHY, ups and downs. He didn't write books himself; his students, the first Admorim, were great Kabbalists who did.
- Hasidut comes from *Chesed*, aiming people toward the qualities of Bina. Through it they could "attract from the public" those whom these books would interest, and make them into groups of Kabbalists.
- "For that time they made great corrections." But the will to receive keeps growing, and 100-150 years later the method wasn't sufficient. What remained was "only the external symbolism," though full of hints worth studying.
- The Baal Shem Tov "didn't take into account the suffering of the people at all. He only took into consideration the eternal correction that can be brought to the world."

| | The Baal Shem Tov's era | Our era |
|---|---|---|
| **Will to receive** | Roughly the third level | Grown to the fourth level |
| **Method** | Kabbalah clothed in Hasidut: stories, customs, symbols | Kabbalah disclosed as itself |
| **The clothing** | Concealed Malchut, emphasized Bina and Chesed | No clothing, only an explanation in a language people can hear |
| **Outcome** | Drew worthy souls; later became popular custom | Still unfolding |

- "When you start asking at what age can you start teaching the children? Well, you have children who are 40 and 50." It depends on development, not age; the Gaon of Vilna was a Kabbalist by 13-15.
- The method itself is old. Moses used it too: general dissemination to the whole nation, then making students of those who were worthy. "Give a general light with which special souls will then work, and then you give them the personal private method."

## Part 4 — The Baal Shem Tov and the Gra

- **Q: How can two great Kabbalists disagree?** "There was no dispute between Baal Shem Tov and the Gra." Like the house of Shammai and the house of Hillel, "both are the words of the Living God." Perhaps they reflect different types of souls: the Gra's method "is a method for individuals."
- Rav Laitman admits, "I still have problems expressing that... It's a problem, bringing it down."

#### Why some Kabbalists resisted dissemination

- Opposition to Kabbalah came from Kabbalists' care not to remove the people's protection while they're inanimate.
- **The sheep:** one dog can lead a thousand sheep in a line. "Now imagine yourself that you give these thousand sheep a shot which increases the will to receive." They'd run around, harm themselves, "bring about revolutions and wars."
- So Kabbalists arranged Torah and Mitzvot as a fence for the still. "And if you exit that boundary... it's a problem already."
- Until the time of the Shla, everyone who wrote, for or against spreading Kabbalah, wrote from attainment. "All these divisions, conflicts, resistances, it's not on our level. They had this responsibility to protect the people."

## Part 5 — Why Not Push Kabbalah on the Orthodox Public

- "If I had the option of giving everyone the wisdom of Kabbalah, I wouldn't do that." Baal HaSulam "was not afraid of it. But he is not me."
- With secular people "there's not much to corrupt," and he can bring a method to whoever wants it. With "the still of Kedusha, it's a huge problem."
- Making Kabbalah's greatness widely known, so that those "in a truly bitter state" can come, is fine. Breaking the framework artificially is not.

> If their desire to receive will grow more than their framework and they will break out... then that's fine. But it's not me that's causing that. That has to come from within.

- "Don't put an obstacle before a blind person." Adding your desire to his is **coercion**, like breaking a child's toy car and saying "let's do something real." "Where's my car? Why did you break it?"
- Courses for the Orthodox that only add flavor to their still education are Hasidism, not Kabbalah, and no one objects. A "left line" that criticizes everything "they would immediately break me, and justly."
- Baal HaSulam wrote that abandoning the internality brings all the world's problems, "but you can see that he didn't manage to do anything about this either." Rabash tried too. The change will come from the surrounding light and from desires that grow "from year to year" until the framework can't hold them.

#### Even those who knock on the door

- **Q: Should we accept Orthodox people who come on their own?** "I also don't think we should do it, for the same reason." One person who joins stands out in his herd and pressures the others.
- There's also political power involved. Rabash told him the Baal Shem Tov built mass Hasidism partly out of fear of a head-on clash with those who opposed him. "So I'm the same." Accepting even one means "a direct fight with them. For what?"
- "We don't need to be world reformers. If I can give a certain solution to those who are ready for correction, that is my job." Awakening the will to receive in someone who has no question only corrupts him.

## Part 6 — The Difference Between Baal Shem Tov and Baal HaSulam

- "I don't think there's a difference" in the method itself; Baal HaSulam says he has nothing to dispute with the Baal Shem Tov, "a giant."
- The difference is that the Baal Shem Tov had a way to bring Kabbalah to the public: clothed in Hasidism, stories, ceremonies, and Admorim who comforted people during the pogroms. Baal HaSulam had no such clothing, "we also don't."
- Today a clothing would "appear not real." Instead "we need to find a special language for people within the language of Kabbalah." Saying "desire to receive" or "screen and reflected light" means nothing to a newcomer. "It's not a clothing but rather an explanation."
- **The whole method of Hasidism:** "He concealed the Malchut and increased the Bina, the measure of Chesed." Kabbalah reveals the Malchut, the desire to receive, and works with it. Those whose desire demanded correction came to him, "and from them he made Kabbalists."

## Part 7 — Great and Small Kabbalists, Feeling and Mind

- **Q: What makes a great Kabbalist?** The electrician, the physicist, and the child who presses the switch all use electricity. The more exalted and concealed the causes you know, the greater the Kabbalist. "It's not an intellectual feat"; attainment comes gradually, by degree, and also depends on the type of soul.
- **Answering questions:** "I'm not going into my mind ever." The question turns into an emotional question in him, he gets an emotional answer on his degree, and passes it through the intellect "as much as you can accept it, as much as I can express it."
- Rabash wrote, but couldn't speak clearly: newcomers from Tel Aviv asked after ten lessons, "Which language is he speaking?" Expressing attainment "has nothing to do with the degree."

#### The will to receive and the mind

- "We are feeling creatures." The mind only explains the reason for what I feel and helps the will to receive reach a better state. "If the will to receive is completely full, it doesn't need the mind."
- The will to receive fills every cell, "but where is it? It's not there," like another body inside our body, a spiritual dimension.
- Feelings can't pass from one vessel to another. "The feeling itself has no words, it's light." To share it, I turn it into symbols and letters, which is why there are many ways to convey an idea.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The individual and the collective follow the same law** and pass through the same stages: excitement, recognition of evil, doing good, crossing the barrier.
2. **Stage one is building the Kli.** The will to receive must grow until it wants spirituality, even the next world, before there's anything to correct.
3. **Only the society can enlarge that desire** while spirituality is hidden; a sleepy society puts me to sleep.
4. **There is no coercion in spirituality.** Lishma is taught only to those whose desire has already broken out of their framework on its own.
5. **Every generation needs a method fitted to its will to receive.** The Baal Shem Tov clothed Kabbalah in Hasidut; today it must be disclosed directly, in a language people can understand.
6. **The method is the same since Moses:** a general light for everyone, a personal method for those who come.
7. **We are feeling creatures;** the mind only helps express and convey what is felt.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Individual and Collective** | Governed by the same laws; the public goes through the same stages of development as one person. |
| **"I the Lord do not change"** | HaVaYaH is uniform and complete; no part of creation behaves differently from the whole. |
| **Order of the Work** | The Rambam's stages: first Torah and Mitzvot for self-benefit, Lishma revealed only later. |
| **Kli for Spirituality** | A will to receive grown large enough to want spirituality, the precondition for any correction. |
| **Sacred Still (Domem de Kedusha)** | A public held by its education in a stable, protective framework of Torah and Mitzvot. |
| **No Coercion in Spirituality** | Adding your desire to someone who isn't asking; forbidden, like breaking a child's toy. |
| **Hasidut** | The Baal Shem Tov's method: Kabbalah clothed in stories and customs, concealing Malchut and emphasizing Bina and Chesed. |
| **Gra (Gaon of Vilna)** | A Kabbalist equal in degree to the Baal Shem Tov whose method suited individuals; their paths unite at the end of correction. |
| **Dissemination** | General light for everyone, through which worthy souls come and receive the personal method. |
| **Feeling and Mind** | The will to receive feels; the mind explains and conveys feelings through symbols. |
