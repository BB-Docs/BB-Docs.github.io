### Opening

From this lesson on, the first part of the morning lesson begins combining Rabash's letters with the articles, starting with Letter 13. Rabash wrote it to close students, "with hints, intimations." The Gemara's strange statement, that one who engages in Torah and good deeds "and buries his sons" has all his iniquities forgiven, means burying the concepts received from the environment that contradict the path of bestowal. Rav Laitman draws out what the letter hints at, and what we must do: connect our very worst point with the highest point, the Creator, and raise it to Him to be corrected. He also explains why falls must be met at once by a prepared mechanism, the group, that brings us back up, and why all our free choice lies in building that environment inside the world rather than escaping it.

## Part 1 — What the Letter Hints At

- This letter "is of a sort you write to students, to people who are close to you," full of hints. Rabash points to the division after Baal HaSulam passed away.
- There were two groups in the people of Israel. Some said it is enough to be proficient in the Gemara and Mishnah: by knowledge we'll be awarded more knowledge until perhaps we attain the high knowledge, "and that is sufficient."
- That means going "with the mind, with the intellect, and not above reason." In this kind of work a person comes to questioning the beginnings, and this corresponds to the **shell (Klipa)** against the mind.
- This is the residents of the **Galilee**, who were not meticulous with their words and learned from more than one teacher. The residents of **Judea** were meticulous: everything in the holy tongue, words of bestowal, from one teacher. Following other teachers instead of Baal HaSulam "is actually the whole problem."

#### Burying the sons

- "Buries his sons" means burying all the concepts and perceptions received from the environment that contradict the true path of the Creator. Those perceptions will be revived at the end of correction and even deserve gratitude for the work they caused, but for now they must be buried.
- Rav Laitman sums it up: "You need to bury all your understandings and only change the heart." The prayer must come "from the heart, not the mind."

## Part 2 — Connecting the Worst Point with the Highest

- The difficulty in all the spiritual work is connecting **three points together**: the very worst point in a person, his actions, and the Creator, the crown, the pure force of bestowal.
- A person can't understand how these can be "together, not adjacent, simply in one point." How can the Creator be together with "that point of filth, the very worst, the lowest thing in the person?"
- It is specifically where a person "does not run from one point and adhere to the other, but to both," and by his actions connects them, that the connection happens. He elevates all of his very worst things to the Creator and wants Him to take charge of them, to clean them.
- "The mother will come and clean her son." The mother is **Bina**; the light comes and corrects those horrific qualities. "And in truth they aren't bad. That's just how we feel them."

> And that is actually called raising MAN. I don't take from myself some kind of awakening towards the Creator. No, I need to rise to Him with all of my dirtiest things.

#### What the worst things are

- The worst things aren't the lust for sex, honor and money. "These are very trivial things," and they fall away from a person by themselves.
- "The worst things are those which disconnect me from Him." Of course they come mixed with human things, but those are what I must scrutinize and raise.
- Raising **MAN (Mayin Nukvin)**, the female waters, means raising my lacks, the **recognition of evil**: "I need to feel the evil, its stench," and elevate it as what I want to get rid of, so He can clean and correct it.
- Otherwise what would you rise with? "You dress nicely and you're pretty to look at and you have nice good intentions." That isn't something you can turn to the Creator with.
- Don't make some compromise and call it the middle line. "Forget all those ideas." Connect the points, and "this is called Go to the Craftsman Who Made Me."

## Part 3 — From Disconnection to Connection

- When I can no longer justify my disconnections, I begin to see "how He gave you all these things deliberately and there is nothing bad about them. They are just means for connection." Your "entire sewage," everything you discover, is given so that through it you will come to need Him.
- **Q: Are these things bad in themselves, or because they cause disconnection?** "The worst thing about them is that they cause disconnections." They don't vanish; "they now become links in the chain of connection."
- I need to justify Him for creating these qualities in me. And if I raise them and demand, "and other than demand, what word do we have that's stronger? Insist," then they become a connection.

#### An example from life

- Suppose I did something that seemed bad to you. You tolerated it, kept quiet and covered it with a nice attitude, but it caused distance.
- Then you can't hold it in anymore. You come and demand, "Why do you keep doing this?" I explain that my intention was the opposite, to do good to you. Once that is revealed, the misunderstanding, the disparity of form, "causes greater connection."
- So it is with the Creator: the disruptions ripen in us again and again "until we're suffocated" and come to Him and shout. Then it's revealed that all these things came to tie us back to Him.

#### Coarseness with a screen

- These things themselves become the **records of coarseness (Reshimot de Aviut)**, records that distance us from the Creator. But according to the **record of clothing (Reshimo de Hitlabshut)**, I receive a power from above, acquire a **screen**, make a **coupling by striking** with reflected light.
- Then the record of coarseness becomes such a connection "that the record of clothing is worthless next to it." It opens the entire depth of the **Partzuf**, and the Creator enters the vessels.
- "And so we say about coarseness that without a screen it's bad and with a screen it's good."
- **Q: What am I crying about, the situation He puts me through, or that I forget Him?** "It's both things. Don't make all these distinctions. The main thing is to cry."

## Part 4 — "My Prayer Next to My Bed"

- Abba Binyamin said he regretted all his life that his prayer was not "next to my bed." Bed (mita) means down, a fall. He didn't regret the fall itself, "because the path of the Lord is that of ascent and descent."
- What he regretted was that when he fell, he did not immediately grow stronger and pray to the Creator for help. The fall should be "between north and south," right and left, on the desire to receive in the heart, not in the mind.
- "We must always make sure that the fall from the side of the obstructors will not take up a lot of time." Rav Laitman marked this in bold: "This is very important. We need to build this mechanism around us."

#### Building the mechanism

- A person in a descent is "like a person who is sick or dead or asleep." He has exited holiness, the will to receive is in charge, foreign thoughts fill him, "and there's nothing more to do" by himself.
- So, like someone critically injured, he must fall into trusted hands that take care of him and bring him back to life. That needs means prepared in advance, so that when he falls "he will immediately rise."
- When he rises to connection, he can raise specifically the worst points, the ones that obstruct him most. Then a response comes from above, "the power in order to bestow," and with it we cross the barrier.
- This determines the pace, and as the Introduction to the Talmud of the Ten Sefirot explains, apart from the pace "we don't actually have an impact on anything." So the work is to be connected to the goal "as many minutes as possible in the day."

#### Only the general can do it

- Connect three points together: me, the group (the mechanism through which I sustain myself and amplify my address to the Creator), and the Creator Himself. Then "I truly aim towards the Creator with one line."
- "Simply the power of the group can sustain and hold each and every one such that a person falls and immediately floats back up," and jumps back on his feet. "That's only the general power of the group can do."
- We must go through every state already in our **Reshimot**, "our spiritual genome," the predetermined links of the chain we descended through from the root of our soul. All we can do is accelerate the times.
- Besides speed, this brings inspiration and a different feeling of development. A person accepts the descents gladly: "He's always up. And he descends down only in order to bring more and more flavors to this feeling of connection."

## Part 5 — Entirely Dependent on the Environment

- **Q: Is my force of thought and prayer enough of a defense against descents, or do we need more systems?** "We need a lot." In the end a person realizes he depends on the society.
- Apart from the most basic bodily desires, which even someone alone in a forest would have, "all the other things come to us through the environment." So we need an environment of our own that replaces everything received through education, fashion and outside influences.
- "I am 100% dependent on the society in which I find myself." The environment I choose and build must be stronger than the one outside, so that going out "should feel to me as though I am simply going out into a desert."
- Look how strong some closed societies are: a person can go away for months and stay loyal because they influenced him so strongly. "We need to build a system to counter the external system."
- The Creator arranged the external system very wisely to "disturb us precisely in the ways we need." All advancement happens against the disturbances. Like the servant whose minister disguised himself as a robber, through the battle "you acquire wisdom and understanding" and come to know the Creator's qualities, which only look negative to the will to receive.

#### Nothing else to do

- **Q: Is caring within the group enough to protect me?** If you're in a society that truly cares about progressing correctly to the Creator, invest in it and make yourself 100% dependent on it, "then nothing else is needed."
- As the article on freedom says, there's no other way to control our fate. Invest in yourself only "in order to connect with the society, to bestow upon the society, and to annul yourself before the society. There is nothing else."
- There are four factors: the foundation and how it develops, the environment and how it develops. "My soul is the same soul," the same seed that must grow into me and not "some oat stalk." Only through the environment can it develop. That's why people send their children to Oxford rather than let them wander the streets.
- **Q: And the millions over thousands of years who failed to reach adhesion?** Most failed "because of this principle of the society." There are other calculations from above, but "we need to deal with what's in our hands," and there free choice applies to us 100%.

## Part 6 — Living in Two Systems at Once

- **Q: How do I practice disconnecting from the mechanism during the day, so I can return to it?** We're obliged to be involved in the world the Creator organized: to serve in the army, work, marry, have children, deal with all its problems.
- Within this large society, with its stereotypes, fashions, trends and news, "you need to build your own society right there within it. Not to go off somewhere else and isolate yourself." The work is to live within both systems together.
- The external society constantly pushes and challenges your inner society to grow stronger, "so it's forbidden to distance yourself from the general environment." If you can't stay connected all day, "it's a problem with your society because it's not strong enough."
- Some people disconnect easily, immersed in science or in their own trance. That won't happen with us: "We will receive disturbances that are precisely measured," according to each one's time, state and strength.
- The Rabbi told of his childhood classroom: when he wanted to run away, he was struck with a stick and then had to kiss the hand that struck him. "You will reach a state where the hand that strikes is truly a blessed hand."

#### No isolation

- **Q: What if we build our own community, like a kibbutz, without outside distractions?** "No, no, no. We must not isolate ourselves." It seems easy to escape the chaos, but then "we'd be given other disturbances, much worse ones among ourselves."
- The tendency now is the opposite: to enter the general population and publicize. "Correction does not come through isolation from the public. That is absolutely no."

## Part 7 — Disturbances and Pain

- **Q: Do the disturbances themselves matter?** They come "precisely according to what a person needs." They aren't really disturbances: the light presses against the Reshimot and brings interactions we perceive as disturbances. Like pressure on a balloon, they are there "so that in response to them you'll develop and expand."
- **Q: Do I need to understand which disruption came and why?** No. "Wherever a person thinks, that is where he is," so adhere to the Creator above them as quickly as possible.
- We're not yet in the stage of immersing in them, understanding them and developing through them; that comes later in the stages of **Ibur, Yenika and Mochin**. "Right now we're only building our resistance." If a person begins chewing over them, "that already turns into a Klipa."
- **Q: Does a person become apathetic to pain with time?** Never. He changes his sensitivity. As with a wound that must be opened to heal, compared with the greater harm, "this pain is already felt not so strongly."
- We are beings of feeling. I assign every sensation a value by its importance and choose accordingly. "The willingness to suffer" builds the vessel. It's not that you suffer; your evaluation has changed, and you can give up the small fulfillments without feeling pain.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Bury the sons.** Concepts received from the environment that contradict the path of bestowal must be buried; change the heart rather than the understandings.
2. **One teacher, one language.** Going with the intellect is the shell against the mind; the true Torah is learned in the holy tongue of bestowal from one teacher.
3. **Raise the worst with the highest.** Connect the worst point, the actions and the Creator into one point, and raise the recognition of evil as MAN for the mother, Bina, to clean.
4. **The worst things are disconnections.** Brought to the Creator with insistence, they become links in the chain of connection; coarseness with a screen is good.
5. **Prayer next to the bed.** Falls are part of the path; what matters is rising at once, through a mechanism prepared in advance, which sets the pace of development.
6. **Everything depends on the environment.** Beyond bodily desires all comes from society, so build an inner society stronger than the outside one; there's nothing else to do.
7. **Live in both worlds; don't isolate.** Measured disturbances strengthen the inner society; adhere to the Creator above them rather than delving into them.
