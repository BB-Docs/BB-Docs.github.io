### Opening

The lesson opens with a hard admission: the more a person actually advances in spirituality, the more concealed the Creator becomes and the more empty his own effort starts to feel — exactly backwards from how effort works in this world. Rav Laitman and an unusually long, probing Q&A spend the whole lesson working through what that means in practice: how a person survives paying more and receiving what feels like a blow in return, what it actually means to "cross the barrier," why real prayer can only ask for closeness and never prescribe the method, and why a person can't reach or correct his own desire at all — only draw the surrounding light that reaches in and does it for him.

## Part 1 — Concealment Grows With Advancement, and the Paradox of "Payment"

Rav Laitman ties the article to the Exodus itself — Pharaoh, the ten plagues, the exile deepening right up until the exit — as the pattern for how concealment actually works: it grows precisely as a person advances, not the other way around.

> "To the extent in which a person approaches spirituality, he sees himself as more far from spirituality. The more he invests forces, the more he sees that he's losing."

Asked, in effect, why anyone would keep laboring for what feels like a blow each time, he locates the one place a person can still find help — never inside himself:

> "He has nothing to do but to receive the recognition of the greatness of the Creator from the group, not for himself... because the recognition of the Creator, the group can't give him — they're in the same state like him. But to hear how great the Creator is... that's called environment, society, rav, books, all that's around a person, not the forces within a person."

## Part 2 — What "Crossing the Barrier" Actually Means

Reading Rabash's image of a leaking vessel, Rav Laitman explains why a person's own exertion can never be enough on its own:

> "His vessel has got holes in it, it's got leaks, everything he grasps or perceives exits... until he comes to a point where he has no forces to do anything with this vessel, but rather to ask for the Creator to correct the vessel... How do we close these pores? With a screen in order to bestow. And then the vessel becomes whole."

Pressed to define the barrier concretely, he insists it isn't produced by the person's own despair or effort, but given:

> "It's the Creator who transfers me... He gives inside my desire and my forces... This means that he's giving me a screen. And it's called that I crossed the barrier. Prior to that, I didn't have a screen."

## Part 3 — The Circle of Light and Vessel, and Why Sophistication Doesn't Matter

A student names the apparent trap directly — that only the light can correct a person, but the light won't act until the person wants it, and only the light can produce that very want:

> "Only the light can correct me. Only I will invite it. And only it can make me want it... So what will I do so that he will want me, so he will give me a desire for him?"

Rav Laitman doesn't resolve the circle — he says it simply is the shape of ascent and descent. He then draws a sharp distinction between intellectual grasp and real progress, contrasting a crude, undeveloped listener with a subtle philosopher who grasps everything "from the air":

> "It's completely unrelated to their advancement, because everything depends on the labor... practically everything only follows the account of the exertion."

## Part 4 — Why This Generation Specifically Needs Kabbalah

Asked, in effect, why earlier generations didn't need this method, Rav Laitman explains that they felt godliness directly, out of their own refinement, and so had no need of Kabbalah's explicit study:

> "They felt them from within themselves because they were refined. Because of that, they didn't need Kabbalah, they didn't put in labor... And for us, it's the opposite. We are at the end... the final vessels, the coarse vessels. There's great concealment until you can move."

## Part 5 — Praying Correctly, and Why You Can't Touch Your Own Desire

Rav Laitman first states the rule for genuine prayer — asking for closeness itself, never prescribing the mechanism:

> "The correct prayer is without any conditions. I'm asking for what is considered in your eyes to advance. And for me, in advance, whatever I will accept, as much as I will accept, I'll accept it as progress."

When a student asks about specifically requesting "the light of AB-SAG," Rav Laitman challenges the premise — a person below the barrier cannot actually know what he's asking for at that level. He then explains why self-effort can't touch desire at all, using an extended anatomical image of a spark of light traveling from the eye through the nervous system to a "center of pleasure" that no surgeon could ever reach:

> "I have no access, no approach to my will to receive, no access... beyond that, there is already truly the part within us called the part that feels. This is a spiritual vessel. No one can reach there... What enters into my eye also enters a spark of the light... In the beginning, there was a spark of light. In the end, there's a vessel, the will to receive. That's it."

## Part 6 — The Father and the Crying Baby

Rav Laitman turns to Rabash's own parable for why the Creator doesn't simply grant what's asked for on the surface:

> "A father walks down the street with his child, the baby's crying, the father isn't even looking at him... The child doesn't want a book, he wants a soccer ball... The father doesn't say anything, but the father bought the book, and he's holding the book... So the desire for the ball — will it not be scrutinized? That desire gives him, causes him to cry... until he'll want the father and the book."

The crying itself does the work: it's how the child's own desire gets exposed as unsuited to what's actually good for him, until it's reshaped into wanting the very thing he first refused.

## Part 7 — Turning to the Souls, and Why the Goal Must Come First

Rav Laitman describes connection to other souls as a matter of pure necessity, not sentiment — like needing more hands to move something too heavy to lift alone:

> "To reach degree 125 of the end of correction, you have to move something heavy that every time becomes more and more heavy. So as much as you want to rise on those degrees, you need to connect to more souls and more souls... To ascend to the first degree, it's enough to annul yourself and connect to others... Second degree, a hundred. Third degree, a thousand, and so on."

He warns, though, that this only works if the goal comes first — a group formed around anything else (comfort, charisma, convenience) becomes just another self-serving commune:

> "The plea to society can only be after I know what the goal is, that the goal is to reach adhesion with the Creator. Then my plea to society will be correct. Otherwise... it'll be egoistic."

Asked how to tell whether one's own effort is genuinely qualitative and not just quantity of labor, he points to a single, reliable sign: happiness that arises from the exertion itself, not from any particular result.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Concealment grows, not shrinks, as a person genuinely advances — the sense of "losing ground" the more one invests is the expected shape of real spiritual labor, not a sign of failure.
2. Recognition of the Creator's greatness can never be generated from within a person's own forces; it can only be received from the environment — the group, the books, the Rav.
3. "Crossing the barrier" means receiving a minimal screen (intention to bestow) as a gift from the Creator, not producing it through one's own despair, prayer, or effort.
4. Advancement is measured strictly by exertion, not by intellectual sophistication — a crude listener who invests fully outpaces a subtle thinker who grasps everything but invests little.
5. Earlier generations felt godliness directly through their own refinement and had no need of Kabbalah's explicit method; this generation's coarseness is exactly why the method is needed now.
6. A person cannot touch or correct his own desire directly — only the surrounding light, drawn through study and social/group action, can reach and reshape it.
7. Connection to other souls is a structural necessity for ascent, not a matter of preference — but it only serves the goal if the goal (adhesion with the Creator) is fixed first; otherwise the group becomes just another egoistic commune.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Screen (Masach) | The acquired capacity to receive in order to bestow; described here as what "closes the pores" of a leaking vessel and is what a person actually receives upon crossing the Machsom. |
| Machsom | "Barrier" — crossed not through a person's own effort or despair but by receiving a screen (minimal intention to bestow) as a gift from the Creator. |
| Emanator (Maatzil) | "The one who emanates" — invoked here to explain that a person never controls his own desire directly; only by turning to the Emanator can a person's desire actually be changed. |
| Surrounding Light (Ohr Makif) | Light not yet inside a person's vessel that nonetheless reaches and reshapes his desire from outside it; the only thing, per this lesson, that can actually touch and correct the will to receive. |
| AB-SAG | A specific designation for surrounding light referenced in the Q&A when a student asks how to pray for it directly; Rav Laitman questions whether a person below the barrier can really know what he's asking for at that level. |
| Keter | "Crown" — the highest of the five-part Partzuf structure (Keter, Chochma, Bina, Zeir Anpin, Malchut); named here in explaining why this final generation, with all five parts "revealed" in coarse form, faces such heavy concealment. |
| Ibur | "Embryo"/gestation — used here for the minimal, barely-detectable measure of adhesion ("small like an Ibur") a person may already have just beyond the barrier. |
