### Opening

The article closes on the image of "straight legs" in prayer — the right leg (the right line) and the left leg (the left line) both needing to be straight and equal before a prayer can be accepted. Rav Laitman opens the lecture from exactly that image to work through the article's title question: what does it actually mean, in the work, to "not add and not take away"? His answer is that this has nothing to do with quantity, effort, or speed — it means keeping a constant, deliberately renewed balance between the right line (importance of spirituality, received only above reason) and the left line (the plain, uncorrected truth about oneself), so that the two together — never one alone — can generate something new: the middle line.

## Part 1 — "Do Not Add and Do Not Take Away" Means Balance, Not Quantity

Rav Laitman immediately reframes the commandment away from anything quantitative.

> "To not to add or subtract is not a matter of quantity or power or... the run too fast. Or don't be lazy... So don't add or subtract means always keep yourself balanced. What does it mean balanced? To cause the action of the middle line to allow the Creator to make a correction on you."

Balance requires, first, standing firmly on the right line — the importance of spirituality and of the Creator as "a force of bestowal" — without letting the left line overpower and control that state. That importance, he stresses, is received only above reason; within reason, a person has at most a faint branch of it, "as though" felt in the organs, built specifically through effort invested in the group.

## Part 2 — Why the Middle Line "Doesn't Exist" on Its Own

Asked implicitly by the article's own logic, Rav Laitman makes a sharper claim: the middle line is not a third, independently existing thing.

> "The middle line itself actually doesn't exist. There's no such thing as a third thing. There's the qualities of the Creator, the right line... and the qualities of the created being... that's the left line. And the middle line is the correction — to what extent the created being can be compatible to the Creator."

Neither line, he says, is truly "us." The Creator's qualities aren't ours, and the created being's raw nature isn't ours either — both are, in his words, "two angels" given to a person to work with, not to be identified with. A person becomes "man" (Adam) only in the middle line, and only by refusing to be swept up by either line alone — standing instead at a neutral point between them, even if that point is, at first, nothing more than the single point in the heart.

> "As it says, 'Go to the craftsman who made me' — the right line — and 'there's none else besides Him' — the left line."

## Part 3 — Building the Right Line: Faith Above Reason as Deliberate Practice

In the Q&A, Rav Laitman describes concretely how to work the right line. It begins with research: seeing what the Creator's nature is said to be — great, whole, bestowing, wanting to give reward for effort — and then deliberately building faith in that description, "as though" it were already felt.

> "You're not deceiving yourself. You're working on a force... Like Rabash writes, as though you feel in your organs that you came to the sensation of the Creator, the sensation of importance, greatness, adhesion."

He is explicit that this "as if" is not self-delusion but a conscious, registered act — a person knows he is building the feeling on purpose, which is precisely what distinguishes genuine right-line work from simply being carried by a mood.

## Part 4 — The Left Line, the Power of Prayer, and the Answer from Above

Once the right line has been built, a person turns to the left and finds the opposite: no corrected qualities, foreign thoughts, lowliness relative to what was just felt on the right. Rav Laitman is precise about what happens when the two are combined correctly.

> "If you've connected both of them correctly, you have both of them at a maximum intensity, as they complete each other and no one controls the other. This means you've come to a true prayer... And for that prayer you receive something called middle line."

The middle line, when it comes, is described as something genuinely new — not composed by the person, the way the prayer itself is, but received: "a vessel of the soul from above," desires that belong to neither the right line (the Creator's qualities) nor the left line (one's own uncorrected nature), but to a true, independent "I" — the beginning of the soul, built from screen and reflected light.

## Part 5 — The Two Shells: Losing Balance to Left or Right

Rav Laitman names the failure mode on each side directly, calling each one a shell (Klipa).

> "This is called the shell of the right line... [saying] everything is in the hands of heaven, the Creator sets up everything, and who am I here? I just nullify and I'm always under his control."

The mirror failure — collapsing entirely into the left, insisting "I have no power and no nothing" — is the shell of the left. Both are described as a person losing the ability to choose or exit: "when you are there, you feel yourself as being in the correct, whole state, and then you don't have any preparation, no possibility of exiting." The two lines are not, in themselves, dangerous — but taken alone, without the deliberate merging into a middle line, either one can masquerade as wholeness while actually trapping a person.

## Part 6 — Live Q&A: How Do You Know You're Really on a Line?

A student asks how one can genuinely say "now I'm on the right line, now I'm on the left" rather than simply being thrown between moods. Rav Laitman's answer turns on conscious control, not on which line a person happens to feel.

> "If you are in one of them without your own control over this, it's not above, it's not below — it's simply that you are sunken under them... Right means you determine that this is right for me and I go for it... It's not like they are to the right or to the left of you. There's nothing. It's all desires, qualities, thoughts that you divide into right and left."

Someone swept by despair or by euphoria isn't on a line at all in the meaningful sense — he is simply being controlled by whichever "angel" currently has hold of him, which Rav Laitman calls being "an angel" rather than being human. Only a deliberately chosen, examined stance counts as genuinely working a line.

## Part 7 — Live Q&A: The Role of Society, the Barrier, and Constant Renewal

Asked where a person gets the actual strength to build and hold this balance, Rav Laitman points to the group.

> "The importance of the Creator... I can receive... from the friends. And then use it. But for that I need to invest in the friends, for then it comes as a force that can be used."

He also distinguishes preparation from the real thing: before the Machsom (barrier), all of this work is necessarily "as if" — practice, comparable to children's play that nonetheless trains a person for later real action. Only after the barrier does a person actually possess corrected qualities of bestowal and reception to work with. And in every case, the balance itself is never a finished state.

> "Balance between the right line and the left line must be renewed every second. You cannot be ever in a balance... It's like a missile heading toward the target."

Asked how to hold this in ordinary daily life — "at store, at work, in bed, while studying" — his answer is that this constant re-balancing, done consciously, using both intellect and feeling together rather than letting either dominate, is itself the work; there is no separate technique beyond doing it, repeatedly, with the group's help.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. "Do not add and do not take away" is not about quantity, effort, or pace — it means keeping a constant, deliberately balanced stance between the right line and the left line.
2. The middle line has no independent existence; it is the degree of correction — of compatibility with the Creator — produced specifically by correctly balancing the right and left lines, brought about through the light that reforms.
3. Neither line is truly "you" — the Creator's qualities (right) and the created being's raw nature (left) are both described as "angels" a person works with, not identities to be absorbed into; the true "I" is a new point built only in the middle.
4. The right line is built deliberately, through effort and society, as a conscious "as though" — genuine faith above reason, not self-deception, and not something attained within reason.
5. Combining the right and left lines at full, balanced intensity produces genuine prayer; the middle line that answers it is received from above as something new, not manufactured by the person.
6. Losing balance in either direction — total self-nullification on the right, or total despair on the left — is called a shell (Klipa): a state that feels whole but leaves no way to exit or choose.
7. Genuine line-work is defined by conscious, deliberate choice, not by which feeling currently has hold of a person; being swept by mood in either direction is not "above" or "below" reason but simply being controlled.
8. Balance must be rebuilt every moment — spirituality has no fixed resting state — and the strength to do this comes specifically from investment in the group (society).
