### Opening

The Zohar says the world was created "for Hesed." But isn't Hesed just kindness between people, Reuben being good to Shimon? Rav Laitman explains that Hesed is both the **correction of creation** and its **purpose**: the force the will to receive takes on in order to be corrected, and the very quality of the Creator that creation must reach. Along the way he explains why Hesed is Bina, why the correction can't go straight at the whole will to receive, why bestowal must be cut off from any result in the vessels of reception, and where our spiritual vessels actually are: in the friends.

## Part 1 — Hesed: Correction and Purpose of Creation

- "A created being is a desire to receive." All its measures together make up the whole of creation other than the Creator.
- **Hesed** is the force creation receives in order to correct itself, the upper Hesed called "His desire to do good to His created beings."
- So Hesed is both the correction and the goal, "because the thought of creation itself, in its entirety, is ultimately called Hesed," as written: "A world of Hesed shall be built." All states come only from Hesed and lead only toward it.
- Reaching the purpose means acquiring the qualities of the purpose. So the force of **Din** (judgment, the will to receive) takes on the qualities of Hesed, "and accordingly it is considered to possess Hesed, to receive Hesed, and even its fulfillment is Hesed. Everything becomes one and the same."

#### The will to receive always remains

- **Q: Does the will to receive remain throughout the correction?** "The will to receive is not a quality." It is matter without form; a quality is the form it takes on.
- It stays, and is revealed more strongly the more a person can correct it. The forms he makes from it become correspondingly higher forms of Hesed.
- First it takes on Bina's qualities and suppresses its own: **Katnut** (smallness). Once it has all of Bina's qualities, it works with its own together with Bina's, "and they both went together," until it reaches **Keter**.

## Part 2 — The Angels' Dispute

- At man's creation, Hesed said, "Let him be created, for he does mercy." Truth said, "Let him not be created, for he is all lies."
- If the will to receive can take on the qualities of bestowal and act as if it were a will to bestow, "then certainly it is worthwhile for the will to receive to exist."
- The real question is the **stony heart** (Lev HaEven), which can't take on Hesed. It must be "cast down to the earth" while we work with the rest, "and afterward truth shall spring forth from the earth." Even that part will be corrected at the end.
- Acts of Hesed are "clearly directed toward bestowal," and through them a person is gradually corrected until all desires except the stony heart are used in order to bestow. This is "bestowing in order to bestow, without any expectation of anything in return."

#### Why the correction is indirect

- "If we confront the entire will to receive directly, there's nothing we can do." We must mix it with Bina's will to bestow, set the stony heart aside, restrict, and gradually let Bina's qualities emerge from it.
- The angels' dispute "is not really a dispute. It simply explains the manner in which correction is possible." That is why there must be the **shattering**: "After that breaking, it becomes possible to reach the end of correction."
- **Hesed** corresponds to Bina, the correction of creation; **Truth** corresponds to the will to receive itself, the purpose.

## Part 3 — Why Bina and Not Keter?

- **Keter** is the will to bestow with no connection to the will to receive. "The Creator has no matter. He simply has the quality of giving." We have a linguistic problem: we can't speak of desire or lack there.
- A created being understands Keter only through Bina: "Hokhmah at its end, which feels that it wants to resemble Keter." Like electricity, known only through electrons moving in a wire, "we cannot perceive these things outside of their dressing in matter." Keter is the tip of the Yud; what we perceive is His relation to us: that He bestows.
- After acquiring all of Bina's qualities, Malchut attains the very point of **existence from absence** that emerges from Keter, standing "right at the boundary where the Creator and the created being meet in the thought of creation."

#### How AB becomes Hesed

- **Q: If all corrections come from Bina, how is AB (Hochma) called Hesed?** First, Hochma at its end gives birth to Bina. Second, the light of Hochma bestowing in a vessel of reception awakens in it a desire "to resemble not itself but the source of the light."
- When the screen refines from Tabur to Peh, the light leaves the Guf and returns to the Rosh, and now "the created being is already present in this light, and through it discovers the Creator's attitude towards it." I attain not just the light that fills me "but the one who fills me."
- In the Reshimot a new lack is born: to adhere to the One who filled me. "So the result of AB is that I want to resemble Keter," and so Bina emerges. That is why **AB equals Hesed in Gematria** (72).

## Part 4 — Hesed Between Created Beings

- **Q: Hesed is between people, so why is it Bina, which concerns the relation to the Creator?** The Creator relates to us in the measure of Hesed, and we must absorb it to become like Him. But He can express His attitude only toward the other created beings. "So there's no difference between relating to the other created beings or relating to the Creator."
- The Creator is "the light that fills the vessel in full," relating to the whole vessel equally. To be in equivalence of form with it, "I need to perform the same exact action." If He made a cup, I must make such a cup.
- Baal HaSulam writes that the more developed a person is, the wider his circle: the group, the society, the nation, the whole world.
- **Q: In what sense is Hesed the purpose?** Doing good to His creations doesn't mean only filling them. That's the first step. It means they "will enjoy His position, His state, being like Him," the bestower, and this measure is called Hesed. It must be used with all of our will to receive.
- Created beings doing Hesed with one another means "they correct themselves and mix together through the quality of grace and not only through the will to receive." As in the article on **Arvut**, the general correction is a world where each one cares for everyone else. "That's why the world was created."

## Part 5 — Bestowing Is Good, But Only After Restriction

- **Q: If the world was created for Hesed, why is it concealed from us that bestowing is good?** "If I knew that bestowing is good... I'd bestow and bestow and bestow," but that wouldn't be bestowal. "Oh, I feel so good, I'm bestowing" — is that called good?
- "You cannot reach the quality of bestowal before cutting yourself off from any outcome that might emerge." First make a **restriction** over each action, so its result is not felt with selfish pleasure in your vessels of reception. "And after that bestow and you'll see how good it is."
- Where will you feel it? In **reflected light**, in vessels "that you will acquire from the lacks of others. Those you bestow to, they will be your vessels."

#### Your vessels are in the friends

> Other than the point in the heart, there is no vessel with which you can work in spirituality... In him and in him and in him, all those sitting here, in all of them, your vessels exist.

- These are your parts from the **shattering of the vessels**. You need to gather them from everyone, feeling that "they all belong to you, that they're within your heart, just as they are."
- "You will enter spirituality with the vessel that exists in everyone, in the group. And nothing else will help you." Like a paycheck deposited "in their bank accounts," it is arranged by a standing order, "you for me and I for you." "That's called **Arvut**."

#### What restriction from below means

- The Creator already helped us: we exist only because of the shattering, whose sparks of bestowal bring a little light into the will to receive. Without even that tiny measure of Hesed, the will to receive would be "dead."
- Restriction from below is a request that "accumulates in the heart... a demand that comes from all the efforts." It summons a force from above, the light, which lifts a person above the vessel so he stops paying attention to whether he feels good or bad. Baal HaSulam defines it as one who yearns yet holds back his will. "It's difficult to explain."

## Part 6 — The Chicken Coop

- The article describes a person who, in an ascent, looks at the world like a chicken coop or children playing. How can he want to bestow upon them?
- "It doesn't mean that I'm now belittling them." People live by the program the Creator put in them; they go through the degrees of preparation "through this unconscious kind of development."
- The same with a student who reveals all his coarseness and egoism: "you need to tolerate him because he's developing," as long as he doesn't go against the society's framework. These are natural phenomena.
- "In the next moment they will be like you... and without tending to them, then you won't become similar to the Creator." In his awareness he isn't like you, "but as a soul, he is already at the end of correction."

#### Taking the lacks of the whole world

- "The Kabbalist takes a lack from the whole world, even the lacks that they don't feel." The masses suffer from the lack of the revelation of God, and these lacks can be raised upward.
- Beginners have very great lacks, and when a friend bestows upon a friend, "he incorporates his lack." Those closer to spirituality give bigger desires, but the masses give too.
- Even the inanimate: "You will feel the lack that exists in stones... how they also have this kind of inanimate cry... to be filled by the sensation of the Creator. All of nature is crying out that way."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Hesed is both the means and the goal.** It corrects the will to receive and is the very quality of the Creator that creation must reach.
2. **The will to receive is matter; Hesed is its form.** The desire remains and grows; what changes is the form it takes, from Katnut to working together with Bina up to Keter.
3. **Correction is indirect.** Mix the will to receive with Bina, set aside the stony heart, and correct gradually; the shattering makes this possible.
4. **Keter is known only through Bina.** AB awakens to know the One who filled it, and so Bina is born; AB equals Hesed in Gematria.
5. **Hesed toward people is Hesed toward the Creator.** He shows His attitude only through the other souls, so equivalence means doing the same toward them.
6. **Restrict first, then bestow.** Bestowal must be cut off from any result in the vessels of reception; its goodness is felt in reflected light.
7. **Your vessels are in the friends.** Except for the point in the heart, every vessel for spirituality is gathered from the lacks of others, through Arvut.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Hesed (Mercy, Grace)** | The quality of bestowal: the correction of creation and its purpose. |
| **Din (Judgment)** | The will to receive, which takes upon itself the qualities of Hesed. |
| **Hesed and Truth** | The angels' arguments for and against man's creation; Hesed is Bina (correction), Truth is the will to receive (purpose). |
| **Stony heart (Lev HaEven)** | The part of the will to receive that can't take on Hesed until the end of correction. |
| **Katnut** | The first correction, when the will to receive takes on Bina's qualities and suppresses its own. |
| **Keter** | The will to bestow with no connection to reception; attainable only through Bina. |
| **AB = Hesed (72)** | The Partzuf of Hochma whose result is the wish to resemble its Giver, giving birth to Bina. |
| **Existence from absence** | The point emerging from Keter that Malchut attains at the boundary of the thought of creation. |
| **Restriction from below** | A demand built from efforts that draws the light lifting a person above his vessel's sensations. |
| **Reflected light** | Where the goodness of bestowal is felt, in vessels gathered from the lacks of others. |
| **Arvut** | The mutual guarantee, "you for me and I for you," through which each one's vessels are in the others. |
| **Chicken coop** | The image of the unconscious world, to be tolerated and loved since each soul is already at the end of correction. |
