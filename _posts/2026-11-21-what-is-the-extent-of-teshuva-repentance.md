### Opening

The soul is a part of God above, separated from the whole only by the disparity of form that the will to receive brings. Teshuva, "returning," means coming back to that place, and the prophet gives its measure: "unto the Lord your God," until there is only one authority. For the general public, repentance concerns the act and possible transgressions. For the individuals, who want the intention to be for the Creator as well, the truth about the evil is shown from above, and that need becomes the vessel for His help. Rav Laitman explains what motion means in spirituality, the difference between returning for my sake and for His, why every corrected desire is already a commandment, and then answers at length on intention, the stony heart, the preparation stage, and where the "I" actually is.

## Part 1 — Motion in Spirituality

- In spirituality there is no physical place or motion. "We work by the desire, by changing the desire, and this is called motion": from small to great, from one desire to another, in quantity, quality and nature.
- Motion has only two directions, toward the Creator or away from Him, "because in spirituality, there are only two objects, the Creator and the created being." Moving toward Him means "greater equivalence of form."
- "Return unto the Lord your God" means "your desire should become equal to His desire." It is called returning "because we were in that place to begin with." We were moved away deliberately, so we would feel as far from Him as one end from the other, and gradually discover the distance until we recognize it as evil and begin to erase it.

## Part 2 — For My Sake or for His

- There are two attitudes to why the distance is bad. Either "I feel bad from it," and I want to close the gap so I'll feel good: "this is called Lo Lishma." Or I restrict the sensation of my desires, rise above them, and want to return by equivalence of form "because in this it is not I who enjoys, but He enjoys": that is Lishma. "There must be a reason in my action, so it is either He or I."
- Why suppress my primary nature, the will to receive that was born from the sensation of the Creator? "When the created being rises above its nature, it receives the Creator's nature instead."
- Then the will to receive itself changes to conform to Him, "and then a person is rewarded not only with a filling, but with the actual degree of the Creator." The repentance is not to return to the state as it was before, "but truly unto the Lord your God."

#### Every corrected desire is a commandment

- Every action on the will to receive that takes only the Creator's will into account "is called a Mitzvah," and in it "I actually already return." "Ten grams of some desire, I work with it, I bring it closer to the Creator in the very smallest way... in it, I've already fulfilled 'unto the Lord your God.'"
- The force for this is the reforming light. "Just as the light creates the will to receive, it also gives to the will to receive the ability to work with the intention to bestow." The light that corrects is called Torah, and the desire working in order to bestow is called commandments.
- We should picture this even now, in concealment, without imagining we're already in spirituality: if we completed 620 such actions, "we would reach full conformity with the Lord your God." "This is in fact the extent of the repentance."

## Part 3 — I, the Creator and the Action

- **Q: Are leaving my nature and uniting I, my action and the Creator into one point two processes?** No. "These three points together, I, my action, and the Creator, uniting together, that is already the end."
- Before that, each must be truly scrutinized. The "I" is my will to receive in order to receive, "the opposite of the Creator," which requires the recognition of evil. Then, who exactly is the Creator, what is the desire to bestow? Third, the exact action by which I join my point to His: "a coupling by striking through the screen."
- In terms of the vessel: a will to receive, a screen and light. Each must be judged twice, "from within my will to receive, and how I would judge them from within the desire to bestow." "I always need two points to determine the correct attitude towards something."

#### Left, right and adhesion

- Looking from the will to receive is the left line: "I want to reach that same purpose of creation in order to receive." From the recognition of evil I find the forces to ask for correction and rise above the vessel, judging it as one delighted in mercy (Hafetz Hesed).
- When the light of AB-SAG corrects me, I scrutinize what pleasure I could bring the Creator with this desire, which requires knowing the right line, His qualities. "By this, I tie the right line and the left line."
- "The point of adhesion is not that I match myself with Him." Similarity in action is the means; "the result of this action brings me to adhesion." Adhesion "is not self-annulment within the Creator so that I'm as if erased... Rather, adhesion is a state in which I attain His status."

## Part 4 — Intention Comes Only from Bina

- **Q: Rabash writes that the soul was in the whole and only then divided through the will to receive. Is there a soul before the will to receive?** "The desire does not change. It receives an intention in order to bestow, or an intention in order to receive."
- The will to receive by itself has no intention: like a baby who grabs something, or a lion devouring a deer, "it is nature." Only because it connected with Bina and broke together with it did it receive "an addition in using it," a point of attitude toward the Creator. "The will to receive is neutral."
- "In the world of AK, we have no intentions at all. That's why it's called the world of Keter, in which there are no corrections. All our corrections begin from the connection between Bina and Malchut." Baal HaSulam (Preface, item 57): in Galgalta, AB and SAG "there is not yet even a root for the four worlds of ABYA."
- The five screens of AK are intentions the Creator gives "as an example" for the five parts of the Malchut of Ein Sof below the Tabur: what Malchut must do if it wants to return "unto the Lord your God." A created being is a will to receive that also has the qualities of the Creator in it, so it sees how opposite it is.

## Part 5 — The Stony Heart

- **Q: If my root is in the second restriction, how can I relate to the first?** "We don't need to, and we cannot reach them." After I adapt all my actions to Galgalta, AB, SAG, MA and BON within the second restriction, Malchut itself is corrected, except "my root, the stony heart, which I cannot correct."
- "Correcting the stony heart means being rewarded with adhesion." Correction is going through all the actions except the stony heart, "performing 620 Mitzvot"; the stony heart is how they are all connected from above, and by it "I am rewarded with the level of the Creator," rising to GAR, the 8th, 9th and 10th millennia.
- The root of the created being is the second restriction and the shattering, or Adam HaRishon's intention to receive in the Tree of Knowledge in order to bestow, "a plan, like a Rosh, and then it broke." The stony heart existed before me and doesn't connect with Bina, so "in the second restriction, the stony heart is behind the curtain."
- **Q: So most of what I call "I" isn't really me?** The sensation of "I" comes from the merging of phase four and phase two, where the stony heart isn't present, though it is much greater. In each action I scrutinize how far I can use Malchut through Bina, and so I scrutinize the stony heart from its limit. What happens to it at the end, "from the words in the books you can draw a thousand conclusions... It doesn't keep me from living, believe me."

## Part 6 — The Preparation Stage and the "I" Between

- **Q: What is the role of the preparation stage?** "You're making very great scrutinies now," in double and single concealment, beginning the actions you'll complete in reward and punishment and eternal love. "The intentional sins and mistakes, you are committing them now."
- **Q: What are the two sides I compare, my good and bad attitude to the Creator, or attitude versus none?** "It doesn't matter. Everything comes mixed together." But Kabbalah begins only from relating to the Creator: "if He's not in your field of vision... it's not considered that you're in this at all."
- Without point two, the spark that fell into the will to receive, "I have nothing to say to him. He's just still in the degree of a beast." When it awakens, "it's called a human, because from it one can become similar to the Creator."

#### Neither phase four nor phase two

- **Q: Can I hold on to point two as an objective truth for scrutiny?** No. Both phase two and phase four change every second. "Phase two is not you, phase four is not you... You are the one who is built out of these two things in the middle between them." "Before that, there's no creation at all... This is called the middle line."
- Scrutinize your attitude toward both and use them to reach the Creator above both. To draw close means "that both phase two and phase four operate in me at full strength... I exploit them to the end, and then I'm equal to the Creator. But I look at them as two sides that do not belong to me at all."
- Before the barrier: "Don't cry from the suffering with which phase four wants to bring you down," and don't just declare how good the Creator is as phase two would impose. "Be between them."

## Part 7 — Commandments in the Body and in the Soul

- **Q: Isn't this like the practical commandments, the same actions?** There are Torah and commandments done with hands, legs and tongue, and those done with the soul. "If I engage only in physical actions without doing the same operations in the soul, this is called a commandment without an intention, it's like a body without a soul." Baal HaSulam writes at the end of the Introduction to the Book of Zohar that drying out the Torah this way causes the evil in the world.
- During study, prayer, eating and work, "every single moment you need to think about the goal you want to achieve through this action." Thinking of the goal during study draws "the light that reforms until it begins to show you yourself." But "the thought you think is not adhesion... The action is if you have a screen that unites your desire with the Creator."
- Parts 11 and 12 of the Study of the Ten Sefirot explain the spiritual meaning of the prayer: the Shema before sleep, the midnight correction, the blessings, the Eighteen.

#### Whom to speak to

- Dissemination is only to those in whom the two points have begun to connect, who already ask what the meaning of life is. Otherwise, "why break what he has for him?" Let him ripen in phase four; all of development, advertising and television hurries him to the recognition of evil.
- A traditional upbringing (the still of holiness) gives calm and direction "until a person gets a second point, a spark." Rabash once spoke in Gateshead and the students were set alight, "two weeks later, no one remembered anything": you can awaken someone for a moment, but without the second point he returns to his state. Still, reading Kabbalah books helps a person develop.
- The sages who instituted the practical commandments all had attainment. "They knew in advance that we were going to go into exile, so they sealed it very strongly": these actions accelerate development even without intention, "within 1,000 or 2,000 years" instead of over 10,000.
- **Q: How can we understand in order to bestow and in order to receive?** "Only by revealing the Creator," beyond the barrier. Below it "we were as dreamers." The 620 desires divide into ten Sefirot; we feel only the general result, as we feel the world. "The desire itself is neither good nor bad, but the intention is actually the difference between correction and no correction."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Spiritual motion is change of desire.** It goes only toward the Creator or away, measured by equivalence of form.
2. **The extent of Teshuva is "unto the Lord your God."** Not a return to the starting point but attaining His actual degree, one authority.
3. **Lishma means He is the reason.** Returning because I feel bad is Lo Lishma; rising above my nature so that He enjoys gives me His nature.
4. **Every corrected desire is a commandment.** Even ten grams of desire used in order to bestow is already returning; 620 such actions complete it, by the force of the reforming light.
5. **Intention, not desire, is corrected.** The will to receive is neutral nature; intention enters only through its connection with Bina, so all corrections start there.
6. **The "I" is built between phase two and phase four.** It belongs to neither; it is the middle line formed by using both fully toward the Creator.
7. **Acts need a soul.** Practical commandments accelerate development, but study and every action need the thought of the goal, which draws the light that reforms.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Teshuva (Repentance/Returning)** | Returning to the root by equivalence of form, "unto the Lord your God," until there is a single authority. |
| **Spiritual motion** | A change in the quantity, quality or nature of desire, either toward or away from the Creator. |
| **Lo Lishma / Lishma** | Drawing near because it is good for me, versus because He enjoys, which brings the Creator's nature. |
| **Mitzvah (commandment)** | Any action on the will to receive done only with the Creator's will in mind; 620 complete the correction. |
| **Two authorities / one authority** | The will to receive divides man from the Creator; correction restores "your God" in the singular. |
| **Intention** | In order to receive or in order to bestow; not in the desire itself but added through Bina's connection with Malchut. |
| **Stony heart (Lev HaEven)** | The root desire that never mixed with Bina; not corrected directly, but its "correction" is adhesion. |
| **Point two (spark)** | The spark of phase two in the will to receive; when it awakens, a person becomes "human." |
| **Middle line** | The "I" built between phase four and phase two, using both fully to reach the Creator. |
| **Adhesion (Dvekut)** | The result of equivalence in action: attaining the Creator's status, not erasing oneself. |
| **Commandment without intention** | A body without a soul; still accelerates development, but does not by itself advance to the goal. |
