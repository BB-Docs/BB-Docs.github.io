### Opening

Each Ten rotates its head every week. But if the friends must be equal in order to connect, how can one of them stand above the rest as the head? Where does the head get his strength, what is his actual work, and what keeps him from controlling the others? In this short conversation, Rav Laitman answers the students' questions about the role of the **head of the Ten** (Rosh HaAsiriya).

## Part 1 — Extending a Head's Term

- The Ten reported that everyone was happy with their head, Ilan, and wanted to keep him. Rav Laitman agreed to give him another week.
- **Q: Can a Ten keep someone as head for two or three weeks?** Yes, if the whole Ten agrees, and it is good for the society to know that this happens.
- The weekly rotation is the norm. Keeping the same head for another week "shouldn't be a habit," but once in a while it can happen with the agreement of the whole Ten.
- **Q: Do we feel the need for a head at all?** Without a head, "there's nothing." Nothing can exist for a long time without a head. "It only depends who is the head, what is his role, how much he understands, how we relate to him."

## Part 2 — Equality and the Head

#### The head is not part of the equality

- **Q: Many friends ask why we need a head if we need equality.** Equality is needed in the Ten, but they don't understand how a head works together with it.
- Rav Laitman's answer: **the head of the Ten does not belong to the Ten's equality.**
- On one hand, we learned that in order to connect we need to be equal. On the other hand, we don't unite in order to be equal. "This is completely not the same goal."
- We unite in order to be one body, to reach one thought and one desire, and to feel good in this.

#### We are still only preparing

- **Q: If there's a head, he's already not equal. So how does he help?** We have not yet entered unity. "We're still not one. We only prepare ourselves."
- A student summed it up: the equality needs to be in the **body**, the friends. The head doesn't need to be equal. Rav Laitman agreed.

| | Body (the friends) | Head |
|---|---|---|
| Equality | Must be equal among themselves | Not part of the equality |
| Role | Give the head his forces | Responsible for the connection among everyone |

## Part 3 — Where the Head Gets His Forces

- **Q: Where does the head receive his forces — the creative, pushing, influencing forces toward the Ten?** "From the friends."
- So the head, like a head in a body, receives his strength from the body for the coming week.
- **Q: Do I wait to feel them, or do I initiate and come to serve them?** Rav Laitman's starting point: you are willing to be the head of the Ten if they agree to it. Their agreement is what gives the head his place.

## Part 4 — The Work of the Head Is Internal

- **Q: What is the main work of the head?** "I cannot tell you. You have to write this down for yourself." There is no fixed, permanent definition.
- Being a good head means agreeing to accept the Ten that is below you, everyone who agrees to the Ten.
- "The work is in your agreement." The work of the head is internal work.
- **Q: Should I look for outside activities and exercises to add?** "No, no, no." Not by doing things on the outside, but from within: to hold the friends, bring them to agreement, and push them from within.
- The head is responsible for a certain order in the Ten, and he is concerned only with how the Ten behaves in itself. Outside the Ten, "he has no right to determine anything."

#### When the term ends

- Once a friend's week is over, his responsibility toward the Ten as head is over.
- **Q: Why not keep adding responsibility in holiness?** "We'll add more and more." For now, holding it all the time is difficult.

## Part 5 — Unity, Decisions and the Fear of Control

#### What the friends should feel from the head

- The friends should feel from the head that he is uniting them.
- He unites them "by relating to everyone with the same importance," so that no one is more than another.

#### Unanimous decisions

- **Q: Friends, mostly in Israel, are used to deciding together and unanimously. Where does the head come in? Is his vote above everyone's?** Rav Laitman: the head is responsible for the connection among everyone. When pressed whether a decision belongs to the Ten or to the head, he answered briefly: "If you do it, it'll be so."

#### Fear of control

- **Q: Friends are afraid the head will control them and use his forces in a negative way.** "It could happen."
- But all of them are the Ten, and any one of them can be the head. That's why they switch every week. "How much control can one have in one week?"

## Part 6 — How Tens Connect to Each Other

- **Q: A Ten is connected to other Tens. Through whom do they connect, if not through the head? Where is the interface?**
- A student suggested the interface is the common goal, and Rav Laitman didn't contradict this: an individual in one Ten doesn't connect to a person in another Ten, and the head of one Ten doesn't connect to the head of another. What connects the Tens is the general goal alone.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **Nothing lasts without a head.** A Ten needs a head, and everything depends on how he understands his role and how the friends relate to him.
2. **Equality is for the body, not the head.** The friends must be equal among themselves; the head stands outside that equality. The goal of uniting is to become one body with one thought and desire, not equality as an end in itself.
3. **The head's forces come from the friends.** His place and his strength rest on their agreement.
4. **The head's work is internal.** Not outside activities, but holding the friends, bringing them to agreement and pushing them from within. Each head has to define this work for himself.
5. **Uniting means equal importance.** The head unites the Ten by relating to every friend with the same importance.
6. **Weekly rotation protects against control.** Anyone can be the head, and no one holds the role long enough to dominate. Extending a term is fine occasionally, with the whole Ten's agreement, but not as a habit.
7. **Tens connect through the goal.** Not through individuals or through their heads, but through the common general goal.

## Key Terms & Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Ten (Asiriya)** | The basic working group of about ten friends in which the spiritual work is done. |
| **Head of the Ten (Rosh HaAsiriya)** | The friend who leads the Ten for a week, by rotation. He is responsible for the order and connection inside the Ten and has no authority outside it. |
| **Rosh / Guf (Head / Body)** | Here applied to the Ten: the friends are the body that must be equal and that gives the head his forces; the head is outside that equality. |
| **Equality** | The condition among the friends that allows them to connect. It is a means, not the goal. |
| **One body, one desire** | The goal of uniting: to reach one thought and one desire and to feel good in this. |
| **Internal work** | The head's real job: holding the friends, bringing them to agreement and pushing them from within, rather than organizing outside activities. |
| **Rotation** | Changing the head of the Ten every week, so that anyone can be the head and no one can control the others. |
| **Common goal** | The only connection between one Ten and another. |
