### Opening

The second part of the article explains "I labored and found." The Torah exists only in one who puts himself to death over it, because man was created with a will to receive and must go against his nature to adhere to the Creator. The general public is educated to work for reward; individuals want only the work, since only then can they be sure they aren't deceiving themselves. So Abraham, rewarded with greeting the Shechina, longed for "greeting guests," the ascents and descents in which he could still overcome and serve without reward. Like the lender who complains that his friend showed him the checks too soon, both sides show their love. The Creator took the sun out of its sheath, revealing His face, only after the concealment had served its purpose: that a person could come to aim to bestow. Rav Laitman takes this to the question of choice, and then to the practical form of bestowal: bestowing upon the group as the exercise for bestowing upon the Creator.

## Part 1 — Choice Is the Only Act

- Everything begins from the Creator. He set the conditions, chiefly that everything would be by equivalence of form. Had He filled Malchut in the ordinary way, there would be no bread of shame, no restriction, and Malchut would never yearn for correction. "All the conditions that are revealed at the end are already there at the beginning."
- What is left to man: "each and every moment when he feels that he is not exactly under the Creator's rule, he is to make a choice that he wants to be under the Creator's rule." The rule of the body "is also not our own rule, it's an angel of our body, a foreign force."
- "It's not that there's me and I choose. Rather, what is born from the choice is called man." Choosing by the will to receive isn't me; acting by the will to bestow received from above isn't me either.
- In the terms of the system: Galgalta veEinayim are the qualities of the Creator. The AHP that decides to be corrected like Galgalta veEinayim, receiving in order to bestow, "just deciding, that is called man." "That choice is the act. There is no other act in spirituality."

#### The stages of choice

- First I choose the goal. Second, I choose restriction, detaching from the nature I was born with. Then I choose to receive the Creator's qualities from above. Finally I choose to use my nature to resemble the Creator, lifting the restriction to the extent that my vessels of reception can work like vessels of bestowal.
- Whatever I do with forces and knowledge received from above "is not called doing. In this world it's called doing." Fuel burns when you light it; that's its nature, not an act.

## Part 2 — Why the Way Goes Through the Group

- **Q: Where is the choice of environment?** Adam HaRishon, before the shattering, would simply have been commanded and acted. But since he was shattered, "I have to receive my vessels anew. So I have to connect with others." That's the demand from the vessels.
- The demand from the lights: corrections come only by equivalence of form, and anything in spirituality must be whole and eternal, so I can't practice it opposite the Creator directly. "So I can do this either opposite the Rav or opposite the friend," as in the article on the completion of The Zohar, where the work still operates even while I'm incomplete.
- As a person becomes more complete he sees only himself and the Creator, and then only the Creator.

#### Seeing godliness through the group

- "If I don't see it through the group, then I'm mistaken. Then I see Him in my vessels of reception." I don't see spirituality; I imagine it. Imagined through the friends, it comes out close to true spirituality, because relating to the society shapes me to resemble bestowal.
- "The group doesn't let you be wrong about it. Whether you like it or not, the truth is in front of you."
- Annulment works the same way. Saying "I nullify before the Creator" is easy, since He is great and will surely do good. "How do you know you're willing? You have friends. Throw away your opinion about anything that they do. Just bow before them." The shattering into many souls isn't because the work is hard alone; it's impossible alone. "We're building such vessels here that can't be built otherwise."
- Even though it's still "an egoism of the society," it's "already diluted for me with several others like me."

## Part 3 — "We," Not "I," in Prayer

- "The time of study is prayer." Whatever I ask for, personal trouble included, I should see through the group: not that I want it in particular, but that the whole group should have it, "and I am amongst all the friends."
- The Eighteen Benedictions are composed this way: "we, us. There's no me there... I'm the last there."
- Seeing oneself as part of a crowd, the inner group within the soul and the external group, "situates him more correctly between him and the Creator." "It's impossible to connect to divinity without there being at least some kind of compatibility, equivalence of form through the society."

## Part 4 — Bestowing Upon the Group

- **Q: Everything revolves around love of the Creator. What is its source?** As the Creator bestows on us, we must reach equivalence of form and bestow like Him. But you can't bestow upon the Creator: "however much you'd want to, ultimately you will be bestowing to yourself." So, as Rabash writes, "take the group. Perform these exercises towards them instead of the Creator."
- It's a test: if you feel "to the Creator I would bestow, but the group, for lack of choice," then you don't yet feel they are one and the same. "The first condition for performing any kind of spiritual action... is to not take oneself into account."
- "What does it mean to bestow upon the group? Take their mind, take their desire, take everything that's theirs other than the forces. The forces are yours, and do what they want."

#### Like the restriction of Malchut

- Malchut of Ein Sof received, felt shame, and restricted: never again to receive into herself, only above herself, through a screen and reflected light.
- "And so with you, if you walk with the group... you simply shut that off. You don't receive anything into it, no calculations." "Above yourself you build all of your actions," with what the friends want of you. "That's the entire exercise." It will still be in order to receive, "but on a completely different level."
- It applies to each friend and to the group as a whole. Rabash says the simplest is to bring a friend contentment: "just check what he needs and what I can do for him."

## Part 5 — Importance Puts Everything in Order

- **Q: Bestowing on the group, or being influenced by it?** "It is one and the same." I can't bestow on something that isn't important to me, and to the extent it's important, I'm affected by it. "The child is the head of the family... Because he is dear to them, so they are influenced by him." So "work on importance, and then that importance will put everything else in order."
- Importance comes from feeling that everything comes to me through the society. Directly from the Creator comes only "a very small candle," the first awakening, the point in the heart. Everything else comes "through this magnifying glass of the society," because all my vessels from the shattering are in the friends.
- Care for each friend "because he has a part of me in him," even in a beastly way. "The closer to the ego, the better. It'll transform less, it'll be more stable. If it's only because I want to adhere to the Creator, I'll forget after one second all that philosophy."
- "Other than the work in the society, there truly is nothing." Without it, advancement is slow, with much suffering, possible only for special souls. And "in truth, the society needs to work on the person" more than the person on himself.

#### Not like the outside

- **Q: What's the difference from education outside?** A park sign saying "keep our city clean" also builds belonging to society. "The difference is the goal always": a clean city, or clean vessels and adhesion to the Creator.
- That's why Baal HaSulam was accused of being a communist. The words sound alike, but his stem "from the foundation of reality, how the Creator created everything," while theirs stems from wanting to rule. Unity and mutual love are the laws of the first nine; when the Klipa takes from the first nine, "it speaks in that manner too."

## Part 6 — Coarseness and Refinement in the Study

- **Q: What deficiency should I bring to the lesson?** Not a prayer for strength to be for the society. During the study of the Ten Sefirot, "ask for the force from above to pull me upward to spirituality, to the Creator, through the society." "Not that the society is the goal... That is simply idol worship. If at the very top the Creator doesn't stand, all the rest will fail."
- We are made of Malchut and Keter. From Keter, refinement: "as devoted as possible to the society." From Malchut, coarseness: a desire to attain as great a spirituality as possible. "As much as these two ends are in opposition, I open up my vessel and I'm worthy of receiving."
- "The study is the main prayer. All 24 hours you have to wait for that moment when you open the book." You need a very big desire to draw the force, and equivalence with it to receive it. "This is truly the means for success."

## Part 7 — The Lesson Went Dead

- A week after the seating was rearranged, the lesson died: the main group no longer felt the honor of being there, and no one else was envious of their places. "How will we come to something if in a week it's done?"
- "Each should seek fuel, either envy or fear. Whatever there is." Not to sit in someone's place; "if you would sit there, you would fall asleep." The one in front has a problem too: "no one's pushing him from behind."
- "Come to them with a demand." The main group is the one through which the Rav relates to all the students. "You receive the abundance through him, from me, from above... Each one depends on the others."
- **Q: How can I demand of someone who doesn't owe me an answer?** "Search, search. You want to calm down from my answer. I'm not gonna let you." Internal or external, good or bad, pressure or responsibility: "I don't care what form it will take, but we have to be in motion." "Extract it from the society through competition, envy, hate, connection, whatever you want. But do it."
- Rav Laitman admits he didn't go through this himself: he was connected to Rabash, "it was different," and by nature isn't built for a society. "But you have to search."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. **The work, not the reward.** Individuals want only room to labor; like Abraham longing for guests, they prefer the work in which they know they aren't serving for reward.
2. **Choice is the only spiritual act.** What is born from the choice to be under the Creator's rule is called man; everything else comes from above.
3. **The way goes through the group.** After the shattering, my vessels are in the friends, and only through them can I imagine spirituality correctly and test my annulment.
4. **Bestow upon the group instead of the Creator.** Take the friends' mind and desire, use your own forces, and build your actions above yourself, like Malchut's restriction.
5. **Work on importance.** Bestowing and being influenced are one; importance comes from seeing that everything reaches me through the society.
6. **Coarseness and refinement in the study.** Devotion to the society and a great desire for spirituality, with the Creator, not the society, as the goal.
7. **Keep moving and search.** When the atmosphere dies, find fuel in envy, fear and demands on the friends, since each depends on the others.
