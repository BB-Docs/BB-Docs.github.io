### Opening

The lesson opens with the article's core puzzle: the Zohar says slander is punished as severely as death, using the serpent's slander of the Creator as its proof text — but how can slander between one person and another carry the same weight as slander against the Creator Himself? Rav Laitman's answer reframes the whole category: there is, in truth, no such thing as slander between people at all. Every disturbance, from wherever it comes, is ultimately a message about the Creator, and "slander" only ever means whatever weakens a person's relationship to Him. From there the live Q&A turns concrete and personal, working through two vivid stories — a poisoned question that could derail a person's entire path, and a single skeptic who destroyed a room of a hundred potential insurance buyers — to show just how much damage one voice can do, and what a person can actually do about it.

## Part 1 — Concealment Is What Lets Us Choose Bestowal

Rav Laitman opens by dismantling the idea that anything is truly "against" anything else:

> "In order to clarify one thing, something appears opposite it — that's the whole point. It appears that way because of our lack of understanding and the lack of a comprehensive view... although this is indeed how it appears, the purpose is for even the worst thing to become a support, a foundation for the next structure, in the next degree."

He extends this directly to concealment itself. Concealment isn't an obstacle placed between a person and Kedusha (holiness) — it's what makes bestowal possible at all:

> "The concealment exists in order to enable the lower one to acquire vessels of bestowal. For if there were no concealment, we would see the illuminated, perfect state where everything is known and everything is seen — then there would certainly be no possibility of detaching ourselves from the will to receive."

He's careful to locate exactly what's concealed, and what isn't:

> "Kedusha itself is not concealed. You can feel it. However, the moment a person gives up the pleasure of Kedusha for oneself, at that very moment they feel and see Kedusha... It is our vessels of reception that conceal spirituality from us."

The mechanism he offers is a choice between two channels:

> "If there are two channels through which we can perceive spirituality, the interference exists only in the second channel. It is as though because we want to receive for ourselves, we try to draw spirituality into that channel. But if we want to perceive spirituality not for ourselves, but in order to work with it as befits it, in equivalence of form with it, then we see it and we can work with it."

## Part 2 — From Curiosity to the Grace of Holiness (Chen)

Rav Laitman is blunt that a person cannot actually want spirituality in any genuine sense, at least not at first:

> "I cannot truly desire spirituality in and of itself, because it's completely opposite to me... What I really want is to get rid of suffering. I'm given all kinds of suffering in this world, and I have to escape from that suffering. So it seems to me that I'm attracted to spirituality because I want it. Really, I cannot want it."

What actually pulls a person forward, he says, is something smaller and more indirect — curiosity absorbed from the environment, which obligates a person to study and act:

> "Through study and hearing from the environment and society, together with my small will to receive in relation to spirituality — or let's simply call it a kind of curiosity which I develop by hearing from others that it's worthwhile, that it's abundant, that it's great — through that I become attracted to it. I obligate myself to study and to work, to do actions that help attract the surrounding light. When I attract the surrounding light, I receive the Chen d'Kedusha, the grace of holiness — something I've never had before."

He names this "grace" carefully — not yet a desire, since there's no real deficiency behind it, just an attraction:

> "It's not yet a desire, because there's no deficiency for it. Rather, it's simply endearing to me. That's all. That's really the feeling. There's no natural barrier to it. I simply love it, I'm drawn to it. There's something about it — Chen, grace or charm, that's exactly the right word."

This grace is what tips a person from working purely for themselves toward including some consideration for the Creator — the beginning of the shift from Lo Lishma toward Lishma:

> "That's called Lo Lishma, that brings one to Lishma. The transition is already there. It's like in the shattering of the vessels — in the vessel, there is a spark of Bina in Malchut. And through that spark, Malchut can later be corrected."

## Part 3 — Equivalence of Form: What "Good That Does Good" Really Means

Asked what the "goodness and delight" the Creator wants to give actually means, Rav Laitman ties it directly to the Creator's own degree, not merely a gift handed down:

> "The goodness and delight that the Creator wished to bestow upon His created beings refers to the Creator's own degree. There is no other goodness and delight than what He Himself is... Anything less than that cannot be called 'to do good to His created beings.' Anything less would simply be, so to speak, taking pity on the created being."

The goal, he says, is not to receive from the Creator or merely to feel Him, but to attain His actual state — equivalence of form:

> "We need to reach His state, not to receive from Him and not merely to feel Him... This is called equivalence of form, and this is called adhesion. As it is written, 'From Your actions we know You.' Through His actions, we come to know Him, we become equivalent to His actions, and through becoming equivalent to His actions, we simply exist on the same level, at the same degree."

He describes the mechanics of this correction desire by desire, framed around 620 individual desires matched to 620 lights:

> "I have 620 desires, and each desire needs to become equal to the Creator in the way it is used. I correct a particular desire so that it will be in order to bestow... So I end up with 620 such vessels that bestow back to the Creator, just as He, through the 620 lights, bestows into these vessels. That's what it means for the created being to become exactly like the Creator."

## Part 4 — What Slander Actually Is, and How to Work With It

This sets up the article's central question directly. Rav Laitman's answer narrows "slander" to a single target:

> "The evil tongue, or slander, refers only to what is against the Creator. It is not slander if I say something about you to a third person — that's actually good speech. But true slander is only in relation to the Creator, because in reality we have no relationship with anything else except our relationship with Him. Whatever prevents us from relating to Him correctly — any message, whether from our inner voice or from an external source, that can weaken or corrupt my attitude toward Him — that is called slander, or evil tongue... Ultimately, all disturbances can be called slander."

But recognizing this isn't a license to simply repeat pieties. He lays out an actual process: examine the disturbance, trace it to its source in your own vessels, feel how opposed it is, and only then can it be handed to the Creator:

> "You don't need to go around saying 'God bless' all day. You need to say it after you examine yourself and contend with this slander, with this evil tongue — after you understand where it comes from, from your own vessels, then you recognize its source, you descend to that source and feel how opposite it is and how it disturbs you from attaining the goal. Then you truly say that this slander is evil to you, because it is evil toward the Creator. Through that, you become united with the Creator with respect to that slander — and only then can you ask the Creator to correct it. Before you become united with Him with respect to the evil, you cannot ask Him to correct it."

## Part 5 — Q&A: Two Stories — the Poisoned Question and the Insurance Lecture

Asked what happens when the disturbance seems to come specifically through a friend, Rav Laitman answered with a personal story from his own early days with the group. A fellow disciple — whose grandfather had studied directly with Baal HaSulam — pulled him aside on the walk home from Shabbat prayers and voiced his own doubt about the path: "What process? You have to devote your whole life to this — maybe 20 years just to begin to feel and attain. Who can really go through with it?" When Rabash later heard what had been said to him, his reaction was immediate:

> "When Rabash heard what had been said, he wanted to kill him — he really wanted to kill him. He told me, 'This is slander. He injected poison into you. Now that you've heard it, that question will stay with you the whole path, throughout the entire path. You won't be able to get rid of it. You'll constantly have to struggle with it. You're not allowed to listen to things like that.'"

The lesson he drew from it was a standing instruction for how to treat this kind of talk inside a group:

> "This is truly called slander — it's something that weakens you, something that can plant another thought in you, another path, something that diverts you even slightly from the goal you need to reach. In the end it's entirely to your detriment... So you must not listen to such things, and if there are these kinds of rumors and conversations in the society, then you need to remove those immediately."

A second, unrelated story made the same point from ordinary life. In 1975, while serving in the army, Rav Laitman worked as an insurance agent on the side. At one recruiting lecture, a single skeptic in the audience began raising objections:

> "There was this one man in the audience who was against it. He began asking the lecturer questions and started introducing all kinds of arguments about why it wasn't worthwhile. He destroyed the entire room. There was maybe about 100 people, maybe 70 or 80 — not one of them wanted to buy insurance afterward... That really was slander in its purest form. Just look at how much damage one person can do. Because he goes along with your present nature, it's very easy for him to do that. Had he been speaking against your nature, he would not have been able to cause nearly as much harm."

The practical instruction that follows applies to both stories equally:

> "The moment you hear something bad, or something that can weaken you, leave that place immediately... We think, 'No, I'll overcome it, so I'll become even stronger because of it.' Nothing will come of that. It will remain inside you. It will return to you in other situations when you won't feel so heroic anymore."

## Part 6 — Q&A: Condemning vs. Slandering, True Criticism, and the Voice That's "All True"

A student asked whether slandering the Creator is worse than condemning Him outright. Rav Laitman said yes, and explained the difference in terms of where the negativity originates:

> "I condemn Him because I feel that I am not corrected — this is my natural response. If I'm displeased with life, it means I'm condemning Him... But it's me saying it from my own sensation of evil, and gradually I correct that feeling. But if I hear external slander about Him, then I catch an addition — a negative addition from outside. Imagine we assemble to gather our good attitude toward Him, our desire for Him, one from another — and God forbid each one takes from the other slander against the Creator or the goal. One such rumor can destroy a lot of work you've done beforehand."

Asked whether criticism inside the group could itself become a form of slander, he drew a clear line between purposeful and destructive criticism:

> "Criticism should exist, but very purposeful, focused, and creative criticism. We have gathered here to discuss a problem and how to fix it, how to improve it. Without criticism, without seeing the evil, it's impossible to advance."

The most difficult case raised was the hardest kind of "slander" to resist — the kind that's entirely true. Rav Laitman illustrated it with his own mother's regular phone calls:

> "She tells me, 'You're sick, you're weak, you have children, a family — have pity, have a care, worry about your health a bit more. How can you not sleep at night?' And all of it's true. It's a long list, but I don't want to... it's completely true, just except for one thing: that there is a spiritual goal. From the point in the heart, that she does not speak. She just talks from the heart about all these problems, and she's right. What can I do?"

Asked how to counter this kind of voice — since on its own plane it's simply correct — he said there is no direct rebuttal, only the choice not to listen:

> "You cannot destroy it. You don't have the power, because it speaks from nature. You can only ask the Creator to change nature, and by that He will replace your evil tongue with a good tongue. You yourself can never do that."

Returning to the insurance-lecture story, he was asked how such a voice could be neutralized in the moment. His answer was to use it, not fight it directly:

> "You cannot contend with it on the same plane where you speak with them, the normal human plane — he is correct... But you then take it in a way that's beneficial, and you see that it indeed came with the purpose of helping you see the evil. It needs to be under your supervision, and then it's very good — you see it as an aid against oneself."

## Key Themes & Takeaways

1. Nothing is truly "opposite" or "against" anything else — apparent opposition and concealment exist only so that even the worst-seeming thing can become a foundation for the next degree.

2. Kedusha itself is never concealed; only the direct pleasure available to the vessels of reception is under restriction. Concealment is what makes choosing bestowal over reception possible at all.

3. A person cannot genuinely desire spirituality at first — what draws them forward is curiosity absorbed from the environment, which leads to study and action, which draws the surrounding light and produces the Chen d'Kedusha (grace of holiness), the first real taste of Lishma within Lo Lishma.

4. The goal is not to receive from the Creator or merely feel Him, but to attain His own state — equivalence of form, corrected desire by desire (620 desires matched to 620 lights).

5. Slander/evil tongue applies only to what damages a person's relationship with the Creator; anything said between people is not slander in this sense. Correcting it requires first tracing the disturbance to its source in one's own vessels before asking the Creator to correct it.

6. Two stories — a friend's discouraging question about "20 years" of work, and a single skeptic who talked a room of 100 people out of buying insurance — show how much damage one voice can do, especially when it aligns with a person's own existing doubts. The instruction is not to fight such talk on its own terms but to leave it, remove it from the group immediately, and never assume you're strong enough to absorb it unaffected.

7. The hardest slander to resist is the kind that's entirely true on its own plane (illustrated by a mother's health-and-safety concerns) — it can't be argued with, only redirected by asking the Creator to change one's nature, or, in a public setting, turned into a tool for self-examination rather than an obstacle.

| Chen (Chen d'Kedusha) | "Grace" or "charm" — the felt attraction to holiness a person receives through the surrounding light, before any real desire or deficiency for it exists; the first sign of Lishma emerging within Lo Lishma. |
| Kedusha | Holiness; described here as never itself concealed — only the direct pleasure available to the vessels of reception is restricted. |
